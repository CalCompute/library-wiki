# Empire AI

## Why they matter

- The Assembly Floor analysis says CalCompute "mirrors a similar computing cluster that is already being established in New York state" ([2026-10-14 SB-53 Assembly Floor analysis, p. 5](../sources/2026-10-14%20SB-53%20Assembly%20Floor%20analysis.md)). The working group report also names it ([California Report on Frontier AI Policy 2025-06-17](../sources/California%20Report%20on%20Frontier%20AI%20Policy%202025-06-17.md)).
- In New York law, the Empire AI consortium is "the not-for-profit corporation created to construct and manage the institute". The institute is a state-owned facility at the University at Buffalo ([NY Economic Development Law 361 Empire AI (web)](../sources/NY%20Economic%20Development%20Law%20361%20Empire%20AI%20%28web%29.md), subds. 1(b) and 2).
- The FY 2026 New York budget gives Empire AI "$90 million in capital funding" for more computing power, SUNY access and new members ([Hochul Empire AI expansion 2025-05-09 (web)](../sources/Hochul%20Empire%20AI%20expansion%202025-05-09%20%28web%29.md)).
- New York's new Center for AI Responsibility and Research uses Empire AI "to independently test powerful AI systems" ([Hochul NY AI science and safety research launch (web)](../sources/Hochul%20NY%20AI%20science%20and%20safety%20research%20launch%20%28web%29.md)).
- Its terms of use limit principal investigators to faculty or staff of "a consortium member institution". Its systems "presently can only be used for non-confidential data" ([Empire AI terms and conditions of use (web)](../sources/Empire%20AI%20terms%20and%20conditions%20of%20use%20%28web%29.md)).
