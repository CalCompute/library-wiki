# California Institute for Regenerative Medicine

## Bottom line

- The California Institute for Regenerative Medicine (CIRM) funds regenerative medicine research in California. The Independent Citizens' Oversight Committee (ICOC) approves its awards ([CIRM allowable costs and cofunding FAQ 2025-04 (web)](../sources/CIRM%20allowable%20costs%20and%20cofunding%20FAQ%202025-04%20%28web%29.md), question 3).

## Why they matter

- CIRM "was created to benefit California researchers and organizations". Non-California organizations "may only apply to the CLIN2: Clinical Trial Stage Projects" ([CIRM allowable costs and cofunding FAQ 2025-04 (web)](../sources/CIRM%20allowable%20costs%20and%20cofunding%20FAQ%202025-04%20%28web%29.md), question 1).
- A "California Organization" must have more than half of its W-2 employees in California. Its Principal Investigator (PI) must be "physically located in California" ([CIRM common requirements and definitions memo (web)](../sources/CIRM%20common%20requirements%20and%20definitions%20memo%20%28web%29.md), p. 1).
- A for-profit applicant can give co-funding in cash, in equity warrants to CIRM, or in both ([CIRM allowable costs and cofunding FAQ 2025-04 (web)](../sources/CIRM%20allowable%20costs%20and%20cofunding%20FAQ%202025-04%20%28web%29.md), Warrant-Based Co-Funding Requirement, question 1).
