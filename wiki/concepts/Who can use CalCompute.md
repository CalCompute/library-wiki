# Who can use CalCompute

_This page reports what the sources say. It is not a position of the consortium._

## Positions

- **Statute.** (b)(1) "Fostering research and innovation that benefits the public" and (b)(2) "Enabling equitable innovation by expanding access to computational resources" ([SB-53 Gov Code 11546.8](../sources/SB-53%20Gov%20Code%2011546.8.md)).
- **Sponsors and author.** "academics and startups" ([SB-53 Assembly Floor analysis 2025-09-08](../sources/SB-53%20Assembly%20Floor%20analysis%202025-09-08.md)). "free and low-cost access to compute for startups and academic researchers" ([Wiener SB-53 expansion release 2025-07-09](../sources/Wiener%20SB-53%20expansion%20release%202025-07-09.md)). "AI researchers and smaller developers", with the community college system ([Wiener SB 294 announcement 2023-09-13](../sources/Wiener%20SB%20294%20announcement%202023-09-13.md)).
- **Ability to pay.** UC plans access that "does not depend on a campus's or laboratory's ability to pay" ([UC Regents B4 Update on AI 2026-09-16, p. 7](../sources/UC%20Regents%20B4%20Update%20on%20AI%202026-09-16.md)).
- **UC inter-campus recharge.** UC San Diego's TritonAI Gateway takes requests from its faculty, staff, researchers and sponsored student projects, and from other UC campuses "through an inter-campus recharge agreement" ([TritonAI developer API FAQ (web)](../sources/TritonAI%20developer%20API%20FAQ%20%28web%29.md)).
- **Florida.** The University of Florida's HiPerGator "can be used by faculty and their students and researchers and their collaborators at all Florida colleges and universities"; class allocations are free and research allocations are bought ([UF AI HiPerGator page (web)](../sources/UF%20AI%20HiPerGator%20page%20%28web%29.md)).
- **Ohio.** A fully subsidized OSC account needs "a full-time faculty member or research scientist at an Ohio college or university". Students and other colleagues "must be working with an eligible primary investigator" ([OSC supercomputers (web)](../sources/OSC%20supercomputers%20%28web%29.md)). "Commercial and nonprofit clients purchase services at set rates" ([OSC getting started and fees (web)](../sources/OSC%20getting%20started%20and%20fees%20%28web%29.md)).
- **Open research, no financial gain (ACCESS).** ACCESS allocations "are awarded for open research intended for publication together with support for educational and training activities". Users must not use ACCESS "for financial gain nor any unlawful purpose" ([ACCESS acceptable use policy (web)](../sources/ACCESS%20acceptable%20use%20policy%20%28web%29.md)).
- **Any U.S. institution, for-profit included (Texas).** The Texas Advanced Computing Center (TACC) sets a rule for each principal investigator (PI). The PI must work at "a US domiciled institution that is eligible for receiving and executing US federal, state, or local research grants", including "non-profit or for-profit entities". Users outside the United States can join "if affiliated with a US PI" ([TACC user policies (web)](../sources/TACC%20user%20policies%20%28web%29.md)).
- **Member institutions only (New York).** Empire AI PIs "must be faculty or staff currently employed and/or appointed by a consortium member institution and domiciled in the US". The Executive Director can approve an exception ([Empire AI terms and conditions of use (web)](../sources/Empire%20AI%20terms%20and%20conditions%20of%20use%20%28web%29.md)).
- **Any U.S. academic researcher, shared capacity (OSPool).** The OSPool "is accessible to any researcher affiliated with a US academic institution". "Capacity is allocated following a Fair-Share policy" ([OSPool service page (web)](../sources/OSPool%20service%20page%20%28web%29.md)).
- **Sector and region priority.** The F3i Supercomputing Center gives priority access to California-based "startups, researchers, and educators in agriculture and biotech — with priority for Central Valley users" ([F3i Supercomputing Center (web)](../sources/F3i%20Supercomputing%20Center%20%28web%29.md)).
- **California presence as the test (CIRM).** A CIRM "California Organization" needs more than 50% of its W-2 employees "domiciled full-time in California", and its PI "must be physically located in California while overseeing all project activities" ([CIRM common requirements and definitions memo (web)](../sources/CIRM%20common%20requirements%20and%20definitions%20memo%20%28web%29.md), p. 1). "Non-California organizations may only apply to the CLIN2: Clinical Trial Stage Projects" ([CIRM allowable costs and cofunding FAQ 2025-04 (web)](../sources/CIRM%20allowable%20costs%20and%20cofunding%20FAQ%202025-04%20%28web%29.md), question 1).
- **Open allocation.** Chips for Humanity passes compute to AI safety researchers outside the frontier labs "through a fair and open process for deciding who gets it" ([Chips for Humanity homepage (web)](../sources/Chips%20for%20Humanity%20homepage%20%28web%29.md)).
- **Approved researchers, restricted data.** California's Cradle-to-Career Data System uses the NORC Data Enclave, "a high-performing, user-friendly environment where approved researchers could access, share, and work with restricted-use data" ([NORC Cradle-to-Career data enclave (web)](../sources/NORC%20Cradle-to-Career%20data%20enclave%20%28web%29.md)).
- **Review by data level.** The Cradle-to-Career Data System (C2C) has three levels. Public tools need no review. Aggregate data gets an expedited review with "One month to receive a data file". Individual-level data needs "a legal agreement, IRB, proof of training, disclosure review, and final review" ([C2C Request Access to Data (web)](../sources/C2C%20Request%20Access%20to%20Data%20%28web%29.md)). Its draft intake form lists requestor types such as "Media" and "State Agency" ([C2C DTAB 5 research request and secure enclave update (web)](../sources/C2C%20DTAB%205%20research%20request%20and%20secure%20enclave%20update%20%28web%29.md), Attachment A).
- **Outside researchers on a UC data platform.** The California Policy Lab hosts projects "led by non-CPL or CPL-affiliated researchers within the University of California system and beyond" on its Secure Data Hub ([California Policy Lab hosted projects (web)](../sources/California%20Policy%20Lab%20hosted%20projects%20%28web%29.md)).
- **Email domain as the test.** On the National Compute Grid, "an eligible `.edu`, `.mil` or `.gov` address" gets a public research organization at sign-in. For other addresses, the FAQ says: "anything else is placed on the waitlist" ([National Compute docs Public Research (web)](../sources/National%20Compute%20docs%20Public%20Research%20%28web%29.md); [National Compute docs FAQ (web)](../sources/National%20Compute%20docs%20FAQ%20%28web%29.md)). The pool is "open to commercial work". A `.edu` organization shares its training artifacts at tier 2 "as a condition of the program" ([National Compute docs Public Research (web)](../sources/National%20Compute%20docs%20Public%20Research%20%28web%29.md)).
- **Federal mission alignment, open research only (DOE).** Access to the U.S. Department of Energy's ASCR computers "is open to the scientific and engineering research community including universities, industry, and the national labs" ([DOE ASCR accessing ASCR facilities (web)](../sources/DOE%20ASCR%20accessing%20ASCR%20facilities%20%28web%29.md)). At NERSC, a request "will be evaluated partly on the basis of alignment with a DOE program mission". "Purely proprietary research is not allowed" ([NERSC ERCAP allocation form (web)](../sources/NERSC%20ERCAP%20allocation%20form%20%28web%29.md)).
- **Vetted users, export rules (NERSC).** NERSC vets each prospective user, which "generally takes less than a week", and the project's principal investigator then approves the user ([NERSC user account policies (web)](../sources/NERSC%20user%20account%20policies%20%28web%29.md)). "Foreign nationals are generally permitted to access NERSC, provided they fully comply with applicable export control regulations". Access "is strictly prohibited for individuals or entities in embargoed countries, listed on restricted party lists, or engaged in prohibited end-use activities" ([NERSC Appropriate Use Policy (web)](../sources/NERSC%20Appropriate%20Use%20Policy%20%28web%29.md)).
- **Any researcher, by peer review (DOE leadership computing).** At ALCF, "any researcher in the world with a large-scale computing problem can apply for time", through "competitive, peer-reviewed allocation programs" ([ALCF 2025 annual report allocation programs (web)](../sources/ALCF%202025%20annual%20report%20allocation%20programs%20%28web%29.md)).
- **Emergency allocation by expert panel (2020).** For the COVID-19 High Performance Computing Consortium, "An expert panel comprising top scientists and computing researchers will assess the public health benefit of the proposed work" ([DOE COVID-19 HPC Consortium Q&A 2020-03-30 (web)](../sources/DOE%20COVID-19%20HPC%20Consortium%20Q%26A%202020-03-30%20%28web%29.md)).
- **Vetted users of a federal platform.** Executive Order 14363 tells the Secretary of Energy to "establish procedures to ensure the highest standards of vetting and authorization of users and collaborators" ([Executive Order 14363 Launching the Genesis Mission (web)](../sources/Executive%20Order%2014363%20Launching%20the%20Genesis%20Mission%20%28web%29.md)).
- **Free national user lab, by peer review (Switzerland).** "The computing resources of CSCS are made available to Swiss and international researchers free of charge through the User Lab" ([CSCS brochure 2026 (web)](../sources/CSCS%20brochure%202026%20%28web%29.md)).
- **Open calls across a region (Europe).** "European scientists and users from the public sector and industry can benefit from EuroHPC supercomputers via the EuroHPC Access Calls no matter where in Europe they are located" ([EuroHPC JUPITER launch release 2025-09-05 (web)](../sources/EuroHPC%20JUPITER%20launch%20release%202025-09-05%20%28web%29.md)). The AI Factories give "free, customised support" to SMEs and startups ([EuroHPC JU AI Factories (web)](../sources/EuroHPC%20JU%20AI%20Factories%20%28web%29.md)).
- **Principal investigators first (Stanford blueprint).** A 2021 Stanford HAI white paper recommends that eligibility "start with academics who hold "Principal Investigator" (PI) status (i.e., most faculty) at U.S. colleges and universities", plus agencies that contribute datasets. "Students working with PIs should presumptively gain access" ([Stanford HAI National Research Cloud blueprint 2021 (web)](../sources/Stanford%20HAI%20National%20Research%20Cloud%20blueprint%202021%20%28web%29.md), p. 12).
- **U.S. researchers, nonprofits and startups (NAIRR plan).** The task force for the National Artificial Intelligence Research Resource (NAIRR) wrote a federal plan. It says users "should be U.S.-based or affiliated with U.S. organizations, to include academic institutions, non-profit organizations, and startups or small businesses" ([NAIRR Task Force final report 2023 (web)](../sources/NAIRR%20Task%20Force%20final%20report%202023%20%28web%29.md), p. v).
- **Institutional email for industry projects (NAIRR Pilot).** The Deep Partnerships call is open to U.S. researchers in academia, nonprofits, government and "startups and small businesses with federal grants". It does not accept a personal email address ([NAIRR Pilot deep partnerships (web)](../sources/NAIRR%20Pilot%20deep%20partnerships%20%28web%29.md)).
- **Domestic users first (Japan).** From fiscal year 2024, ABCI gives compute "primarily for public use by domestic industry, academia, and government, particularly national research institutes and universities, startups, etc." ([ABCI about (web)](../sources/ABCI%20about%20%28web%29.md)).
- **Registered and approved users (India).** IndiaAI offers compute to "academia, MSMEs, startups, research community, governments, public sector agencies and other entities registered and approved by IndiaAI". Government entities are 26 of its 100 onboarded users ([IndiaAI compute capacity (web)](../sources/IndiaAI%20compute%20capacity%20%28web%29.md)).
- **Companies and researchers (United Kingdom).** The AI Research Resource gives compute so that "small and medium-sized enterprises and the research and development (R&D) community" can drive AI innovation ([UK AI Research Resource publication page (web)](../sources/UK%20AI%20Research%20Resource%20publication%20page%20%28web%29.md)).
- **Organizations, not individuals (NSF grants).** NSF takes proposals from "Two- and four-year IHEs (including community colleges) accredited in, and having a campus located in the U.S." and from U.S. nonprofit research organizations. State and local governments "may be eligible" when a solicitation allows it. "Unaffiliated individuals in the U.S. and unaffiliated U.S. citizens are not eligible to receive direct funding support from NSF." ([NSF PAPPG Chapter I eligibility (web)](../sources/NSF%20PAPPG%20Chapter%20I%20eligibility%20%28web%29.md), I.E).
- **Outside parties with student records (FERPA).** A contractor or other third party can work with education records if it is "under the direct control of the agency or institution with respect to the use and maintenance of education records" and meets three other conditions ([ED FERPA who is a school official (web)](../sources/ED%20FERPA%20who%20is%20a%20school%20official%20%28web%29.md)).
