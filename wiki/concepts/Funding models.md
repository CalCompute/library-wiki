# Funding models

_This page reports what the sources say. It is not a position of the consortium._

## Positions

- Under (j), UC may receive private donations only when CalCompute sits within UC ([SB-53 Gov Code 11546.8](../sources/SB-53%20Gov%20Code%2011546.8.md)).
- Executive Order N-6-26 lists programs that direct "a portion of revenue generated by AI companies" to public good ([Executive Order N-6-26](../sources/Executive%20Order%20N-6-26.md)).
- ACCESS gives compute "at no cost" ([ACCESS homepage](../sources/ACCESS%20homepage.md)).
- **Consortium cost.** The Government Operations Agency estimated "$2.5 million" for the consortium's work ([SB-53 Assembly Floor analysis 2025-09-08](../sources/SB-53%20Assembly%20Floor%20analysis%202025-09-08.md)).
- **Surcharge on oversight fees (GovOps report).** A Government Operations Agency report of Nov. 16, 2026 recommends that the state "impose a surcharge on the fees that large frontier developers pay for IVO oversight" to begin funding CalCompute. It says "the specific surcharge level must be worked out by the legislature" ([GovOps AI Safety in 2026 and Beyond (web), p. 50](../sources/GovOps%20AI%20Safety%20in%202026%20and%20Beyond%20%28web%29.md)).
- **University central funding.** Arizona State University's Enterprise Technology funds standard use of its CreateAI platform within monthly project limits, and "ASU may evaluate additional funding, allocation, top-up, or departmental chargeback approaches" ([ASU CreateAI usage costs (web)](../sources/ASU%20CreateAI%20usage%20costs%20%28web%29.md)).
- **Faculty buy-in to shared clusters.** Purdue built its community clusters "using funds from grants, faculty startup packages, and institutional sources", and RCAC pools "funds from different disciplines" ([Purdue RCAC Community Clusters (web)](../sources/Purdue%20RCAC%20Community%20Clusters%20%28web%29.md)). A buying group gets a priority queue "restricted to the number of cores to which access was purchased", and a standby queue "is open to anyone" for about 4 hours a job ([Purdue RCAC Community Cluster Queues policy (web)](../sources/Purdue%20RCAC%20Community%20Cluster%20Queues%20policy%20%28web%29.md)).
- **State-funded center with credits.** OSC "is always free for Ohio classroom usage, and academic researchers in Ohio qualify for credits that largely or completely offset fees" ([OSC getting started and fees (web)](../sources/OSC%20getting%20started%20and%20fees%20%28web%29.md)).
- **State budget line item (Ohio).** The Ohio Supercomputer Center (OSC) has its own General Revenue Fund line item of $5,086,000 in each of fiscal years 2025 to 2027. OSC is also "made available to private industry on a cost-recovery basis" ([Ohio LSC 2025 catalog of budget line items, Higher Education (web)](../sources/Ohio%20LSC%202025%20catalog%20of%20budget%20line%20items%2C%20Higher%20Education%20%28web%29.md), GRF 235510, p. 355).
- **Public-private partnerships.** "Governments are increasingly co-investing in large-scale data centre and compute projects through PPPs" ([WEF Rethinking AI Sovereignty 2026 (web), Section 2.1, p. 11](../sources/WEF%20Rethinking%20AI%20Sovereignty%202026%20%28web%29.md)).
- **Philanthropic gift.** "In 2020, The Robert N. Noyce Trust made a gift to the University of California system to launch the UC Noyce Initiative"; the page gives no amount ([UC Noyce Initiative homepage (web)](../sources/UC%20Noyce%20Initiative%20homepage%20%28web%29.md)).
- **Donor gifts for university buildings.** John A. and Susan Sobrato gave Santa Clara University $100 million for "a 300,000-square-foot, state-of-the-art facility for transformational STEM education" ([Santa Clara University Sobrato 100 million gift 2017 (web)](../sources/Santa%20Clara%20University%20Sobrato%20100%20million%20gift%202017%20%28web%29.md)). Lynne and Marc Benioff gave $100 million to build the UCSF Children's Hospital at Mission Bay ([UCSF Childrens Hospital Benioff 100 million gift 2010 (web)](../sources/UCSF%20Childrens%20Hospital%20Benioff%20100%20million%20gift%202010%20%28web%29.md)). Mark and Mary Stevens "have donated more than $100 million to USC programs and entities" ([USC Today Mark Stevens returns to Board of Trustees (web)](../sources/USC%20Today%20Mark%20Stevens%20returns%20to%20Board%20of%20Trustees%20%28web%29.md)).
- **Foundation grants for AI and data.** The Patrick J. McGovern Foundation "has awarded nearly $500 million in charitable contributions". One 2026 grant is "to build shared AI infrastructure for low-income household stability" ([McGovern Foundation Our Grants (web)](../sources/McGovern%20Foundation%20Our%20Grants%20%28web%29.md)).
- **Large California foundations.** The Hewlett Foundation "awarded $631 million in grants" in 2024, "nearly three-fourths" of it as flexible funding ([Hewlett Foundation about us (web)](../sources/Hewlett%20Foundation%20about%20us%20%28web%29.md)). The James Irvine Foundation "provided $160.2 million in grants in 2025 to organizations in California" ([Irvine Foundation about us (web)](../sources/Irvine%20Foundation%20about%20us%20%28web%29.md)). The Chan Zuckerberg Initiative has awarded "approximately $7.22 billion in grants" since 2015 ([CZI homepage (web)](../sources/CZI%20homepage%20%28web%29.md)). The Packard Foundation gives "a minimum indirect cost rate of 15-25% on all project grants" ([Packard Foundation history (web)](../sources/Packard%20Foundation%20history%20%28web%29.md)).
- **Philanthropy for state AI advice.** CCST's AI Science Residency Program "is made possible by philanthropic contributions including donations from Omidyar Network and Ralston Family Fund" ([CCST artificial intelligence program (web)](../sources/CCST%20artificial%20intelligence%20program%20%28web%29.md)).
- **Unrestricted early funding for nonprofits.** Fast Forward tells funders: "The biggest unlock you can provide is unrestricted, upfront capital that allows teams to experiment, learn, and build the infrastructure that makes scale possible." ([Fast Forward 2025 AI for Humanity Report (web)](../sources/Fast%20Forward%202025%20AI%20for%20Humanity%20Report%20%28web%29.md), Section 4). In a 2026 survey, "57% of executives report that their organizations have no AI budget in place" ([NTEN Bridgespan State of Nonprofit AI 2026 (web)](../sources/NTEN%20Bridgespan%20State%20of%20Nonprofit%20AI%202026%20%28web%29.md), p. 26).
- **Partner network support.** CCST "operates in partnership with, as well as receives financial and mission support from, a network of public and private higher-education institutions and Federally funded research institutions" ([CCST about (web)](../sources/CCST%20about%20%28web%29.md)).
- **Nonprofit compute buyer.** Chips for Humanity says: "We are an independent nonprofit, so funders can give specifically for compute." ([Chips for Humanity homepage (web)](../sources/Chips%20for%20Humanity%20homepage%20%28web%29.md)).
- **Pooled regional contributions with a federal share.** NSF's AI Infrastructure Hubs "leverage contributions from research institutions, philanthropic organizations, state and local governments, and the private sector". NSF invests in "each hub's consortium coordination, AI infrastructure workforce development and AI for science faculty training" ([NSF news AI Infrastructure Hubs (web)](../sources/NSF%20news%20AI%20Infrastructure%20Hubs%20%28web%29.md)).
- **Federal cooperative agreement.** The National Data Platform has a five-year, $24.5 million NSF Cooperative Agreement, one of two Category I awards in an "$83 million, agency-wide investment" ([SDSC National Data Platform release 2026-08-10 (web)](../sources/SDSC%20National%20Data%20Platform%20release%202026-08-10%20%28web%29.md)).
- **Federal operating supplement apart from acquisition.** In the NSF Advanced Computing Systems and Services (ACSS) program, user support and operating costs come in a separate supplement. NSF 23-518 sets them at "up to 20% of the total resource acquisition cost per year" ([NSF 23-518 Advanced Computing Systems and Services solicitation (web)](../sources/NSF%2023-518%20Advanced%20Computing%20Systems%20and%20Services%20solicitation%20%28web%29.md)). NSF 24-583 lowers this to "up to 15%" and raises Category I awards to "between $10,000,000 and $20,000,000" ([NSF 24-583 Advanced Computing Systems and Services 2.0 solicitation (web)](../sources/NSF%2024-583%20Advanced%20Computing%20Systems%20and%20Services%202.0%20solicitation%20%28web%29.md)).
- **Federal construction budget, then operations funding.** NSF requests $201,000,000 for the Leadership-Class Computing Facility (LCCF) in FY 2026, on a Total Project Cost of $457.45 million. NSF estimates that operations "will cost approximately $40 million annually", for an initial five years with a possible renewal ([NSF FY2026 budget request LCCF chapter (web)](../sources/NSF%20FY2026%20budget%20request%20LCCF%20chapter%20%28web%29.md), pp. MREFC-14 and MREFC-17).
- **Shared-services fee.** For Sherlock, "the annual cost of participating in Sherlock’s shared-services model may be comparable to the cost of adding only a limited number of staff". The release gives no figure ([SDSC Sherlock FedRAMP release 2026-09-21 (web)](../sources/SDSC%20Sherlock%20FedRAMP%20release%202026-09-21%20%28web%29.md)).
- **Vendor in-kind pledge to a federal pilot.** NVIDIA committed "$30 million in technology contributions over two years" to the NAIRR pilot ([NVIDIA NAIRR pilot contribution 2024-01-24 (web)](../sources/NVIDIA%20NAIRR%20pilot%20contribution%202024-01-24%20%28web%29.md)). A trade article says the DGX Cloud part is "worth $24 million" ([FedTech NAIRR pilot NVIDIA contribution breakdown 2024 (web)](../sources/FedTech%20NAIRR%20pilot%20NVIDIA%20contribution%20breakdown%202024%20%28web%29.md)).
- **Federal appropriations to many agencies.** The NAIRR Task Force estimates "$2.6 billion over an initial six-year period", with "$2.25 billion" for resources "through appropriations to multiple Federal agencies". It plans "a new $750 million investment" every two years ([NAIRR Task Force final report 2023 (web)](../sources/NAIRR%20Task%20Force%20final%20report%202023%20%28web%29.md), p. vii).
- **Vendor in-kind support.** Under a 2024 MOU, "NVIDIA to help enable access to cutting-edge AI hardware, software, and cloud computing resources for educational and research initiatives". The release gives no amount ([Governor California NVIDIA collaboration 2024-08-09 (web)](../sources/Governor%20California%20NVIDIA%20collaboration%202024-08-09%20%28web%29.md)).
- **Discounted statewide licence.** State departments "purchase Claude at a 50% discount through a centralized procurement program", and cities and counties get "the same pricing" ([CDT Anthropic partnership expansion 2026-07 (web)](../sources/CDT%20Anthropic%20partnership%20expansion%202026-07%20%28web%29.md)).
- **Startup cloud credits.** AWS Activate gives "promotional credits based on your startup's funding stage": up to $5,000 for a self-funded startup and up to $200,000 through an Activate Provider ([AWS Activate startup credits (web)](../sources/AWS%20Activate%20startup%20credits%20%28web%29.md)).
- **Nonprofit cloud credits.** AWS gives nonprofits and public libraries "up to $5,000 USD in AWS Promotional Credit" through TechSoup. An organization gets one grant each fiscal year ([AWS Nonprofit Credit Program (web)](../sources/AWS%20Nonprofit%20Credit%20Program%20%28web%29.md)). Microsoft gives an approved nonprofit a "$2,000 USD annual Azure Sponsorship grant" that does not roll over ([Microsoft nonprofit Azure grant activation (web)](../sources/Microsoft%20nonprofit%20Azure%20grant%20activation%20%28web%29.md)).
- **Vendor grants with cash and credits.** The AWS Imagine Grant gives U.S. 501(c) nonprofits up to $200,000 of "unrestricted financial support" and up to $100,000 in credit; educational institutions cannot apply ([AWS Imagine Grant program (web)](../sources/AWS%20Imagine%20Grant%20program%20%28web%29.md)). The Google.org Accelerator: Generative AI gave a "share of $30 million in funding" with Google Cloud credits. Academic institutions could apply ([Google.org Accelerator Generative AI 2025 open call (web)](../sources/Google.org%20Accelerator%20Generative%20AI%202025%20open%20call%20%28web%29.md)).
- **Research credits by proposal.** Nebius gives researchers GPU cloud credits; "The exact amount is determined individually based on each proposal" ([Nebius research grants (web)](../sources/Nebius%20research%20grants%20%28web%29.md)). Microsoft pledged "$20 million in Microsoft Azure compute credits" to the NAIRR pilot ([Microsoft NAIRR pilot pledge 2024-01-24 (web)](../sources/Microsoft%20NAIRR%20pilot%20pledge%202024-01-24%20%28web%29.md)).
- **Nonprofit seat discounts.** Anthropic gives nonprofits "a discount of up to 75% on Team and Enterprise plans" ([Anthropic Claude for Nonprofits (web)](../sources/Anthropic%20Claude%20for%20Nonprofits%20%28web%29.md)). OpenAI's Feb. 6, 2026 update gives "up to a 75% discount on ChatGPT Business or ChatGPT Enterprise" ([OpenAI for Nonprofits announcement (web)](../sources/OpenAI%20for%20Nonprofits%20announcement%20%28web%29.md)). Google gives Google Workspace for Nonprofits at $0 for each user and Business Standard at "75%+ off standard pricing" ([Google for Nonprofits Workspace offer (web)](../sources/Google%20for%20Nonprofits%20Workspace%20offer%20%28web%29.md)).
- **Cost-recovery fees.** The California Policy Lab is "funded mainly by foundation grants" and charges hosted projects fees that "merely recoup our costs"; UC approves the rates each year ([California Policy Lab hosted projects (web)](../sources/California%20Policy%20Lab%20hosted%20projects%20%28web%29.md)).
- **Commitments for buildout, then cost recovery (New Jersey).** NJSCI's initial commitments "are expected to fund buildout and the first several years of operations". The Executive Director is to implement "a board-approved cost recovery and participation model for partner institutions and other authorized users" ([NJSCI Executive Director posting (web)](../sources/NJSCI%20Executive%20Director%20posting%20%28web%29.md)).
- **State general fund to a public university.** State General Funds are "approximately 44% of the University’s current core funds" at UC. Most of them are not designated for a program ([UC 2025-26 budget detail (web)](../sources/UC%202025-26%20budget%20detail%20%28web%29.md), pp. 12, 17). "For every State dollar invested in research, UC expends approximately nine dollars from federal, private, and other non-State sources" (p. 71).
- **System budget request for shared compute.** The California Community Colleges ask for "$9 million one-time and $12 million ongoing Proposition 98 General Fund" for an AI partnership. The partners are UC San Diego, the San Diego Supercomputer Center (SDSC) and CENIC. The one-time part buys "inference-optimized AI hardware within CENIC AIR". The ongoing part pays "hosting, power, staffing, and maintenance" ([CCC 2027-28 Shared Advocacy Request Appendix E (web)](../sources/CCC%202027-28%20Shared%20Advocacy%20Request%20Appendix%20E%20%28web%29.md), p. 4; [CCC 2027-28 Shared Advocacy Request Executive Summary (web)](../sources/CCC%202027-28%20Shared%20Advocacy%20Request%20Executive%20Summary%20%28web%29.md), p. 5).
- **Pass-through with an administrative fee.** CDT bills users of California Managed Cloud (CAMC) the cloud provider's cost plus a 17% fee. Support is $801 for each virtual machine each month ([CDT California Managed Cloud CAMC (web)](../sources/CDT%20California%20Managed%20Cloud%20CAMC%20%28web%29.md)).
- **State earmark for a supercomputer center.** UC's 2023-24 table lists $2,550,000 for the "San Diego Supercomputer Ctr." ([UCOP 2023-24 state general fund allocations (web)](../sources/UCOP%202023-24%20state%20general%20fund%20allocations%20%28web%29.md), p. 1).
- **State capital and member match.** New York's FY 2026 budget gives Empire AI "$90 million in capital funding" ([Hochul Empire AI expansion 2025-05-09 (web)](../sources/Hochul%20Empire%20AI%20expansion%202025-05-09%20%28web%29.md)). In Massachusetts, six universities "will match" a $31 million state grant for AICR ([MassTech AICR builder selection (web)](../sources/MassTech%20AICR%20builder%20selection%20%28web%29.md)).
- **Member dues.** Linux Foundation members in each class "shall pay such dues, fees and assessments" as the Board approves ([Linux Foundation bylaws (web)](../sources/Linux%20Foundation%20bylaws%20%28web%29.md), Section 3.5). Project Jupyter's Executive Council raises and manages "all Project funding and sponsorships" ([Jupyter Executive Council page (web)](../sources/Jupyter%20Executive%20Council%20page%20%28web%29.md)).
- **Sliding-scale membership by budget.** Apereo Institutional Members pay from "$1,080.00" for an annual budget of "$0 to $25 million USD" to "$8,100.00" for "More than $4 billion USD" ([Apereo institutional members (web)](../sources/Apereo%20institutional%20members%20%28web%29.md)). Community membership is "available at no cost" ([Apereo join us (web)](../sources/Apereo%20join%20us%20%28web%29.md)).
- **Project-directed member funds.** Apereo Sustaining Members fund one project directly, and the funds "are managed solely by that project’s management committee" ([Apereo sustaining members (web)](../sources/Apereo%20sustaining%20members%20%28web%29.md)).
- **In-kind member contributions instead of dues.** The Genesis Mission Consortium has "no annual membership dues at this time", though its Executive Committee "may establish dues in the future". Members contribute "technical infrastructure, personnel time, data, or financial or in-kind support" ([Genesis Mission Consortium FAQ (web)](../sources/Genesis%20Mission%20Consortium%20FAQ%20%28web%29.md)).
- **Donated cloud credits.** In 2018, UC Berkeley's JupyterHub "subsists on cloud credits" from Microsoft and Google. The paper says that "The reliance on free cloud credits is further not guaranteed forever" ([SciPy Majora-4af1f417-002 JupyterHub paper (web)](../sources/SciPy%20Majora-4af1f417-002%20JupyterHub%20paper%20%28web%29.md), pp. 15 to 16).
- **National funding for a shared service.** Canada's Syzygy JupyterHub has a system manager "paid for by Compute Canada", with grants from the federal government ($4.5 million) and Alberta ($1 million) ([SciPy Majora-4af1f417-002 JupyterHub paper (web)](../sources/SciPy%20Majora-4af1f417-002%20JupyterHub%20paper%20%28web%29.md), p. 16).
- **Member-financed compute pool.** The National Compute Grid paper says: "The Grid is a private undertaking, financed and operated by its members, at no cost to the taxpayer." Members pay in Grid Credits for completed work ([National Compute Grid paper (web)](../sources/National%20Compute%20Grid%20paper%20%28web%29.md), Abstract and Section 6).
- **Data center capital by source.** Morgan Stanley Research estimates $2.9 trillion of data center capital spending for 2025 to 2028. It gives "$1.4tr Covered by Hyperscaler Cash Flows" and "$800bn Opportunity for Private Credit"; debt, securitized credit and other capital give the rest ([Morgan Stanley AI Market Trends 2026 (web)](../sources/Morgan%20Stanley%20AI%20Market%20Trends%202026%20%28web%29.md)).
- **Federal indirect cost rates.** On federal awards, "Negotiated indirect cost rates must be accepted by all Federal agencies". A recipient without a negotiated rate "may elect to charge a de minimis rate of up to 15 percent of modified total direct costs (MTDC)" ([eCFR 2 CFR 200.414 Indirect costs (web)](../sources/eCFR%202%20CFR%20200.414%20Indirect%20costs%20%28web%29.md), § 200.414(c)(1) and (f)). The text of Sept. 30, 2024 set that rate at "10%" ([eCFR 2 CFR 200.414 as of 2024-09-30 (web)](../sources/eCFR%202%20CFR%20200.414%20as%20of%202024-09-30%20%28web%29.md), § 200.414(f)). NSF's PAPPG 24-1 still gives "the *de minimis* rate of 10%" ([NSF PAPPG 24-1 Chapter II (web)](../sources/NSF%20PAPPG%2024-1%20Chapter%20II%20%28web%29.md), II.D.2.f(viii)).
- **No indirect costs on cloud (CloudBank).** CloudBank "is managed by UC San Diego (UCSD), which does not charge indirect costs (IDC) on cloud resources". UC San Diego and the University of Washington were among the first campuses "to adopt a policy of not assessing IDC on commercial cloud resources" ([CloudBank FAQ (web)](../sources/CloudBank%20FAQ%20%28web%29.md)). Its users agree not to "Run cloud services that will incur costs beyond the amount awarded in your CloudBank fund" ([CloudBank Acceptable Use Policy (web)](../sources/CloudBank%20Acceptable%20Use%20Policy%20%28web%29.md)).
- **No voluntary cost sharing on federal grants (NSF).** On NSF proposals, "inclusion of voluntary committed cost sharing is prohibited". "Mandatory cost sharing will only be required for NSF programs when explicitly authorized by the NSF Director, the NSB, or legislation." ([NSF PAPPG 24-1 Chapter II (web)](../sources/NSF%20PAPPG%2024-1%20Chapter%20II%20%28web%29.md), II.D.2.f(xii)). A computer service can be a direct cost "only where it is institutional policy to charge such costs as direct charges" (II.D.2.f(vi)(d)).
- **Need-based discounts for schools and libraries (E-Rate).** E-Rate discounts run from "20%" to "90%". The discount depends on National School Lunch Program eligibility and on urban or rural location ([USAC E-Rate Discount Matrix (web)](../sources/USAC%20E-Rate%20Discount%20Matrix%20%28web%29.md)).
- **Subsidies filed by a network consortium (CENIC).** CENIC calls itself "the largest E-Rate consortium in the United States". It files E-Rate and California Teleconnect Fund (CTF) requests for its members, and this "returns over $25 million in annual subsidies". In the state Broadband Infrastructure Grant (BIG), CENIC secured "$6,525,000 in federal E-Rate funds, reducing the net cost to $550,000, or 8% of the total construction cost" ([CENIC 2024-2026 Community Report PDF (web)](../sources/CENIC%202024-2026%20Community%20Report%20PDF%20%28web%29.md), pp. 26 and 38).
- **Federal grants for institutions by need (Titles III and V).** To be eligible, an institution needs an "enrollment of needy students" and Core Expenses below the average of its type. For 2-year public institutions the average is "$19,020" for each full-time equivalent student ([ED Title III and V eligibility notice FY2025 (web)](../sources/ED%20Title%20III%20and%20V%20eligibility%20notice%20FY2025%20%28web%29.md)).
- **State research grants with applicant co-funding (California, CIRM).** At the California Institute for Regenerative Medicine (CIRM), co-funding "may not include “in-kind” or similar types of support". For-profit applicants "may elect to fulfill all or a portion of the minimum co-funding requirement by agreeing to issue equity warrants to CIRM" ([CIRM common requirements and definitions memo (web)](../sources/CIRM%20common%20requirements%20and%20definitions%20memo%20%28web%29.md), p. 4). In the CLIN2 example, the co-funding requirement is "50% of total allowable project costs" ([CIRM allowable costs and cofunding FAQ 2025-04 (web)](../sources/CIRM%20allowable%20costs%20and%20cofunding%20FAQ%202025-04%20%28web%29.md), Warrant-Based Co-Funding Requirement, question 3).
- **State money with a nonstate match (California institutes).** For the four UC Institutes for Science and Innovation, "Every dollar of state funds appropriated for these institutes shall be matched by at least two dollars ($2) of nonstate funds" ([Ed Code 92900-92901 Cal ISI](../sources/Ed%20Code%2092900-92901%20Cal%20ISI.md), § 92901(e)).
- **One-time state capital, then a small operating line (California institutes).** In 2000 the state gave the four UC institutes "$170 million in one-time General Fund over two years" for facilities, then $4.8 million a year from 2002-03. UC reports core funding of "$16.6 million, consisting of state funds and campus funds" ([LAO 2021-22 Cal Institutes for Science and Innovation (web)](../sources/LAO%202021-22%20Cal%20Institutes%20for%20Science%20and%20Innovation%20%28web%29.md), p. 1).
- **Regional and national co-funding (Europe).** JUPITER "is fully owned and co-funded half by the European High Performance Computing Joint Undertaking (EuroHPC JU)". The German federal research ministry and the North Rhine-Westphalia ministry pay the other half in equal parts ([EuroHPC JUPITER launch release 2025-09-05 (web)](../sources/EuroHPC%20JUPITER%20launch%20release%202025-09-05%20%28web%29.md)).
- **Joint federal and state funding (Germany).** The Gauss Centre for Supercomputing "is jointly funded by" the federal research ministry and the ministries of three states ([Gauss Centre for Supercomputing about (web)](../sources/Gauss%20Centre%20for%20Supercomputing%20about%20%28web%29.md)).
- **National budget in three parts (Canada).** Budget 2024 gave "$2 billion over five years". The strategy gives up to $700 million to commercial AI data centres and up to $1 billion to public supercomputing. It gives up to $300 million to an AI Compute Access Fund ([Canadian Sovereign AI Compute Strategy (web)](../sources/Canadian%20Sovereign%20AI%20Compute%20Strategy%20%28web%29.md)).
- **Subsidised GPU hours (India).** Under the IndiaAI Mission, more than 38,000 GPUs "are available at ₹65 per hour" ([PIB IndiaAI document doc2026210784401 (web)](../sources/PIB%20IndiaAI%20document%20doc2026210784401%20%28web%29.md)). The IndiaAI table shows a subsidy and a bill of materials for each award ([IndiaAI compute capacity (web)](../sources/IndiaAI%20compute%20capacity%20%28web%29.md)).
- **Prepaid points (Japan).** ABCI users buy points in advance: "In FY2026, 1 ABCI Point costs 220 yen (tax included)." Points that are not used by March 30 expire, and "The usage fee will not be refunded" ([ABCI usage fees (web)](../sources/ABCI%20usage%20fees%20%28web%29.md)).
- **Multi-year national budget (Switzerland).** The Swiss Confederation funds CSCS. "Important investments in computing infrastructure are possible by carrying the investment budget over different years" ([CSCS brochure 2026 (web)](../sources/CSCS%20brochure%202026%20%28web%29.md)).
- **Income by source (Switzerland, 2022).** In 2022, CSCS income was CHF 57.9 million. ETH Zurich gave CHF 21.8 million. The ETH Board gave CHF 23.4 million for HPCN investments and PASC. "Services for paying customers" gave CHF 8.3 million ([CSCS annual report 2022 (web)](../sources/CSCS%20annual%20report%202022%20%28web%29.md), p. 78).
- **Cost-recovery tariffs for partners (Switzerland).** Under ETH Zurich's terms for CSCS, "The partner shall bear all costs related to the provisioning of HPC resources." ETH Zurich/CSCS sets the tariffs for cost recovery, and pay-per-use resources "may be available at a higher tariff" ([ETH Zurich RSETHZ 437.1 CSCS regulations (web)](../sources/ETH%20Zurich%20RSETHZ%20437.1%20CSCS%20regulations%20%28web%29.md), section 11).
- **Campus condo clusters.** At CSU Long Beach, faculty who invest in the central HPC "will receive priority to resources they purchased in addition to idle resources" ([CSULB High Performance Computing article (web)](../sources/CSULB%20High%20Performance%20Computing%20article%20%28web%29.md)). At Stanford, the Provost funds the base of the Sherlock cluster. PI owners add nodes with grant funds and pay "A modest one-time per-node infrastructure fee" ([Stanford Research Computing facilities (web)](../sources/Stanford%20Research%20Computing%20facilities%20%28web%29.md)).
- **Campus and federal co-funding of one cluster (Colorado).** "Alpine is jointly funded by the University of Colorado Boulder, the University of Colorado Anschutz, Colorado State University, and the National Science Foundation (award 2201538)." The page gives no amounts ([CU Research Computing Alpine overview (web)](../sources/CU%20Research%20Computing%20Alpine%20overview%20%28web%29.md)).
- **Faculty investor model (Florida).** At UF, a faculty member's investment "is converted to a resource allocation". Hardware investments buy equipment "for the full extent of its useful lifetime (5 years)", and service investments buy use for a shorter period ([UF Research Computing Groups and Allocations (web)](../sources/UF%20Research%20Computing%20Groups%20and%20Allocations%20%28web%29.md)).
- **Colocation recharge.** SDSC charges monthly recharge rates for rack space, for example "$572.60" a rack at the UC rate ([SDSC colocation services (web)](../sources/SDSC%20colocation%20services%20%28web%29.md)).
- **Campus funding for course compute.** UC Berkeley's DataHub reports cloud costs of "$5" for each student each semester. It is "completely free of cost" to instructors and students ([Berkeley CDSS DataHub page (web)](../sources/Berkeley%20CDSS%20DataHub%20page%20%28web%29.md)). The Campus AI Sandbox pilot studies cost drivers "to enable a sustainable funding model" ([Berkeley Campus AI Sandbox page (web)](../sources/Berkeley%20Campus%20AI%20Sandbox%20page%20%28web%29.md)).
- **Free campus allowance, no guarantee.** UC Berkeley gives faculty a free Savio allowance. "As a service provided at no cost to researchers, an FCA does not guarantee access to the full amount of SUs" ([Berkeley Savio Faculty Computing Allowance (web)](../sources/Berkeley%20Savio%20Faculty%20Computing%20Allowance%20%28web%29.md)).
- **Compute in kind with business co-funding (UK AIRR).** AIRR routes give "only compute resource" in line with the Subsidy Control Act 2022. For feasibility studies and industrial research, compute can be "up to 70%" of project costs for a micro or small organisation. "Academic organisations are not subject to this requirement" ([UKRI AIRR Gateway route guidance (web)](../sources/UKRI%20AIRR%20Gateway%20route%20guidance%20%28web%29.md)).
- **University venture fund with a profit share.** The Berkeley SkyDeck Fund invests "$200K into each startup that goes through SkyDeck". It "shares one-half of fund profits with UC Berkeley" ([Berkeley SkyDeck homepage (web)](../sources/Berkeley%20SkyDeck%20homepage%20%28web%29.md)).
