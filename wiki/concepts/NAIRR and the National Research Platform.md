# NAIRR and the National Research Platform

_This page reports what the sources say. It is not a position of the consortium._

## Positions

- **Origin.** SB 294 built CalCompute on Stanford's National Research Cloud proposal, which led to NAIRR ([Wiener SB 294 announcement 2023-09-13](../sources/Wiener%20SB%20294%20announcement%202023-09-13.md)).
- **Federal statute.** 15 U.S.C. 9415 set up a 12-member NAIRR Task Force. Its plan must name "an appropriate agency or organization responsible for the implementation, deployment, and administration of the Resource" and a plan to sustain it "through Federal funding and partnerships with the private sector". In the statute, NAIRR gives researchers and students "access to compute resources, co-located with publicly-available, artificial intelligence-ready government and non-government data sets" ([15 USC 9415 National AI Research Resource Task Force (web)](../sources/15%20USC%209415%20National%20AI%20Research%20Resource%20Task%20Force%20%28web%29.md), §9415(b)(2) and (g)(1)).
- **Stanford blueprint.** A 2021 Stanford HAI white paper recommends a "dual investment strategy" for compute: negotiated commercial cloud first, and "a pilot for public infrastructure". For structure, it recommends a Federally Funded Research and Development Center (FFRDC) first, and later a public-private partnership ([Stanford HAI National Research Cloud blueprint 2021 (web)](../sources/Stanford%20HAI%20National%20Research%20Cloud%20blueprint%202021%20%28web%29.md), pp. 11 and 13).
- **Task Force plan.** The task force's 2023 plan has "a cooperative stewardship model": one administrative home agency, a Steering Committee of agencies, and "an independent Operating Entity". It estimates "$2.6 billion over an initial six-year period" ([NAIRR Task Force final report 2023 (web)](../sources/NAIRR%20Task%20Force%20final%20report%202023%20%28web%29.md), pp. v and vii).
- **Free allocations.** ACCESS, another NSF program, gives compute "at no cost" ([ACCESS homepage](../sources/ACCESS%20homepage.md)).
- **CENIC AIR.** CENIC says CENIC AIR is the California part of the National Research Platform, built from compute and storage that CENIC members give voluntarily, and that "Any faculty member with a CILogin can get access" ([CENIC AIR initiative (web)](../sources/CENIC%20AIR%20initiative%20%28web%29.md)).
- **Network service for new nodes.** CENIC's 2024-2026 report says it "Successfully deployed Science DMZ as a Service at SDCCD and LACCD, enabling both institutions to quickly install CENIC AIR nodes and contribute to shared AI resources used by the community". A 2026 conference session says "CENIC AIR is now close to 2 years old" ([CENIC 2024-2026 Community Report PDF (web)](../sources/CENIC%202024-2026%20Community%20Report%20PDF%20%28web%29.md), pp. 31 and 37).
- **NAIRR pilot launch.** NSF launched the NAIRR pilot on Jan. 24, 2024, with "10 other federal agencies as well as 25 private sector, nonprofit and philanthropic organizations", in four focus areas: NAIRR Open, Secure, Software and Classroom ([NSF NAIRR pilot launch release 2024-01-24 (web)](../sources/NSF%20NAIRR%20pilot%20launch%20release%202024-01-24%20%28web%29.md)).
- **Vendor pledge to the pilot.** On the same day, Microsoft pledged "$20 million in Microsoft Azure compute credits", access to models through Azure OpenAI Service, and "Resources to enable HIPAA-compliant computing in support of health care research" ([Microsoft NAIRR pilot pledge 2024-01-24 (web)](../sources/Microsoft%20NAIRR%20pilot%20pledge%202024-01-24%20%28web%29.md)). NVIDIA committed "$30 million in technology contributions over two years" ([NVIDIA NAIRR pilot contribution 2024-01-24 (web)](../sources/NVIDIA%20NAIRR%20pilot%20contribution%202024-01-24%20%28web%29.md)). A trade article says the DGX Cloud part is "worth $24 million" ([FedTech NAIRR pilot NVIDIA contribution breakdown 2024 (web)](../sources/FedTech%20NAIRR%20pilot%20NVIDIA%20contribution%20breakdown%202024%20%28web%29.md)).
- **Hubs and NAIRR.** NSF's AI Infrastructure Hubs "are encouraged to integrate with national resources such as the NSF-led National AI Research Resource, providing the ability to surge and share resources beyond local capacity and enable dataset sharing" ([NSF news AI Infrastructure Hubs (web)](../sources/NSF%20news%20AI%20Infrastructure%20Hubs%20%28web%29.md)).
- **National Data Platform.** NSF gave the NDP "a five-year, $24.5 million Cooperative Agreement" under its Integrated Data Systems and Services program. The NDP builds on the National Research Platform, Tapis and CloudBank. The award runs from July 1, 2026 to June 30, 2031 ([SDSC National Data Platform release 2026-08-10 (web)](../sources/SDSC%20National%20Data%20Platform%20release%202026-08-10%20%28web%29.md)).
- **Start-Up allocations.** The NAIRR Pilot Start-Up call gives "three (3) months" on one resource, up to "2,000 GPU-hours", as "an ideal entry point for researchers who are new to NAIRR Pilot resources" ([NAIRR Pilot Start-Up projects (web)](../sources/NAIRR%20Pilot%20Start-Up%20projects%20%28web%29.md)).
- **Resource catalog.** The NAIRR Pilot request system lists 49 resources: 25 compute, 13 cloud platform, 10 program and 1 observing facility. Anthropic, NVIDIA, OpenAI, Groq and SambaNova say they are "currently unable to accept additional NAIRR Pilot awards" ([NAIRR Pilot resource catalog feed (web)](../sources/NAIRR%20Pilot%20resource%20catalog%20feed%20%28web%29.md)).
- **ACCESS and NAIRR.** 12 of the 26 active groups in the ACCESS resource catalog carry the feature "NAIRR Pilot", Expanse and Voyager at SDSC among them. The catalog lists the Prototype National Research Platform as "PNRP - COMING SOON" ([ACCESS resource catalog feed (web)](../sources/ACCESS%20resource%20catalog%20feed%20%28web%29.md)).
- **NAIRR shares on NSF systems.** DeltaAI at NCSA allocates "A portion" of its time through the NAIRR Pilot, with requests "reviewed on a continual basis" ([NCSA DeltaAI allocations (web)](../sources/NCSA%20DeltaAI%20allocations%20%28web%29.md)). Expanse2 at SDSC "will also contribute to the NSF-led National AI Research Resource" and enters production in 2027 ([SDSC Expanse2 award release 2026-09-16 (web)](../sources/SDSC%20Expanse2%20award%20release%202026-09-16%20%28web%29.md)).
- **Scale against industry.** A 2023 Brookings article calls the NAIRR commitment of $2.6 billion over six years "modest in comparison to overall spending by industry" ([Ahmed Thompson Brookings industry influence in AI research 2023-12-05 (web)](../sources/Ahmed%20Thompson%20Brookings%20industry%20influence%20in%20AI%20research%202023-12-05%20%28web%29.md)).
- **Contributed nodes.** NRP pools hardware "across more than 70 institutions". "Institutions contribute nodes and keep priority on their own hardware; when they are not using it, everyone else gets opportunistic access" ([NRP about (web)](../sources/NRP%20about%20%28web%29.md)).
- **CSU nodes on Nautilus.** The TIDE project adds 72 GPUs to Nautilus. CSU users get preferential scheduling on them, and "up to 20% of resources are available to other Nautilus users" ([SDSU TIDE technology (web)](../sources/SDSU%20TIDE%20technology%20%28web%29.md)).
- **Operations Center.** NSF says the NAIRR Operations Center will "coordinate participating resource providers" and "operate the national NAIRR portal". It is to take pilot functions "into the long-term national infrastructure". SDSC at UC San Diego leads it, with the Texas Advanced Computing Center. NSF says the pilot "has supported over 800 research projects" ([NSF NAIRR Operations Center update (web)](../sources/NSF%20NAIRR%20Operations%20Center%20update%20%28web%29.md)).
- **Deep Partnerships.** A NAIRR Pilot track pairs a research team with one industry partner for up to 12 months. "All project results must be open and publishable" ([NAIRR Pilot deep partnerships (web)](../sources/NAIRR%20Pilot%20deep%20partnerships%20%28web%29.md)).
- **Leadership computer in the pilot.** Horizon is "one of the resources available through the NAIRR Deep Partnership Opportunity". A PI can hold only one Horizon allocation, "either through this solicitation or through the NAIRR solicitation" ([TACC LCCF Horizon allocations (web)](../sources/TACC%20LCCF%20Horizon%20allocations%20%28web%29.md)).
- **New name.** The pilot's About page expands NAIRR as "National Architecture for Innovation, Research, and Resource-sharing". Its operations teams include an Allocations Working Group and a Coordination Working Group ([NAIRR Pilot overview (web)](../sources/NAIRR%20Pilot%20overview%20%28web%29.md)).
- **ACCESS governance.** The principal investigators of the ACCESS awards form its Executive Council. Its External Advisory Board has 11 members, nine from "an open nomination process" ([ACCESS governance (web)](../sources/ACCESS%20governance%20%28web%29.md)).

> ⚠️ Contradiction: [NAIRR Pilot overview (web)](../sources/NAIRR%20Pilot%20overview%20%28web%29.md) expands NAIRR as "National Architecture for Innovation, Research, and Resource-sharing". [NSF NAIRR Operations Center update (web)](../sources/NSF%20NAIRR%20Operations%20Center%20update%20%28web%29.md) and [NAIRR Task Force final report 2023 (web)](../sources/NAIRR%20Task%20Force%20final%20report%202023%20%28web%29.md) expand it as "National Artificial Intelligence Research Resource".
