# State AI compute clusters and the Empire AI model

_This page reports what the sources say. It is not a position of the consortium._

## Positions

- **The comparator.** CalCompute "mirrors a similar computing cluster that is already being established in New York state" ([2026-10-14 SB-53 Assembly Floor analysis, p. 5](../sources/2026-10-14%20SB-53%20Assembly%20Floor%20analysis.md)). The working group report names "New York's Empire AI" ([California Report on Frontier AI Policy 2025-06-17](../sources/California%20Report%20on%20Frontier%20AI%20Policy%202025-06-17.md)).
- **New York.** Economic Development Law § 361 sets up "a state-owned research and computing facility" at the University at Buffalo. A not-for-profit consortium operates and manages it ([NY Economic Development Law 361 Empire AI (web)](../sources/NY%20Economic%20Development%20Law%20361%20Empire%20AI%20%28web%29.md), subds. 1(b) and 2). The FY 2026 budget adds "$90 million in capital funding" for computing power, SUNY access and new members ([Hochul Empire AI expansion 2025-05-09 (web)](../sources/Hochul%20Empire%20AI%20expansion%202025-05-09%20%28web%29.md)).
- **New York, Empire AI Beta.** The Empire State Development (ESD) Board approved $40 million to buy the Beta system. The state part of the funding is "up to $340 million in State capital grant investment and $25 million over ten years in SUNY operating funding". Founding institutions and philanthropy give "more than $200 million" ([Hochul Empire AI Beta launch 2025-06-26 (web)](../sources/Hochul%20Empire%20AI%20Beta%20launch%202025-06-26%20%28web%29.md)). Beta came online in August 2026 with "over 300 research projects already queued up". The full-scale Gamma facility is to be complete "by the end of 2027" ([Hochul Empire AI Beta fully online (web)](../sources/Hochul%20Empire%20AI%20Beta%20fully%20online%20%28web%29.md)).
- **New York, AI safety research center.** The New York Center for AI Responsibility and Research at SUNY Binghamton "is supported by the advanced computing power of Empire AI". A "$30 million philanthropic gift" and "an initial $25 million research investment from SUNY" fund it. It is to test "frontier AI systems" and give the state evidence for "oversight of frontier AI Developers" ([Hochul NY AI science and safety research launch (web)](../sources/Hochul%20NY%20AI%20science%20and%20safety%20research%20launch%20%28web%29.md)).
- **Massachusetts.** Cambridge Computer, with Dell Technologies and VAST Data, builds the AICR environment at MGHPCC, with "hundreds of NVIDIA B200 and RTX GPUs". A $31 million state grant and a match from six universities fund it. "By 2030, the total funding is expected to exceed $120 million" ([MassTech AICR builder selection (web)](../sources/MassTech%20AICR%20builder%20selection%20%28web%29.md)). MGHPCC itself "is operated by the Massachusetts Green High Performance Computing Center Inc., a 501(3)c" whose members are six universities ([MGHPCC governance (web)](../sources/MGHPCC%20governance%20%28web%29.md)). MGHPCC bought AICR by a request for proposals that "covers the first of 3 tranches of funding", with a response deadline of April 17, 2025 ([MGHPCC AICR RFP release 2025-03-25 (web)](../sources/MGHPCC%20AICR%20RFP%20release%202025-03-25%20%28web%29.md)).
- **Oregon.** House Bill 5005 of 2023 authorizes $72,680,000 in state bonds for the Oregon State University Collaborative Innovation Complex. Matching funds must be "at least equal to" the bonds ([Oregon HB 5005 2023 A-Engrossed (web)](../sources/Oregon%20HB%205005%202023%20A-Engrossed%20%28web%29.md), sections 1 and 9). A $50 million gift from Jen-Hsun and Lori Huang started the $213 million complex. It is to house a supercomputer "powered by next-generation NVIDIA CPUs, GPUs and networking" ([Oregon State CIC 72 million legislature release (web)](../sources/Oregon%20State%20CIC%2072%20million%20legislature%20release%20%28web%29.md)). The university says the complex "is different from a commercial data center" and will mix on-site, cloud and external computing ([Oregon State Huang Complex FAQ (web)](../sources/Oregon%20State%20Huang%20Complex%20FAQ%20%28web%29.md)).
- **Pennsylvania.** The state budget gave the Pittsburgh Supercomputing Center (PSC) $500,000 in 2020-21 and again in 2021-22 for workforce development and outreach. A $1 million state capital grant helped pay for power and cooling for Bridges-2 ([PSC state funding release 2021-01-21 (web)](../sources/PSC%20state%20funding%20release%202021-01-21%20%28web%29.md); [PSC state funding release 2021-07-12 (web)](../sources/PSC%20state%20funding%20release%202021-07-12%20%28web%29.md)). PSC says that federal and foundation grants "fund PSC’s supercomputers" ([PSC state funding release 2021-01-21 (web)](../sources/PSC%20state%20funding%20release%202021-01-21%20%28web%29.md)). A "$10 million NSF award" funds Bridges-3. Its time goes "primarily" through ACCESS and the NAIRR Pilot ([CMU NSF award for Bridges-3 2026-07 (web)](../sources/CMU%20NSF%20award%20for%20Bridges-3%202026-07%20%28web%29.md)).
- **Indiana (campus model).** Purdue's Rosen Center for Advanced Computing (RCAC) runs a "community cluster program" of more than 20 years ([Purdue RCAC 2025 annual report (web)](../sources/Purdue%20RCAC%202025%20annual%20report%20%28web%29.md)). Researchers buy Gautschi access "in units of 48-cores or one quarter share of a CPU node or 5 GPU-year package for GPU nodes" ([Purdue RCAC Gautschi overview (web)](../sources/Purdue%20RCAC%20Gautschi%20overview%20%28web%29.md)). The report states no State of Indiana funding.
- **Illinois (campus program).** Illinois Computes is "Supported by $30 million in funding from campus", and the University of Illinois System gave "An additional $20 million". Illinois Computes "supplied 20 supplemental nodes" to the NSF-funded DeltaAI, and "Additional funding for DeltaAI comes from the State of Illinois" ([NCSA DeltaAI launch release 2024-11-18 (web)](../sources/NCSA%20DeltaAI%20launch%20release%202024-11-18%20%28web%29.md)).
- **Texas.** The Texas A&M University System's VISION supercomputer, a $45 million NVIDIA DGX H200 SuperPOD, gives "our 12 universities and eight state agencies access to computing power that most institutions could never build alone" ([Texas A&M System VISION supercomputer TOP500 (web)](../sources/Texas%20A%26M%20System%20VISION%20supercomputer%20TOP500%20%28web%29.md)).
- **New Jersey.** New Jersey's Supercomputer Initiative is a "more than $125 million public-private partnership", to be "administered starting in 2027 by NJSCI, an independent nonprofit organization" ([NJ Governor release 2026-10-20 (web)](../sources/NJ%20Governor%20release%202026-10-20%20%28web%29.md)). The state's share comes from a supplemental appropriation of $25,000,000. The NJEDA uses it "to provide a grant to an institution of higher education" for host costs, "including system management, rental payments, salary costs of engineers, researchers, and support staff, security, user access, and research support" ([NJ PL 2025 c404 supplemental appropriation (web)](../sources/NJ%20PL%202025%20c404%20supplemental%20appropriation%20%28web%29.md), sec. 1). The Executive Director posting says that NJSCI works under "a partner agreement with the New Jersey Innovation Authority (NJIA) on an interim basis" and is to "Incorporate and spin out NJSCI as a new independent nonprofit organization" ([NJSCI Executive Director posting (web)](../sources/NJSCI%20Executive%20Director%20posting%20%28web%29.md)).
- **Maryland.** Maryland's Chapter 633 of 2026 puts a Maryland Artificial Intelligence Partnership in the University System of Maryland, and its hubs shall "FACILITATE REMOTE, CLOUD–BASED ACCESS TO CRITICAL INFRASTRUCTURE, INCLUDING LEVERAGING THE SIGNIFICANT COMPUTING POWER OF COLLEGES AND UNIVERSITIES" ([Maryland Chapter 633 of 2026 SB 597 (web)](../sources/Maryland%20Chapter%20633%20of%202026%20SB%20597%20%28web%29.md)).
- **Ohio.** The Ohio General Assembly funds the Ohio Supercomputer Center (OSC) "through the Ohio Department of Higher Education", and Ohio State "serves as the fiscal agent for the Center" ([OSC governance (web)](../sources/OSC%20governance%20%28web%29.md)). Its director writes that OSC, "used by more than 7,715 clients in Ohio", gives "an estimated $50 million in annual cost savings compared to alternative providers" ([OSC 2025-2026 research report (web)](../sources/OSC%202025-2026%20research%20report%20%28web%29.md)).
- **Ohio, budget line items.** In each of fiscal years 2025 to 2027, the state's General Revenue Fund gives OSC $5,086,000. It gives OARnet, the networking division, $3,568,000 ([Ohio LSC 2025 catalog of budget line items, Higher Education (web)](../sources/Ohio%20LSC%202025%20catalog%20of%20budget%20line%20items%2C%20Higher%20Education%20%28web%29.md), pp. 355 and 364). Ohio Revised Code 3333.04(T) tells the chancellor to "Appoint consortia of college and university personnel" for these efforts. Appropriated funds go "to the fiscal agents" ([Ohio Revised Code 3333.04 (web)](../sources/Ohio%20Revised%20Code%203333.04%20%28web%29.md)).
- **Federal regional hubs.** NSF's "$100 million initiative" funds "flexible state and multistate regional consortia" that "build and operate cutting-edge scientific compute for researchers, students and educators in the region". NSF "will initially support up to 10 AI Infrastructure Hubs, with one award made per state or region" ([NSF news AI Infrastructure Hubs (web)](../sources/NSF%20news%20AI%20Infrastructure%20Hubs%20%28web%29.md)).
