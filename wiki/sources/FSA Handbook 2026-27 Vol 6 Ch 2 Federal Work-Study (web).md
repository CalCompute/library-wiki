## Bottom line

- The chapter gives the rules for Federal Work-Study (FWS). Students with financial need work on campus, or off campus for public agencies, nonprofits or some for-profit employers.
- The federal share of FWS wages is generally 75 percent at most. It can be 90 percent for a public agency or nonprofit that cannot afford the cost. At a for-profit employer it is 50 percent at most.

## Background

FSA publishes the handbook for schools that run federal student aid programs. Volume 6 covers campus-based programs. Chapter 2 covers FWS, the community service requirement, the Job Location and Development (JLD) Program and the Work-Colleges program. It cites 34 CFR Part 675.

## Key claims

- "To be eligible for a Federal Work-Study (FWS) job, a student must meet all general eligibility criteria and must have financial need." (Federal Work-Study (FWS))
- "You may also arrange for your school's FWS recipients to be employed off campus by federal, state, or local public agencies or certain private nonprofit or for-profit organizations. Off-campus FWS jobs with federal, state, or local public agencies or private nonprofit organizations must be in the public interest." (FWS Jobs and Employers)
- "In a normal year, your school must use at least 7% of its FWS allocation to employ students in community service jobs" (FWS Jobs and Employers)
- "In general, the federal share of FWS wages paid to a student may not exceed 75%." (Federal & Nonfederal Share of FWS Wages)
- "The federal share can be as much as 90% (and the nonfederal share can be as little as 10%) for students employed at a private nonprofit organization, or at a federal, state, or local public organization or agency under the circumstances described in this paragraph." (Federal & Nonfederal Share of FWS Wages)
- "For off-campus FWS jobs with private for-profit organizations, the federal share of wages paid to students is limited to 50%." (Federal & Nonfederal Share of FWS Wages)

## Evidence and method

Federal program guidance for schools. It restates the regulations with examples and calculations. It gives no data on how many students work in public agencies.

## Limits

The chapter does not give award amounts by school or state. It does not discuss jobs in research computing or AI. The 90 percent rule applies only to organizations "unable to afford the cost of this employment". It covers no more than 10 percent of a school's FWS students.
