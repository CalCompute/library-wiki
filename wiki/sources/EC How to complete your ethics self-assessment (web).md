# EC How to complete your ethics self-assessment (web)

## Citation

European Commission. "EU Grants: How to complete your ethics self-assessment." Version 3.0. Cover date 15 September 2026; page headers and the history table give 15.10.2026. Funding and Tenders Portal guidance. https://ec.europa.eu/info/funding-tenders/opportunities/docs/2021-2027/common/guidance/how-to-complete-your-ethics-self-assessment_en.pdf. Read 2026-11-19.

## Bottom line

- The guide tells applicants to EU grant programmes how to find and deal with ethics issues in a proposal. It is mainly for Horizon Europe, Digital Europe and the European Defence Fund.
- The ethics self-assessment becomes part of the Grant Agreement, so it can create binding duties that the Commission can check later.
- Section 8 covers artificial intelligence. A project must follow the EU Artificial Intelligence Act 2024/1689, and its approach must rest on six prerequisites for ethically sound AI.

## Background

The European Commission publishes the guide for its grant programmes of 2021 to 2027. Version 1.0 came out on 05.03.2021, and version 3.0 updates guidance, law and links. The guide has ten issue areas, from human embryos to the potential misuse of results. For each area it gives background, how to address the issues, and a checklist of documents to keep on file. It is a "how to" guide and not the ethics rules themselves.

## Key claims

- "The ethics self-assessment will become part of your Grant Agreement (in Annex 1, as description of the action, ethics requirements, etc.) and may thus give rise to binding obligations that may later on be checked through ethics checks, reviews or audits." (Important notice)
- "Normally, ethics supporting documents (e.g. copies of ethics approvals, authorisations, permissions, licenses, templates –mentioned below under 'ethics issues checklist') do NOT need to be submitted as part of the proposal, only kept on file and submitted on request." (Important notice)
- "Your project will have to comply with the EU Artificial Intelligence Act 2024/16899. Normally, AI systems developed solely for scientific research will fall outside its scope (see art 2), but it may still apply if the project eventually leads to products that are intended for placement on the EU market or involves testing in real-world conditions." (8.1, p. 37; "16899" is the guide's footnote 9 run into the number)
- "It is therefore important to ensure ethics compliance even in cases where your project does not develop itself an AI based system/technique." (8.1, p. 37)
- "Any use of AI systems or techniques should be clearly described in the project and you must demonstrate their technical robustness and safety (they must be dependable and resilient to changes)." (8.2, p. 37)
- "AI systems must not subordinate, coerce, deceive or manipulate people, and should not create attachment or stimulate addiction." (8.2, Human agency and oversight, p. 38)
- The developed or used AI solutions must "ensure that people are aware they are interacting with an AI system and are informed (in a language and terms understandable by all) about its abilities, limitations, risks and benefits." (8.2, p. 39)

## Evidence and method

An official guidance document for grant applicants. It explains duties and gives checklists. It draws its six AI prerequisites from the Ethics Guidelines for Trustworthy AI of the Commission's High Level Expert Group on AI. It gives no data.

## Limits

This page reads the Important notice, the history of changes and section 8 (artificial intelligence) only. Sections 1 to 7, 9 and 10, including personal data and the misuse of results, were not read. The guide applies to EU grants, not to U.S. or California funding. It does not cover access to computing resources. The cover date (15 September 2026) and the version date (15.10.2026) do not agree.
