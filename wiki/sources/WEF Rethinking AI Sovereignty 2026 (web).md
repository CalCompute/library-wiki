# WEF Rethinking AI Sovereignty 2026 (web)

## Citation

World Economic Forum, AI Global Alliance, in collaboration with Bain & Company. "Rethinking AI Sovereignty: Pathways to Competitiveness through Strategic Investments." White paper, World Economic Forum, January 2026. https://www3.weforum.org/docs/WEF_Rethinking_AI_Sovereignty_Pathways_to_Competitiveness_through_Strategic_Investments_2026.pdf. Read 2026-11-09.

## Bottom line

- The paper says that most economies cannot own the full AI value chain. It says they should invest in their strengths and use partnerships for the rest.
- It reports that governments co-invest in data centre and compute projects through public-private partnerships (PPPs). It names shared regional facilities as one way to scale.

## Background

The World Economic Forum (WEF) AI Global Alliance wrote the paper with Bain & Company, a consulting firm. It builds on the WEF's January 2025 white paper "Blueprint for Intelligent Economies". The paper estimates investment across seven parts of the AI value chain, from energy and raw materials to applications. It then gives five "pathways" for economies with different starting points.

## Key claims

- "AI sovereignty refers to the ability of economies to shape, deploy and govern AI ecosystems in accordance with their own values, whilst ensuring strategic and operational control, flexibility and, ultimately, resilience through a combination of localized investment and trusted international collaboration." (Executive summary, p. 4)
- "Based on investment patterns, however, “AI sovereignty” has been conflated with “AI infrastructure” and data centres." (Executive summary, p. 4)
- "While infrastructure-based AI sovereignty is out of reach for most economies, there are different paths to AI competitiveness." (Executive summary, p. 4)
- "Partnership, not ownership, is a key path forward." (Executive summary, p. 4)
- "To date, more than half of global AI-related investment has been directed towards AI infrastructure, as well as applications and services (Figure 2)." (Section 1.1, p. 6)
- "Governments are increasingly co-investing in large-scale data centre and compute projects through PPPs (e.g. the IndiaAI Mission’s national compute grid18). AI infrastructure is thereby increasingly viewed as a strategic public asset of comparable importance to traditional infrastructure investments such as highways or utilities." (Section 2.1, p. 11)
- "For instance, 87% of US site selectors reported that land, labour or utility shortages had impacted project timelines in 2024." (Section 2.2, p. 12)
- "A balanced investment approach can build domestic capacity for priority workloads while using regional or shared facilities to scale." (Section 2.2, p. 12)

## Evidence and method

This is a white paper with investment estimates. The estimates for 2010 to 2024 come from capital and research spending in public sources, as the note to Figure 2 says. The sources include the International Monetary Fund (IMF), the International Energy Agency (IEA), Epoch AI and Bain & Company. The paper gives ranges, for example "$600 billion" or more for AI infrastructure from 2010 to 2024. It uses case studies of countries, for example Singapore.

## Limits

The paper is about national economies. It does not name California or any U.S. state. It gives no cost for a public compute platform of one state.

A consulting firm co-wrote it, and the paper does not publish its estimation model. This page draws on the Foreword, the Executive summary and chapters 1 and 2. It does not report the five pathways in chapter 3 or the considerations in chapter 4. The paper is under copyright, with all rights reserved, so the library does not rehost it.
