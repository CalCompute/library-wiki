# Anthropic detecting and countering misuse Aug 2025 (web)

## Citation

Anthropic. "Detecting and countering misuse of AI: August 2025." Company news post, Aug. 27, 2025. https://www.anthropic.com/news/detecting-countering-misuse-aug-2025. Read 2026-11-09.

## Bottom line

- Anthropic describes three cases where criminals used its Claude models: a data extortion operation, North Korean remote worker fraud and the sale of AI-made ransomware.
- For each case, Anthropic says it banned the accounts, improved its detection tools and shared indicators with authorities or partners.

## Background

Anthropic is an AI company and the developer of the Claude models. The post summarises its August 2025 Threat Intelligence Report, which is a separate PDF. The post is in the company's Security category. It does not discuss research computing or public compute programs.

## Key claims

- "Agentic AI has been weaponized. AI models are now being used to *perform* sophisticated cyberattacks, not just advise on how to carry them out." (introduction)
- "AI has lowered the barriers to sophisticated cybercrime. Criminals with few technical skills are using AI to conduct complex operations, such as developing ransomware, that would previously have required years of training." (introduction)
- "The actor targeted at least 17 distinct organizations, including in healthcare, the emergency services, and government and religious institutions." ('Vibe hacking' section)
- "We discovered that North Korean operatives had been using Claude to fraudulently secure and maintain remote employment positions at US Fortune 500 technology companies." (Remote worker fraud section)
- "The ransomware packages were sold on internet forums to other cybercriminals for $400 to $1200 USD." (No-code malware section)
- "In each of the cases described above, the abuses we've uncovered have informed updates to our preventative safety measures. We have also shared details of our findings, including indicators of misuse, with third-party safety teams." (Next steps)

## Evidence and method

A vendor's summary of its own threat investigations. It gives case descriptions, simulated example outputs and the company's responses. It gives no count of all misuse cases and no data on how often its detection works.

## Limits

The post covers one provider's commercial models. It does not describe misuse controls for a shared research computing facility, or what an acceptable use policy for such a facility contains. The full report is a separate document and is not in this library.
