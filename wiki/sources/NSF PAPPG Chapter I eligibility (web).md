# NSF PAPPG Chapter I eligibility (web)

## Citation

U.S. National Science Foundation (NSF). "Chapter I: Pre-Submission Information." *Proposal & Award Policies & Procedures Guide (PAPPG) (NSF 24-1)*. Web page, date not stated. https://www.nsf.gov/policies/pappg/eligibility. Copy: [`raw/web/2026-11-12 NSF PAPPG Chapter I eligibility.md`](../../raw/web/2026-11-12%20NSF%20PAPPG%20Chapter%20I%20eligibility.md) (original: `.html` beside it). Read 2026-11-12.

## Bottom line

- Chapter I.E sets who may submit proposals to NSF. Accredited U.S. colleges and universities, "including community colleges", are eligible. So are U.S. nonprofit research organizations and Tribal Nations.
- For-profit organizations, state and local governments, foreign organizations and other federal agencies "may be eligible" under conditions. Unaffiliated individuals are not eligible.
- Proposers "should allow up to six months for programmatic review and processing". Every proposer needs a Unique Entity Identifier (UEI) and an active System for Award Management (SAM) registration.

## Background

The address of this page is the PAPPG eligibility page. The page shows all of Chapter I of PAPPG 24-1. It covers how to submit, the types of funding opportunities and submissions and who may submit. It also covers due dates, registration and processing. A banner on the page names two policy notices, NSF 26-200 and NSF 26-202, that supplement PAPPG 24-1.

## Key claims

- Colleges: "Two- and four-year IHEs (including community colleges) accredited in, and having a campus located in the U.S., acting on behalf of their faculty members." (I.E.1(a))
- Nonprofits: "Independent museums, observatories, research laboratories, professional societies, and similar organizations located in the U.S. that are directly associated with educational or research activities." (I.E.1(b))
- Industry: "the Foundation especially welcomes proposals for cooperative projects involving both IHEs and industry." (I.E.2(a))
- States: "As programmatically necessary and as provided for in a solicitation, State and local governments may be eligible to submit proposals." (I.E.2(b))
- Individuals: "Unaffiliated individuals in the U.S. and unaffiliated U.S. citizens are not eligible to receive direct funding support from NSF." (I.E.3(a))
- Solicitations: "A program solicitation, however, may apply more restrictive eligibility criteria." (I.E)
- Registration: "If a registration is not active, an organization will not be able to submit a proposal." (I.G.2)
- Processing: "Proposers should allow up to six months for programmatic review and processing". (I.H)
- Precedence: "In the case of any inconsistency between the coverage contained in the NSF *Proposal and Award Policies and Procedures Guide*and 2 CFR (including 2 CFR part 200), the coverage specified in 2 CFR applies." (page banner)

## Evidence and method

This is federal agency guidance. It sets categories of eligible proposers and submission rules. It gives no data on how many proposals come from each category.

## Limits

- The page does not cover compute allocations. ACCESS and the National Artificial Intelligence Research Resource (NAIRR) Pilot have their own eligibility rules.
- [NSF PAPPG 24-1 Supplement 1 (web)](NSF%20PAPPG%2024-1%20Supplement%201%20%28web%29.md) changes I.E.1(c) from "Tribal Nations" to "Indian Tribes". It also bars NSF funds to a college with a Confucius Institute contract unless the NSF Director gives a waiver. This copy does not have those changes.
- A single solicitation can change these rules. For example, NSF 26-513 has its own eligibility ([NSF 26-513 solicitation](NSF%2026-513%20solicitation.md)).
