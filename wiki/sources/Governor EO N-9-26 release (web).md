## Bottom line

- Executive Order N-9-26 tells the Government Operations Agency (GovOps) to speed up the start of SB 813 and AB 1405. These are the laws on independent AI verification and AI auditors.
- GovOps, with the Governor's Office of Emergency Services, is to convene experts to recommend law changes. The proposals include onsite verification in frontier AI labs and a "kill switch" for frontier models.

## Background

The release announces Executive Order N-9-26 of Sept. 18, 2026, and links the signed order. It names "the Hugging Face attack" as one of the incidents behind the order. It asks Congress and the President to adopt California's framework. The library does not hold the order itself; [GovOps AI independent oversight and kill switch](GovOps%20AI%20independent%20oversight%20and%20kill%20switch.md) is the GovOps page about it.

## Key claims

- "Through the order, the Governor is directing the Government Operations Agency: to accelerate the implementation timelines for SB 813 and AB 1405, and, in consultation with the Governor’s Office of Emergency Services, to convene national experts to develop recommendations for potential changes to further strengthen state law" (Decisive action to strengthen AI oversight)
- "Require frontier AI companies to embed a designated independent verification organization onsite in their labs to conduct regular audits and evaluations." (Decisive action to strengthen AI oversight)
- "Advance the creation of a “kill switch” for frontier models, with the efficacy of the switch verified on an ongoing basis by an independent verification organization." (Decisive action to strengthen AI oversight)
- "Update definitions of critical safety incidents to include loss-of-control incidents such as the Hugging Face attack." (Decisive action to strengthen AI oversight)
- "The executive order convenes a group of world-leading experts to provide, within two months, a guide for California to reinforce and strengthen its AI safety and security laws." (body)

## Evidence and method

A government press release with political statements. It summarises the order but does not quote it. It describes the incidents in one sentence, with no detail or source.

## Limits

The release does not give the order's text, the experts' names or the new timelines for SB 813 and AB 1405. It does not mention CalCompute. "Critical safety incidents" is a term from SB 53's frontier AI sections, not from § 11546.8.
