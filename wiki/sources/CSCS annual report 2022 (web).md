# CSCS annual report 2022 (web)

## Citation

Swiss National Supercomputing Centre (CSCS). *Annual Report 2022.* Lugano, date of publication not stated (the report covers 2022 and cites a user survey of January 2023). PDF. https://www.cscs.ch/fileadmin/user_upload/contents_publications/annual_reports/AR2022_Final_WEB.pdf. Read 2026-11-18.

## Bottom line

- In 2022 the CSCS User Lab served 139 projects and 2,430 users, with 57,226,000 node hours granted. CSCS had 119 employees.
- Total income was 57.9 million Swiss francs (CHF). ETH Zurich gave 21.8 million, the ETH Board 23.4 million, and paying customers 8.3 million.
- International users took about 35 percent of User Lab use. Domestic researchers get compute mainly through the national calls, twice a year.

## Background

CSCS is the national supercomputing centre of Switzerland, in Lugano. It is a unit of ETH Zurich, the Swiss Federal Institute of Technology in Zurich ([CSCS organization (web)](CSCS%20organization%20%28web%29.md)). This report has six parts: key information, an activity report, the User Lab, science stories, staff insights, and facts and figures. In 2022 the main system was Piz Daint, and the new Alps system was in development. The year saw the end of the Partnership for Advanced Computing in Europe (PRACE) as an allocation platform.

## Key claims

- Mission: "Founded in 1991, CSCS develops and provides the key supercomputing capabilities required to solve challenging problems in science and society." (I Key Information, p. 6)
- Access: "The centre enables world-class research with a scientific user lab that is available to domestic and international researchers through a transparent, peer-reviewed allocation process." (I Key Information, p. 6)
- Industry: "CSCS’s resources are open to academia and are available as well to users from industry and the business sector." (I Key Information, p. 6)
- Key figures: "2022: 139 projects, 2 430 users"; employees "2022: 119"; investments "2022: 12.6 Mio. CHF"; operational costs "2022: 29.9 Mio. CHF". (I Key Information, p. 6)
- National calls: "Swiss computational and computer scientists in need of computing and data resources can continue to apply for up to 1 million node hours in the national Tier-1 calls, open twice a year, with projects starting on April 1 and October 1." (III User Lab, p. 18)
- Routes: "Like in previous years, the User Lab calls are still the primary path to resource allocation for domestic research institutions, but most of the international allocation has been granted in PRACE Tier-0 and CHRONOS calls." (III User Lab, p. 18)
- PRACE: "CSCS successfully completed its commitment as a PRACE hosting member, distributing 7.96 million node hours to 5 Tier-0 proposals." (III User Lab, p. 18)
- Software fund: "The Platform for Advanced Scientific Computing (PASC) funds HPC software development projects that address the broad availability, quality, and performance of software on GPU-accelerated supercomputing platforms." (III User Lab, p. 23)
- Income: "Contribution ETH Zurich 21 770 014.00"; "Contribution ETH Rat - HPCN investments 20 100 000.00"; "Contribution ETH Rat - PASC initiative 3 270 985.00"; "Services for paying customers 8 258 409.23"; "Total income 45 140 999.00 12 807 897.51 57 948 896.51" (User Lab, third party, total). (VI Facts & Figures, Finances, p. 78)
- Partner systems: "In addition to "Piz Daint", CSCS also operates the MeteoSwiss computer and other systems on behalf of partners and houses the ETH Euler computer and the Blue Brain computer." (V Insights, p. 73)
- Europe: "Because Swiss researchers and research infrastructures, like ours at CSCS, face exclusion from European research programmes, we are actively looking for new ways to shape collaborations in Europe and globally." (Welcome from the Director)

## Evidence and method

This is an institutional annual report. The finance table splits income and spending into "User Lab" and "Third Party" columns. Spending was CHF 42.6 million: 16.5 million on personnel, 13.5 million on material and 12.6 million on investments. Material spending includes 0.9 million in PASC grants and 1.2 million for the LUMI system of the European High Performance Computing Joint Undertaking (EuroHPC JU). The State Secretariat for Education, Research and Innovation (SERI) paid the LUMI share.

Usage tables give node hours by research field and by institution, from a total of 44,409,071 node hours. A user survey went to 2,441 users and got 360 answers.

## Limits

- The library read the director's welcome, key information, the activity report and the User Lab introduction. It also read the PASC list, the energy article, and the facts and figures. It did not read the project lists, publication lists or science stories.
- The report gives "User Lab" and "Third Party" columns but does not define them. It does not explain why income exceeds spending by about CHF 15 million.
- A table of User Lab expenses labels its last column "2023". Its figures match the 2022 totals, so the label looks like a misprint.
- The report says Alps "is scheduled to go into operation in 2023" (V Insights, p. 73). A later CSCS brochure says Alps started production in January 2025 ([CSCS brochure 2026 (web)](CSCS%20brochure%202026%20%28web%29.md)). The plan changed after the report.
- The report does not describe governance bodies. For those, see [CSCS organization (web)](CSCS%20organization%20%28web%29.md).
