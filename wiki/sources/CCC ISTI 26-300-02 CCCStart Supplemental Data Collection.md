## Bottom line

- The community colleges move from CCCApply to CCCStart. The memo limits the admissions form. It asks only what is "necessary to admit or process the student, establish the student record, or meet a requirement".
- Its test is a question:
  > "Is this information necessary at the time of admission, or can it reasonably be collected after the student has been admitted?"
- New questions go through a review by the Chancellor's Office and a steering committee.
- This is a data-minimization model for a CalCompute application form.

## Background

The memo is part of the Reimagine Apply initiative. See [CCC ISTI memos index](CCC%20ISTI%20memos%20index.md).

## Key claims

- "Questions regarding citizenship status are no longer included or appropriate for the statewide admissions platform" (p. 2).
- "Admission and onboarding are distinct." Information to "understand, engage, or support a student" goes to local onboarding (p. 2).
- "Historical collection of data elements, local interest in the information, or the possibility that information may be useful later does not, by itself, establish a need to collect it during admission" (p. 3).
- Before a new question, a college checks three things (p. 3):
  - another source has the data;
  - a "clear and documented business, regulatory, or institutional need" exists;
  - the data can wait until after admission.
- "Repeated collection increases student burden and can create conflicting or duplicative records" (p. 3).
- Process: the Chancellor's Office checks for duplicates, then the "Reimagine Apply Steering Committee" reviews and recommends. Approved questions go on an "Approved Supplemental Question List" for reuse (p. 4).
- The memo is from Craig Hayward, "Vice Chancellor, AI and Digital Transformation", with Renee Craig-Marius and Jory Hadsell (p. 1).

## Evidence and method

A primary policy memo of the Chancellor's Office to college officers.

## Limits

- About admissions, not compute.
- The approved list and the review process are not yet out. They "will be distributed as those resources become available" (p. 5).
