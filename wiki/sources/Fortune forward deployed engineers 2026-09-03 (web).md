# Fortune forward deployed engineers 2026-09-03 (web)

## Citation

Fore, Preston. "One of the fastest-growing jobs in Silicon Valley sends engineers straight to customers’ offices to get AI up and running—and pays more than $188,000." Fortune, September 3, 2026. https://fortune.com/2026/09/03/forward-deployed-engineers-fast-growing-six-figure-silicon-valley-job-integrate-ai-with-customers-tech-careers-palantir/. Read 2026-11-11.

## Bottom line

- "Forward-deployed" engineers work at customer sites to set up AI. Lightcast data show that postings for these jobs rose more than 1000% from January to August 2026, against the same period of 2025.
- The median advertised salary is more than $188,000, against about $145,000 for software engineers.

## Background

Fortune is a business magazine. This article in its Success section describes a job type that Palantir made common. It quotes the president of the tech career platform Dice and a Palantir job listing.

## Key claims

- "Job postings for “forward-deployed” engineers rose more than 1000% between January and August 2026, compared to the same period last year—and more than 4,600% compared to 2023, according to Lightcast data." (article)
- "That far outpaces the broader tech job market, where postings grew 13% year over year." (article)
- "Lightcast data shared by Farnsworth found that the median advertised salaries for forward-deployed engineers was more than $188,000—compared to roughly $145,000 for traditional software engineers." (article)
- "Microsoft, Meta, Google, OpenAI and Anthropic currently all have openings for forward-deployed engineering roles." (article)
- "The role requires a mix of technical expertise—including programming, machine learning, generative AI, and tech infrastructure—paired with the consulting, communication, and leadership skills needed to work directly with customers." (How to land a forward-deployed engineering job)

## Evidence and method

Trade press. The figures come from Lightcast job-posting data that Dice shared. The article does not publish the data or the method.

## Limits

The article is about private companies. It does not cover the public sector, education pathways or California figures.
