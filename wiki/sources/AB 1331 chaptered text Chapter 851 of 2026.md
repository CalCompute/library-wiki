## Bottom line

- AB 1331 adds Part 5.8, "Workplace Surveillance of Employees", to Division 2 of the Labor Code, from section 1560.
- An employer must not use a workplace surveillance tool to monitor employees in a workplace bathroom, unless a court order directs it.
- An employee can leave a workplace surveillance tool behind when the employee goes into a bathroom.
- "Employer" includes all branches of state government, cities, counties, school districts and special districts.
- The Labor Commissioner or a public prosecutor can enforce the part. The penalty is up to $500 for each violation.

## Key claims

- "“Workplace surveillance tool” means a system, application, instrument, or device that collects or facilitates the collection of employee data, activities, communications, actions, biometrics, or behaviors by means other than direct observation by a person, including, but not limited to, video or audio surveillance, continuous incremental time-tracking tools, geolocation, electromagnetic tracking, photoelectronic tracking, a photo-optical system, or other means." (Lab. Code § 1560(f))
- "“Employer” includes all branches of state government, or the several cities, counties, cities and counties, and municipalities thereof, or any other political subdivision of the state, or a school district, or any special district, or any authority, commission, or board or any other agency or instrumentality thereof." (§ 1560(d)(1))
- "Unless directed by a court order, an employer shall not use a workplace surveillance tool to monitor or surveil employees in a bathroom located in the workplace." (§ 1561(a))
- "An employee shall have the right to leave behind workplace surveillance tools that are on their person or in their possession when entering a bathroom." (§ 1561(b)(1))
- "In addition to any other remedy, an employer who violates this part may be subject to a penalty of up to five hundred dollars ($500) for each violation." (§ 1563(d))

## Limits

The bill gives no cost estimate and no operative date. The text has no section 1562. An employer can check a tool for "the one-time entry and exit of bathrooms" (§ 1561(b)(3)). A required badge or personal alarm is not a violation under two conditions. It records no audio or video, and it has no "physically embedded or attached artificial intelligence" (§ 1561(c)(2)). The part does not apply to some uses that federal aircraft or defense rules require (§ 1565).

AB 1883 of the same year also adds a Part 5.8 to Division 2 of the Labor Code, from section 1580 ([AB 1883 chaptered text Chapter 853 of 2026](AB%201883%20chaptered%20text%20Chapter%20853%20of%202026.md)). The two bills do not refer to each other.
