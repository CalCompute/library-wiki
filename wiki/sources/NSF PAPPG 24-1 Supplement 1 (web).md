# NSF PAPPG 24-1 Supplement 1 (web)

## Citation

U.S. National Science Foundation (NSF), Grants Policy Branch. "Policy Notice: Implementation of Policy Changes to Proposal and Award Policies and Procedures Guide (PAPPG) 24-1, Supplement 1." NSF 26-200. Issued and effective Dec. 8, 2025. https://www.nsf.gov/policies/document/pappg24-1-supplement-1. Copy: [`raw/web/2026-11-12 NSF PAPPG 24-1 Supplement 1.md`](../../raw/web/2026-11-12%20NSF%20PAPPG%2024-1%20Supplement%201.md) (original: `.html` beside it). Read 2026-11-12.

## Bottom line

- NSF 26-200 changes 16 parts of PAPPG 24-1 for awards made on or after Dec. 8, 2025. NSF deferred its planned PAPPG 26-1.
- It raises the caps for planning, RAPID and EAGER proposals, and it raises the equipment threshold from $5,000 to $10,000.
- Recipients must now "share all data supporting NSF funded publications at the time of publication". Research misconduct now includes misconduct through "artificial intelligence (AI)-based tools".

## Background

NSF issues policy notices while the next PAPPG is on hold. The notice says that Executive Order 14332 requires the Office of Management and Budget (OMB) to change the Uniform Guidance. To stay in line with it, "NSF will defer release of NSF 26-1 PAPPG". The notice lists its changes by PAPPG chapter. A page banner on the PAPPG chapters names a second notice, NSF 26-202, which the library has not read.

## Key claims

- Precedence: "The information set out in this notice takes precedence over existing policies and procedures in PAPPG 24-1. It applies for all financial assistance awarded on or after Dec. 8, 2025." (opening)
- Deferral: "To ensure alignment with the Uniform Guidance, NSF will defer release of NSF 26-1 PAPPG, previously developed and planned for publication in Fiscal Year 2026." (Background)
- Data sharing: "Recipients are required to share all data supporting NSF funded publications at the time of publication. Exceptions to this sharing requirement should be described and justified within the DMSP." (item 5)
- Planning proposals: "Requests may be for up to ~~$100,000~~ <u>$200,000</u> per year (including indirect costs) and for up to two years in duration." (item 6)
- Reviewers: "<u>Full proposals, unless excepted, must be reviewed by at least two reviewers, which can be conducted internally by NSF staff.</u>" (item 7)
- Equipment: "NSF may identify items of equipment having a unit acquisition cost of ~~$5,000~~ <u>$10,000</u> or more where NSF reserves the right to transfer the title to the Federal government". (item 13)
- Confucius Institutes: "no NSF funds may be awarded to an Institution of Higher Education (IHE) that maintains a contract or agreement between the IHE and a Confucius Institute unless specifically approved in a waiver by the NSF Director." (item 3)
- AI tools: research misconduct means fabrication, falsification or plagiarism, "<u>whether committed by an individual directly or through the use or assistance of other persons, entities, or tools, including artificial intelligence (AI)-based tools,</u>". (item 16)

## Evidence and method

This is a federal policy notice. It quotes the PAPPG text with additions underlined and deletions struck through. It gives the legal basis for some items, for example the CHIPS and Science Act of 2022. It gives no data.
