# UC Classification of Information and IT Resources (web)

## Citation

University of California Office of the President (UCOP), UC Systemwide Information Security. "Classification of Information and IT Resources." Web page, date not stated. https://security.ucop.edu/policies/institutional-information-and-it-resource-classification.html. Read 2026-11-12.

## Bottom line

- UC classifies its Institutional Information and IT Resources in four Protection Levels, P1 Minimal to P4 High, and four Availability Levels, A1 to A4.
- The levels come from Part III, Section 8 of UC's Electronic Information Security policy, IS-3.

## Background

This UCOP page summarises the classification levels and links to the full classification standard and to two classification guides. One of them is [UC Protection Level Classification Guide (web)](UC%20Protection%20Level%20Classification%20Guide%20%28web%29.md). UC San Diego's TritonAI services use these levels to say which data users can put on them ([TritonAI developer API FAQ (web)](TritonAI%20developer%20API%20FAQ%20%28web%29.md)).

## Key claims

- Source of the levels: "Below are the classification levels from Part III, Section 8 of UC's Electronic Information Security policy, IS-3." (opening paragraph)
- P4: "Institutional Information and related IT Resources whose unauthorized disclosure or modification could result in significant fines, penalties, regulatory action, or civil or criminal violations. Statutory, regulatory and contract obligations are major drivers for this risk level." (Protection Level table)
- P1: "Public information or information intended to be readily obtainable by the public, but whose integrity is important and for which unauthorized modification is the primary protection concern." (Protection Level table)
- A4: "Loss of availability would result in major impairment to the overall operation of the Location and/or essential services, and/or cause significant financial losses." (Availability Level table)
- Pre-classified types: "A systemwide workgroup has already classified many types of Institutional Information and IT Resources." (opening paragraph)
- The tables are samples: "Consult the Protection Level and Availability Level Classification Guides for definitive classifications." (note under the tables)

## Evidence and method

This is a university system web page with two tables. It summarises a policy and links to the standard. It gives definitions, not data.

## Limits

- The page calls its tables an "Illustrative sample". The full standard is a separate PDF, which the library has not read.
- The page gives no date and no revision history.
- It does not list the controls that each level requires.
