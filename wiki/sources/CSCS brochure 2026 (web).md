# CSCS brochure 2026 (web)

## Citation

Swiss National Supercomputing Centre (CSCS). *CSCS: An Engine for Innovation and Cutting-Edge Research in Switzerland.* Brochure, Lugano, 2026 ("© CSCS 2026"). PDF. https://www.cscs.ch/fileadmin/user_upload/contents_publications/factsheets/brochure/CSCS_DI2026_EN_Final.pdf. Read 2026-11-11.

## Bottom line

- The Swiss National Supercomputing Centre (CSCS) in Lugano is a national high-performance computing (HPC) centre. ETH Zurich operates it, and the Swiss Confederation funds it.
- Its User Lab gives compute free of charge to Swiss and international researchers, through peer review.
- Its main supercomputer is Alps, an HPE Cray EX system, in production since January 2025.

## Background

CSCS dates from 1991. It has 140 employees from 25 countries. This brochure is a short fact sheet about the centre: staff, budget, user programs, building, cooling, storage, network and power. The copy has no page numbers; the sections below use the brochure's headings.

## Key claims

- Operator: "FOUNDING YEAR 1991 LOCATION Lugano OPERATOR ETH Zurich ACTIVITY Supercomputing". (CSCS)
- Users: "CSCS’s resources are available to national and international academia, as well as users from industry and the private sector." (CSCS)
- Free access: "The computing resources of CSCS are made available to Swiss and international researchers free of charge through the User Lab, which consists of two main programs". (User Laboratory)
- Peer review: "The User Program, accessible globally, provides access to cutting-edge and innovative supercomputing resources through an open and transparent peer-review process." (User Laboratory)
- Funding: "The Swiss Confederation funds the centre through the Board of the Federal Institutes of Technology and ETH Zurich." "About 8 Mio. CHF are financed through third party funding." (Annual budget)
- Multi-year budget: "Important investments in computing infrastructure are possible by carrying the investment budget over different years." (Annual budget)
- Services to others: CSCS "supports MeteoSwiss for weather forecasting, operates a cluster for analyzing data from the Large Hadron Collider (LHC) at CERN". (Third-party services)
- Alps: "The installation of “Alps” was completed in 2024 starting production in January 2025." (Supercomputers)
- Cooling: "The supercomputers and buildings are cooled by water extracted from Lake Lugano". (Cooling system)
- Power: "The present electricity supply allows operation of up to 11 Megawatts. This capacity can be increased to a maximum of 25 Megawatts." (Electricity)

## Evidence and method

This is an institutional fact sheet. It gives counts and capacities: staff, storage (100 PB online, 240 PB on tape), network (400 Gbit/s), power (11 MW). It does not give the size of the annual budget, apart from the third-party funding.

## Limits

- The brochure does not give the total annual budget or the cost of Alps.
- It does not give the number of GPUs or the performance of Alps. The ETH Zurich release gives 10,752 NVIDIA Grace Hopper superchips ([ETH Zurich Alps inauguration release 2024-09 (web)](ETH%20Zurich%20Alps%20inauguration%20release%202024-09%20%28web%29.md)).
- It says the User Lab "allocates millions of computing hours", but gives no exact number.
