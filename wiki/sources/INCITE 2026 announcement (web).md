# INCITE 2026 announcement (web)

## Citation

DOE Leadership Computing, Innovative and Novel Computational Impact on Theory and Experiment (INCITE) program. "INCITE program awards supercomputing time to 75 high-impact projects." Media release PDF, dated November 17, 2025 (upload folder 2026/05). https://doeleadershipcomputing.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/123/2026/05/2026INCITEAnnouncement.pdf. Fetched 2026-11-20. Read 2026-11-20. Not rehosted: the host is not a government host and the file states no open licence.

## Bottom line

- The U.S. Department of Energy (DOE) Office of Science gave INCITE time for 2026 to 75 projects. INCITE gives 60 percent of the available time on the leadership computers at Argonne and Oak Ridge.
- The committee received 143 proposals that asked for more than 141 million node-hours. Fourteen of the 75 awards are early career projects.
- INCITE is open to any researcher or research organization in the world. Peer-review panels and a technical review of each facility decide the awards.

## Background

INCITE is the main program of the Argonne Leadership Computing Facility (ALCF) and the Oak Ridge Leadership Computing Facility (OLCF) for access to their computers. The release names three machines: Aurora and Polaris at Argonne, and Frontier at Oak Ridge. It quotes the directors of the two facilities and lists ten awards as highlights. The fact sheets of all 75 awards are in [INCITE 2026 fact sheets (web)](INCITE%202026%20fact%20sheets%20%28web%29.md).

## Key claims

- "The U.S. Department of Energy’s Office of Science has allocated supercomputer access to 75 computational science projects for 2026" (paragraph 1)
- "DOE is awarding 60 percent of the available time on the leadership-class supercomputers at DOE’s Argonne and Oak Ridge national laboratories" (paragraph 1)
- "Open to any researcher or research organization in the world with a computationally or data intensive project, INCITE’s application process is highly competitive." (paragraph 7)
- "Over a four-month period, INCITE proposals are assessed by peer-review panels composed of international experts, with each panel representing a different scientific discipline." (paragraph 7)
- "This year, the committee received 143 total proposals with researchers requesting more than 141 million node-hours across all three systems." (paragraph 7)
- "the INCITE committee commits 10 percent of allocatable time to an early career track aimed at researchers within 10 years from earning their doctorate. This year, 14 early career projects were awarded." (paragraph 7)
- "Rommie Amaro of the University of California, San Diego received 1,228,800 node-hours on Frontier to study the in situ dynamics of the HIV-1 envelope glycoprotein." (Highlights)
- "Brant Robertson of the University of California, Santa Cruz received 470,000 node-hours on Frontier to probe new physics with the intergalactic medium." (Highlights)

## Evidence and method

A two-page media release of about 800 words. It gives the number of awards, the number of proposals, the node-hours that the proposals asked for and ten example awards. It describes the review process in one paragraph. It gives no total of the node-hours awarded.

## Limits

The release does not give the total node-hours awarded, the success rate by field or the share of AI projects. It does not give a cost or a dollar value of the time. The release gives the date Nov. 17, 2025, but its file is in an upload folder of May 2026. The release names 10 of the 75 awards; the fact sheets give the others. The release does not mention CalCompute.
