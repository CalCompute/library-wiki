# BCP 2026-27 Department of Justice AI Transparency Act AB 853 (web)

## Bottom line

- DOJ asks for 4.0 permanent positions and $1,210,000 in 2026-27, then $1,176,000 each year, from the Unfair Competition Law Fund, to enforce AB 853.
- AB 853 (Chapter 674, Statutes of 2025) sets provenance and disclosure duties for large online platforms, capture device makers and generative AI hosting platforms.
- The request includes $150,000 each year for expert witnesses in AI.

## Background

A Budget Change Proposal (BCP) is a department's request for a change to its budget, on the Department of Finance form DF-46. The Consumer Protection Section (CPS) of the Public Rights Division would do the work, under its Unfair Competition Law (UCL) authority. The law's text is in [AB 853 chaptered text Chapter 674 of 2025](AB%20853%20chaptered%20text%20Chapter%20674%20of%202025.md). The same section's proposal for SB 53 is [BCP 2026-27 Department of Justice SB 53 frontier AI act (web)](BCP%202026-27%20Department%20of%20Justice%20SB%2053%20frontier%20AI%20act%20%28web%29.md).

## Key claims

- "The Department of Justice (DOJ) requests a permanent augmentation of 4.0 positions and Unfair Competition Law Fund spending authority of $1,210,000 in 2026-27 and $1,176,000 ongoing to enforce the requirements of the California AI Transparency Act" (Budget Request Summary).
- "Beginning January 1, 2027, the bill requires a large online platform, as defined, to detect whether any provenance data that is compliant with widely adopted specifications adopted by an established standards-setting body is embedded into or attached to content distributed on the large online platform." (A. Problem Statement)
- "Also, beginning January 1, 2027, the law prohibits a GenAI system hosting platform, as defined, from knowingly making available a system that lacks required disclosures." (A. Problem Statement)
- "DOJ expects that these investigations will require multiple experts with potential hourly rates ranging from $500 to $1,000 (or greater) and providing approximately 150 to 300 hours of work per year, for a total of $150,000 a year." (A. Problem Statement, External Consultants)
- "As of 10/1/2025, CPS has 8 DAG vacancies which are in various hiring stages." (A. Problem Statement, Workload History)

## Evidence and method

A state budget request on the DF-46 form. It has a resource history, a workload history in hours, projected outcomes and a fiscal detail sheet in thousands of dollars. The positions are 2.0 Deputy Attorney General IV, 1.0 Associate Governmental Program Analyst and 1.0 Legal Secretary. The projected section workload goes from 101,855 hours (57.4 full-time equivalents) in 2025-26 to 107,271 hours (60.4) from 2026-27.

## Limits

- The proposal does not say whether the Legislature approved the request. It is not the enacted budget.
- The 2026-27 floor report lists $1.2 million a year for DOJ to enforce AB 853 ([Assembly Budget floor report 2026-27 budget 2026-06-27 (web)](Assembly%20Budget%20floor%20report%202026-27%20budget%202026-06-27%20%28web%29.md), p. 124).
- The narrative gives $150,000 a year for consultants. The fiscal detail sheet gives $182,000 a year on line 5340, Consulting and Professional Services, External. The proposal does not explain the difference.
- The Justification names "2.0 DAGs and 1.0 AGPA" and does not name the Legal Secretary.
- It says nothing about CalCompute.
