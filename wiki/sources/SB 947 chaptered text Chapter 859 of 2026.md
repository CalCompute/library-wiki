## Bottom line

- SB 947 adds Part 5.5.5, "Automated Decision Systems in the Workplace", to the Labor Code, from section 1520. The part becomes operative on July 1, 2027.
- An employer must not rely solely on an automated decision system (ADS) for a disciplinary or termination decision. A human must corroborate a decision that relies primarily on ADS output.
- The employer must give the employee a written postuse notice. The employee can ask for a description of the employee's own data that the ADS used.
- "Employer" includes state and local government, the California State University, and the University of California "upon agreement by the regents".
- The Labor Commissioner can enforce the part. The civil penalty is $500 for each violation.

## Key claims

- "“Automated decision system” or “ADS” means any computational process derived from machine learning, statistical modeling, data analytics, or artificial intelligence that issues simplified output, including a score, classification, or recommendation, that is used to assist or replace human discretionary decisionmaking and materially impacts natural persons." (Lab. Code § 1520(c))
- "“Employer” includes all branches of state government, including all cities, counties, charter counties, municipalities, charter municipalities, cities and counties, special districts, transit districts, the University of California upon agreement by the regents, the California State University, community college districts, school districts, or any other governmental entity." (§ 1520(d)(3)(B))
- "An employer shall not rely solely on an ADS when making a disciplinary or termination decision." (§ 1522(b)(1))
- "An employer that primarily relied upon an ADS to make a disciplinary or termination decision shall provide the affected employee with a written postuse notice at the time the employer informs the employee of the decision." (§ 1524(a))
- "An employer who violates this part shall be subject to a civil penalty of five hundred dollars ($500) per violation." (§ 1526.1(e))
- "This part shall become operative on July 1, 2027." (§ 1526.7)

## Limits

The bill gives no cost estimate. The duties of human review and postuse notice apply only to a disciplinary or termination decision. The University of California is an employer under the part only "upon agreement by the regents"; the text does not say if the Regents agreed. The part does not apply to parties under a collective bargaining agreement that explicitly waives it (§ 1526.5). It does not apply to some uses that federal aircraft or defense rules require (§ 1526.6). Section 1526.1(c) says "disciplinary or deactivation decision"; the bill does not define "deactivation".

The bill does not mention research compute or CalCompute.
