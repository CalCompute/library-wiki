## Bottom line

- SB 1159 changes seven California transparency and governance laws. In them, "person", "member of the public" and similar terms do not include artificial intelligence (AI) systems, autonomous agents or robots.
- The seven laws are the Public Records Act, the Legislative Open Records Act, the Bagley-Keene Open Meeting Act, the Ralph M. Brown Act, the Administrative Procedure Act, the California Environmental Quality Act (CEQA) and the California Coastal Act of 1976.
- An agency can use a disclosure verification tool to find out if AI is present. A natural person can still use AI for their own engagement, at the volume of ordinary participation.
- New Government Code section 8319 forbids the knowing use of AI to falsely show that a natural person engaged with a governmental agency.

## Background

Senator Cabaldon ([Christopher Cabaldon](../entities/Christopher%20Cabaldon.md)) wrote the bill, with eight coauthors from the Senate and the Assembly. The text holds the Legislative Counsel's Digest and 13 sections. Section 1 gives findings and the intent of the Legislature. Sections 2 to 12 amend or add a definition section in each of the seven laws, and add section 8319. Section 13 gives the findings that the California Constitution requires for a law on public records and open meetings. The original is the Legislature's XML file of the chaptered text, in its California Markup Language (CAML) format.

## Key claims

- "Artificial intelligence (AI) systems can now be programmed to automatically and continuously engage with governmental processes at scales and speeds that far exceed human capacity." (Section 1(a)(2))
- "“Person,” “interested person,” “member of the public,” and any other similar terms referring to those who may engage with governmental agencies under this division do not include artificial intelligence systems, autonomous agents, robots, or other nonhuman entities, whether physical or digital." (Gov. Code § 7920.520(b)(1), Public Records Act)
- "A public agency may use a disclosure verification tool that meets the criteria described in Section 22757.2 of the Business and Professions Code to determine if artificial intelligence is present." (§ 7920.520(b)(3))
- "This section does not prohibit a natural person from using artificial intelligence, autonomous agents, or robots to facilitate the person’s own engagement with a governmental agency, including through the use of assistive technologies, provided the volume and frequency of the engagement are reasonably consistent with ordinary participation by a natural person." (§ 7920.520(b)(4))
- "A person shall not knowingly use artificial intelligence to falsely represent that a natural person appeared before, submitted information to, or otherwise engaged with a governmental agency." (Gov. Code § 8319(a))
- "In the United Kingdom, AI-powered platforms have already enabled automated generation of planning objections, prompting warnings from experts that such systems will overwhelm public agencies." (Section 1(a)(3))

## Limits

The bill gives no penalty for a violation of section 8319 and no operative date. Each new definition of "artificial intelligence" uses the same words as Government Code section 11546.45.5 ([AB 2885 chaptered text Chapter 843 of 2024](AB%202885%20chaptered%20text%20Chapter%20843%20of%202024.md)). The verification tool criteria are in Business and Professions Code section 22757.2 ([SB 1000 chaptered text Chapter 861 of 2026](SB%201000%20chaptered%20text%20Chapter%20861%20of%202026.md)).

Section 1(c) says that the bill does not imply that "person" includes AI in any other law.

The text has these differences, word for word:

- Section 13 names "Section 9 of this act, which adds Section 54951.5". Section 10 adds section 54951.5; section 9 amends section 11500.
- Public Resources Code section 21066(b)(3) (CEQA) says "verification detection tool". The other sections say "disclosure verification tool".
- Government Code section 11500(c)(2)(B) gives the natural-person text of paragraph (4) in the place of the definition of "assistive technology" that the other sections give.

The bill does not mention research compute or CalCompute.
