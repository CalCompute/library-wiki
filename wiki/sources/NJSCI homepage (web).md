# NJSCI homepage (web)

## Citation

New Jersey Supercomputer Initiative (NJSCI). "The New Jersey Supercomputer Initiative." Homepage, date not stated. https://njsupercomputer.org/. Read 2026-11-18.

## Bottom line

- NJSCI is a "$125+ million" public-private partnership that sets up a shared state supercomputer. It goes live in early 2027.
- The money comes from a $75 million personal gift, a $25 million investment from SHI International and $25 million from the State of New Jersey.
- An independent nonprofit administers the supercomputer from 2027. SHI International hosts and operates it in its New Jersey facilities.

## Background

This is the initiative's own website. It links the Governor's release of October 20, 2026 ([NJ Governor release 2026-10-20 (web)](NJ%20Governor%20release%202026-10-20%20%28web%29.md)). It gives the goals, the partners and the plans for governance and hosting. It links a sign-up form and two job postings, one of them [NJSCI Executive Director posting (web)](NJSCI%20Executive%20Director%20posting%20%28web%29.md).

## Key claims

- "A groundbreaking, $125+ million public-private partnership that will supercharge discovery and opportunity across the Garden State." (top of page)
- "NJSCI will establish a state-of-the-art supercomputer that serves as a shared resource to meet the advanced computing needs of researchers, students, entrepreneurs, businesses, and other organizations across the state." (New Jersey’s Next Breakthrough Starts Here)
- "This resource is expected to double the current aggregated supercomputing capacity of New Jersey’s research universities, while also leveraging the most advanced hardware." (New Jersey’s Next Breakthrough Starts Here)
- "Each institution plans to provide annual contributions to the initiative’s budget. Pathways for additional higher education institutions from across the state to participate will be announced in 2027." (Partners, Higher Education)
- "Chris Malachowsky, co-founder of NVIDIA and a Monmouth County native, and Melody Malachowsky are providing a personal gift of $75 million." (Partners, Technology & Industry Leaders)
- "The State of New Jersey is investing $25 million, which was appropriated for the initiative in the Fiscal Year 2026 budget." (Partners, State Partners)
- "The supercomputer will be administered starting in 2027 by NJSCI, an independent nonprofit organization, and guided by principles including transparent governance and equitable access." (Looking Forward)
- "The supercomputer infrastructure will be hosted and operated by SHI International in its existing New Jersey facilities" (Looking Forward)

## Evidence and method

An initiative homepage. It lists nine higher education founding partners:

- Kean University
- Montclair State University
- New Jersey Institute of Technology (NJIT)
- New Jersey Council of County Colleges
- Princeton University
- Rowan University
- Rutgers University
- Seton Hall University
- Stevens Institute of Technology

It names four state agencies that give support, and the NJ AI Hub. It names an AI Higher Education and Workforce Collaborative for curricula, training and apprenticeships. Working groups guide the first implementation.

## Limits

- The page does not give the hardware, the number of graphics processing units (GPUs), the annual operating cost or the institutions' contribution amounts.
- It does not give the access rules or an allocation process.
- It says "Early 2027" for the start. The job posting says initial users "as early as January 2027".

> ⚠️ Contradiction: This page says that SHI International hosts the infrastructure "in its existing New Jersey facilities". [NJ PL 2025 c404 supplemental appropriation (web)](NJ%20PL%202025%20c404%20supplemental%20appropriation%20%28web%29.md) gives the state's $25,000,000 as a grant to "an institution of higher education" that hosts the supercomputer.
