# NCSA DeltaAI launch release 2024-11-18 (web)

## Citation

Helregel, Andrew. "NCSA Launches DeltaAI." National Center for Supercomputing Applications (NCSA), University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign. News release, posted November 18, 2024, updated April 17, 2026. https://ncsa.illinois.edu/ncsa-launches-deltaai/. Read 2026-11-18.

## Bottom line

- NCSA launched DeltaAI, a system with the NVIDIA GH200 Grace Hopper Superchip. Nearly $30 million in National Science Foundation (NSF) awards fund it, and the State of Illinois gives more funds.
- Researchers get time through ACCESS and the National Artificial Intelligence Research Resource (NAIRR) pilot. University of Illinois researchers also get 10 percent of the NSF allocations and 20 nodes that Illinois Computes paid for.

## Background

NCSA published this news release at the launch of DeltaAI. The system is in the National Petascale Computing Facility on the Urbana-Champaign campus. The release quotes the NCSA director, the chancellor, the provost and the director of Illinois Computes. It describes Illinois Computes, a campus program with its own funds.

## Key claims

- "Funded by nearly $30 million in awards from the U.S. National Science Foundation (NSF) and accessible to researchers across the country through the NSF ACCESS program and the National Artificial Intelligence Research Resource (NAIRR) pilot, DeltaAI will enable scientists and researchers to address the world’s most challenging problems" (paragraph 2)
- "DeltaAI will quadruple NCSA’s AI-focused computing capacity and greatly expand the capacity available within the NSF-funded advanced computing ecosystem." (paragraph 4)
- "University of Illinois researchers will be able to enjoy compute time on DeltaAI through 10% of allocations from the NSF award, and also Illinois Computes, which supplied 20 supplemental nodes integrated into the system." (paragraph 6)
- "Supported by $30 million in funding from campus, Illinois Computes offers computing and data storage resources, technical expertise and support services to researchers from all domains across the University of Illinois. An additional $20 million was provided by the University of Illinois System" (paragraph 8)
- "In response to the enormous interest in Delta through NAIRR, DeltaAI will substantially increase the amount of NSF-supported time available to those projects." (paragraph 13)
- "Additional funding for DeltaAI comes from the State of Illinois." (About DeltaAI)

## Evidence and method

A news release from the operator at launch. It gives the funding sources, the access routes, the campus share and the chip type. It links NSF award OAC 2320345. It gives no node count for the whole system and no State of Illinois amount.

## Limits

The release does not give the total number of nodes or graphics processing units (GPUs). It does not give the operating cost or the State of Illinois amount. It also uses two different capacity figures.

> ⚠️ Contradiction: The body of this release says DeltaAI "will quadruple NCSA’s AI-focused computing capacity". Its "About DeltaAI" box says "Tripling NCSA’s AI-focused computing capacity", the figure that [NCSA DeltaAI award release 2023-07-05 (web)](NCSA%20DeltaAI%20award%20release%202023-07-05%20%28web%29.md) gives ("DeltaAI will triple NCSA’s AI-focused computing capacity").

The award release gives a "$10 million award", and this release gives "nearly $30 million in awards". Both link the same NSF award record. This release does not explain the difference.
