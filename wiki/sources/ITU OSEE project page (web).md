# ITU OSEE project page (web)

## Citation

International Telecommunication Union (ITU), Telecommunication Development Sector (ITU-D). "Open Source Ecosystem Enabler." Project page. No date on the page. https://www.itu.int/en/ITU-D/ICT-Applications/Pages/Initiatives/OSEEPSI/home.aspx. Read 2026-11-11.

## Bottom line

- The Open Source Ecosystem Enabler (OSEE) is a project of ITU and the United Nations Development Programme (UNDP), with support from the European Commission.
- It helps public and private actors in a country adopt open source technologies to deliver digital government services.
- It has three streams: a training framework, Open Source Technical Facilities in two countries to start, and a global knowledge hub.

## Background

ITU is the United Nations agency for information and communication technologies. Its development sector runs this project, with support from the ITU Open Source Programme Office (OSPO). The page states the case for open source in government, lists the streams of work and gives news items. The UN Open Source United portal gives the dates and the budget ([UN Open Source United initiatives (web)](UN%20Open%20Source%20United%20initiatives%20%28web%29.md)).

## Key claims

- Aim: "The Open Source Ecosystem Enabler (OSEE) project seeks to empower local and regional public and private actors to effectively adopt open source technologies for delivering digital government services." (About the project)
- Vendor lock-in: open source technologies "are freely accessible and can be owned by governments themselves, reducing the risk of being locked-in with a specific IT vendor and ensuring governments’ digital sovereignty." (Open-source solutions are important to achieve eGov maturity)
- Skills: "Deploying and maintaining open-source in government requires new skill sets and sustainable business plans for the maintenance of systems." (Open-source solutions are important to achieve eGov maturity)
- Barriers: governments hesitated because of "resource constraints, knowledge gaps, uncertainties regarding costs, and the absence of robust local and global ecosystems". (Open-source solutions are important to achieve eGov maturity)
- Partners and period: "ITU and UNDP, with the generous support from the European Commission, aim to foster improved knowledge and action on using open-source for digital public service delivery. Over a three and half year period". (OSEE promotes DPG ecosystems)
- Technical facilities: "The project will seek to establish Open Source Technical Facilities (OSTF) to address the challenges faced by governments in implementing open-source technologies. It will start with two countries". (Expected outcomes and streams of work)
- Training: the first OSEE training, on "Digital Transformation in the Public Sector", took place in Nairobi for "government officials and policymakers". (News and events)

## Evidence and method

This is a project page of a UN agency. It gives the aims, the partners and the streams of work. It cites a UNDP and ITU report: digital technology "aids 70% of the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) targets". It gives no results or budget.

## Limits

- The page does not discuss compute infrastructure.
- It gives no budget and no start or end date. The UN portal gives "September 2023 to February 2027" and "EUR 3 million" ([UN Open Source United initiatives (web)](UN%20Open%20Source%20United%20initiatives%20%28web%29.md)).
- It does not name the two countries for the technical facilities. The UN portal names Kenya and Trinidad and Tobago as pilot countries.
