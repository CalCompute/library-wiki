# WEF Next-Generation Computing Framework 2026 (web)

## Citation

World Economic Forum, in collaboration with the Global Future Council on Next Generation Computing. "The Next-Generation Computing Framework." White paper, World Economic Forum, October 2026. https://reports.weforum.org/docs/WEF_The_Next_Generation_Computing_Framework_2026.pdf. Read 2026-11-09.

## Bottom line

- The paper says that computing is now national infrastructure. It gives a self-assessment scorecard with seven dimensions, among them compute access, infrastructure, talent and partnerships.
- Each dimension has five levels, from "Aware" to "Anticipatory". The paper says the scorecard is a diagnostic tool, not a ranking.

## Background

The World Economic Forum (WEF) wrote the paper with its Global Future Council on Next Generation Computing. Council members come from universities, research centres and technology companies. The paper covers AI infrastructure, high-performance computing (HPC), quantum, neuromorphic, edge and in-orbit computing. It has three "enabling pillars": sovereignty and interdependence, cybersecurity and resilience, and sustainability.

## Key claims

- "Computing is shifting from a product to a national infrastructure, much like telephone networks, electricity grids and road systems." (Executive summary, p. 4)
- "In 2022, investment in computing infrastructure worldwide reached $97 billion; in 2025, it reached $448 billion. By 2027, however, it is expected to exceed $1.5 trillion – a 15-fold increase in just five years." (Executive summary, p. 4)
- "In this context, sovereignty is better understood as the preservation – the ability of a country, institution or organization to make and execute strategic choices about where systems are hosted, how they are used, who controls the data, how they are updated and under what conditions they can be exited or reconfigured." (Section 2.1, p. 8)
- "Today’s large-scale AI data centres demand gigawatts (GW) of available power." (Section 2.1, p. 9)
- "Can we access frontier compute when we need it?" (Scorecard, Dimension 1 core question, p. 24)
- "Can we access compute without asking permission from a single power?" (Scorecard, Dimension 5 core question, p. 24)
- "The focus here is not frontier researchers but practitioner depth." (Scorecard, Dimension 4, Talent and skills, p. 27)
- "Both the acceleration framework and scorecard serve as starting points and are not built for ranking or benchmarking" (Conclusion, p. 31)

## Evidence and method

This is a white paper from an expert council, not a study with new data. The investment figures cite outside sources in endnotes. The scorecard gives each dimension three sub-indicators, scored 1 to 5. The average of the sub-indicators is the dimension score. Each level has a table of what it "looks like", for example "Single foreign cloud provider; no alternatives" at level 1 of compute and connectivity.

## Limits

The paper is about countries and organizations. It does not name California or any U.S. state, and it gives no state-level data. It gives no cost model for a public compute platform. The WEF disclaimer says the findings do "not necessarily represent the views of the World Economic Forum". The paper is under copyright, with all rights reserved, so the library does not rehost it.

> ⚠️ Contradiction: the paper gives two figures for 2025 investment in computing infrastructure: "$448 billion" (Executive summary, p. 4) and "$459 billion in 2025" (Introduction, p. 6). Both are in this source.
