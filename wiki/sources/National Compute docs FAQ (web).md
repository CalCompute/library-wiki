# National Compute docs FAQ (web)

## Citation

National Compute Labs PBC. "FAQ." National Compute Docs, date not stated. https://docs.nationalcompute.com/faq/. Read 2026-11-11.

## Bottom line

- The FAQ says that National Compute allocates GPU capacity by a price-ordered auction about every 10 seconds, not by queue or reservation. The one guaranteed product is a base load block at a fixed rate for a fixed term.
- A `.edu`, `.mil` or `.gov` address gets a public research organization at sign-in, with $100 of Promo credits. Other users go on a waitlist.

## Background

National Compute Labs PBC publishes the documentation for its compute platform. The FAQ answers questions on allocation, billing, pricing, auction rules, access, storage and acceptable use.

## Key claims

- "Neither: it's **price-ordered**. Every auction tick (roughly 10 seconds) re-runs the market over all demand, and capacity goes to the highest-value bids." (Allocation and base load)
- "The one guaranteed product is a base load block: a fixed block of nodes for a fixed term at a fixed rate, outside the auction." (Allocation and base load)
- "Pricing here is not computed from an external index at all. Prices are **set by the auction**" (Pricing index)
- "A repeated sealed-bid **combinatorial auction**, roughly every 10 seconds." (Pricing and auction mechanics)
- "A `.edu`, `.mil` or `.gov` address gets its own public research organization on the spot, which requests whole GPU nodes from Marshall through Public Research and comes with $100 of Promo credits that expire 30 days after sign-up by default" (Access model)
- "anything else is placed on the waitlist and emailed when a spot opens — early access for public users is available in waves" (Access model)
- "No. Billing today is GPU capacity only — the clearing rate for nodes you hold" (Ancillary costs and storage)

## Evidence and method

Product documentation in question-and-answer form. It states the platform's rules. It gives no prices, no capacity figures and no user counts.

## Limits

The FAQ describes the operator's rules; no outside party checks them. It gives no current prices; it points to the platform's market feed. The page has no date.
