# OSPool service page (web)

## Citation

OSG Consortium. "OSPool: Serving Open Science throughput computing." Web page, date not stated. https://osg-htc.org/services/ospool. Read 2026-11-12.

## Bottom line

- The OSPool is computing capacity "accessible to any researcher affiliated with a US academic institution", shared by a Fair-Share policy.
- Its capacity comes from the members of the OSG Compute Federation, such as campuses and supercomputing centers. Each member sets its own policy for what it gives.
- It suits many small, independent jobs, machine learning among them, that finish or checkpoint within 20 hours.

## Background

The OSG Consortium publishes this page. The OSPool is "Operated by PATh" and uses the HTCondor software. The National Science Foundation (NSF) supports the work under grants 2030508, 1836650 and 2609485. The live counts at the top of the page did not load in the copy the library read.

## Key claims

- Who: "The OSPool is a source of computing capacity that is accessible to any researcher affiliated with a US academic institution. Capacity is allocated following a Fair-Share policy." (What is the OSPool?)
- Route: "Researchers can submit computational work to the OSPool via Access Points operated by the OSG, which serves researchers affiliated with projects at US-based academic, non-profit, and government institutions." (How Can I Harness the OSPool Capacity?)
- Account: "To harness the full capacity of the OSPool you will need to obtain an account via the OSG Portal." (What is the OSPool?)
- AI work: the list of suitable work includes "machine learning and AI executed with multiple independent training tasks, different parameters, and/or data subsets". (What types of work run well on the OSPool?)
- Opportunistic: "The OSPool is made up of mostly opportunistic capacity - contributing clusters may interrupt jobs at any time. Thus, the OSPool supports workloads of numerous jobs that individually complete or checkpoint within 20 hours." (same section)
- Ideal job: 1 CPU and 1 GPU per job, under 10 hours, a few GB of RAM and under 10 GB of input and output. (table, "Ideal Jobs!")
- Contributors: "The computing resources for the OSPool are contributed by members of the OSG Compute Federation, typically campuses, government-supported supercomputing centers, and research collaborations." (Who contributes capacity to the OSPool?)
- Member choice: "The members individually determine their policies for contributing resources, including the amount of resources it contributes and when these resources are available." (same section)
- Support: the documentation and "an active and supportive facilitation team" support "all users on OSG-Operated Access Points". (Learning to use the OSPool)

## Limits

- The page does not say if the OSPool is free. It gives no cost.
- It does not list the contributing institutions. It links to an Institutions page that the library has not read.
- It does not say how many GPUs the OSPool has.
- The two eligibility lines differ: "any researcher affiliated with a US academic institution" and "projects at US-based academic, non-profit, and government institutions".
