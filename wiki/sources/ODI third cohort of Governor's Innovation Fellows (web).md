## Bottom line

- The third cohort of the Governor's Innovation Fellows has 15 state employees from 11 departments and 7 agencies. Agency secretaries and department directors nominated them.
- The Fellows began with a one-week boot camp on June 22, 2026. For 6 months they spend 80 percent of their time on projects for efficiency and service delivery.

## Background

ODI runs the Fellows program under an executive order of July 2025. This blog post announces the third cohort and describes its first training week. It names the ODI staff who led the boot camp and quotes two alumni and three new Fellows. The program page is [Governor's Innovation Fellows program (web)](Governor%27s%20Innovation%20Fellows%20program%20%28web%29.md).

## Key claims

- "This cohort includes 15 Fellows from 11 departments representing 7 agencies. This new group builds upon the work of two former cohorts of state staff." (opening)
- "The Fellows program, managed by ODI, is part of the Governor's executive order from July 2025." (opening)
- "Agency secretaries and department directors nominated the Fellows. They identified staff who can work across departments to solve complex government challenges." (opening)
- "The new cohort came together on June 22 for a week-long training. They learned a product approach built around 4 principles". (Product approach boot camp)
- "For 6 months, the Fellows devote 80% of their time on high priority projects that improve efficiency, service delivery, and customer experience." (quote of Jeffery Marino, Director, ODI)
- "Develop case studies for our Innovation Library to help agencies scale successful ideas". (Building a coalition of Fellows, list of cohort 3 tasks)

## Evidence and method

A state agency blog post. It gives the cohort size, the nomination method and the training topics. It gives no cost, no project list and no results.

## Limits

The post does not say if any Fellow project uses AI or computing resources. It does not give the cost of the program or the time the Fellows spend in training. The cohort 3 page it links to was not read.
