## Bottom line

- SB 1119 adds Business and Professions Code Chapter 11.6, sections 21810 and after, on companion chatbots and children.
- From July 1, 2027, an operator must do a documented child safety risk assessment first. This applies to each new or much changed companion chatbot in California.
- An operator must get independent child safety audits and send summaries to the Attorney General. Public prosecutors, and a harmed child or parent, can sue.

## Background

Senator Padilla wrote the bill, and the Governor signed it on Sept. 10, 2026. It removes the earlier minor-user rules from section 22602 and puts the new chapter in their place. The audit section, 21814, has two versions. Which one applies depends on whether AB 1405 took effect by Jan. 1, 2027 ([AB 1405 bill text](AB%201405%20bill%20text.md)). The library's copy comes from the Legislature's website through the Wayback Machine.

## Key claims

- "This bill would require an operator, as defined, of a companion chatbot to, beginning July 1, 2027, before making a new or substantially modified companion chatbot available to users in the state, do various things with respect to child safety, including perform and document a comprehensive risk assessment" (Legislative Counsel's Digest)
- "“Operator” means a person who makes a companion chatbot available to a user in the state." (Bus. & Prof. Code § 21810.5(j)(1)(A))
- "On or before January 1, 2029, or before an operator first makes a companion chatbot publicly available, whichever is later, an operator shall ensure the performance of a child safety audit." (§ 21814(a)(1)(A)(i))
- "Before January 1, 2032, this section does not apply to an operator that had less than five hundred million dollars ($500,000,000) in gross revenue in the prior calendar year." (§ 21814(c), version that applies with AB 1405)
- "This section shall become operative only if Assembly Bill 1405 of the 2025–26 Regular Session is chaptered and takes effect on or before January 1, 2027." (§ 21814(f), second version)
- "On or before January 1, 2028, the Attorney General shall establish a public incident reporting mechanism for consumers to submit complaints relating to companion chatbots to the Attorney General." (§ 21815)
- A public prosecutor can seek "A civil penalty of not more than five thousand dollars ($5,000) per affected child for each negligent violation." (§ 21816(a)(1)(A))

## Limits

The bill is about consumer chatbots. It does not mention research compute or CalCompute. It gives no cost estimate. The library has no source that says which version of section 21814 is now operative.
