# Littler California 2026 AI employment laws (web)

## Citation

Joy C. Rosenquist, Zoe Argento and Alice Wang. "California's 2026 AI Legislation Wave: New Laws Target AI Throughout the Employment Lifecycle." Littler Mendelson P.C., ASAP, October 14, 2026. https://www.littler.com/news-analysis/asap/californias-2026-ai-legislation-wave-new-laws-target-ai-throughout-employment. Read 2026-11-09.

## Bottom line

- The article says that California's 2026 artificial intelligence (AI) laws regulate how employers use AI, not the technology itself. Their focus is human oversight, transparency and accountability.
- It covers AB 1883, AB 1331, SB 947 (the "No Robo Bosses Act"), SB 951, SB 574 and Assembly Bill 316.

## Background

Littler Mendelson is a labor and employment law firm. Three of its shareholders wrote this ASAP analysis for employers. The article groups the laws under workplace monitoring, AI decisions about workers, AI-driven workforce reductions and AI rules outside human resources. It also names a San Mateo County permit framework for AI-controlled workplace robots and a recent California executive order.

## Key claims

- "The focus of these new AI laws is not on the technology itself, but rather on human oversight, transparency, and accountability." (At a Glance)
- "AB 1883 prohibits employers from using AI-powered workplace surveillance tools that recognize, infer, or predict an employee's emotional state or collect "neural data," meaning information generated through measurements of the central or peripheral nervous system." (California Is Drawing New Boundaries Around Workplace Monitoring)
- "The "No Robo Bosses Act" prohibits employers from relying solely on automated decision systems when making disciplinary or termination decisions." (AI May Assist Managers, But It Cannot Become the Manager)
- "SB 951 expands California's WARN framework by imposing additional notice requirements when a covered layoff occurs "in whole or in substantial part" because an AI system or other automated technology replaces or automates employment positions." (New Rules for AI-Driven Workforce Reductions)
- "The law also contemplates *public* reporting by the Employment Development Department, meaning that AI-related reductions in force may attract greater public attention than traditional workforce restructurings." (New Rules for AI-Driven Workforce Reductions)
- "SB 574 imposes new obligations on attorneys who use generative AI, including requirements relating to verification of AI-generated content, confidentiality of information entered into AI systems, and disclosures regarding AI use in court filings." (AI Regulation Is Expanding Beyond Human Resources)

## Evidence and method

A law firm's legal analysis for employers. It names each law and states its main duty:

- AB 1883: no AI tools that infer an employee's emotional state or collect neural data.
- AB 1331: no workplace surveillance tools in employee bathrooms. The final bill dropped cafeterias, lounges and other common places.
- SB 947: a human reviewer must evaluate an AI-driven discipline or termination, and the employer must give a post-use notice.
- SB 951: more layoff notice duties when AI or automated technology replaces positions.
- SB 574: duties for attorneys who use generative AI.
- Assembly Bill 316: adds Section 1714.46 to the California Civil Code, effective January 1, 2026. A defendant cannot avoid liability by saying that the AI system caused the harm independently.

The article also compares SB 947 with regulations under the Fair Employment and Housing Act (FEHA) and the California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA). It cites no data.

## Limits

The article is advice to private employers. It does not say how the laws apply to public employers or to the state workforce. It does not give chamber votes or effective dates, except for Assembly Bill 316. It says nothing about computing infrastructure or CalCompute.
