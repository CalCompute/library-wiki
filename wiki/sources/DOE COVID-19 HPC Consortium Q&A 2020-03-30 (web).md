# DOE COVID-19 HPC Consortium Q&A 2020-03-30 (web)

## Bottom line

- The COVID-19 High Performance Computing (HPC) Consortium was a public-private partnership. The White House Office of Science and Technology Policy (OSTP), DOE and IBM led it. It gave supercomputer time "at no cost" to COVID-19 researchers.
- An expert panel assessed proposals. A member of the COVID-19 HPC Consortium then worked with each selected team on the terms of access.

## Background

The Under Secretary for Science at DOE answers questions about the COVID-19 HPC Consortium in this article. It started on March 22, 2020. At launch it had DOE laboratories, companies and universities, among them UC San Diego. The article also describes other DOE laboratory work on COVID-19.

## Key claims

- "a unique public-private effort spearheaded by the White House Office of Science and Technology Policy (OSTP), the U.S. Department of Energy (DOE), and IBM – along with a growing group of government, industry, and academic leaders". (introduction)
- "At launch, the consortium includes five DOE laboratories, industry leaders like IBM, Microsoft, Google, and Amazon, and preeminent U.S. universities like MIT, RPI, and UC San Diego." (first answer)
- "Never before has a group of public and private entities–federal government, industry, and academic leaders–organized so quickly and made so much time available on so many of the world's most powerful computers at no cost". (first answer)
- "An expert panel comprising top scientists and computing researchers will assess the public health benefit of the proposed work, with emphasis on projects that can ensure rapid results." (second answer)
- "Once a project is selected for support, a Consortium member will assume responsibility and work with the proposal team to identify the process for access and to discuss any specific terms and conditions that will apply to the offered access." (second answer)
- Researchers submit proposals "via an online portal" at https://www.xsede.org/covid19-hpc-consortium. The proposals are then "reviewed for matching with computing resources from one of the partner institutions." (third answer)
- "Currently, six DOE National Labs are involved in the Consortium: Argonne, Lawrence Berkeley, Lawrence Livermore, Los Alamos, Oak Ridge, and Sandia National Laboratories." (fourth answer)

## Evidence and method

A question-and-answer article by a federal department, one week after the launch. It describes the partners, the review process and the submission route. It gives no count of proposals awarded and no count of computing hours.

## Limits

The article is from March 2020, at the start of the consortium. It does not say how long the COVID-19 HPC Consortium ran or what it produced. It gives "five DOE laboratories" at launch and "six DOE National Labs" one week later; the article does not explain the change. It does not give the cost to any partner.
