# SciPy Majora-4af1f417-002 JupyterHub paper (web)

## Citation

Anthony Suen, Laura Norén, Alan Liang and Andrea Tu. "Equity, Scalability, and Sustainability of Data Science Infrastructure." In *Proceedings of the 17th Python in Science Conference (SciPy 2018)*, pp. 15 to 17, 2018. https://proceedings.scipy.org/articles/Majora-4af1f417-002.pdf. Read 2026-11-11.

## Bottom line

- The paper compares how four types of institutions run JupyterHub, a shared notebook service for data science teaching.
- It finds "an increasing divergence" between well-funded institutions and those with limited IT (information technology) staff.
- It names four pathways: the status quo, institutional grants, a national JupyterHub and regional hubs.

## Background

The authors are at UC Berkeley and New York University. The four cases are UC Berkeley, small liberal arts colleges, wealthy private universities and a Canadian national model. The paper uses more than 10 interviews with faculty and IT staff and the documentation of 20 institutions. It is open access under a Creative Commons Attribution License, as its first page states. It appeared in the 2018 SciPy conference proceedings.

## Key claims

- Main finding: "liberal arts colleges, community colleges, and other institutions with limited IT staff and experience have greater difficulty setting up and maintaining JupyterHub, compared to well-funded private institutions or large public research universities with a deep technical bench of IT staff." (Abstract, p. 15)
- Berkeley scale: the JupyterHub "has quickly expanded to now support over 1,000 students in Data 8 each semester and more than 3,000 students in Berkeley’s Data Science connectors, modules, and upper division courses." (Case Study 1, p. 15)
- Berkeley funding: "Berkeley’s JupyterHub infrastructure, which subsists on cloud credits, is supported by long running industry relations and partnerships with cloud vendors like Microsoft and Google." (Case Study 1, p. 15)
- Risk: "The reliance on free cloud credits is further not guaranteed forever and requires regular negotiations with public cloud vendors." (Case Study 1, p. 16)
- Staff: student and postdoc staff "typically do not advance their careers by doing SysAdmin work, leading to a lack of consistent support staff and a consequent lack of consistent expertise." (Case Study 1, p. 16)
- Cost: "If done in an uncoordinated way, the costs can skyrocket if departments independently contract with cloud providers and IT consultants to set up their own JupyterHubs." (Case Study 3, p. 16)
- Canada: "Syzygy grew to become the largest federally funded JupyterHub and is utilized by more than 8,000 students across 15 universities in Canada." (Case Study 4, p. 16)
- Canadian funding: "The system manager is paid for by Compute Canada, and further grants from the Canadian federal government ($4.5m) and Alberta ($1m) support professors and teachers." (Case Study 4, p. 16)
- Grants: "grants to hire staff to deploy Jupyterhub is non-scalable given the high costs of hiring IT staff with such specialized experience." (Conclusion, p. 17)
- Regional hubs: "establishing several regional hubs can reduce the burden of deployment and maintenance costs that individual universities experience today." (Conclusion, p. 17)
- Pilot: "The West Big Data Innovation Hub, UC Berkeley, and Microsoft will be launching a pilot program by setting up a Kubernetes cluster using Azure for a small group of Western U.S. universities to pilot their JupyterHubs starting in the Summer of 2018." (Conclusion, p. 17)

## Evidence and method

This is a short conference paper with four qualitative case studies. The evidence is interviews, public documentation and counts of students and classes. It gives no cost figures for any deployment except the Canadian grants.

## Limits

- The paper is from 2018. The student counts, staffing and the 2018 pilot may have changed since.
- It gives no dollar cost for the Berkeley deployment or the cloud credits.
- It does not cover California's community colleges or the California State University as separate cases.
- The paper does not report how the 2018 pilot turned out.
