# Governor AI Cyber Defense Program release (web)

## Citation

## Bottom line

- On Aug. 10, 2026, the Governor directed state agencies to set up an AI Cyber Defense Program within the California Cybersecurity Integration Center (Cal-CSIC).
- The agencies must also expand AI-enabled cyber defenses for local governments and critical infrastructure partners, and name an AI Cybersecurity Officer in every state agency.
- The release ties the program to federal cuts. It names the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA) budget, MS-ISAC funding and the State and Local Cybersecurity Grant Program.

## Background

The release quotes Government Operations Agency Secretary Nick Maduros and Cal OES Director Caroline Thomas Jacobs. It also has one statement with no name next to it in the downloaded copy. It lists earlier state actions, for example SB 53 and Cal-Secure 2.0 ([Cal-Secure 2.0 (web)](Cal-Secure%202.0%20%28web%29.md)).

## Key claims

- "Establish an AI Cyber Defense Program within the California Cybersecurity Integration Center to leverage AI for vulnerability detection, network hardening, and incident response to strengthen the state’s cybersecurity posture and protect critical infrastructure." (directions to agencies)
- "Expand access to advanced cybersecurity capabilities for local governments and critical infrastructure partners, including AI-enabled defenses." (directions to agencies)
- "Designate an AI Cybersecurity Officer in every state agency." (directions to agencies)
- "Recent disclosures by leading AI developers demonstrated that advanced AI systems were capable of independently carrying out sophisticated cyber operations during controlled testing environments, exposing novel risks." (body)
- "The Trump administration’s proposed budget for Fiscal Year 2027 would cut the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency by roughly $707 million, about a 30% reduction from earlier funding levels" (federal programs list)
- "Federal funding for the Multi‑State Information Sharing and Analysis Center ended in late 2025." (federal programs list)

## Evidence and method

A government press release. It gives directions and quotes, not program text. Its federal figures link to trade press and an opinion piece, not to federal budget documents.

## Limits

The release gives no budget, staff count or start date for the program. It does not say which order or document holds the directions. It does not mention research compute or CalCompute.
