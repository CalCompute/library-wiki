# CCC 2027-28 Shared Advocacy Request Appendix E (web)

## Citation

California Community Colleges Board of Governors and Chancellor's Office. "Appendix E: The Future of Learning and Generative Artificial Intelligence Budget Request." 2027-28 Budget and Legislative Request, "Leading California’s Future." https://www.cccco.edu/-/media/CCCCO-Website/docs/2027-28-Shared-Advocacy-Request-Appendix-E.pdf. Copy: [`raw/web/2026-11-11 CCC 2027-28 Shared Advocacy Request Appendix E.md`](../../raw/web/2026-11-11%20CCC%202027-28%20Shared%20Advocacy%20Request%20Appendix%20E.md) (original: `.pdf` beside it). Read 2026-11-11 (all six pages). No date in the appendix; the executive summary is dated September 2026.

## Bottom line

- The California Community Colleges (CCC) ask the state for five AI investments in 2027-28, all from Proposition 98 General Fund.
- One asks for $9 million one-time and $12 million ongoing for a partnership with UC San Diego, the San Diego Supercomputer Center (SDSC) and CENIC. It would buy inference hardware in the CENIC AI Resource (CENIC AIR) and give all 116 colleges access.

## Background

Appendix E is the AI part of the system's 2027-28 Budget and Legislative Request ([CCC 2027-28 Shared Advocacy Request Executive Summary (web)](CCC%202027-28%20Shared%20Advocacy%20Request%20Executive%20Summary%20%28web%29.md)). It gives the reason and amount for each of five proposals. They are AI professional development, a Technology Interoperability Block Grant, shared agentic AI infrastructure, AI workflows through CENIC AIR, and an AI Innovation Fund.

## Key claims

- "we request $9 million one-time and $12 million ongoing Proposition 98 General Fund to establish an intersegmental artificial intelligence partnership with UC San Diego (UCSD), the San Diego Supercomputer Center (SDSC), and Corporation For Education Network Initiatives In California (CENIC), expanding access to the CENIC AI Resource (CENIC AIR) to all community colleges." (p. 4)
- "The one-time capital investment would fund inference-optimized AI hardware within CENIC AIR, granting all 116 community colleges access to sovereign supercomputing infrastructure powering California’s top research institutions." (p. 4)
- "The ongoing funding would support the hosting, power, staffing, and maintenance required to sustain the infrastructure, along with the systems engineering, technical training, platform integration, and governance needed to operationalize it systemwide." (p. 4)
- "California’s community colleges currently face a fragmented and increasingly costly AI environment, with individual colleges and technology systems relying on commercial platforms or developing separate capabilities that are difficult to secure and maintain across the system." (p. 4)
- "We request $10 million one-time and $30 million ongoing Proposition 98 General Fund to launch AI professional development and literacy efforts." (p. 1)
- "We request $100 million one-time Proposition 98 General Fund for a Technology Interoperability Block Grant." Of this, 5% is for statewide coordination and "$95 million distributed to districts" (p. 2)
- "we request a modest investment of approximately $6 million one-time and $3 million ongoing Proposition 98 General Fund to establish a shared agentic AI enablement layer" (p. 3)
- "we request $25 million one-time Proposition 98 General Fund to establish the Artificial Intelligence Innovation Fund." The grants "would support the development of open-source solutions" (p. 5)

## Evidence and method

A budget request from a public higher education system to the state. It states needs and amounts. It gives one outcome figure: AI tutoring tools reduced "failure rates in foundational mathematics from 35% to 11%" (pp. 4 to 5), with no study cited. It also points to the 2015 Higher Education Innovation Awards as a model (p. 5).

## Limits

A request, not an appropriation. The appendix gives no hardware size, GPU count or cost per college. It does not say how the colleges would govern access to CENIC AIR. The closing paragraph names an "Artificial Intelligence and Cybersecurity Initiative" (p. 6), a title that no section of the appendix uses. The PDF text splits some words; the quotations restore them.
