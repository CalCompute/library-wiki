# SB-53 Senate Judiciary analysis 2025-04-08

## Citation

California State Senate, Judiciary Committee (Senator Thomas Umberg, Chair), analysis of SB 53 (Wiener), version of March 27, 2025, for the hearing of April 8, 2025. <https://sjud.senate.ca.gov/system/files/2025-04/sb-53-wiener-sjud-analysis.pdf>. They are lines 10-28, 49-73 and 132-165 of a full transcription. `[...]` marks the cuts.

## Bottom line

- In March 2025 the bill was mainly CalCompute plus whistleblower protections. Its subject was "CalCompute: foundation models: whistleblowers".
- The CalCompute text then already had the mission, University of California (UC) placement and the three parts of CalCompute. It also had the seven Report elements of the enacted § 11546.8.
- The appointing authorities differ from the enacted law. The analysis names the President pro Tempore of the Senate, not the Senate Rules Committee.

## Background

The Senate Judiciary Committee heard SB 53 on April 8, 2025. The bill then had two parts: whistleblower protections at frontier AI developers and the CalCompute consortium. The large-developer transparency rules came later, in July 2025 ([Wiener SB-53 expansion release 2025-07-09](Wiener%20SB-53%20expansion%20release%202025-07-09.md)).

## Key claims

- **Digest.** "This bill establishes a consortium tasked with developing a framework for a public cloud computing cluster that advances the ethical development and deployment of AI for the public good."
- **Item 1** restates subd. (a) and (b), with the consortium "within GovOps", the Government Operations Agency.
- **Item 2** joins subd. (e) and (c): labor and workforce laws, and "reasonable efforts to ensure that CalCompute is established within the University of California (UC) to the extent possible."
- **Item 3** restates subd. (d)(1) to (3). **Item 4** gives the January 1, 2027 date and the seven elements, (a) to (g), in the enacted words.
- **Item 5, members.**
  > 5. Provides that the consortium shall, consistent with state constitutional law, consist of 14 members selected from specified fields and industries. Eight members are to be selected by GovOps, and the President pro Tempore of the Senate and the Speaker of the Assembly shall each select three members.
- **Comment 2, the resource gap.** The committee quotes the Washington Post: "Meta aims to procure 350,000 of the specialized computer chips [...] In contrast, Stanford's Natural Language Processing Group has 68 GPUs for all of its work." It also quotes: "Nearly 70 percent of people with PhDs in AI end up in private industry compared with 21 percent of graduates two decades ago".
- **Comment 2, Fei-Fei Li (quoted in the Post).** "The public sector is now significantly lagging in resources and talent compared to that of industry".
- **Author's statement.** SB 53 is "Establishing a process to create a public cloud-computing cluster (CalCompute) that will conduct research into the safe and secure deployment of large-scale artificial intelligence (AI) models."

## Evidence and method

A committee staff summary of the bill. The resource-gap figures come from a Washington Post article, footnote 1, which the excerpt does not give in full. That article cites "a Stanford report" and "a 2023 report". These are second-hand figures.

## Limits

- The GPU and PhD figures are press figures.
- The author's statement says CalCompute itself "will conduct research". The statute makes CalCompute a platform with staff, not a research body.

> ⚠️ Contradiction: Item 5 says the "President pro Tempore of the Senate" selects three members. Enacted § 11546.8 (g)(3) gives that power to the "Senate Rules Committee" ([SB-53 Gov Code 11546.8](SB-53%20Gov%20Code%2011546.8.md)). The text changed between March 27 and July 8, 2025 ([SB-53 Assembly Privacy analysis 2025-07-08](SB-53%20Assembly%20Privacy%20analysis%202025-07-08.md)).
