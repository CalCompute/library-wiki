# Jupyter decision-making guidelines (web)

## Citation

Project Jupyter. "Decision-Making Guide." Web page in the Jupyter Governance site, date not stated. https://jupyter.org/governance/decision-making. Read 2026-11-11.

## Bottom line

- The guide tells every Jupyter council how to make decisions: consensus seeking first, then a formal vote when needed.
- A vote runs for seven days. A simple majority of non-blank votes decides a yes-or-no question. Quorum is 50%.
- Members must take part in at least two-thirds of formal votes each year.

## Background

Project Jupyter publishes this guide in its governance site. It applies to all governing bodies of the project, which it calls "councils". It has required parts and optional parts. The Executive Council can step in when the process has problems. The site states the content licence "Creative Commons Zero v1.0 Universal (CC0-1.0)".

## Key claims

- Councils: "All official Jupyter projects, working groups, governing bodies, and official initiatives must have a clearly-defined and documented council." (Required aspects of decision making)
- Method: "Formally, these councils make decisions using consensus seeking with an option to call a vote to move the decision forward." (Required aspects of decision making)
- Vote period: "After the proposal is seconded by another member of the council, members have seven days to vote." (Calling a vote)
- Threshold: "The decision will be determined by a simple majority of non-blank votes for binary decisions (i.e., approving a proposal) and ranked choice for multi-class decisions (one among many, or several among many)." (Calling a vote)
- Participation: "All members of a council are required to participate in at least 2/3 of formal votes of that council per calendar year". (Voter participation and quorum)
- Quorum: "The quorum for all formal votes will be 50% and a “blank” option will always be included, with the “blank” option counting towards the quorum but not included in totals for calculating results." (Voter participation and quorum)
- Record: "the council will record the decision, e.g., in Team Compass issues for councils whose workflow is on GitHub or other equivalent and publicly visible mechanisms." (Recording)
- Reversibility: councils "should explicitly distinguish between decisions that are two-way doors (easy to reverse later) and one-way doors (difficult or impossible to reverse later)." (Optional aspects of decision making)

## Evidence and method

This is the project's own procedural rule. It states steps, periods and thresholds. It gives no data on how often councils vote.

## Limits

- The guide does not give a threshold above simple majority for any decision. Removal of a council member needs two-thirds, in the pages of each council ([Jupyter Executive Council page (web)](Jupyter%20Executive%20Council%20page%20%28web%29.md); [Jupyter Software Steering Council (web)](Jupyter%20Software%20Steering%20Council%20%28web%29.md)).
- Councils can adopt stricter rules. The guide does not list which councils do.
