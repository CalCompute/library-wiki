# USAC E-Rate Discount Matrix (web)

## Citation

Universal Service Administrative Company (USAC). "Discount Matrix." E-Rate program, one-page PDF, created or revised January 2024. https://www.usac.org/wp-content/uploads/e-rate/documents/samples/Discount-Matrix.pdf. Read 2026-11-12.

## Bottom line

- The E-Rate discount matrix sets the discount that a school district or library system gets on eligible equipment and services.
- The discount depends on the share of students eligible for the National School Lunch Program (NSLP) and on whether the applicant is urban or rural. It runs from 20% to 90%.
- At 75% to 100% NSLP eligibility, the discount is 90% for Category One (C1) and 85% for Category Two (C2). Tribal libraries get 90% for both from FY 2024.

## Background

USAC publishes this one-page matrix for E-Rate applicants. It points applicants to a "Calculating Discounts" page for more detail. It does not say what E-Rate is or who funds it.

## Key claims

- Purpose: "Applicants use the discount matrix to determine the correct discount level for a school district or library system."
- Calculation: "To receive discounts on eligible equipment and services, an applicant must calculate the percentage discount that it (and the entities it represents) is eligible to receive."
- Rows: "Less than 1%": "20%" urban and "25%" rural; "1% to 19%": "40%" and "50%"; "20% to 34%": "50%" and "60%"; "35% to 49%": "60%" and "70%"; "50% to 74%": "80%" and "80%".
- Top row: "75% to 100%": "90% for Category One (C1) and 85% for Category Two (C2)".
- Tribal libraries: "*For Tribal libraries, the highest C2 discount level was increased from 85% to 90% starting with FY 2024."

## Evidence and method

This is a one-page program table. The library read the PDF with its column layout, because the Markdown copy mixes the columns. It gives no data on applicants or funds.

## Limits

- The matrix does not define Category One and Category Two services, or "urban" and "rural".
- It does not say whether E-Rate covers computing or cloud services.
- It does not give the size of the fund or how many California applicants use it.
