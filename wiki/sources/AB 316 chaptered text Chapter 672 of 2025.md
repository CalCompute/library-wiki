## Bottom line

- AB 316 adds section 1714.46 to the Civil Code.
- A defendant who developed, modified or used artificial intelligence (AI) cannot assert that the AI autonomously caused the harm to the plaintiff.
- The defendant keeps every other affirmative defense. The defendant can give evidence on causation, foreseeability and comparative fault.

## Background

## Key claims

- "In an action against a defendant who developed, modified, or used artificial intelligence that is alleged to have caused a harm to the plaintiff, it shall not be a defense, and the defendant may not assert, that the artificial intelligence autonomously caused the harm to the plaintiff." (Civ. Code § 1714.46(b))
- "“Artificial intelligence” means an engineered or machine-based system that varies in its level of autonomy and that can, for explicit or implicit objectives, infer from the input it receives how to generate outputs that can influence physical or virtual environments." (§ 1714.46(a))
- "This section does not limit or preclude a defendant from presenting either of the following: (1) Any other affirmative defense, including evidence relevant to causation or foreseeability. (2) Other evidence relevant to the comparative fault of any other person or entity." (§ 1714.46(c))

## Limits

The section applies to civil actions. It sets no penalty and no duty to report. It does not define "autonomously". The bill gives no operative date.

The bill does not mention research compute or CalCompute.
