## Bottom line

- NSF asks for proposals from organizations to serve as resource providers in the Advanced Computing Systems and Services (ACSS) program. Category I capacity awards go up to $10,000,000; Category II prototype awards go up to $5,000,000. Each runs up to five years.
- At least 90 percent of each resource is to go to researchers through an open, peer-reviewed national allocation process. Operating costs come in a separate supplement of up to 20 percent of acquisition cost a year.

## Background

NSF posted this solicitation on Nov. 30, 2022. It replaces NSF 20-606, and NSF 24-583 replaces it ([NSF 24-583 Advanced Computing Systems and Services 2.0 solicitation (web)](NSF%2024-583%20Advanced%20Computing%20Systems%20and%20Services%202.0%20solicitation%20%28web%29.md)). The award type is a cooperative agreement.

Deadlines alternate between Category I and Category II, from Feb. 21, 2023 to Oct. 28, 2025. Allocation goes through the Advanced Cyberinfrastructure Coordination Ecosystem: Services & Support (ACCESS) or an NSF-approved alternative.

## Key claims

- "Resource providers would (1) provide advanced cyberinfrastructure (CI) resources in production operations to support the full range of computational- and data-intensive research across all of science and engineering (S&E), and (2) ensure democratized and equitable access to the proposed resources." (Synopsis of Program)
- "NSF anticipates that at least 90% of the provisioned resource will be available to the S&E community through an open peer-reviewed national allocation process" (Synopsis of Program)
- "It is anticipated that, during submission cycles accepting Category I proposals, 1-3 Category I awards will be made at up to $10,000,000 per award for up to five years. During cycles accepting Category II proposals, 1-4 Category II awards will be made at up to $5,000,000 per award for up to five years." (Award Information)
- "User support and operating costs are expected to be up to 20% of the total resource acquisition cost per year for each deployed Category I or Category II system/service for up to five years." A proposer can ask for an extra 5 percent "along with very strong justification". (Award Information)
- "This category particularly targets capabilities and/or services for small- to mid-scale jobs (from one to a few thousand cores per job) across broad areas of S&E, including support for “long-tail science” applications, as well as new classes of applications, such as artificial intelligence/machine learning/deep learning applications." (II, Category I)
- "It is expected that 96% of applications submitted to the proposed system should complete without having to be resubmitted because of a failure in the hardware or system software." (V.A, Resource Reliability and Usability)
- "NSF requires that, when averaged over a month, production resources should be unavailable as a result of scheduled and unscheduled maintenance no more than 5% of the time." (V.A, Resource Reliability and Usability)
- "Operating cost estimates should also include (if applicable) the cost of power and physical security, the cost of external network connectivity ... and costs associated with leasing machine room space, if necessary." (V.A, Operations Plan)
- "Inclusion of voluntary committed cost sharing is prohibited." (V.B, Cost Sharing)
- "An organization may submit only one proposal but may be a subawardee on other proposals responding to this solicitation." (IV, Limit on Number of Proposals per Organization)

## Evidence and method

A federal funding solicitation. The library read the summary of program requirements and Sections I to IV: introduction, program description, award information and eligibility. It read the Project Description sections and budget rules in Section V. It read the review process in Section VI and the award conditions and reporting in Section VII.

Three types of organization can apply:

- United States institutions of higher education;
- United States nonprofit research organizations;
- NSF-sponsored federally funded research and development centers (FFRDCs).

A Category I resource must be in production within 12 months of award. A Category II resource must move to production in the final 24 months.

## Limits

- NSF has archived the solicitation. It gives rules for proposals, not the results of the competition.
- Section III prints "$10,00,000" as the Category I limit. The summary and Section V.B give "$10,000,000".
- The programmatic terms and conditions are "TBD" in Section VII.B. It says that NSF negotiates more reporting with each resource provider before award.
- It sets no rule for the use of the 10 percent of a resource outside the national allocation process.
- The award conditions include a COVID-19 safety clause from Executive Order 14042. The later solicitation does not have it.
