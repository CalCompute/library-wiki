## Bottom line

- SB 574 sets rules for generative artificial intelligence (GenAI) in legal work. It adds Business and Professions Code section 6068.1 and Code of Civil Procedure sections 180 and 1282.1.
- An attorney must not delegate the practice of law to GenAI. An attorney who uses it must protect confidential information, verify the output and tell the court.
- A paper filed in court must not contain a citation that the responsible attorney has not personally verified.
- An arbitrator must not delegate any part of the decision to a GenAI tool.
- The Judicial Council must revisit its Standard 10.80 for the further development of GenAI.

## Key claims

- "An attorney shall not delegate the practice of law to generative artificial intelligence." (Bus. & Prof. Code § 6068.1(a)(2))
- "Verify the accuracy of generative artificial intelligence outputs, including, but not limited to, the accuracy of all case and statutory citations." (§ 6068.1(a)(3)(B)(i))
- "Disclose the use of generative artificial intelligence to the court for all documents submitted to the court and consider whether to disclose the use of generative artificial intelligence if it is used to create content provided to the public." (§ 6068.1(a)(3)(C))
- "A brief, pleading, motion, or any other paper filed in any court shall not contain any citations that an attorney responsible for submitting the pleading has not personally verified, including any citation provided by generative artificial intelligence." (Code Civ. Proc. § 128.7(b)(2)(A))
- "An arbitrator shall not delegate any part of their decisionmaking process to any generative artificial intelligence tool." (Code Civ. Proc. § 1282.1(b)(1))
- "The Judicial Council shall publicly revisit, and revise as necessary, Standard 10.80 of the California Standards of Judicial Administration to incorporate any necessary changes to reflect the further development of generative artificial intelligence." (Code Civ. Proc. § 180)

## Limits

The bill gives no cost estimate and no operative date. Section 6068.1 sets no penalty of its own. The bill does not print Standard 10.80. The copy does not mark which words of sections 6173 and 128.7 are new.

The bill does not mention research compute or CalCompute.
