## Bottom line

- SB 951 amends the California Worker Adjustment and Retraining Act (Cal/WARN Act), Labor Code sections 1400.5 and 1401. It adds section 1402.7.
- An employer gives more information in the 60-day notice when artificial intelligence (AI) or other automated technology causes a mass layoff, relocation or termination.
- The notice gives the number and type of jobs, the job functions that the technology automates and the category of AI system.
- The Employment Development Department (EDD) publishes a summary of these notices and a quarterly statewide summary.
- On or before Jan. 1, 2028, EDD reports to the Legislature on the effects of AI on business hiring practices.

## Key claims

- "An employer required to give notice of a mass layoff, relocation, or termination caused in whole or in substantial part by an AI system or other automated technology replacing or automating employment positions shall additionally include in the notice all of the following information:" (Lab. Code § 1401(d)(1))
- "The job functions performed by those replaced workers that will be automated by AI or other automated technology." (§ 1401(d)(1)(B))
- "The notice shall include the statement “This notice is for a technology displacement” at the top of the notice." (§ 1401(d)(1)(D))
- "The department shall also post a quarterly statewide summary of technology displacements reported pursuant to this article." (§ 1401(d)(2))
- "On or before January 1, 2028, the Employment Development Department shall submit to the Legislature a report on artificial intelligence’s effects on business hiring practices, including its impact on industries and occupations at the state and regional level." (§ 1402.7(b))
- "“Technological cessation in hiring” means the permanent ending of hiring or contracting for a particular occupation or position caused in whole or in part by the employer’s use of artificial intelligence or other automated technology" (§ 1402.7(a)(2))

## Limits

The bill gives no cost estimate and no operative date. The notice duty applies to a "covered establishment": an industrial or commercial facility with 75 or more employees (§ 1400.5(b)). The bill does not define "in substantial part". The EDD report includes "one or more" of four items, so the text does not require all of them (§ 1402.7(c)). The bill repeals section 1402.7 on Jan. 1, 2029. The copy does not mark which words of sections 1400.5 and 1401 are new.

The bill does not mention research compute or CalCompute.
