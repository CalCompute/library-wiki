# Civil Rights Council release automated-decision systems regulations approved 2025-06-30 (web)

## Citation

California Civil Rights Department (CRD). "Civil Rights Council Secures Approval for Regulations to Protect Against Employment Discrimination Related to Artificial Intelligence." Press release, June 30, 2025. calcivilrights.ca.gov. https://calcivilrights.ca.gov/2025/06/30/civil-rights-council-secures-approval-for-regulations-to-protect-against-employment-discrimination-related-to-artificial-intelligence/. Read 2026-11-19. Copy: `raw/web/2026-11-19 Civil Rights Council release automated-decision systems regulations approved 2025-06-30.html`.

## Bottom line

- The California Civil Rights Council got final approval for regulations on employment discrimination through artificial intelligence (AI), algorithms and other automated-decision systems. The rules take effect Oct. 1, 2025.
- The rules say that an automated-decision system can break California law if it harms applicants or employees based on protected characteristics. Employers must keep employment records, automated-decision data included, for at least four years.

## Background

The CRD enforces California's civil rights laws. The Civil Rights Council, with CRD staff, issues the regulations that carry out those laws. This release reports the approval and lists what the regulations do. The full text is on the Council's page, which the library has not read. See [California Civil Rights Department](../entities/California%20Civil%20Rights%20Department.md).

## Key claims

- "New rules are set to go into effect on October 1, 2025" (Subtitle)
- "The newly approved regulations provide increased clarity on how existing antidiscrimination laws apply to the use of artificial intelligence in employment decisions." (Paragraph 1)
- "Make it clear that the use of an automated-decision system may violate California law if it harms applicants or employees based on protected characteristics, such as gender, race, or disability." (What Do These Regulations Do?)
- "Ensure employers and covered entities maintain employment records, including automated-decision data, for a minimum of four years." (What Do These Regulations Do?)
- "Affirm that automated-decision system assessments, including tests, questions, or puzzle games that elicit information about a disability, may constitute an unlawful medical inquiry." (What Do These Regulations Do?)

## Evidence and method

A press release of about 1,000 words with the site's navigation text. It gives quotes from two Council members and the CRD Director, the reason for the rules, the process, and a four-item summary.

## Limits

The release does not give the regulation text, section numbers or the definition of "automated-decision system". It names more than 40 public comment letters but does not summarize them.
