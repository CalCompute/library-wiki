# CSET State of AI-Related Apprenticeships (web)

## Citation

Koslosky, Luke, and Jacob Feldgoise. "The State of AI-Related Apprenticeships." Data brief, Center for Security and Emerging Technology (CSET), Georgetown University, February 2025. doi: 10.51593/20220042. https://cset.georgetown.edu/wp-content/uploads/CSET-The-State-of-AI-Related-Apprenticeships.pdf. Read 2026-11-09.

## Bottom line

- From 2013 to 2023, U.S. Registered Apprenticeship programs registered over 19,000 new apprentices in AI-related occupations. Their average completion rate is 68 percent, 25 points above all non-military apprenticeships.
- California registered 1,823 of them, third after Missouri and Texas. AI-related apprenticeships were only 2.4 percent of new non-military apprenticeships in the peak year, 2022.

## Background

CSET is a research center at Georgetown University. The brief uses the Department of Labor's Registered Apprenticeship Partners Information Database System (RAPIDS). An "AI-related" apprenticeship is one in an occupation that shares skills with the work of building AI systems. The apprentices are not necessarily in AI roles.

## Key claims

- "We identified a total of over 19,000 new apprentices in AI-related occupations." (Executive Summary)
- "On average, 68% of apprentices in AI-related occupations completed their program, which is 25 percentage points higher than the completion rate of all (non-military) apprenticeships." (Executive Summary)
- "From 2013 through 2023, the top ten performing states registered 62% of new apprentices in AI-related occupations. Five of those states are not among the most populous states. Missouri, Texas, and California were the top three states in terms of new AI-related apprentices registered." (Executive Summary)
- "Despite growth, even in its peak year of 2022, new AI-related apprenticeships made up only 2.4% of all new non-military apprenticeships." (Conclusion, p. 26)
- "Between 2013 and 2023, the top occupations in AI-related apprenticeships were Application Developer, Computer Programmer, and Cyber Security Support Technician." (Methodology)
- "Federal and state governments should continue to support apprenticeship initiatives to sustain momentum, normalize apprenticeships as a valuable pathway for workers in AI-related and other technical fields, and expand pathways into quality jobs for a wide range of workers." (Conclusion, p. 26)

## Evidence and method

A data brief from a research institute, with a DOI. It analyses public apprentice records from RAPIDS for 2013 to 2023, matched to CSET's AI workforce taxonomy. Appendix B gives the state counts; California has 1,823. Military apprentices in the United Services Military Apprenticeship Program (USMAP) are left out.

## Limits

About 28 percent of RAPIDS records have no occupation code, and the brief leaves them out (Appendix A). The brief does not show that apprentices work in AI roles after they complete. It does not separate public sector sponsors from private ones. The text gives three totals: "over 19,000", 18,980 since 2015, and 19,257 for 2013 to 2023.
