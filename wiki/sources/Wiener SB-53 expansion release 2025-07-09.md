# Wiener SB-53 expansion release 2025-07-09

## Citation

Office of Senator Scott Wiener (D-San Francisco), "Senator Wiener Expands AI Bill Into Landmark Transparency Measure Based on Recommendations of Governor's Working Group", press release, Sacramento, July 9, 2025. <https://sd11.senate.ca.gov/news/senator-wiener-expands-ai-bill-landmark-transparency-measure-based-recommendations-governors>. Wiki copy: [raw/legislative/Wiener SB-53 expansion release 2025-07-09.md](../../raw/legislative/Wiener%20SB-53%20expansion%20release%202025-07-09.md). Read 2026-10-15.

They are lines 10-14, 18, 27, 52-54 and 69 of a full transcription. `[...]` marks the cuts.

## Bottom line

- The author's office describes CalCompute as more settled than the statute does. It says "housed at the University of California", with "free and low-cost access to compute for startups and academic researchers."
- The sponsors, Encode AI, Economic Security Action California and the Secure AI Project, frame CalCompute as "a public option for cloud computing".
- The release says SB 53 "retains" CalCompute. CalCompute was in the bill before the July 2025 expansion.

## Background

On July 9, 2025, Senator Wiener announced amendments that added frontier AI transparency rules to SB 53. The rules draw on the Governor's working group report. The CalCompute provisions stayed.

## Key claims

- **CalCompute retained.**
  > SB 53 retains provisions — called "CalCompute" — that advance a bold industrial strategy to boost AI development and democratize access to the most advanced AI models and tools. CalCompute will be a public cloud compute cluster housed at the University of California that provides free and low-cost access to compute for startups and academic researchers.
- **Benefits of AI (line 27).**
  > Recent advances in AI have delivered breakthrough benefits across several industries, from accelerating drug discovery and medical diagnostics to improving climate modeling and wildfire prediction. AI systems are revolutionizing education, increasing agricultural productivity, and helping solve complex scientific challenges.
- **New York comparison.** CalCompute helps "California secure its global leadership as states like New York establish their own AI research clusters."
- **Sponsors.** "SB 53 is sponsored by Encode AI, Economic Security Action California, and the Secure AI Project."
- **Teri Olle, co-sponsor.** The bill "would create a public option for cloud computing – the critical infrastructure necessary to fuel innovation and research. CalCompute would democratize access to this powerful resource that is currently enjoyed by a tiny handful of wealthy tech companies, and ensure that AI benefits the public."

## Evidence and method

An advocacy statement by the bill's author and sponsors. It gives no data. The benefits passage names fields but gives no examples or sources.

## Limits

- "Housed at the University of California" is stronger than subd. (c), which says "reasonable efforts [...] to the extent possible".
- "Free and low-cost access" and "startups and academic researchers" do not appear in § 11546.8.
- The release names the co-sponsor two ways: "Economic Security Action California" and "Economic Security California Action".

> ⚠️ Contradiction: The release says CalCompute "will be [...] housed at the University of California". Subd. (c) says only that "The consortium shall make reasonable efforts to ensure that CalCompute is established within the University of California to the extent possible." ([SB-53 Gov Code 11546.8](SB-53%20Gov%20Code%2011546.8.md)).

- The release says SB 53 "retains" CalCompute, and the April 8, 2025 analysis already uses the name.
