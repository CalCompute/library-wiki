## Bottom line

- NSF requests $201,000,000 in fiscal year (FY) 2026 for the Leadership-Class Computing Facility (LCCF). The Total Project Cost of $457.45 million "remains intact".
- The Texas Advanced Computing Center (TACC) leads the LCCF. Its core system, Horizon, goes in leased space at a Sabey Inc. data center. NSF estimates operations at "approximately $40 million annually".

## Background

This is the LCCF chapter of the NSF budget request to Congress for FY 2026. It describes the facility, its design and construction history, its governance, its cost and schedule, its reviews and its risks. LCCF has four Distributed Science Centers:

- the Atlanta University Center Consortium (AUCC);
- the National Center for Supercomputing Applications (NCSA) at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign;
- the Pittsburgh Supercomputing Center (PSC);
- the San Diego Supercomputer Center (SDSC).

The NSF award record for construction is [NSF award 2323116 LCCF construction API record (web)](NSF%20award%202323116%20LCCF%20construction%20API%20record%20%28web%29.md).

## Key claims

- "LCCF, led by the Texas Advanced Computing Center (TACC) at the University of Texas at Austin, is envisioned as a distributed facility that will provide unique computational and data analytics capabilities, as well as critical software and services, for the nation’s S&E research community to enable discoveries that would not be possible otherwise." (Brief Description, p. MREFC-14)
- "the partnership with SDSC will focus on supporting Machine Learning (ML) and instrument data analytics in scientific workflows, and methods to democratize access to LCCF." (Brief Description, p. MREFC-14)
- "In August 2024, the project signed a colocation agreement with Sabey Inc. to provide 32,000 square feet of customized datacenter space for high-density utility hosting of the primary LCCF computing system called Horizon." (Project Status, p. MREFC-15)
- "The PD is advised by two groups: the Technology Advisory Board and the Science Advisory Board." (External Governance Structure, p. MREFC-17)
- "The project began construction in July 2024. The project construction schedule has a duration of 34 months, providing for facility acceptance in FY 2027." (Cost and Schedule, p. MREFC-17)
- "Contingent on the availability of funds, NSF plans to support LCCF operations and maintenance for an initial five years with a possibility of renewal for another five years. The current estimate is that LCCF operations will cost approximately $40 million annually." (Future Operations Costs, p. MREFC-17)
- "With the eventual discontinuation of NSF support for LCCF, the main data center will be returned to the colocation provider, and the computing system will be repurposed by the recipient." (Future Operations Costs, p. MREFC-18)
- "In particular, the facility will be a key element in advancing a national research infrastructure to broaden access for the entire nation to critically-needed AI resources, as envisioned in the White House National AI Research Resource Task Force report." (Meeting Intellectual Community Needs, p. MREFC-16)

## Evidence and method

A federal budget justification for one major facility. It gives the FY 2026 request, the Total Project Cost (TPC), the design review dates, the construction schedule, the operations estimate and the risks. NSF oversight goes through a Program Officer in the Office of Advanced Cyberinfrastructure (OAC) and an Integrated Project Team. The chapter says that independent cost estimate reviews informed the risk-adjusted TPC. It also has an Education and Public Outreach effort with a Visitor Center at TACC.

## Limits

- The chapter does not give the size of Horizon in processors or performance. It names Dell and NVIDIA only as partners in "technology discussions".
- It does not say how users get time on LCCF or what share goes to each user group.
- It does not split the $40 million operations estimate by cost type.
- The chapter has no date. It reports events up to early 2025 and expects the data center space in March 2026.
