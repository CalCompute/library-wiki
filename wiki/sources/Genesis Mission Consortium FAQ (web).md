# Genesis Mission Consortium FAQ (web)

## Citation

Genesis Mission Consortium. "Frequently Asked Questions." Web page, date not stated. https://genesismissionconsortium.org/frequently-asked-questions. Read 2026-11-18.

## Bottom line

- The Genesis Mission Consortium charges no annual membership dues "at this time". Members contribute capabilities, resources and expertise instead. Universities can join.
- The Genesis Mission Partnership Exchange is a free online platform to find partners. Membership is not necessary to use it. It holds a Resource Inventory for authorized users.

## Background

The Genesis Mission Consortium is a public-private partnership for the U.S. Department of Energy (DOE) Genesis Mission. RTI International, operating as TechWerx, administers it ([DOE Genesis Mission Consortium launch release 2026-02-09 (web)](DOE%20Genesis%20Mission%20Consortium%20launch%20release%202026-02-09%20%28web%29.md)). This page answers questions in two parts: general questions on membership, and questions on the Partnership Exchange.

## Key claims

- The page defines the Genesis Mission as an effort "to harness the power of super intelligence (SI) to accelerate scientific discovery, strengthen national security, and ensure America leads in energy and emerging technologies." (What is the Genesis Mission Consortium?)
- "Membership opportunities are available to a range of organizations, including companies, universities, national laboratories, nonprofits, and other stakeholders." (How can I get involved?)
- "There are no annual membership dues at this time, though the Consortium's Executive Committee may establish dues in the future if needed." (Are there any costs?)
- "Instead, members are expected to contribute relevant capabilities, resources, and expertise, such as technical infrastructure, personnel time, data, or financial or in-kind support, to advance the Genesis Mission." (Are there any costs?)
- "University members may contribute high-value resources, such as scientific datasets, experimental facilities, or computing resources, and/or make institution-level commitments to SI-for-science workforce development." (Can universities join?)
- "No. Consortium membership is not required to create a profile or use the platform." (Do I need to be a member to use the Partnership Exchange?)
- "The platform includes both keyword-based and SI-assisted matching capabilities." (How are suggested partners identified?)
- "The Partnership Exchange is currently available at no cost to participants." (Is there a cost?)
- "Authorized users, including Genesis Mission RFA Phase I awardees, can browse resources and submit resource requests through the platform." (What is the Resource Inventory?)

## Evidence and method

An institutional FAQ page. It states the terms of membership and the functions of the Partnership Exchange. It gives no figures, member names or dates.

## Limits

- The page names an Executive Committee but does not say who sits on it or how members choose it.
- It does not say what the Resource Inventory holds, or who other than Phase I awardees is an authorized user. It does not spell out "RFA".
- It says that the Partnership Exchange does not select project teams or decide funding eligibility.
- The page has no date, so the "no annual membership dues" term can change.
