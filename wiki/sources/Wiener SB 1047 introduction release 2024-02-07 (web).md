# Wiener SB 1047 introduction release 2024-02-07 (web)

## Citation

Office of Senator Scott Wiener (D-San Francisco). "Senator Wiener Introduces Legislation to Ensure Safe Development of Large-Scale Artificial Intelligence Systems and Support AI Innovation in California." Press release, Sacramento, February 7, 2024. <https://sd11.senate.ca.gov/news/senator-wiener-introduces-legislation-ensure-safe-development-large-scale-artificial-1>. Read 2026-10-20.

## Bottom line

- SB 1047 was the 2024 bill that came before SB 53. Its release says the bill "establishing CalCompute", a cluster for "startups, researchers, and community groups".
- It adds "community groups" as users. SB 53's statute names no users.
- SB 1047 also required cloud companies "to provide transparent pricing and avoid price discrimination".

## Background

It followed the SB 294 intent bill ([Wiener SB 294 announcement 2023-09-13](Wiener%20SB%20294%20announcement%202023-09-13.md)). Governor Newsom vetoed SB 1047 in September 2024 ([SB-53 Senate GO analysis 2025-03-25 (web)](SB-53%20Senate%20GO%20analysis%202025-03-25%20%28web%29.md)). SB 53 carried the CalCompute provisions forward in 2025.

## Key claims

- **CalCompute established (lede).** "The bill also invests in the future of California's AI industry by establishing CalCompute, a public cloud computing cluster that will allow startups, researchers, and community groups to participate in the development of large-scale AI systems."
- **Senator Wiener.**
  > SB 1047 will also promote the growth of the AI industry by establishing CalCompute, a public AI research cluster that will allow startups, researchers, and community groups to participate in the development of large-scale AI systems. By providing a broad range of stakeholders with access to the AI development process, CalCompute will help align large-scale AI systems with the values and needs of California communities.
- **Research role.** SB 1047 "creates a new public cloud-computing cluster, CalCompute, that will conduct research into the safe and secure deployment of large-scale AI models, while allowing smaller startups, researchers, and community groups to participate".
- **Pricing.** The bill "requires cloud-computing companies and frontier model developers to provide transparent pricing and avoid price discrimination."
- **Training cost.** Models at "10^26 flop would cost over $100,000,000 to train".
- **Sponsors.** "SB 1047 is sponsored by the Center For AI Safety Action Fund, Encode Justice, and Economic Security California."
- **Teri Olle, Economic Security California.**
  > The AI market is dominated by a handful of corporate actors and this essential legislation takes the first critical step in fostering greater innovation and openness to serve the public interest. With the development of a public cloud like CalCompute, we can harness the potential of AI for good, accessing data to advance the next breakthroughs in healthcare, technology and disaster response
- **Nathan Calvin, Center for AI Safety Action Fund.** The state should make "sure AI innovations are accessible to academic researchers and startups."
- **Evan Conrad, San Francisco Compute Company.** "we're excited to see support for academic research and startups within the bill."
- **State context.** The release cites Governor Newsom's September 2023 Executive Order and the November 2023 GenAI report.

## Evidence and method

An advocacy statement by the author and sponsors. It cites a survey of AI researchers and links to federal documents. It gives no data on CalCompute.

## Limits

- Governor Newsom vetoed SB 1047. It is legislative history for SB 53, not law.
- The release does not say where CalCompute sits or who governs it.
- The release names "Encode Justice". Later SB 53 sources name "Encode" and "Encode AI". This release does not say they are the same organization.

> ⚠️ Contradiction: The release says SB 1047 is "establishing CalCompute" and "creates a new public cloud-computing cluster". Enacted § 11546.8 (a) establishes "a consortium that shall develop [...] a framework for the creation of a public cloud computing cluster" ([SB-53 Gov Code 11546.8](SB-53%20Gov%20Code%2011546.8.md)).

> ⚠️ Contradiction: The release names "startups, researchers, and community groups" as users. Enacted subd. (f)(2)(D) asks the Report for "a process for determining which users and projects will be supported by CalCompute" ([SB-53 Gov Code 11546.8](SB-53%20Gov%20Code%2011546.8.md)). The statute names no user group.
