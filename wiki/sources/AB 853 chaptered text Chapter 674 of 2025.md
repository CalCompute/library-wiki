## Bottom line

- AB 853 amends the California AI Transparency Act in the Business and Professions Code. It moves the operative date of the chapter to Aug. 2, 2026.
- From Jan. 1, 2027, a large online platform must detect provenance data, show it to users and not strip it.
- From Jan. 1, 2027, a hosting platform must not knowingly make available a generative artificial intelligence (GenAI) system that does not place the required disclosures.
- From Jan. 1, 2028, a capture device manufacturer must give users the option of a latent disclosure in the content that a new device captures.
- The civil penalty is $5,000 for each violation. The Attorney General, a city attorney or a county counsel can collect it.

## Background

The bill's author is Assembly Member Wicks, as the Digest prints the name. The Governor approved it on Oct. 13, 2025.

The text holds the Legislative Counsel's Digest and seven sections. The 2024 act is [SB 942 chaptered text Chapter 291 of 2024](SB%20942%20chaptered%20text%20Chapter%20291%20of%202024.md). AB 853 amends sections 22757.1, 22757.4 and 22757.6, and adds sections 22757.3.1, 22757.3.2 and 22757.3.3. A 2026 bill amends section 22757.3.1 again ([AB 2713 chaptered text Chapter 856 of 2026](AB%202713%20chaptered%20text%20Chapter%20856%20of%202026.md)). ## Key claims

- "Detect whether any provenance data that is compliant with widely adopted specifications adopted by an established standards-setting body is embedded into or attached to content distributed on the large online platform." (Bus. & Prof. Code § 22757.3.1(a)(1))
- "A large online platform shall not, to the extent technically feasible, knowingly strip any system provenance data or digital signature that is compliant with widely adopted specifications adopted by an established standards-setting body from content uploaded or distributed on the large online platform." (§ 22757.3.1(b))
- "A GenAI system hosting platform shall not knowingly make available a GenAI system that does not place disclosures pursuant to Section 22757.3." (§ 22757.3.2(a))
- "“Large online platform” means a public-facing social media platform, file-sharing platform, mass messaging platform, or stand-alone search engine that distributes content to users who did not create or collaborate in creating the content that exceeded 2,000,000 unique monthly users during the preceding 12 months." (§ 22757.1(h)(1))
- "A violator of this chapter shall be liable for a civil penalty in the amount of five thousand dollars ($5,000) per violation to be collected in a civil action filed by the Attorney General, a city attorney, or a county counsel." (§ 22757.4(a)(1))
- "This chapter shall become operative on August 2, 2026." (§ 22757.6)

## Limits

The bill gives no cost estimate. It does not print section 22757.3, which holds the disclosure duties of a covered provider. Section 22757.1(g) defines "GenAI hosting platform", and section 22757.3.2 uses "GenAI system hosting platform". A capture device manufacturer complies "only to the extent technically feasible and compliant with widely adopted specifications" (§ 22757.3.3(b)). The copy shows the 2025 text of section 22757.3.1; AB 2713 gives the later text.

The bill does not mention research compute or CalCompute.
