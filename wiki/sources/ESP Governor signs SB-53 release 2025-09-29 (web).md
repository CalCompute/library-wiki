# ESP Governor signs SB-53 release 2025-09-29 (web)

## Citation

Economic Security Project. "Governor Newsom Signs SB 53, Establishing California as Global Leader in AI Safety and Innovation." Press release, Sacramento, September 29, 2025. https://economicsecurityproject.org/news/governor-newsom-signs-sb-53/. Read 2026-10-20.

## Bottom line

- The co-sponsor of SB 53 calls CalCompute "a public option" that will "dilute this concentrated market". This is the sponsor's statement of intent.
- The release says compute for advanced AI "is dominated by just three companies: Amazon, Google, and Microsoft". It gives no source.
- The release says the law "creates CalCompute". The statute only sets up a consortium to write a framework. The release is advocacy.

## Background

The Economic Security Project (ESP) published this release on the day Governor [Gavin Newsom](../entities/Gov.%20Gavin%20Newsom.md) signed SB 53. Its California affiliate, Economic Security California Action, co-sponsored the bill. The same group co-sponsored SB 1047 in 2024 ([ESP Action CalCompute public cloud release 2024-02-08 (web)](ESP%20Action%20CalCompute%20public%20cloud%20release%202024-02-08%20%28web%29.md)). The Governor's own release for the same day is [Newsom SB-53 signing release 2025-09-29](Newsom%20SB-53%20signing%20release%202025-09-29.md).

## Key claims

- Subtitle: "Landmark legislation creates transparency requirements for advanced AI systems and establishes a public option for cloud computing".
- SB 53 is "legislation that creates CalCompute, a public computing infrastructure designed to broaden access to artificial intelligence development resources".
- "The legislation follows recommendations from Governor Newsom's working group of leading AI experts established after his veto of Sen. Wiener's SB 1047 last year."
- "The bill is co-sponsored by Economic Security California affiliate Economic Security California Action."
- Teri Olle, "Director of Economic Security Project California": "Until today, Big Tech execs have had full control of AI, they've had no guardrails to prevent widespread harm".
- Olle: "What's more, we have an investment in the computing power that will allow for AI innovations that serve the public for generations to come."
- "the law creates CalCompute, a public cloud computing cluster that provides AI infrastructure for startups, researchers, and entrepreneurs that will democratize access and enable innovators to use AI to solve our most pressing public problems."
- "Currently, computing power needed for advanced AI development is dominated by just three companies: Amazon, Google, and Microsoft."
- "CalCompute will provide a public option, dilute this concentrated market, and create AI solutions that are desperately needed."
- "California has a history of making strategic public investments in emerging technologies. CalCompute follows this tradition by creating space for innovations that benefit society broadly."
- "SB 53 ensures that the computing power needed to fuel these technological advances benefits the public, not just the few tech companies that currently control the lion's share of the market."
- The release cites "80% of adults surveyed by Gallup this spring" who "said they favor safety and security over speed in AI development."

## Evidence and method

An organization press release. It links the Governor's release, the working group's site, a Gallup poll and news stories. It gives no source for the three-company claim.

## Limits

- The release is advocacy. It states the sponsor's goals in the present tense, as if the law already built a cluster.
- The three-company claim has no data or source in the release.
- "Public option" is the sponsor's term. The statute does not use it.

> ⚠️ Contradiction: The release says "the law creates CalCompute, a public cloud computing cluster that provides AI infrastructure for startups, researchers, and entrepreneurs". § 11546.8 (a) sets up "a consortium that shall develop, pursuant to this section, a framework for the creation of a public cloud computing cluster to be known as "CalCompute."" ([SB-53 Gov Code 11546.8](SB-53%20Gov%20Code%2011546.8.md)). The statute governs.

> ⚠️ Contradiction: Olle says "we have an investment in the computing power". The statute contains no funds for a cluster. Subdivision (f)(2)(B) asks the Report for "An analysis of the cost to the state to build and maintain CalCompute and recommendations for potential funding sources" ([SB-53 Gov Code 11546.8](SB-53%20Gov%20Code%2011546.8.md)).

> ⚠️ Contradiction: The Governor's release calls the consortium "CalCompute" ([Newsom SB-53 signing release 2025-09-29](Newsom%20SB-53%20signing%20release%202025-09-29.md)). This release calls the cluster "CalCompute". The statute agrees with this release on the name. Both releases go past the statute on what the law does.
