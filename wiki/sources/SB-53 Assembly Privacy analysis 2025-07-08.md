## Bottom line

- The committee's summary of the bill (items 17 to 28) already matches the enacted § 11546.8 almost item for item.
- The University of California is a registered supporter of SB 53.
- Item 28 shortens subd. (k). It drops the words "or other measure".

## Background

The Assembly Committee on Privacy and Consumer Protection heard SB 53 on July 16, 2025. The bill's subject then was "CalCompute: foundation models: whistleblowers". The analysis compares the bill with SB 1047 (2024), which also had a CalCompute consortium.

## Key claims

- **Items 17 to 27** restate § 11546.8 (a) to (j). The analysis calls the Government Operations Agency "GovOps". The items cover the mission, University of California (UC) placement, the three parts of CalCompute and labor laws.
- **Item 28.**
  > 28) Requires that CalCompute become operative only upon an appropriation.
- **SB 1047 comparison.**
  > The bill also would have established, within GovOps, a consortium tasked with developing a framework for a public cloud computing cluster, CalCompute, to support the safe development and deployment of AI.
- **Why CalCompute.**
  > This initiative responds to the fact that academic institutions currently lack sufficient computing power to conduct research at the scale of large developers. This creates a resource and research gap, where the academic institutions, typically responsible for studying the safe and effective use of new technologies, are unable to keep pace with advancements at the AI frontier.
- **International AI Safety Report.** "The recently released International AI Safety Report, developed by nearly 100 internationally recognized experts from 30 countries led by Turing Award winner Yoshua Bengio, sets forth three general risk categories associated with frontier models: malicious use, malfunctions, and systemic risk."
- **Systemic risks, from that report.** "Such risks include labor market disruption, global AI research and development concentration, market concentration, single points of failure, environmental risks, privacy risks, and copyright infringement."
- **Systemic risk.** "Due to the high costs of developing AI systems, a small number of large technology companies dominate the frontier model market".
- **Evaluator access (quoted in the analysis).** The feedback highlighted "the immature third-party evaluation ecosystem, the lack of evaluation standards and broader measurement science, and the current barriers for evaluators to acquire access to the AI models and systems they evaluate."
- **Industrial policy.**
  > Moreover, a core function of government, is to craft sound industrial policy that encourages the growth of new markets serving the public good when economic forces do not support that market.
- **UC placement.** The committee writes: "The consortium must prioritize locating CalCompute within the University of California system."
- **Support letter (quoted in the analysis).** CalCompute is "a public cloud computing cluster for use by academics and startups in California", which "mirrors a similar computing cluster that is already being established in New York state." The letter says SB 53 "thoughtfully implements the recommendations of the Report", meaning the Governor's working group report.
- **Registered Support: University of California.** The list of supporters under "REGISTERED SUPPORT / OPPOSITION" includes "University of California". UC is a registered supporter of SB 53.

## Evidence and method

The analysis summarizes the bill and gives the committee staff's comments. The "resource and research gap" claim has no cited data in the excerpt. Footnote 33 and note 34 support the systemic risk paragraph, but the excerpt does not give them.

## Limits

- The excerpt does not show the "Proposed Amendments" the heading names.
- "Must prioritize" is the committee's paraphrase. Subd. (c) says "make reasonable efforts to ensure [...] to the extent possible".
- The industrial policy passage is about AI auditing.
- The excerpt shows "University of California" in the support list but not the rest of the list.
- The file's header says "Hearing Date: July 8, 2025". The analysis itself says "Date of Hearing: July 16, 2025" and "As Amended July 8, 2025".

> ⚠️ Contradiction: Item 28 says "Requires that CalCompute become operative only upon an appropriation." Subd.

> July 8 is the amendment date.
