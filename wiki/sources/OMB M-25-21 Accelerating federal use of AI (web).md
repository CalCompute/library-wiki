# OMB M-25-21 Accelerating federal use of AI (web)

## Bottom line

- M-25-21 tells federal agencies to speed up their use of AI, with three priorities: innovation, governance and public trust. It rescinds and replaces OMB Memorandum M-24-10.
- Each agency must name a Chief AI Officer within 60 days. Each CFO Act agency must publish an AI strategy within 180 days.
- For "high-impact AI", agencies must apply minimum risk management practices within 365 days, including pre-deployment testing and an AI impact assessment. A Chief AI Officer can waive a practice.

## Background

OMB issued the memorandum under Executive Order 14179 of Jan. 23, 2025. It applies to all executive branch agencies, including independent regulatory agencies. The memorandum has an overview and an appendix with the detailed requirements: AI strategies, sharing of resources, procurement, the workforce, governance bodies and risk management. Sections 4(a) and 4(b) of the appendix do not apply to the Intelligence Community. Compare the state guidance for California agencies in [California GenAI guidelines for public sector procurement uses and training 2024-03 (web)](California%20GenAI%20guidelines%20for%20public%20sector%20procurement%20uses%20and%20training%202024-03%20%28web%29.md).

## Key claims

- "This memorandum rescinds and replaces Office of Management and Budget (OMB) Memorandum M-24-10, Advancing Governance, Innovation, and Risk Management for Agency Use of Artificial Intelligence." (Overview, p. 1)
- "Agencies must also reuse resources that enable AI adoption, such as agency data, models, code, and assessments of AI performance." (Guidance on federal use of AI, p. 2)
- "Agencies should ensure that their AI projects have access to adequate IT infrastructure, including high-performance computing infrastructure specialized for AI training and inference, where necessary." (Appendix, footnote 10)
- "Within 60 days of the issuance of this memorandum, the head of each agency must retain or designate a Chief AI Officer (CAIO)." (Appendix, Chief AI Officers)
- "AI is considered high-impact when its output serves as a principal basis for decisions or actions that have a legal, material, binding, or significant effect on rights or safety." (Appendix, Section 4(a))
- "In conducting pre-deployment testing, if an agency does not have access to the underlying AI source code, models, or data, the agency must use alternative test methodologies, such as querying the AI service and observing the outputs or providing evaluation data to the vendor and obtaining results." (Appendix, Section 4(b))
- "Agencies must complete an AI impact assessment before deploying any high-impact AI use case." (Appendix, Section 4(b))

## Evidence and method

A federal policy memorandum. It states duties and deadlines for agencies. It gives no data. The copy is a scan with text; some characters are wrong in the copy (for example "0MB" for "OMB").

## Limits

This page reads the overview, the strategy and Chief AI Officer passages, footnotes 9 to 12, and the high-impact AI sections 4(a) and 4(b). It does not read the full lists of presumed high-impact uses in Section 6 or the reporting tables. The memorandum covers federal agencies only. It does not set rules for states, universities or research computing programs. The address gives "2025/02", but the memorandum carries the date April 3, 2025.
