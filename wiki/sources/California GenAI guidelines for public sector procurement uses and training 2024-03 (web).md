## Bottom line

- The guidelines are the state's interim guidance on generative artificial intelligence (GenAI) in state entities. They cover how to buy it, use it and train staff for it, under section 3.a of Executive Order N-12-23.
- Every state entity must prepare for "incidental" GenAI purchases. An entity that buys GenAI on purpose has five more steps, a CIO risk evaluation and a consultation with CDT.
- Training comes in three phases: executives, legal, labor and privacy staff first; then program and technical staff; then the general workforce.

## Background

Executive Order N-12-23, signed on Sept. 6, 2023, told GovOps, DGS, CDT and the California Cybersecurity Integration Center to issue these guidelines. Five state bodies wrote them. They build on the state's November 2023 report on the benefits and risks of GenAI. They call themselves interim guidance until a final policy in 2025.

[GovOps generative AI executive order page (web)](GovOps%20generative%20AI%20executive%20order%20page%20%28web%29.md) lists the document. The later state policy is in the State Administrative Manual: see [SAM 4986.9 GenAI procurement (web)](SAM%204986.9%20GenAI%20procurement%20%28web%29.md) and [CDT Technology Letter 25-01 (web)](CDT%20Technology%20Letter%2025-01%20%28web%29.md).

## Key claims

- "This document builds upon the State of California: Benefits and Risks of Generative Artificial Intelligence Report published in November 2023 and serves as interim guidance as California seeks to publish the final procurement and training policy in 2025, after extensive piloting, research and stakeholder engagement." (Executive Summary, p. 2)
- "Each state entity is responsible for the ethical, transparent and trustworthy deployment of GenAI in their organization and must assess the impact to the state workforce." (Section III, p. 9)
- "All state entities are required to conduct and submit an inventory of all uses of GenAI – intentional and incidental – to the California Department of Technology." (Section III, p. 10)
- "Deployment of GenAI technologies must be evaluated through a risk assessment based on the National Institute of Standards and Technology's AI Risk Management Framework, as well as relevant portions of the SAM and State Information Management Manual (SIMM)." (Section V, p. 13)
- "A written solicitation is required every time a state entity is purchasing GenAI. Use of acquisition methods that do not require a solicitation, such as the Fair and Reasonable Acquisition Method, is prohibited when purchasing GenAI." (Section VI, p. 14)
- "State entities must assign a GenAI subject matter expert to assist with contract management functions." (Section VI, p. 14)
- "General workforce roles should receive general education and training before GenAI tools are deployed." (Section IV, Phase 3, p. 10)

## Evidence and method

A state guidance document of 16 pages, in seven sections. It states duties and process steps for state entities. It gives a table of common GenAI use cases, taken from the November 2023 report. It gives no cost data or evaluation results.

## Limits

The guidelines are interim and dated March 2024. They do not give the risk assessment form itself; they point to SIMM 5305-F and a "GenAI Toolkit". They say nothing about compute capacity, hosting or the cost of GenAI. Later policy changes some steps. The guidelines require a CDT consultation for every GenAI purchase. [SAM 4986.9 GenAI procurement (web)](SAM%204986.9%20GenAI%20procurement%20%28web%29.md), revised November 2025, requires the consultation only for moderate or high risk, and lets a low-risk purchase continue.
