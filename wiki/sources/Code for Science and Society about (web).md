# Code for Science and Society about (web)

## Citation

Code for Science & Society (CS&S). "Organization." About page, codeforsociety.org. Date not stated. https://www.codeforsociety.org/about. Read 2026-11-19.

## Bottom line

- Code for Science & Society is a U.S. public charity, based in Portland, Oregon, that works on public interest technology.
- It partners with projects in research, open data, open source software and digital infrastructure. It also works with "nonprofits, funders, universities, and other stakeholders" on community governance and participatory funding.

## Background

The page is the organization's own description of its mission and strategy. It links to pages on its people, its history since 2016, its projects and its financial and legal documents. It gives its employer identification number (EIN).

## Key claims

- "We work to advance the power of data to improve the social and economic lives of all people through public education, scientific research, and technology development and deployment." (Our mission)
- "Since 2016, CS&S has built programs and united projects aligned with our mission to create a thriving public interest technology community." (History)
- "We promote technology initiatives that are equitable, open-source, and sustainable to advance global access to information." (Our strategy)
- "To support work in our mission area, we also partner with nonprofits, funders, universities, and other stakeholders to explore community governance, develop participatory funding programs, and explore new ways to centering power in communities." (Our strategy)
- "Code for Science and Society is a registered US public charity." (footer)

## Evidence and method

A mission statement of about 500 words. It states the organization's aims and the kinds of partners it works with. It names no specific project, budget or result.

## Limits

The page does not list the projects that CS&S supports or the services it gives them. It does not give its budget or funders. Its financial documents are on a separate page that the library has not read.
