## Bottom line

- NSF award 2614022 to Carnegie Mellon University (CMU) funds Bridges-3 at the Pittsburgh Supercomputing Center (PSC). It is a $10,000,000 cooperative agreement, all obligated in fiscal year (FY) 2026.
- The abstract says that Bridges-3 continues the role of PSC in the NSF Advanced Computing Systems and Services (ACSS) program. It names "seamless interoperability" with three national resources.
- The three are the Advanced Cyberinfrastructure Coordination Ecosystem: Services & Support (ACCESS), the National AI Research Resource (NAIRR) and the Leadership-Class Computing Facility (LCCF).

## Background

This is the NSF awards API record for the Bridges-3 award, in JavaScript Object Notation (JSON) format. The Office of Advanced Cyberinfrastructure (OAC) manages it. The principal investigator (PI) is Bruno Ricardi de Abreu. The title starts "Category I", the capacity category of the ACSS program ([NSF 24-583 Advanced Computing Systems and Services 2.0 solicitation (web)](NSF%2024-583%20Advanced%20Computing%20Systems%20and%20Services%202.0%20solicitation%20%28web%29.md)). The CMU news story on the award is [CMU NSF award for Bridges-3 2026-07 (web)](CMU%20NSF%20award%20for%20Bridges-3%202026-07%20%28web%29.md).

## Key claims

- "Bridges-3 is the continuation of PSC’s long standing role in providing production quality national cyberinfrastructure through the NSF Advanced Computing Systems & Services (ACSS) program." (abstract)
- "Bridges-3 offers a balanced, converged environment that integrates advanced GPU accelerated computing, high performance, large-memory CPUs, a hardened all flash parallel file system, and a high bandwidth InfiniBand fabric" (abstract)
- "The overall architecture is informed by science drivers drawn from the critical scientific domains listed in memorandum M-25-34/NSTM-2 from the Office of Science and Technology Policy" (abstract)
- The abstract names "seamless interoperability with national platforms and resources such as the Advanced Cyberinfrastructure Coordination Ecosystem: Services & Support (ACCESS) capability, the National AI Research Resource (NAIRR), and the Leadership-Class Computing Facility (LCCF)". (abstract)
- Estimated total: "10000000". Obligated: "FY 2026 = $10,000,000.00". Start "06/01/2026", end "05/31/2027". Fund program: "Innovative HPC". (record)

## Evidence and method

A federal award record from the NSF awards API. It gives the recipient, the PI and co-PIs, the program officer, the dates, the amounts and an abstract. The $10,000,000 total matches the "$10 million NSF award" in the CMU story.

## Limits

- The record does not give the number of graphics processing units (GPUs) or the peak performance.
- It does not say how users get time on Bridges-3. The CMU story says allocation is "primarily" through ACCESS and the NAIRR Pilot.
- The award period ends May 31, 2027. The CMU story expects full operation in summer 2027. The record does not say whether NSF extends or supplements the award.
- The record gives no operating cost supplement.
