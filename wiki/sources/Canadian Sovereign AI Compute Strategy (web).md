# Canadian Sovereign AI Compute Strategy (web)

## Citation

Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada (ISED). "Canadian Sovereign AI Compute Strategy." Government of Canada web page. Date modified: 2026-06-04. https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/ised/en/canadian-sovereign-ai-compute-strategy. Read 2026-11-11.

## Bottom line

- Canada's Budget 2024 gave $2 billion over five years, from 2024–25, for AI compute.
- The strategy has three elements, with these maximum amounts:
  - $700 million for private sector data centres (the AI Compute Challenge);
  - $1 billion for public supercomputing infrastructure;
  - $300 million for an AI Compute Access Fund.
- The public element includes a new Canadian-owned supercomputer and a secure government facility. It also gives up to $200 million to existing public compute.

## Background

ISED is a department of the Government of Canada. This page sets out the strategy. It says that a public consultation in the summer of 2024 heard from "more than 1,000 stakeholders". It links to a "What We Heard Report" and to the pages of each program, which this library does not hold.

## Key claims

- Budget: "Budget 2024 announced $2 billion over five years, starting in 2024–25, to launch new initiatives that will give Canadian researchers and AI companies the tools they need to be competitive globally." (What is AI compute?)
- Private investment: "The government is investing up to $700 million to support the Canadian AI ecosystem through increased domestic AI compute capacity that leverages Canada's natural competitive advantages in energy, land and climate." (Mobilizing private sector investment)
- AI Compute Challenge: it "will focus on projects from companies, consortiums and academic-industry partnerships to establish fully integrated AI data-centre solutions ready for commercial deployment." (Mobilizing private sector investment)
- Public infrastructure: "The second element of the strategy includes a transformational investment of up to $1 billion to build public supercomputing infrastructure that will meet the needs of researchers, government and industry." (Building public supercomputing infrastructure)
- Sovereign system: "This state-of-the-art, Canadian-owned and located supercomputing system will support researchers and a cross-section of industry". (Building public supercomputing infrastructure)
- Secure facility: "A smaller secure computing facility, to be led by Shared Services Canada (SSC) and the National Research Council of Canada (NRC), will also be established for government and industry to perform research and development, including for national security purposes." (Building public supercomputing infrastructure)
- Near term: "In the near term, up to $200 million will be provided to augment existing public compute infrastructure to address immediate needs." It names the NRC, the AI Institutes and the Digital Research Alliance of Canada. (Building public supercomputing infrastructure)
- Access Fund: "The Access Fund will support the purchase of AI compute resources by Canadian innovators and businesses. Through an investment of up to $300 million". (AI Compute Access Fund)

## Evidence and method

This is a government program page. It gives the budget amounts, the three elements and their aims. It cites a public consultation but gives none of its results on this page.

## Limits

- The page lists the $200 million for existing infrastructure under "This investment includes the following", in the $1 billion element.
- It gives no dates for the new supercomputer, and no size in GPUs or performance.
- It does not give the eligibility rules of the Access Fund. It links to a separate page.
- The page writes the amounts with "$" and does not name the currency.
