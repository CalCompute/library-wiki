## Bottom line

- DOJ asks for 8.0 permanent positions and $2,204,000 General Fund in 2026-27, then $2,137,000 each year. The money is for the Transparency in Frontier Artificial Intelligence Act (TFAIA, SB 53).
- The work is enforcement, review of whistleblower reports and an annual report to the Legislature.
- The positions are in the Consumer Protection Section of the Public Rights Division. The request includes $250,000 each year for outside consultants and expert witnesses.

## Background

A Budget Change Proposal (BCP) is a department's request for a change to its budget, on the Department of Finance form DF-46. SB 53 gives the Attorney General the enforcement of the Act. The proposal puts the work in the Consumer Protection Section (CPS), which "already leads the Departments technology-related enforcement". The same section's proposal for AB 853 is [BCP 2026-27 Department of Justice AI Transparency Act AB 853 (web)](BCP%202026-27%20Department%20of%20Justice%20AI%20Transparency%20Act%20AB%20853%20%28web%29.md).

## Key claims

- "The Department of Justice (DOJ) requests a permanent augmentation of 8.0 positions and General Fund spending authority of $2,204,000 in FY 2026-2027 and $2,137,000 ongoing for the implementation of the Transparency in Frontier Artificial Intelligence Act (TFAIA) (SB 53), including the review of whistleblower complaints, publication of a legislative report, and enforcement of the Act’s requirements." (Budget Request Summary)
- "SB 53 charges the Attorney General with the exclusive and ongoing authority to enforce these provisions, creating a clear legislative expectation that the Department of Justice will investigate violations and ensure compliance." (A. Problem Statement)
- "SB 53 permanently expands the Attorney General’s enforcement responsibilities by granting exclusive authority to enforce the Act’s provisions, with civil penalties of up to $1,000,000 per violation." (B. Justification)
- "To successfully pursue these actions, CPS will require support from consulting and testifying expert witnesses with deep technical expertise as well as related support services from outside consultants." (A. Problem Statement, External Consultants)
- "The anticipated costs of these external consultants $250,000, are based on similar/current consultant costs." (A. Problem Statement, External Consultants)

## Evidence and method

A state budget request on the DF-46 form. It has a resource history, a workload history in hours, projected outcomes and a fiscal detail sheet in thousands of dollars. The positions are 3.0 Deputy Attorney General IV, 2.0 Associate Governmental Program Analyst, 1.0 Information Technology Specialist III and 2.0 Legal Secretary. The projected section workload goes from 101,855 hours (57.4 full-time equivalents) in 2025-26 to 112,667 hours (63.4) from 2026-27. The cover sheet marks the proposal as one with information technology components.

## Limits

- The proposal does not say whether the Legislature approved the request. It is not the enacted budget.
- The 2026-27 floor report lists $2.2 million and 8 positions in 2026-27 and $2.1 million ongoing for SB 53 at DOJ ([Assembly Budget floor report 2026-27 budget 2026-06-27 (web)](Assembly%20Budget%20floor%20report%202026-27%20budget%202026-06-27%20%28web%29.md), p. 125).
- It says nothing about CalCompute.
- It does not split the cost between whistleblower review, the report and enforcement.
- The library holds only Government Code section 11546.8 of SB 53 ([SB-53 Gov Code 11546.8](SB-53%20Gov%20Code%2011546.8.md)), not the sections that the Attorney General enforces.
