# WEF Future of Jobs Report 2025 (web)

## Citation

World Economic Forum (WEF). "Future of Jobs Report 2025." Insight report, World Economic Forum, January 2025. ISBN 978-2-940631-90-2. https://reports.weforum.org/docs/WEF_Future_of_Jobs_Report_2025.pdf. Read 2026-11-09.

## Bottom line

- A survey of over 1,000 employers expects 170 million new jobs and 92 million displaced jobs from 2025 to 2030. The net gain is 78 million.
- AI and big data are the fastest-growing skills. In the United States, 94 percent of surveyed firms expect AI and information processing to change their operations. AI and Machine Learning Specialists lead net role growth there.

## Background

This is the fifth edition of the report from the WEF Centre for the New Economy and Society. It rests on the Future of Jobs Survey of employers in 55 economies. It has a global analysis in Part I and one profile for each economy and industry. The WEF says the findings do not necessarily reflect its own views.

## Key claims

- "This year’s edition captures the perspectives of over 1,000 employers – representing more than 14 million workers across 22 industry clusters and 55 economies" (Preface, p. 4)
- "In the next five years, 170 million jobs are projected to be created and 92 million jobs to be displaced, constituting a structural labour market churn of 22% of the 1.2 billion formal jobs in the dataset being studied." (Section 2.1, Figure 2.1, p. 18)
- "AI and big data top the list of fastest-growing skills, followed closely by networks and cybersecurity as well as technology literacy." (Key findings, p. 6)
- "if the world’s workforce was made up of 100 people, 59 would need training by 2030." (Key findings, p. 6)
- "Skill gaps are categorically considered the biggest barrier to business transformation by Future of Jobs Survey respondents, with 63% of employers identifying them as a major barrier over the 2025-2030 period." (Key findings, p. 6)
- "Additionally, 94% of firms in the United States expect AI and information processing technologies to transform their operations in the next five years." (Section 5.1, p. 74)
- "Funding for - and provision of - reskilling and upskilling are seen as the two most welcomed public policies to boost talent availability." (Key findings, p. 6)

## Evidence and method

An insight report from an international membership organisation. Its main evidence is an employer survey. Respondents give expectations for 2025 to 2030, and the report extrapolates them to job totals with International Labour Organization employment data. The United States economy profile (pp. 214 to 215) gives survey shares for that economy. It shows 23 percent labour-market churn and 35 percent skill disruption.

## Limits

The report is about 293,000 words, mostly economy and industry profiles. This page draws on the Preface, the Key findings, Section 2.1, the United States paragraph in Section 5.1 (p. 74) and the United States economy profile (pp. 214 to 215). The figures are employer expectations, not observed outcomes. The report has no California figures and no separate analysis of state government employers.
