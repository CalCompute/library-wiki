# CENIC 2024-2026 Community Report PDF (web)

## Citation

Corporation for Education Network Initiatives in California (CENIC). "Community Report, July 2024–2026." PDF, 42 pages. Date not printed; the PDF file was made 2026-09-25, and the report gives figures "As of June 30, 2026". https://assets.cenic.org/publications/Final-Community-Report-9-25-1026_med.pdf. Read 2026-11-17.

## Bottom line

- CENIC's report for July 2024 to June 2026. CENIC runs the California Research and Education Network (CalREN), with "more than 8,000 miles of optical fiber" and "over 20 million users".
- The Charter Associates govern CENIC through the board. CENIC AIR is "the California portion of the National Research Platform (NRP)".
- As an E-Rate consortium, CENIC returns "over $25 million in annual subsidies" to members. In the Broadband Infrastructure Grant (BIG), federal E-Rate funds paid $6,525,000 of about $7,075,000 in construction costs.

## Background

CENIC is the nonprofit that operates CalREN. This community report covers two fiscal years, to June 30, 2026, at the start of CENIC's 30th anniversary year. It has six parts: anniversary, strategic direction, governance and membership, blog stories, the 2026 Biennial Conference, and technical operations. The landing page is [CENIC 2024-2026 Community Report (web)](CENIC%202024-2026%20Community%20Report%20%28web%29.md). See also [CENIC About (web)](CENIC%20About%20%28web%29.md) and [CENIC membership (web)](CENIC%20membership%20%28web%29.md).

## Key claims

- "Established in 1997, the Corporation for Education Network Initiatives in California (CENIC) is the nonprofit organization operating the California Research and Education Network (CalREN), a high-capacity Internet-based network backbone consisting of more than 8,000 miles of optical fiber and thousands of network connections reaching out from the backbone to member institutions throughout the state, for a total of 54,000 CENIC-managed network miles." (p. 13)
- "CENIC is governed by representatives of the Charter Associate institutions that it serves: California’s Community Colleges, the California K–12 system, the California Public Libraries, the California State University, the University of California, and independent universities (the California Institute of Technology, the Naval Postgraduate School, Stanford University, and the University of Southern California)." (p. 13)
- "The CENIC AI Resource (CENIC AIR) functions as the California portion of the National Research Platform (NRP), which integrates its 50+ member institutions’ Science DMZs into a high-capacity national system that enables secure processing and transfers of large scientific datasets for research and instruction." (p. 13)
- "Director seats are held by representatives of the CENIC Charter Associates — the segments of the California research and education community for and by which CENIC was created, as well as significant segments that joined later. Directors typically serve three-year terms." (p. 14)
- "The plan for the future of CalREN follows these four core elements: (1) the network must be cost-effective and keep costs flat over time; (2) it must be reliable and resilient; (3) it must meet the capability and capacity needs of all CENIC segments; and (4) it must be “facilities efficient,” deploying cost-saving innovative technologies that decrease both capital and operating expenses, often in partnership with other networks." (p. 8)
- "Successfully deployed Science DMZ as a Service at SDCCD and LACCD, enabling both institutions to quickly install CENIC AIR nodes and contribute to shared AI resources used by the community." (p. 37)
- "CENIC AIR is now close to 2 years old. CENIC helped add an “AI Education” identity to what started as a “research platform” and, within the last year, expanded us into a driver of economic impact through engagement among community colleges, CSUs, and their local industries." (p. 31, conference session "AI Infrastructure for All")
- "As the largest E-Rate consortium in the United States, the PMO also manages E-Rate and California Teleconnect Fund (CTF) submissions on behalf of eligible members. The management of these processes returns over $25 million in annual subsidies to our members, saves them hours of staff time, and ensures compliance with program requirements." (p. 38)
- "To date, the program has funded roughly $7,075,000 in construction costs, with CENIC securing $6,525,000 in federal E-Rate funds, reducing the net cost to $550,000, or 8% of the total construction cost." (p. 26, Broadband Infrastructure Grant)
- "Funds are allocated through the CDE, which contracts with CENIC to serve as the program’s third-party administrator." (p. 26)
- "The proposed changes would narrow these programs and eliminate eligibility for many CENIC member sites that have historically relied on these subsidies, and that would be catastrophic for schools, libraries, and community colleges." (p. 25, on FCC E-Rate and CPUC CTF rulemaking)
- "The CalREN backbone serves over 12,000 sites and more than 20 million Californians, spans more than 8,000 miles of CENIC-owned and managed fiber and last-mile fiber with hundreds of optical components, and manages more than 54,000 total miles of network." (p. 35)

## Evidence and method

A two-year report from the operator, about 8,000 words. It gives:

- network figures for CalREN (pp. 8, 13 and 35-37);
- circuit counts by member segment, July 1, 2024 to June 30, 2026 (p. 39);
- Network Operations Center ticket and alarm counts for 2024, 2025 and 2026 to June 30 (p. 41);
- the BIG program costs and a 2025 survey of 127 education stakeholders (p. 26);
- the board as of June 30, 2026 (pp. 14-15) and the members by class (pp. 17-18).

Pages 20 to 33 summarise blog posts and conference sessions. Each has a link to the full item.

## Limits

- The financial statement for 2023 to 2025 (p. 19) is an image. The Markdown copy has no text for it, so the library has not read CENIC's revenue or costs.
- The report does not give CENIC AIR's hardware, GPU count, users or cost. The conference session summaries are abstracts, not results.
- The BIG survey figures (80, 92 and 90 percent) come from CENIC's own survey; the report does not give its method.
- The NRP member count here ("50+") is lower than the "more than 70 institutions" in [NRP about (web)](NRP%20about%20%28web%29.md).
