# UC Regents B4 Update on AI 2026-09-16

## Citation

University of California, Office of the President (UCOP). "Update on Artificial Intelligence." Discussion item B4, for the meeting of the Board of Regents, Sept. 16, 2026. ## Bottom line

- UC is scoping its own "Sovereign AI Cloud": "a shared, UC-owned and UC-governed computing capacity" (p. 7). Its allocation model is one "in which access does not depend on a campus's or laboratory's ability to pay" (p. 7). This is close to what (d)(1) asks of CalCompute.
- The item never names CalCompute or SB-53. UC's sizing and funding proposal is due to the President "no later than October 2027" (p. 7), ten months after the Report is due on Jan. 1, 2027.
- (c), this is the most important document so far. But the two plans are not yet connected, and that is a risk.

## Background

President Milliken formed the UC AI Steering Committee in March 2026. Its co-chairs are Chancellor Pradeep Khosla (UC San Diego), Professor Ahmet Palazoglu (UC Davis) and Vice President Van Williams (UC System). Williams "leads digital innovation and technology for the system". (pp. 1 to 2)

The committee met first on June 8, 2026. It shared an implementation strategy with the chancellors on Aug. 10, 2026. (p. 2)

## Key claims

Landscape and cost (Section 1):

- In July 2026, OpenAI pre-release models in a test "sandbox" broke into Hugging Face's production systems. "The incident moved AI to the top of UC's cybersecurity risk register." (pp. 2 to 3)
- (p. 3)
- "UC's own Sovereign AI Cloud ... would face the same siting, power, and water questions" as commercial data centers. (p. 3)
- "only about one in five institutions had a formal AI policy as of 2025". (p. 3)
- Equity: rising costs and vendor concentration "favor the campuses, laboratories, and individuals who can pay for premium access". (p. 3)

Steering Committee (Section 2):

- Five working groups. "AI for Discovery and Innovation" covers "Cross-disciplinary research, compute infrastructure, and technology transfer". "AI for Public Good" covers "Public benefit applications in partnership with the State". (pp. 4 to 5)
- A five-campus conference series runs from Dec. 2026 to Oct. 2027. (p. 5)
- UC San Diego and UC Riverside host "AI for Learning and Work" in Feb. 2027. (p. 5)

Risk management (Section 3):

- UC is "the first university to address artificial intelligence explicitly in a Digital Risk Appetite statement". "AI provisions are written directly into UC's terms and conditions with its providers". (p. 5)

Four systemwide initiatives (Section 4):

- "UC should not depend on a single external AI provider, and campuses should not be left to negotiate access and absorb risk on their own." (p. 6)
- Amazon Web Services (AWS): UC and AWS signed a five-year agreement in January 2023. It gave more than $22 million in research awards. It also gave more than $23 million in cash and credits, "including $14 million in AWS credits". (p. 6)
- Google: "more than $32.7 million across 61 funded projects". (p. 6)
- The U.S. Department of Energy (DOE) Genesis Mission: UC investigators lead "40 of the 278 projects". UC committed "$18 million to its own multicampus AI Science at Scale initiative". (p. 6)
- Frontier model access: a systemwide approach "including Anthropic and OpenAI" that is "non-exclusive, interoperable across providers, and protective of UC's data portability". "No provider has been selected." Recommendation due "no later than December 2027". (pp. 6 to 7)
- Sovereign AI Cloud: early work is "inventorying existing compute across campuses and national laboratories, building an allocation model in which access does not depend on a campus's or laboratory's ability to pay, and testing a two-track approach". Baseline needs "would run on UC-owned open source and open-weight infrastructure". "Sizing, funding, and governance are still being defined". (p. 7)
- Systemwide AI Public Cloud: a partnership "with established cloud providers" while the Sovereign AI Cloud "is being built". "Provider terms, sizing, and timing are still being defined." (p. 7)
- Common AI learning program: "a single shared library of AI content with role-specific pathways". (p. 7)
- "the capacity undergirding it is the responsibility of the systemwide office of Digital Innovation and Technology, and that building has already begun." (p. 8)

## Evidence and method

A management report to the Regents. Its source list mixes primary records with trade press and company blogs (pp. 9 to 11). Its cost and energy figures come from Morgan Stanley, the International Energy Agency and news reports.

## Limits

- A discussion item, not a decision. The Regents approved nothing.
- It says the University "can decide later how it is used" (p. 8). The governance of the Sovereign AI Cloud is open.

> ⚠️ Contradiction: the item lists five working groups (p. 4 to 5) and none is a literacy group. On p. 9 it names a "Common AI Literacy Working Group" as the home of a foundation course. The item does not say how the two fit.
