## Bottom line

- This CSUF help article tells campus employees how to request access to three high-performance computing (HPC) resources.
- The three are Nautilus of the National Research Platform (NRP), and the campus clusters Turing and Kepler.
- Faculty, staff, management, teaching associates and graduate assistants submit the request. Students get access through a faculty request.
- On Nautilus, student access is for students who assist with research. It needs a renewal each semester. On Turing and Kepler, faculty can request access for students in their courses.

## Background

This is an article in the IT Knowledge Base of CSUF, a California State University (CSU) campus. The form opens from the website of the Titan Supercomputing Center ([CSUF Titan Supercomputing Center (web)](CSUF%20Titan%20Supercomputing%20Center%20%28web%29.md)) and needs a campus sign-in. The article gives the steps for each resource and each request type, then the steps for students to upload an SSH key.

## Key claims

Who can request (opening paragraph):

- "This article covers how CSUF faculty, staff, management, teaching associates, and graduate assistants can submit a request for Titan Supercomputing resources, including requesting access on behalf of a student."

Nautilus ("Nautilus"):

- "If you use **Nautilus** or the **National Research Platform (NRP)** resources in your research, publications, or projects, please acknowledge their support appropriately."
- The requester must "Review the citation information, NRP FAQ, and the Nautilus Acceptable Use Policy. Then place a checkmark to agree to comply with the Acceptable Use Policy."
- "The Request access on behalf of students option allows faculty to request access to resources for students who are assisting with research."
- "Note that access must be renewed each semester."

Turing ("Turing"):

- "The Request access on behalf of students option allows faculty to request access to resources for students who are enrolled in their course(s)."
- "The Software/Application option allows faculty to request additional software/applications be added to Turing."

What the faculty member gives (each request type):

- "Enter a brief explanation of the research that will be conducted."
- For students: "Campus-Wide ID (CWID)", "CSUF email address", "Login ID", "First Name" and "Last Name".
- After submission: "You will see the RITM number assigned to your request and receive an email confirmation."

## Evidence and method

This is a step-by-step help article with screenshots of the form. It describes the request route. It gives no hardware, no rates, no review criteria and no review time.

## Limits

- The article does not say who reviews a request, by what criteria, or how long a review takes.
- It gives no cost to the user, and no hardware of Nautilus, Turing or Kepler.
- It does not say if people outside CSUF can get access.
- The Kepler section repeats the Turing text. Its Software/Application option says software can "be added to Turing", and it points to "the TSC Turing website link".
- A Nautilus request asks for a "namespace". The article does not explain the term.
- The article gives a support email address. This page does not copy it.
