## Bottom line

- The head of ODET speaks on the Global Digital Compact (GDC). He says it recognizes "open AI models, as well as open access to training data, and compute resources." This is a UN basis for open access to compute.
- Government OSPOs work as "coordination bodies designed to reduce duplication, promote reuse of software assets". Their success "depends less on technical tooling and more on mandate, authority, and cross-government engagement."
- AI panelists asked for "better training data, shared infrastructure, and regulatory cooperation across borders."

## Background

UN Open Source Week 2025 was June 16 to 20, 2025, with "over 1,000 participants from 75 countries". ODET and OICT hosted it, with Germany, the Dominican Republic and Nigeria (p. 4). See [UN Open Source Week 2025](UN%20Open%20Source%20Week%202025.md).

## Key claims

- Amandeep Singh Gill, opening of Day 3: "Open source is explicitly recognized in the GDC: open AI models, as well as open access to training data, and compute resources" (p. 16).
- Breakout "Advancing Open Source AI for the Public Good": "openness must extend beyond code to training data, documentation, governance, and safety practices" (p. 13).
- Breakout "Open Source Skills in the AI Era": panelists "stressed the need for collaboration between industry, academia, and policymakers to upskill the workforce at scale" (p. 13).
- Panel on newly launched OSPOs: the offices "marked a shift from ad hoc enthusiasm to formal, resourced programs". An OSPO "becomes a space where shared governance meets real delivery" (p. 17).
- Government OSPOs in Kenya and Trinidad and Tobago are "coordination bodies designed to reduce duplication, promote reuse of software assets, and align open source practices with national digital strategies" (pp. 19-20).
- "the effectiveness of an OSPO depends less on technical tooling and more on mandate, authority, and cross-government engagement." Problems: "legal and procurement uncertainties, and concerns around long-term sustainability" (p. 20).
- AI panel: "digital sovereignty should mean collective strength, not isolation" (p. 21).
