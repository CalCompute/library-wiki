# Cryst Expanding Academia's Role in Public Sector AI (web)

## Citation

Kevin Klyman, Aaron Bao, Caroline Meinhardt, Daniel Zhang, Elena Cryst and Russell Wald. "Expanding Academia's Role in Public Sector AI." Issue brief, Stanford Institute for Human-Centered Artificial Intelligence (HAI), December 4, 2024. https://hai.stanford.edu/policy/expanding-academias-role-in-public-sector-ai. Read 2026-11-06.

## Bottom line

- A Stanford HAI issue brief of December 4, 2024, with Elena Cryst among its six authors.
- It says that academia falls behind industry in frontier AI research in funding, compute and talent. It asks governments to keep investing in public sector AI.

## Background

Stanford HAI publishes this brief on its policy pages. The web page gives the abstract, the key takeaways, the introduction and the first section, with the author list. This is a profile page. Elena Cryst's entity page lists it; no reading list does.

## Key claims

- "This brief analyzes the disparity between academia and industry in frontier AI research and presents policy recommendations for ensuring a stronger role for academia in public sector AI." (abstract)
- "Companies have over 1000x more compute than universities, and they produce AI models that are 50x larger." (Key Takeaways)
- "Governments should continue investing in public sector AI. Academia must be at the forefront of training the next generation of innovators and advancing cutting-edge scientific research in the public interest." (Key Takeaways)
- "In 2023, industry produced 51 notable machine learning models, far outstripping academia's 15". (The Industry-Academia Divide in AI)
- "By 2020, the balance had shifted decisively in favor of industry, with nearly 70 percent of new AI PhDs pursuing careers in the private sector." (The Industry-Academia Divide in AI)
- Authors: "Kevin Klyman", "Aaron Bao", "Caroline Meinhardt", "Daniel Zhang", "Elena Cryst", "Russell Wald". (Authors)

## Evidence and method

A policy brief from a university research institute. It cites counts of models, graphics processing unit (GPU) purchases, investment and salaries from other studies.

## Limits

- The web page does not give the brief's full recommendations.
- The page does not say which author wrote which part.
