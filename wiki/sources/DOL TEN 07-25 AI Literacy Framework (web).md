## Bottom line

- The U.S. Department of Labor (DOL) issues an Artificial Intelligence (AI) Literacy Framework for the public workforce and education systems. The guidance is voluntary.
- The framework defines AI literacy, with a primary focus on generative AI.
- It gives five foundational content areas and seven delivery principles for AI literacy training.
- DOL calls the framework a starting point and says it will update it.

## Background

The Employment and Training Administration (ETA) of DOL sent this notice on Feb. 13, 2026. It goes to state workforce agencies, workforce boards, American Job Centers, ETA grantees, community colleges, apprenticeship agencies and state education agencies. The notice is three pages. Attachment I is the framework, in 11 pages. Attachment II is a one-page graphic of the framework. The notice builds on Training and Employment Guidance Letter (TEGL) 03-25 of Aug. 26, 2025.

## Key claims

- "DOL defines AI literacy as a foundational set of competencies that enable individuals to use and evaluate AI technologies responsibly, with a primary focus on generative AI, which is increasingly central to the modern workplace." (Attachment I, p. I-1)
- "The attached AI Literacy Framework provides voluntary guidance to support the evaluation of high-quality AI literacy content and its effective integration into education and workforce systems" (notice, item 4)
- "In an increasingly AI-driven economy, every worker will need baseline AI literacy skills to succeed, regardless of industry or occupation." (Attachment I, p. I-1)
- "This framework helps agencies integrate AI literacy into Workforce Innovation and Opportunity Act (WIOA) services and other programs, while adapting to regional labor market needs." (Attachment I, pp. I-3 to I-4)
- "For some workers, this may include digital literacy skills, device access, or broadband connectivity, especially in settings where AI tools require stable internet access or use non-intuitive interfaces." (Attachment I, p. I-9, principle 4)
- "Workers remain responsible for the decisions and outputs they produce with AI tools and should avoid treating AI responses as final or authoritative without review." (Attachment I, p. I-7)

The five foundational content areas are (Attachment I, pp. I-5 to I-7):

- Understand AI Principles
- Explore AI Uses
- Direct AI Effectively
- Evaluate AI Outputs
- Use AI Responsibly

The seven delivery principles are (Attachment I, pp. I-8 to I-11):

- Enable Experiential Learning
- Embed Learning in Context
- Build Complementary Human Skills
- Address Prerequisites to AI Literacy
- Create Pathways for Continued Learning
- Prepare Enabling Roles
- Design for Agility

## Evidence and method

A federal advisory notice with a guidance framework. The framework says it "reflects extensive input from employers, training providers, state and local agencies, and other workforce and education stakeholders". It does not name them and gives no data. The notice lists six references, among them Executive Order 14277 and the AI Action Plan.

## Limits

The notice gives no funding and sets no requirement. It does not name California, a state program or a compute resource. It does not say how to measure AI literacy. The library does not have TEGL 03-25.

The dol.gov address refuses scripts, so the library's copy is the Wayback Machine capture of the same address.

The notice does not mention CalCompute.
