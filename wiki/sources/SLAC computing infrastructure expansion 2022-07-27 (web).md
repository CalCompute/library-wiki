# SLAC computing infrastructure expansion 2022-07-27 (web)

## Citation

Gnida, Manuel. "SLAC expands and centralizes computing infrastructure to prepare for data challenges of the future." SLAC National Accelerator Laboratory, news, July 27, 2022. https://www6.slac.stanford.edu/news/2022-07-27-slac-expands-and-centralizes-computing-infrastructure-prepare-data-challenges. Read 2026-11-11.

## Bottom line

- Stanford is building SRCF-II, a second building of the Stanford Research Computing Facility (SRCF) on the SLAC grounds. It "will double the current data center capabilities, for a total of 6 megawatts of power capacity."
- SRCF-II will host the SLAC Shared Science Data Facility (S3DF), the Rubin Observatory U.S. data facility (USDF) and a data hub for LCLS users. The S3DF "will be able to support 80% of SLAC's computing needs".

## Background

SLAC is a DOE national laboratory that "is operated by Stanford University for the U.S. Department of Energy's Office of Science." This news item describes the data center extension and the three data facilities it will hold. It gives the data rates of new SLAC instruments as the reason for the work.

## Key claims

- "Construction of an annex to the Stanford Research Computing Facility (SRCF) began in November 2021 and is expected to be completed in the second half of 2023." (photo caption)
- "SRCF-II will double the current data center capabilities, for a total of 6 megawatts of power capacity." (introduction)
- The S3DF "will become a computing hub for all data-intensive experiments performed at the lab." (A hub for SLAC's Big Data)
- "In fact, everyone at the lab will be able to use available computing resources." (A hub for SLAC's Big Data)
- "All in all, the S3DF will be able to support 80% of SLAC's computing needs, while 20% of the most demanding scientific computing will be done at supercomputer facilities offsite." (A hub for SLAC's Big Data)
- The unified approach "includes a cost model that will help pay for future upgrades and growth". (Multiple services under one roof)
- "SRCF-II construction is a Stanford project. Large parts of the S3DF infrastructure are funded by the Department of Energy's Office of Science." (last paragraph)

## Evidence and method

A news item from the laboratory, with quotes from SLAC staff. It gives the power capacity, the construction dates, data rates and the split of computing needs. It gives no construction cost and no system sizes.

## Limits

The item is from July 2022, before construction ended. It does not say if SRCF-II opened on time. It names "a cost model" but does not describe it. It gives no dollar amounts.
