# F3i Supercomputing Center (web)

## Citation

F3 Innovate (F3i). "AI Supercomputing Center." Web page, date not stated. https://f3i.org/supercomputing-center. Read 2026-11-09.

## Bottom line

- The F3i Supercomputing Center is a partnership of three bodies. F3 Innovate leads it. The San Diego Supercomputer Center (SDSC) at UC San Diego gives the cyberinfrastructure. California State University (CSU), Fresno hosts and operates it.
- It gives priority access to California-based startups, researchers and educators in agriculture and biotech, and first priority to Central Valley users. Data Challenges are its core program.

## Background

F3 Innovate is a Fresno nonprofit that works on agriculture. This page on its website introduces the center and links to a sign-in portal at portal.f3i.org. It sets out the role of each partner, who gets priority access and how its Data Challenges work. It promotes a satellite imagery challenge and links to an earlier Frost Risk Data Challenge.

## Key claims

- "F3 Innovate leads the center and its programs, the San Diego Supercomputer Center at UC San Diego provides cyberinfrastructure and AI systems architecture, and California State University, Fresno hosts and operates the physical compute environment." (Introducing the F3i Supercomputing Center)
- "A small agricultural startup can't afford a GPU cluster. A graduate student can't license the datasets." (Why we built the Supercomputing Center)
- "The Supercomputing Center exists so a problem named on Monday by a Madera County orchardist can be modeled on Tuesday by a CSU Fresno team — on infrastructure that would otherwise cost six figures to access." (Why we built the Supercomputing Center)
- "**California-based** startups, researchers, and educators in agriculture and biotech — with priority for Central Valley users." (Priority access)
- "F3 Innovate leads the center, its programs, and workforce development partnerships." (Leadership)
- "Data Challenges are a core activation program of the Center. They turn real industry problems into structured AI projects where companies, researchers, and students work with shared datasets and defined use cases." (Data Challenges)

## Evidence and method

An institutional program page. It names the three partners and their roles, the priority user groups and a three-step Data Challenge process:

1. "Identify the Problem": growers and industry partners define the challenges.
2. "Build and Test Solutions": teams develop models and prototype tools with real-world data and advanced computing.
3. "Pilot and Commercialize": growers pilot the top solutions, and validated solutions move to commercialization.

The current challenge is a four-week sprint. Participants use free satellite imagery to map underperforming zones in Central Valley orchards.

## Limits

The page does not give the hardware or the number of graphics processing units (GPUs). It does not give the cost, the funding source, the start date or an allocation process. It does not say whether access is free. It does not say which user groups other than Californians can use the center.
