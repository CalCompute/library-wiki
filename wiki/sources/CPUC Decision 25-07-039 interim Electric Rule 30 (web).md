# CPUC Decision 25-07-039 interim Electric Rule 30 (web)

## Citation

California Public Utilities Commission (CPUC). Decision 25-07-039, "Decision Partly Granting and Partly Denying Pacific Gas and Electric Company's Motion for Interim Implementation of Electric Rule Number 30," Application 24-11-007. Adopted July 24, 2025; date of issuance July 28, 2025. 53 pages and Attachment 1. https://docs.cpuc.ca.gov/PublishedDocs/Published/G000/M574/K875/574875643.PDF. Read 2026-11-19. Copy: [`raw/web/2026-11-19 CPUC Decision 25-07-039 interim Electric Rule 30.md`](../../raw/web/2026-11-19%20CPUC%20Decision%2025-07-039%20interim%20Electric%20Rule%2030.md) (original: `.pdf` beside it).

## Bottom line

- The CPUC lets Pacific Gas and Electric Company (PG&E) use Electric Rule 30 on an interim basis. The rule connects large retail customers at transmission level (50 to 230 kilovolts).
- Under the interim rule, the customer pays the initial costs of the transmission facilities, not the ratepayers. The decision defers refunds and interest to the final decision.
- PG&E received 40 such applications in 2023 and 2024, "mostly from data centers", for a requested load of 8,422 megawatts.

## Background

PG&E filed Application 24-11-007 on Nov. 21, 2024, for a new tariff, Electric Rule 30. On Jan. 24, 2025, PG&E asked the CPUC to let it use the rule before the final decision. This decision answers only that motion. The Public Advocates Office (Cal Advocates), The Utility Reform Network (TURN) and the Joint Community Choice Aggregators opposed the motion.

The CPUC announced the decision in a release of July 24, 2025: [CPUC release interim Electric Rule 30 2025-07-24 (web)](CPUC%20release%20interim%20Electric%20Rule%2030%202025-07-24%20%28web%29.md).

## Key claims

- "This decision allows for interim implementation for transmission-level customers who provide advance or actual cost payments and voluntarily prefund up to 100 percent of specific transmission network upgrades." (Summary, p. 2)
- "The decision requires new transmission-level customers seeking retail services to be responsible for the initial costs of all transmission facilities, rather than those costs being borne by ratepayers." (Summary, p. 2)
- "Therefore, no rate recovery is authorized as part of this decision." (Summary, p. 3)
- Current tariffs cover wholesale transmission connections and distribution line extensions, "but not for retail customer energization at transmission levels." (Section 1.1, p. 6)
- "PG&E received 40 transmission-level interconnection requests in 2023–2024, mostly from data centers and large-load customers seeking retail service at transmission levels totaling a requested load of 8,422 megawatts." (Finding of Fact 7, p. 43)
- "The interim implementation of Electric Rule 30 is projected to reduce application processing time from 18–22 months to 2–5 months." (Finding of Fact 9, p. 44)
- TURN "states that the massive size of the data center load (70% of the transmission-level service connection applications are data center load) increases the likelihood of causing or accelerating the need for expensive transmission system upgrades". (Section 4.3, p. 24)
- Cal Advocates argues "the 40 applications submitted with PG&E are speculative due to the uncertain nature of data centers and their high rate of project abandonment." (Section 12, p. 42)
- "Under interim implementation, Type 4 Facilities will be eligible when transmission-level customers provide 100 percent of the project cost as a pre-funded loan." (Ordering Paragraph 1.c, p. 51)

## Evidence and method

- A final decision of the CPUC on a motion, with findings of fact, conclusions of law and an order.
- The evidence is the record of the proceeding: PG&E's application, motion and testimony, and the responses of the parties.
- The decision defines four facility types. Types 1 to 3 connect the customer; Type 4 is an upgrade of the existing grid (Section 1.1, pp. 7-8).
- PG&E must file a quarterly report on the agreements it signs and the work on each project (Ordering Paragraph 6, p. 52).
- Attachment 1 lists the data that each Tier 2 Advice Letter must give. Among them are the "Expected maximum peak demand from the project (megawatts)" and the state plans that count the load.

## Limits

- The decision applies only to the PG&E service territory and only until a later or final decision.
- It does not decide refunds, interest or the repayment of pre-funded loans. The final decision is to decide them.
- The 40 applications are requests, not built load. Cal Advocates calls them "speculative". The decision does not name the customers.
- The decision does not give the cost of a connection for one customer.
- Figure 1.1, a diagram of the four facility types, is an image. The Markdown copy has no text for it.
