# Berkeley VCR Cal-ICOR compute hub launch (web)

## Citation

Leven, Rachel. "UC Berkeley to Launch First-Of-Its-Kind Compute Hub." UC Berkeley Office of the Vice Chancellor for Research (VCR), news, September 23, 2024. https://vcresearch.berkeley.edu/news/uc-berkeley-launch-first-its-kind-compute-hub. Read 2026-11-11. Cached copy: `.cache/web/2026-11-11 Berkeley VCR Cal-ICOR compute hub launch.md`.

## Bottom line

- The California Education Learning Lab gave UC Berkeley a three-year, $1.5 million grant for the California Interactive Computing Open Resource (Cal-ICOR). It is a JupyterHub course hub for California public colleges and universities.
- Berkeley staffs and runs the hub. The investigators expect it to support more than 5,000 students by fall 2027.

## Background

The VCR office publishes research news for UC Berkeley. The article reports the grant and its co-principal investigators, John DeNero and Matthew Sherburne. It names four early user campuses and the Learning Lab director.

## Key claims

The grant (opening paragraphs):

- "UC Berkeley experts received a $1.5 million grant from the California Education Learning Lab (Learning Lab) to launch a first-of-its-kind computing technology hub for the state's public colleges and universities."
- "It will be staffed and run by Berkeley for several California higher education institutions, saving money, aligning curricula and facilitating transfer pathways for STEM students."
- "This three-year grant awarded this month by Learning Lab, a state-funded grantmaking organization, builds on past Berkeley and California efforts to improve data science education equity and outcomes."
- "This platform is modeled after the Canadian cloud-based platforms Syzygy and Callysto"

Scale (middle of article):

- "The principal investigators expect this hub, dubbed the California Interactive Computing Open Resource, to support more than 5,000 students across California by fall 2027. They said this infrastructure could eventually reach more than 50,000 students"

Staff and first users (later paragraphs):

- "This platform, which four staff members from CDSS and the College of Engineering will help develop and support, will take shape quickly. San Jose State University, the City College of San Francisco, Santa Barbara City College, and California State University, Long Beach may be able to use it as early as this spring."
- Lark Park, the Learning Lab's director: "Eventually, we hope that every California community college, CSU, or UC that wants to be part of the hub can join"

## Evidence and method

This is a campus news article at launch. It reports the grant amount, term and expected reach. It gives no usage data.

## Limits

- The article describes plans in 2024. It does not report what the hub has done since. Later Cal-ICOR pages, for example [Cal-ICOR hub usage](Cal-ICOR%20hub%20usage.md), cover that.
- It does not say how Cal-ICOR pays for its cloud costs after the grant ends.
