# ACCESS MATCH services (web)

## Citation

ACCESS, U.S. National Science Foundation. "Match Services Overview." Web page on the ACCESS Support site, date not stated. https://support.access-ci.org/match. Read 2026-11-12.

## Bottom line

- ACCESS MATCH Services connects researchers with experts who help them choose a system, run on a supercomputer and fix code problems.
- An engagement is short, "typically 1-5 sessions with a mentor", and it is free.
- The route is office hours, then a request form. MATCH staff then scope the work and find an expert.

## Background

ACCESS is the National Science Foundation (NSF) program that allocates national research computers. This page on the ACCESS Support site describes MATCH, its mentoring service. It lists three completed example engagements, one of them at the University of California San Diego.

## Key claims

- Purpose: "ACCESS MATCH Services connects researchers with experts to help you select the right system, run on a supercomputer, and solve basic code and research problems." (overview)
- Size: "Short engagements (typically 1-5 sessions with a mentor)" (MATCH Engagements)
- Steps: "An ACCESS member identifies a support need and attends Office Hours to discuss." "We help scope engagement requests and connect you with the right expert." (Steps, 1 and 2)
- Records: "When the goal is achieved notes are logged to benefit the community." (Steps, 3)
- Timeline: "The timeline for an engagement will vary—from a one-hour session to multiple sessions spread over several months—depending on your specific needs." (FAQ)
- Cost: "There is no cost to you for a MATCH engagement." (FAQ)
- Example: "Adapting a GEOspatial Agent-based model for Covid Transmission (GeoACT) for general use", University of California San Diego, "Status: Complete". (Example Engagements)

## Evidence and method

This is a service page. It gives the steps and three examples. It gives no counts of engagements or experts.

## Limits

- The page does not say who the experts are, how many there are or who pays them.
- It does not say how long a request waits before a match.
- It does not say if a person without an ACCESS allocation can ask for an engagement.
