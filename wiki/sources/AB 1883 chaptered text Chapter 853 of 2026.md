## Bottom line

- AB 1883 adds Part 5.8, "Workplace Surveillance Tools", to Division 2 of the Labor Code, from section 1580.
- An employer must not use a workplace surveillance tool that uses artificial intelligence (AI) to recognize or infer an employee's emotional state.
- An employer must not use such a tool to collect neural data.
- "Employer" includes state government, the University of California, the California State University, cities, counties and community college districts.
- The Labor Commissioner or a public prosecutor can enforce the part. The penalty is up to $500 for each violation.

## Key claims

- "An employer shall not use a workplace surveillance tool that uses artificial intelligence on employees for either of the following: (1) Recognizing, or making inferences or predictions about, an individual’s emotional state. (2) Collecting neural data." (Lab. Code § 1581(a))
- "“Neural data” means information that is generated by measuring the activity of an employee’s central or peripheral nervous system, and that is not inferred from nonneural information." (§ 1580(e))
- "All branches of state government, the University of California, the California State University, a city, county, city and county, whether general law or charter, special district, transit district, school district, community college district, or any other state or local governmental entity." (§ 1580(d)(2)(A))
- "This section does not prohibit an employer from using a workplace surveillance tool to ensure safety, or otherwise if it does not meet the specifications in subdivision (a)." (§ 1581(b))
- "An employee, the Labor Commissioner, or a public prosecutor may recover a penalty under this part as a statutory penalty paid to the employee or a civil penalty, but not both, for the same violation." (§ 1582(d)(2))

## Limits

The bill gives no cost estimate and no operative date. The part does not apply to some uses that federal aircraft or defense rules require (§ 1583). The Digest says "reasonable necessary" where section 1583(b) says "reasonably necessary".

The capture is of the Legislature's billNavClient page for the bill, at an address with a referral tag. The billTextClient capture of Oct. 1, 2026 still shows the enrolled version.

AB 1883 names the University of California as an employer with no condition. SB 947 of the same year names it "upon agreement by the regents" ([SB 947 chaptered text Chapter 859 of 2026](SB%20947%20chaptered%20text%20Chapter%20859%20of%202026.md)). AB 1331 of the same year also adds a Part 5.8 to Division 2 of the Labor Code, from section 1560 ([AB 1331 chaptered text Chapter 851 of 2026](AB%201331%20chaptered%20text%20Chapter%20851%20of%202026.md)). The two bills do not refer to each other.

The bill does not mention research compute or CalCompute.
