## Bottom line

- Digital Realty owns or has investments in 310 data centers, 118 of them in the United States. In California, it has 11 data center buildings in Silicon Valley, 5 in San Francisco and 2 in Los Angeles, and more through joint ventures.
- Third parties supply its power, and utilities are its largest expense. The filing says that AI makes power and cooling upgrades harder and more expensive.

## Background

Digital Realty is a real estate investment trust (REIT) with offices in Dallas, Texas. It sells data center space, colocation and interconnection. A Form 10-K is the annual report that a public company must file with the SEC. This filing covers the year to Dec. 31, 2025. It gives the portfolio by metropolitan area, the risk factors and the audited financial statements.

## Key claims

- "As of December 31, 2025, our portfolio included 310 data centers (including 89 data centers held as investments in unconsolidated entities), of which 118 are located in the United States" (Item 1, Business)
- "We offer ~2.9 GW of total in-place IT capacity to meet growing demand, with ~770 MW under construction and >5 GW of future development capacity" (Item 1, PlatformDIGITAL table)
- "We believe our colocation and Turn-Key Flex® facilities are effective solutions for customers who may lack the bandwidth, capital budget, expertise or desire to provide their own extensive data center infrastructure, management and security." (Item 1, Business)
- "For customers who possess the ability to build and operate their own infrastructure, our Powered Base Building® solution provides the physical location, requisite power and network access necessary to support a state-of-the-art data center." (Item 1, Business)
- Silicon Valley: 11 data center buildings, 1,175 thousand net rentable square feet, 81.7 percent occupied. San Francisco: 5 buildings, 844 thousand square feet, 60.1 percent. Los Angeles: 2 buildings, 750 thousand square feet, 86.6 percent. Managed unconsolidated entities add 4 buildings in Silicon Valley (100.0 percent) and 2 in Los Angeles (81.9 percent). (Item 2, Our Portfolio table)
- Silicon Valley gives "3.5" percent of total annualized rent. (Item 1A, table by metropolitan area)
- "We rely on third parties to provide power to our data centers, and we cannot ensure that these third parties will deliver such power in adequate quantities, at acceptable levels of power quality, or on a consistent basis." (Item 1A, Risk Factors)
- "our data centers may be susceptible to power shortages and planned or unplanned outages caused by these shortages or load-shedding requirements by governmental or quasi-governmental entities." (Item 1A, Risk Factors)
- "our power and cooling systems are difficult and expensive to upgrade or expand, especially as we design our data centers to the specifications of new and evolving technologies, such as AI, which are more power-intensive." (Item 1A, Risk Factors)
- "With regard to utilities expense, which is our largest expense category, the vast majority of the expense is passed directly through to our customers" (Item 1A, Risk Factors)
- "For example, an earthquake in California, could significantly impact multiple properties, the aggregate deductible amounts could be significant and the limits we purchase could prove to be insufficient" (Item 1A, Risk Factors)
- "fuel storage tanks are present at most of our properties" (Item 1A, Risk Factors)
- Solution types: "(0 to 1 MW)" for "Small (one cabinet) to medium (75 cabinets) deployments", with "Contract length generally 2-5 years"; "(> 1 MW)" with "Contract length generally 5-10+ years". (Item 1, Data Center Solution Types table)
- New leases of 0 to 1 megawatt (MW) in 2025 average $318 of base cash rent for each rentable square foot each year. New leases of more than 1 MW average $313. (Item 7, Leasing Activities table)
- Total operating revenues are $6,112,692 thousand in 2025 and $5,554,968 thousand in 2024. (Item 7, results of operations)

## Evidence and method

A legal filing that the company makes under the Securities Exchange Act of 1934. The portfolio and lease tables give counts, square feet, occupancy and rents at Dec. 31, 2025. The financial statements have an audit. The risk factors are the company's own statement of risks, not measured data. The energy and water data in Item 1 are for 2024, and cover only managed assets.

## Limits

- The filing gives no power capacity in megawatts for each California metropolitan area. It gives square feet only.
- It does not give the address or the power source of each California data center.
- It does not give GPU or computer counts. Customers own the equipment in the space.
- The rents are averages for all metropolitan areas, not California prices.
