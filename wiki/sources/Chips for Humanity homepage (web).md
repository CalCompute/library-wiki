# Chips for Humanity homepage (web)

## Citation

Chips for Humanity (Hofvarpnir Studios dba Chips for Humanity). "Compute for AI safety research." Web page, 2026; no exact date stated. https://chipsforhumanity.org/. Read 2026-11-09.

## Bottom line

- Chips for Humanity is a nonprofit that gets compute for AI safety researchers outside the frontier labs. It secures large amounts of compute and passes it on at cost.
- It says safety research outside the labs gets about $1 for every $500 of lab research compute. An outside safety researcher has about two graphics processing units (GPUs), against about 200 at Anthropic.
- It plans to give compute free through "a fair and open process" and to publish where the compute goes.

## Background

The footer names the publisher: "Hofvarpnir Studios dba Chips for Humanity, a nonprofit." The homepage gives the problem, the reasons to act now and what the organization does. It quotes a post by Geodesic Research on how hard it was to buy a large cluster. It shows charts of GPUs for outside safety research, GPU rental prices on Vast.ai and forecasts from the AI Futures Project. Its [sources page](Chips%20for%20Humanity%20sources%20%28web%29.md) gives the method for each number.

## Key claims

- "Chips for Humanity is a nonprofit helping researchers outside the frontier labs get the compute their work needs." (Compute for AI safety research)
- "The frontier labs put about 2% of their research compute into safety. For every $500 the labs spend on research compute, safety research outside the labs gets about $1." (Safety research outside the labs is underfunded)
- "Geodesic Research spent six months, and much of its senior staff’s time, getting one." (Buying a big cluster is hard)
- "The only intervention that removes this bottleneck is for a funder to establish (or back) an entity that owns compute contracts and allocates GPU time across their grantees." (Buying a big cluster is hard, quote from Geodesic Research)
- "A safety researcher outside the labs has about two GPUs, and up to eight at the best-funded teams." (Outside researchers make do with far less)
- "Renting the newest chips costs about twice what it did a year ago; older chips cost the same." (Supply is getting tighter)
- "We secure large amounts of high-end compute and pass it on at cost to safety research groups that need a lot of it quickly." (Get compute fast)
- "As funding grows, we’ll give compute to researchers for free, through a fair and open process for deciding who gets it." (Give it away)
- "We will publish where it goes: which projects get compute, and how much." (Independent and transparent)

## Evidence and method

A nonprofit's own homepage. Its numbers are its own estimates from public figures: Anthropic, Epoch AI, Bloomberg, tax filings and fellowship budgets. It calls them "Rough estimates". The 2027 GPU paths are scenarios, not measurements. The price chart uses Vast.ai data from 500.farm.

## Limits

The page does not name staff, funders or a board. It does not say how much compute it holds now, or give prices or terms for its users. It does not describe the "fair and open process" for free compute. It covers only AI safety research, not other research fields or public sector users. The homepage rounds its $558 ratio to $500, as its sources page says.
