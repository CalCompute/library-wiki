# NSF PAPPG 24-1 Chapter III merit review (web)

## Bottom line

- NSF reviews every proposal against two criteria that the National Science Board (NSB) approves: Intellectual Merit and Broader Impacts.
- A Program Officer recommends an award or a decline. Only "an appointed NSF Grants and Agreements Officer" can commit funds.
- Review "may take up to six months". A proposer can appeal a decline for financial or administrative reasons within 30 days.

## Background

Chapter III of the PAPPG describes how NSF processes and reviews proposals. It covers the merit review principles and criteria, reviewer selection, file updates, budget talks, the funding recommendation, the award decision and the appeal process. It also covers what review information goes to the principal investigator (PI) and what NSF releases to the public.

## Key claims

- Criteria: "All NSF proposals are evaluated through use of two National Science Board approved merit review criteria." (III.A.2)
- Both criteria: "<u>Both</u> criteria are to be given <u>full consideration</u> during the review and decision-making processes; each criterion is necessary but neither, by itself, is sufficient." (III.A.2)
- Resources: one review question is "Are there adequate resources available to the PI (either at the home organization or through collaborations) to carry out the proposed activities?" (III.A.2)
- Reviewers: reviewers should have, "To the extent possible, diverse representation within the review group." The factors include "type of organization represented, reviewer diversity, age distribution and geographic balance." (III.B)
- Budget talks: "When such discussions result in a budget reduction of 10% or more from the amount originally proposed, a corresponding reduction should be made in the scope of the project." (III.D)
- Time: "Because of the large volume of proposals, this review and consideration process may take up to six months." (III.E)
- Authority: "only an appointed NSF Grants and Agreements Officer may make commitments, obligations, or awards on behalf of NSF or authorize the expenditure of funds." (III.E)
- Appeal: the request for review "must be received by the Foundation within 30 days after the date of the declination notice." (III.F.2.c(ii))
- Feedback: after a decision, the PI receives "copies of all reviews used in the decision (with any reviewer-identifying information redacted)". (III.G)

## Evidence and method

This is federal agency guidance. It describes a review process and gives no data on review outcomes. It links a process timeline as a separate PDF, which the library has not read.

## Limits

- The chapter does not give success rates or review times in practice.
- It does not apply to compute allocations. ACCESS and the National Artificial Intelligence Research Resource (NAIRR) Pilot have their own review rules.
- [NSF PAPPG 24-1 Supplement 1 (web)](NSF%20PAPPG%2024-1%20Supplement%201%20%28web%29.md) adds a rule to III.B: at least two reviewers for a full proposal, and NSF staff can be reviewers. This copy does not have that text.
