# SB-53 Senate GO analysis 2025-03-25 (web)

## Citation

California State Senate, Committee on Governmental Organization (Senator Steve Padilla, Chair; consultant Brian Duke), analysis of SB 53 (Wiener), version amended February 27, 2025, for the hearing of March 25, 2025. 11 pages. <https://billtexts.s3.amazonaws.com/ca/ca-analysishttps-leginfo-legislature-ca-gov-faces-billAnalysisClient-xhtml-bill-id-202520260SB53-ca-analysis-382673.pdf>. Read 2026-10-20.

The URL is a bill-text mirror, not the Legislature's own site. The analysis is a Senate document. The excerpt page is [SB-53 Senate GO analysis 2025-03-25](SB-53%20Senate%20GO%20analysis%202025-03-25.md).

## Bottom line

- It is the first hearing of the bill, on March 25, 2025.
- The CalCompute text already has the mission, the University of California (UC) placement and the three parts of CalCompute. It also has the seven Report elements, in the enacted words.
- The appointment scheme differs from the enacted law. The Secretary of the Government Operations Agency (GovOps) selects eight members, and the Senate President pro Tempore and the Assembly Speaker select three each.
- The committee says: "This bill does not establish CalCompute". Supporters frame the users as "academics and startups".

## Background

The Senate Committee on Governmental Organization (GO) heard SB 53 first. The Senate Judiciary Committee heard it next, on April 8, 2025 ([SB-53 Senate Judiciary analysis 2025-04-08](SB-53%20Senate%20Judiciary%20analysis%202025-04-08.md)). The bill then had two parts: CalCompute and whistleblower protections. The subject line was "CalCompute: foundation models: whistleblowers". The transparency rules came in July 2025 ([Wiener SB-53 expansion release 2025-07-09](Wiener%20SB-53%20expansion%20release%202025-07-09.md)). The author's office announced the bill on February 28, 2025 ([Wiener SB-53 introduction release 2025-02-28 (web)](Wiener%20SB-53%20introduction%20release%202025-02-28%20%28web%29.md)).

## Key claims

Page numbers are those of the PDF.

- **Digest (p. 1).**
  > This bill establishes, within the Government Operations Agency (GovOps), a consortium to develop a framework for the creation of a public cloud computing cluster to be known as "CalCompute," as specified.
- **"This bill" items 1 to 6 (pp. 2-3)** restate subd. (a) to (f) of the enacted § 11546.8. Item 2: "Requires the consortium to make reasonable efforts to ensure that CalCompute is established within the University of California (UC) to the extent possible." Item 3 lists "A fully owned and hosted cloud platform" and the two kinds of "Necessary human expertise". Item 6 lists the seven Report elements, a to g.
- **Item 7, member fields (p. 3).** The consortium has "14 members selected from among all of the following": UC and other research institutions and national laboratories, "impacted workforce labor organizations", stakeholder groups, "Experts in technology and AI", and "Personnel from other relevant departments and agencies, as necessary."
- **Item 8, appointments (p. 3).**
  > Requires the Secretary of GovOps to select eight members of the consortium, and the President pro Tempore of the Senate and the Speaker of the Assembly to each select three members.
- **Item 9 (p. 3).** "if CalCompute is established within the UC, the UC may receive private donations for the purpose of implementing CalCompute."
- **Item 10 (p. 4)** restates subd. (k): "Provides that the above provisions shall become operative only upon an appropriation in a budget act, or other measure, as specified."
- **Author statement (p. 5).** SB 53 works by "Establishing a process to create a public cloud-computing cluster (CalCompute) that will conduct research into the safe and secure deployment of large-scale AI models."
- **Cloud computing (p. 6).** "Cloud computing is the on-demand access of computing resources [...] over the internet with pay-per-use pricing." The committee names Amazon Web Services, Google Cloud Platform, Microsoft Azure, IBM Cloud and Oracle Cloud as examples.
- **CalCompute comment (p. 7).**
  > This bill does not establish CalCompute, however, it does specify that if CalCompute is eventually established within the UC, the UC may receive private donations for the purposes of implementing CalCompute.
- **Supporters' case (p. 7).**
  > Supporters argue that a public CalCompute is a step towards democratizing access to advanced computational resources currently out of reach for many academics and startups in California. CalCompute can potentially lower the barriers to entry for innovative research and development in AI, and a shared resource can foster cross-sector collaboration, driving economic growth by leveling the playing field for smaller players that might otherwise be outpaced by large private firms.
- **Frontier model cost (pp. 7-8).** Frontier models are "often costing hundreds of millions or billions of dollars". The whistleblower rules cover companies that trained a model with compute "that costs at least $100 million."
- **SB 1047 (pp. 8, 10).** "Similarly to this bill, SB 1047 would have also established a consortium in GovOps to develop a framework for the creation of CalCompute". Governor Newsom vetoed SB 1047.
- **Suggested amendments (p. 9).** The author agreed to add unpaid service with expenses, and "The working group shall be dissolved upon submission of the report". The text numbers these "11547.61(h)" and "11547.61(i)". They are subd. (h) and (i) of the enacted § 11546.8.
- **Related bill (p. 10).** "SB 893 (Padilla, 2023) would have required GovOps, the Governor's Office of Business and Economic Development, the California Privacy Protection Agency, and CDT to establish the California AI Research Hub". It was "Held in the Assembly Appropriations Committee Suspense File".
- **Fiscal (p. 10).** "Appropriation: No Fiscal Com.: Yes Local: No".
- **Support (p. 10).** Co-sources: "Secure AI Project", "Economic Security California Action" and "Encode". Other supporters include "Center for Human-Compatible Artificial Intelligence, UC Berkeley" and "Compute Exchange". Opposition: "None received".
- **Co-sources on CalCompute (p. 11).**
  > SB 53 would also set in motion CalCompute, a public cloud computing cluster for use by academics and startups in California. Computational resources are essential for AI research, and CalCompute would make those resources more accessible to California's top universities and startups, helping to catalyze additional research into beneficial applications of AI and supporting in particular smaller startups for a healthier innovation ecosystem.

## Evidence and method

A committee staff summary of the bill, with background, the author's statement and the supporters' letter. The cloud computing background has no citations.

## Limits

- It describes the February 27, 2025 version. The text changed before enactment.
- The committee's cloud examples are commercial services only. It gives no public or nonprofit examples.
- The author's statement and the supporters' arguments are advocacy. The committee reports them and does not test them.

> ⚠️ Contradiction: Item 8 gives eight members to the Secretary of GovOps and three each to the "President pro Tempore of the Senate" and the "Speaker of the Assembly". Enacted § 11546.8 (g) ties each appointer to a field: four plus four by the Secretary of Government Operations, three labor representatives by the Speaker, and three stakeholder representatives by the "Senate Rules Committee" ([SB-53 Gov Code 11546.8](SB-53%20Gov%20Code%2011546.8.md)). The April 8 analysis has the same March scheme ([SB-53 Senate Judiciary analysis 2025-04-08](SB-53%20Senate%20Judiciary%20analysis%202025-04-08.md)).

> ⚠️ Contradiction: Item 7 includes "Personnel from other relevant departments and agencies, as necessary." Enacted subd. (g) has no such category. It has four fixed categories ([SB-53 Gov Code 11546.8](SB-53%20Gov%20Code%2011546.8.md)).

> ⚠️ Contradiction: The co-sources call CalCompute a cluster "for use by academics and startups". Subd. (f)(2)(D) leaves the users to the Report: "a process for determining which users and projects will be supported by CalCompute" ([SB-53 Gov Code 11546.8](SB-53%20Gov%20Code%2011546.8.md)).

> ⚠️ Contradiction: The author's statement says CalCompute "will conduct research into the safe and secure deployment of large-scale AI models." Subd. (d) describes a platform and staff: "A fully owned and hosted cloud platform" and "Necessary human expertise" ([SB-53 Gov Code 11546.8](SB-53%20Gov%20Code%2011546.8.md)).
