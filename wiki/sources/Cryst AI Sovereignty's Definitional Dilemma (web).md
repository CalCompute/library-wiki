# Cryst AI Sovereignty's Definitional Dilemma (web)

## Citation

Juan N. Pava, Caroline Meinhardt, Elena Cryst and James Landay. "AI Sovereignty's Definitional Dilemma." News article, Stanford Institute for Human-Centered Artificial Intelligence (HAI), February 17, 2026. https://hai.stanford.edu/news/ai-sovereigntys-definitional-dilemma. Read 2026-11-06.

## Bottom line

- A Stanford HAI article of February 17, 2026, by four authors, with Elena Cryst among them.
- It says that "AI sovereignty" has no stable definition. It asks governments to say why and where in the AI technology stack they want more agency.

## Background

Stanford HAI publishes this article in its news section. The authors' note gives each author's position. This is a profile page. Elena Cryst's entity page lists it; no reading list does.

## Key claims

- "Governments worldwide are racing to control their AI futures, but unclear definitions hinder real policy progress." (summary)
- "We see four reasons why this is the case and urge governments to pursue conceptual clarity by specifying and balancing why and where they want to rebalance their AI dependencies and recognizing the trade-offs." (introduction)
- "Achieving domestic development across the entire AI stack (the strongest interpretation of sovereignty) is prohibitively costly and unnecessary for most countries." (Reorienting AI Sovereignty Narratives)
- "Elena Cryst is the director of policy and society at Stanford HAI". (authors' note)

## Evidence and method

A short analysis by researchers at a university institute. It cites national programs, such as the Sovereign AI Unit of the United Kingdom, as examples.

## Limits

- It is not peer reviewed.
- It says a white paper on AI sovereignty is to follow. The library does not hold that paper.
