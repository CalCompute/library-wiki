# UK AI Research Resource publication page (web)

## Citation

Department for Science, Innovation and Technology (DSIT), Department for Business, Innovation, Science and Trade, and UK Research and Innovation (UKRI). "AI Research Resource." GOV.UK publication page. Published 17 July 2025; last updated 28 September 2026. https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ai-research-resource. Read 2026-11-11. The page states: "All content is available under the Open Government Licence v3.0, except where otherwise stated".

## Bottom line

- The AI Research Resource (AIRR) is a set of UK supercomputers that gives AI compute to researchers, academia and industry.
- Users reach AIRR through Isambard-AI at the University of Bristol and Zenith at the University of Cambridge.
- AIRR is a partnership of the UK government, UKRI, the two universities and five technology companies: AMD, Dell, HPE, Intel and Nvidia.

## Background

GOV.UK is the website of the UK government. This page collects the AIRR documents. They are two access calls, a description of the supercomputers, and UKRI guidance. The guidance covers the Gateway route, the Rapid Access route, the AIRR Portal and distributed peer review. This library holds only this page, not the documents it lists. The update history gives the dates of the calls.

## Key claims

- What it is: "The AI Research Resource (AIRR) is a suite of advanced supercomputers that provides AI-specialised compute capacity to researchers, academia, and industry." (summary)
- Systems: "The AIRR offers compute access to users through Isambard-AI at the University of Bristol and Zenith at the University of Cambridge." (Details)
- Users: "Providing specialised compute capacity enables small and medium-sized enterprises and the research and development (R&D) community to drive innovation and advancement in AI." (Details)
- Partners: "AIRR is a partnership between: AMD, Dell, HPE, Intel, Nvidia, UK government, UK Research and Innovation (UKRI), University of Bristol, University of Cambridge". (Details, a list on the page)
- Closed calls: "Banner added to the 'Cloud access call' attachment to advise applications for that call have closed." (update of 28 September 2026) "The AI open access call is now closed." (update of 17 July 2026)
- Innovator route: "AIRR Innovator route open for applications and AIRR Innovator route guidance published." (update of 7 November 2025)
- Department name: "References to DSIT amended to BIST." (update of 28 September 2026)

## Evidence and method

This is a government publication index page. It gives the purpose, the systems, the partners and a dated history of calls. The detail of each access route is in the documents it lists.

## Limits

- The page gives no budget, no GPU count and no performance figures.
- It does not give the terms of each access route. The UKRI guidance pages, which this library does not hold, give them.
- The page lists the publisher as three bodies. The update of 28 September 2026 changes DSIT to "BIST". The page does not say what BIST stands for; its "From" line names the "Department for Business, Innovation, Science and Trade".
