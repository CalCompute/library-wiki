# BCP 2026-27 Cal OES SB 53 large developers (web)

## Bottom line

- Cal OES asks for two positions and $618,000 General Fund each year from 2026-27 to carry out SB 53 (Chapter 138, Statutes of 2025).
- The money pays for a way to report critical safety incidents and a confidential channel for summaries of catastrophic risk assessments. It also pays for an annual report to the Legislature.
- The two positions are an analyst in the California Cybersecurity Integration Center (Cal-CSIC) and an attorney.

## Background

A Budget Change Proposal (BCP) is a department's request for a change to its budget, on the Department of Finance form DF-46. The cover sheet gives the date that the proposal went to the Legislature. The department director signed this one on December 30, 2025. A companion proposal of the California Department of Technology covers that department's SB 53 work ([BCP 2026-27 Department of Technology SB 53 large developers (web)](BCP%202026-27%20Department%20of%20Technology%20SB%2053%20large%20developers%20%28web%29.md)).

## Key claims

- "The Office of Emergency Services requests two positions and $618,000 General Fund in FY 2026-27 and ongoing to establish a mechanism to be used by a frontier developer or the public to report critical safety incidents and a mechanism to confidentially submit summaries of assessments of potential catastrophic risk resulting from use of frontier models" (Budget Request Summary).
- "In addition, Cal OES must report findings of critical incidents to the Legislature by January 1, 2027, and annually thereafter." (A. Problem Statement)
- "California is home to 32 of the top 50 AI companies worldwide." (B. Justification)
- "Cal OES is requesting one (1.0) Associate Government Program Analyst within the California Cybersecurity Integration Center (Cal-CSIC) to create the reporting mechanism, collect and aggregate relevant data for the report, and draft the annual legislative report." (B. Justification)
- "Cal OES is requesting one (1.0) Attorney III to provide legal advice and counsel in the drafting of regulations, as needed, and the development of the reporting mechanism, including the drafting and negotiating of software contracts and agreements regarding information storage and sharing." (B. Justification)
- "Implementation will begin July 1, 2026." (E. Implementation Plan)

## Evidence and method

A state budget request on the standard DF-46 form. It gives a problem statement, a justification and a fiscal detail sheet in thousands of dollars. The fiscal sheet gives $387,000 for personal services and $231,000 for operating expenses and equipment each year. Salaries are $79,000 for the analyst and $152,000 for the attorney, with $19,000 of overtime.

## Limits

- The proposal does not say whether the Legislature approved the request. It is not the enacted budget.
- The 2026-27 floor report lists $618,000 and two positions for this work in the 2026-27 budget agreement ([Assembly Budget floor report 2026-27 budget 2026-06-27 (web)](Assembly%20Budget%20floor%20report%202026-27%20budget%202026-06-27%20%28web%29.md), p. 128).
- It cites Business and Professions Code section 22757.13 and following. This library holds only Government Code section 11546.8 of SB 53 ([SB-53 Gov Code 11546.8](SB-53%20Gov%20Code%2011546.8.md)).
- It says nothing about CalCompute.
- It does not say which software or service will carry the reporting channels.
