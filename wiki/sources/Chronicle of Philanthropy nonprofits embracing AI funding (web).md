# Chronicle of Philanthropy nonprofits embracing AI funding (web)

## Citation

Eden Stiffman. "Nonprofits Are Embracing AI, but Many Struggle to Find Funding." The Chronicle of Philanthropy, October 2, 2025. https://www.philanthropy.com/news/nonprofits-are-embracing-ai-but-many-struggle-to-find-funding/. Read 2026-11-09.

## Bottom line

- A news story on the 2025 AI for Humanity report by Fast Forward, a nonprofit accelerator, from a survey of nearly 200 nonprofits.
- Funding for systems, tools and talent is the top need of "AI-powered" nonprofits. Funders often give money only after a nonprofit shows results.

## Background

The Chronicle of Philanthropy publishes news on the nonprofit world. A senior editor wrote this story. Fast Forward ran the survey in the spring with support from Google.org. Faculty at Indiana University, the University of Minnesota and Wayne State University led the research. The story uses Visilant, an eye-care nonprofit in India, as its example.

## Key claims

- The report separates "“AI-powered” groups, like Visilant, for which the technology is central to their impact, and “AI-assisted” organizations, which often use off-the-shelf tools such as ChatGPT, Google Gemini, and Anthropic’s Claude for back-office tasks" (opening)
- "Forty percent of AI-powered nonprofits have been using AI for a year or less, and 30 percent have budgets under $500,000, the survey found." (Rapid Growth, Tight Resources)
- "Eighty-four percent of AI-powered nonprofits cite funding for systems, tools, and talent as their greatest need. Nearly half say adopting AI has raised their expenses." (Top Challenges)
- "Nearly half of respondents cited privacy risks, while 41 percent pointed to the lack of in-house technical expertise." (Top Challenges)
- "More than three-quarters of AI-powered nonprofits said they rely on public data-sets to train their models, but many of those U.S. data-sets are at risk of disappearing." (Top Challenges)
- "The report urges donors to break that cycle with earlier, flexible support, not just for programs, but by investing in engineers, data staff, and infrastructure to ensure AI is safe and equitable." (On-the-Ground Impact)
- "“The onus is on grant makers,” she said. “Nonprofits can only do what funders allow them to do within their budgets.”" (Experimentation Everywhere, Shannon Farley, Fast Forward)

## Evidence and method

A news story about a survey report, with one case study. The story gives these figures:

- Fast Forward accelerator applicants that call themselves AI-powered: 13 of 247 in 2024, and 379 of 782 in 2026.
- AI-powered nonprofits with the smallest budgets serve a median of just under 2,000 people. Above $5 million in budget, the median is 7 million people.
- Visilant's revenue grew from $100,000 in 2024 to $2 million, with a $1.5 million grant from Google.org.
- 78 percent of the nonprofits in the study use AI in an "assisted" way.

The story links to the report at Fast Forward. The library has no copy of the report itself.

## Limits

- The story says the voluntary survey "skews toward more tech-forward organizations".
- The story carries the date October 2, 2025, but it gives an applicant count for 2026. The page does not say if anyone updated the story.
- It does not mention shared compute, CalCompute or the Report.
