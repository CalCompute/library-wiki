# FedTech NAIRR pilot NVIDIA contribution breakdown 2024 (web)

## Citation

Nyczepir, Dave. "When It Comes to Integrating AI Into Agencies' Work, the NAIRR Is a Start." FedTech Magazine (CDW), April 1, 2024. https://fedtechmagazine.com/article/2024/04/when-it-comes-integrating-ai-agencies-work-nairr-start. Read 2026-11-11.

## Bottom line

- A trade magazine article puts NVIDIA's contribution to the National Artificial Intelligence Research Resource (NAIRR) pilot at "about $30 million worth of resources".
- It gives three parts: DGX Cloud compute worth $24 million, 1,000 software licence seats that sell for $4.5 million, and training worth about $1.5 million.

## Background

FedTech Magazine is a CDW publication about technology in federal agencies. Its page links to CDW product pages. A senior editor wrote the article three months after the pilot launched. All the figures come from an NVIDIA spokesperson whom the article does not name. NVIDIA's own launch post gives the total of $30 million without the breakdown ([NVIDIA NAIRR pilot contribution 2024-01-24 (web)](NVIDIA%20NAIRR%20pilot%20contribution%202024-01-24%20%28web%29.md)).

## Key claims

- "NVIDIA is offering about $30 million worth of resources to the NAIRR, the spokesperson says." (section "NIVIDIA Brings AI Resources to the NAIRR")
- "The first offering is access to the NVIDIA DGX Cloud product, effectively 1,000 node months of computing time on clusters stood up for the NAIRR, worth $24 million." (same section)
- "The second offering is 1,000 seats of software licenses for NVIDIA AI Enterprise, a bundle of AI frameworks for training, inferencing and data analytics that sells for $4.5 million, the spokesperson says." (same section)
- "Lastly, NVIDIA is offering an AI and data analytics training curriculum called the NVIDIA Deep Learning Institute, as well as a series of hackathons, boot camps and workshops around AI and high-performance computing worth about $1.5 million" (same section)
- "Launched this January, the pilot is primarily concerned with democratizing the NSF academic research community's access to AI computing resources, not that of other agencies." (introduction)
- "For NVIDIA's part, it will learn about the effectiveness of its AI products in applications they weren't necessarily designed for." (section "Understanding How AI Can Help Agencies")

## Evidence and method

A trade press article. Its figures are the statements of one company spokesperson. It gives no documents, contracts or usage data. The dollar values are the company's own valuation of in-kind resources, not cash.

## Limits

- The spokesperson is not named.
- The article does not say how NVIDIA values a node month or a licence seat.
- It does not report how much of the contribution researchers used.
- The article says the first call for allocations "ended March 1". It says a second call could come "as soon as this month". Later pilot pages give the current routes: [NAIRR Pilot resource allocations (web)](NAIRR%20Pilot%20resource%20allocations%20%28web%29.md).
