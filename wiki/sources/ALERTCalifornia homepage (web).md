# ALERTCalifornia homepage (web)

## Citation

ALERTCalifornia, UC San Diego. "ALERTCalifornia." Homepage, date not stated (© 2026). https://alertcalifornia.org. Read 2026-11-09.

## Bottom line

- ALERTCalifornia is a public safety program of UC San Diego. It runs a network of wildfire cameras across California.
- UC San Diego researchers build the cyberinfrastructure that processes, stores and shows the camera data.

## Background

This is the homepage of ALERTCalifornia, a program based at the University of California San Diego. It gives the size and range of the camera network. It quotes the Governor, the California Department of Forestry and Fire Protection (CAL FIRE) and the U.S. Forest Service. It shows partner logos and short profiles of the program's investigators. The copyright line names the Regents of the University of California.

## Key claims

- "Based at the University of California San Diego, ALERTCalifornia is a public safety program working to understand natural disasters and determine short and long-term impacts on people and the environment to inform management decisions." (introduction)
- "ALERTCalifornia has more than 1,300 high-definition, pan-tilt-zoom cameras deployed across California (as of November 2026), providing a 24-hour backcountry network with near-infrared night vision to monitor disasters such as active wildfires." (View live camera feeds)
- "1,200 cameras deployed (as of February 2026)" (camera count panel)
- "Researchers at UC San Diego have taken the lead in creating the cyberinfrastructure to process, store, manage and visualize the massive amounts of incoming data from these camera installations." (Network + Technology)
- "Currently HPWREN provides wide area wireless internet access for ALERTCalifornia throughout southernmost California including San Diego, Orange, Los Angeles, Inyo and Riverside counties." (team profile, Frank Vernon)
- "CAL FIRE has been spearheading this effort from the very beginning, proactively partnering with UC San Diego and embracing innovation in their constant efforts to make California more resilient to wildfires." (Governor Gavin Newsom quote)

## Evidence and method

A program homepage. It gives camera counts, camera range and sweep times, and quotes from public officials with links to their sources. It names the High Performance Wireless Research and Education Network (HPWREN) as the network for southern California. The partner section shows logos only: CAL FIRE, Western Digital, the California Governor's Office of Emergency Services, PG&E, Southern California Edison, SDG&E, Microsoft and Esri. The library reads the names from the logo files.

## Limits

The page gives no budget, funding source, hardware detail or data volume. It does not say who can use the data or on what terms. It links to a Terms of Use page and an FAQs page that the library has not read. It does not describe the partners' roles.

> ⚠️ Contradiction: The page gives two camera counts: "more than 1,300" cameras "as of November 2026" and "1,200 cameras deployed (as of February 2026)". The two counts have different dates. The page does not say which is current.
