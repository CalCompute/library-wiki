# Assembly Budget floor report 2026-27 budget 2026-06-27 (web)

## Citation

Assembly Budget Committee (Jesse Gabriel, Chair). "Floor Report, 2026-2027 Budget." California State Assembly, June 27, 2026. https://abgt.assembly.ca.gov/media/8133. ## Bottom line

- This is the Assembly Budget Committee's summary of the final 2026-27 budget agreement of June 26, 2026. It gives total spending of $351.7 billion, of which $251.5 billion is General Fund.
- The agreement funds SB 53 (Chapter 138, Statutes of 2025) at three departments. They are the Office of Emergency Services (Cal OES), the Department of Justice (DOJ) and the Department of Technology (CDT).
- It does not name CalCompute.

## Background

This report has one part for each subject area, for example Higher Education, Energy and General Government. The report has the date June 27, 2026, before the votes: the Legislature "is expected to vote on around 20 bills" on June 29, 2026 (Overview, p. 2). The report reads 47,000 words; this page covers its overview and its items on technology, AI, data centers and higher education.

## Key claims

- "Across the General Fund, the state’s many special funds, and selected bond funds, the final budget includes spending of $351.7 billion for 2026-27, including $251.5 billion for 2026-27" (Overview, p. 2)
- "Approves $618,000 General Fund and two positions in 2026-27 and ongoing to establish a mechanism to be used by a frontier developer or the public to report critical safety incidents and a mechanism to confidentially submit summaries of assessments of potential catastrophic risk resulting from the use of frontier models (artificial intelligence systems), as mandated by Chapter 138, Statutes of 2025 (SB 53, Wiener)." (Office of Emergency Services, p. 128)
- "Cal OES must report findings of critical incidents to the Legislature by January 1, 2027, and annually thereafter." (Office of Emergency Services, p. 128)
- "Includes $2.2 million General Fund and 8 positions in 2026-27 and $2.1 million ongoing for the implementation of SB 53 (Wiener), Chapter 138, Statutes of 2025." (Department of Justice, p. 125)
- "Includes General Fund (GF) budget authority (ongoing) for 1.0 Information Technology Manager II (ITM II) and $360,000 (one-time) for temp help/consultants in Fiscal Year (FY) 2026-27 to implement Chapter 138, Statutes of 2025 (SB 53)." (California Department of Technology, p. 112)
- "Approves an augmentation of $560,000 dollars in Technology Services Revolving Fund (TSRF) to redirect 5.0 existing positions to address a significant and sustained increase in statewide technology procurement workload related to Generative AI (EO N-12-23)" (California Department of Technology, p. 112)
- "Approves the Governors $22 million General Fund onetime for contingency funding with for the Middle-Mile Broadband initiative." (California Department of Technology, p. 112)
- "Appropriates $218,000 from the Public Utilities Commission Public Advocates Office Account and 1 position ongoing for the Public Advocates Office to meet increasing workload associated with data center growth and the impacts on the energy sector." (Energy, p. 77)
- "Includes trailer bill language that moves California Education Learning Lab from GO-LCIE to GovOps." (Secretary for Government Operations Agency, p. 102)
- "Includes $5 million for the CA Institute for Science and Innovation (including CITRIS)." (Higher Education, University of California, p. 50)

Other AI items in the agreement:

- $1.2 million a year for DOJ to enforce the California AI Transparency Act, AB 853 (p. 124).
- $717,000 in 2026-27 and $463,000 in 2027-28 for the California Artificial Intelligence Cybersecurity Collaboration Playbook at Cal OES (p. 128).
- $408,000 in 2026-27 and $286,000 in 2027-28 for CDT to support AB 979 (p. 112).
- $360,000 in 2026-27 and $350,000 a year after for the Fair Political Practices Commission (FPPC), for AB 2355. This is "including $100,000 AI-detection software annual license fee" (p. 115).
- "a one-time increase of $7.2 million State Highway Account for Generative Artificial Intelligence (Gen AI) in the state highway system" (Transportation, p. 83).
- $1 million for "scale-up and ongoing operation of “Poppy, California’s Digital Assistant.”" (California Department of Technology, p. 112).
- $2.2 million in 2026-27, and $4.5 million from 2028-29, for "artificial intelligence notetaking functionality within CDCR’s electronic health record system" (p. 119).

## Evidence and method

A summary of a budget agreement by a committee of the Legislature, written before the floor votes. It gives the amounts, fund sources and positions of each action. It gives no analysis of need and no data on outcomes.

## Limits

- The report does not name CalCompute or Government Code section 11546.8. Its SB 53 items are for Cal OES, DOJ and CDT.
- The report has a date before the votes of June 29, 2026. It does not say which bills passed or what the Governor signed.
- The CDT item for SB 53 gives one position and $360,000 one time. It does not give the total of $604,000 in the request ([BCP 2026-27 Department of Technology SB 53 large developers (web)](BCP%202026-27%20Department%20of%20Technology%20SB%2053%20large%20developers%20%28web%29.md)). It does not repeat the request's "supportive role in CalCompute".
- The Caltrans item is $7.2 million. [BCP 2026-27 May Revision Caltrans generative AI roadways (web)](BCP%202026-27%20May%20Revision%20Caltrans%20generative%20AI%20roadways%20%28web%29.md) requests $16,428,000. The report does not say why the amounts differ.
- The California State University part says that "the state would plan to provide UC with one-time back payments" (p. 50). The report does not say if "UC" is an error for CSU.
