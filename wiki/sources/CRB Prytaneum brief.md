# CRB Prytaneum brief

## Citation

Kevin Esterling. "Prytaneum: A Web-Based Town Hall Platform Designed for Public Meetings." CRB Nexus Research in Brief, guest author. California Research Bureau (CRB), California State Library. Published July 12, 2024, modified Sept. 23, 2026 (dates from the page metadata; the page text shows no date). https://www.library.ca.gov/crb/nexus/briefs/prytaneum/. ## Bottom line

- Prytaneum is an online town hall platform from the Laboratory for Technology, Communication and Democracy (TeCD-Lab) at UC Riverside. It uses AI to sort public comments by topic.
- A California state commission used it in a pilot listening session in June 2024. A state body published this account of it.
- The author directs the lab that built the platform. The brief describes the platform. It does not evaluate it.

## Background

The brief spells his name "Kevin Esterling".

The brief supports both. It names the commission and the June 2024 date.

The raw note also says "Democracy Fund grant 2019; IES $2M curriculum grant" (line 97). The brief names the Democracy Fund and the Institute of Education Sciences (IES) as supporters. It gives no year and no amount. That part of the note rests on other sources.

## Key claims

- What it is (Overview): "Developed by TeCD-Lab at UC Riverside, Prytaneum is a new, highly-interactive online town hall virtual platform specifically designed for hosting public meetings."
- AI use (A Web-Based Town Hall Platform): "The curation algorithm is trained to a library of topics, each relying on factually-accurate, non-partisan, and policy-relevant reading material."
- Comparison (same section): the brief says it creates "opportunities for democratic engagement unavailable through existing, commercially available webinar or streaming platforms".
- Pilot (same section): "a recent pilot listening session from June 2024, hosted by the California Mental Health Services Oversight and Accountability Commission (the 'Commission') and by the TeCD-Lab". The topic was "K-12 school-based mental health services".
- Topics (same section): "we used the AI algorithm to extract five relevant topics: school-based mental health services, parental support, stigma, funding, and early intervention and prevention."
- Session format (same section): for each prompt, "the commission staff shared the corresponding efforts underway at the commission, then the community members shared their views, and then the commission staff reviewed the community perspectives and responded."
- Outreach (same section): the lab made an outreach website "in both English and Spanish".
- Funding (Developed by UC Riverside TeCD-Lab): support from "the Democracy Fund, the Institute of Education Sciences, the UCR OASIS program, and UCR Information and Technology Services".
- Status (same section): "The platform is still in development and testing likely until the end of Summer 2024." Also: "the plans for licensing the platform are still in progress."

## Evidence and method

A short brief by the platform's developer, published by a state research bureau as a guest piece. It describes one pilot. It reports no participant count, no survey of participants and no outcome measure.

## Limits

- The author leads the lab that built the platform. The claims about inclusion and diversity are the developer's claims, not test results.
- The status facts date from 2024. The platform's status in 2026 is not in this brief.
- The brief does not say how many people took part in the pilot.
