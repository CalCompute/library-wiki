# NJSCI Executive Director posting (web)

## Citation

New Jersey Innovation Authority (NJIA), for the NJ Supercomputer Initiative (NJSCI). "Executive Director." Job posting on Lever, date not stated. https://jobs.lever.co/NJStateOfficeofInnovation/f2a8d5fa-5deb-49d4-bb8d-791a03945de8. Read 2026-11-18.

## Bottom line

- The NJSCI seeks a founding Executive Director to take a shared statewide computing resource from buildout into operations. Initial users can start "as early as January 2027".
- An interim partner agreement with NJIA governs the role. The posting plans to spin NJSCI out as an independent nonprofit with its own governing board.

## Background

This is a full-time, on-site job posting for New Jersey. It describes the initiative, the first-year priorities, the responsibilities and the qualifications. The salary is $250,000 to $375,000 a year. A "fiscally-sponsored initiative of the National Center for Civic Innovation" hires the person. The initiative's homepage links the posting ([NJSCI homepage (web)](NJSCI%20homepage%20%28web%29.md)).

## Key claims

- "The NJ Supercomputer Initiative (NJSCI) is a new statewide effort to establish and operate a shared advanced computing resource for New Jersey’s research universities, community colleges, K-12 institutions, government agencies, and additional stakeholders." (About the Initiative)
- "Initial commitments are expected to fund buildout and the first several years of operations; the Executive Director will be responsible for securing the initiative's long-term financial footing." (About the Initiative)
- "The Executive Director and NJSCI will be governed by a partner agreement with the New Jersey Innovation Authority (NJIA) on an interim basis, and ultimately will report to the Initiative’s governing board, once fully established as an entity." (Position Summary)
- "Incorporate and spin out NJSCI as a new independent nonprofit organization, subject to terms set forth by NJIA’s grant agreement." (Initial Priorities)
- "Develop governance, allocation, security, and service policies for board approval, in consultation with partner institutions." (Initial Priorities)
- "Implement and maintain a board-approved cost recovery and participation model for partner institutions and other authorized users." (Finance and sustainability)
- "Recruit, retain, supervise, and develop the initiative’s staff from a small launch team of about 12 people, including core initiative staff, contractors, and loaned staff, to a sustained team of ~25 core initiative staff." (People and partners)
- "Ensure sound security and data governance for a shared facility serving academic and government users, and determine with the board and institutional stakeholders which regulated workloads, if any, the environment will be authorized to support." (Operations and service delivery)

## Evidence and method

A job posting. It states the planned structure, staff size, first-year tasks and pay. The initial priority areas are "health and life sciences, energy and climate resilience, and foundational, responsible, secure AI". The services planned for users are compute allocations, storage, technical support, training and research facilitation. The posting lists three tests of success:

1. "A facility in production and used across every institution type in the state."
2. "An organization that is financially self-sustaining beyond its initial commitments."
3. "An independent entity standing on its own governance."

## Limits

- The posting does not give the hardware, the budget or the amount of the initial commitments.
- It does not give the allocation policy or the cost recovery rates. It says that the Executive Director develops them for board approval.
- It does not name the board members or the host organization.
- It has no date. It describes plans, not results.
- The homepage also links a separate posting for an Interim Executive Director (Chief Operating Officer). The library has not read it.
