## Bottom line

- The California Community Colleges (CCC) Chancellor's Office tells colleges to test agentic AI in isolated sandboxes. It also asks for a human in the loop and vetted enterprise tools for student data.
- The memo names six enterprise products that it says protect institutional data. This is a list of the AI vendors already in the CCC system.
- It also shows a demand for safe test environments that colleges must now build alone.

## Background

This is the second memo in the CCC spring 2026 AI guidance series. It builds on ISTI 25-300-01 (AI literacy). It asks colleges to review the guidance before the end of the spring 2026 term.

## Key claims

- Scenario A: AI bots for student support need "Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) protocols" and a tested "escalation path" to a human. (p. 2)
- Scenario B: "it is best practice to evaluate agentic tools in a sandbox environment before broader deployment." Technical leaders should use "isolated physical or virtual environments and dedicated 'low-privilege' API keys" and test for prompt injection. (pp. 2 to 3)
- Suggested HITL procedures: human authorization, identity mapping of agent actions to a human, limited access, boundary setting and disclosure. (p. 3)
- Scenario C: faculty and students should use tools "under institutional or enterprise licenses, such as Nectir and Playlab, that are vetted and compliant with FERPA". (pp. 3 to 4)
- For deployers: "prioritize enterprise subscriptions (e.g., Google Workspace for Education, ChatGPT Edu, Microsoft 365 Copilot, Claude Enterprise Plan, Nectir, Playlab)" that keep data out of model training. (p. 4)
- Before connecting a tool to Canvas, deployers should confirm it meets "WCAG 2.2 Level AA accessibility and user privacy standards". (p. 4)
- Leadership should define permitted use "through tiered models, such as Open, Conditional, Restricted, or Closed". (p. 5)
- Leadership should align security with "the cybersecurity allocation provided in AB 178" and the CCC Security Center. (p. 5)

## Evidence and method

Guidance with example scenarios. It names one pilot ("the Cali financial aid pilot") but gives no results.

## Limits

- Recommendations only. No data on how many colleges follow them.
- The product list is a set of examples, not a procurement decision.
- The memo names FERPA (the Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act) and SOC 2, but it gives no test for compliance.
