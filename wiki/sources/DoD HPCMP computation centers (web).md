# DoD HPCMP computation centers (web)

## Citation

High Performance Computing Modernization Program (HPCMP), Department of War (formerly the Department of Defense). "Computation Centers." Web page, date not stated. https://www.hpc.mil/solution-areas/computation-centers. Read 2026-11-11.

## Bottom line

- The HPCMP gives high performance computing to the Department's research, development, test and evaluation (RDT&E) and acquisition engineering (AE) communities. It does this through five DoW Supercomputing Resource Centers (DSRCs).
- Four Affiliated Resource Centers (ARCs), a help desk and a Data Analysis and Visualization (DAV) Center add to the five DSRCs. The latest systems come from Liqid and Penguin Computing.

## Background

The HPCMP is a federal program. Its program office is in Vicksburg, Mississippi. This page describes the centers that operate its supercomputers. The page uses the name "Department of War (DoW)".

## Key claims

- "The High Performance Computing Modernization Program (HPCMP) delivers world-class commercial, high-end, high-performance computational capabilities to the Department of War's (DoW's) Research, Development, Test and Evaluation (RDT&E) and Acquisition Engineering (AE) communities through its five DoW Supercomputing Resource Centers (DSRCs)." (first paragraph)
- "Each Center is a robust collection of high-performance computing (HPC) capabilities, which include: large-scale HPC systems, high-speed networking, multi-petabyte archival mass storage systems, and customer support services." (second paragraph)
- "Complementing our DSRCs and providing additional services to the DoW HPC community are four Affiliated Resource Centers (ARCs), an HPC Help Desk, and a Data Analysis and Visualization (DAV) Center." (second paragraph)
- "All five Centers were provided new supercomputing capabilities from Liqid and Penguin Computing during the latest supercomputer acquisition cycle." (fourth paragraph)
- The supercomputers let the communities "test and model defense systems that cannot be modeled in the real-world due to time, financial, physical, or safety constraints." (fourth paragraph)

## Evidence and method

A federal program page. It names the kinds of centers, their count and the vendors of the latest systems. It gives no system sizes, no budget and no user count.

## Limits

The page does not name the five DSRCs or their locations. It does not give the date or the cost of the latest acquisition cycle. It does not say who outside the Department can use the systems.
