# CROSS About

## Citation

Center for Research in Open Source Software (CROSS), UC Santa Cruz. "About." Web page, cross.ucsc.edu, last modified May 4, 2026. https://cross.ucsc.edu/about/. Read 2026-10-26.

## Bottom line

- CROSS started in 2015 with "a generous gift of $2,000,000 from alumnus Sage Weil". It had three founding industry members: Toshiba America Electronic Components, SK Hynix Memory Solutions and Micron Technology.
- It is a UC unit that companies fund as "Members of Industry & Government". The page lists what industry, students and the campus each get.
- The page gives no membership fee, no member rights in decisions and no current member list.

## Background

Carlos Maltzahn founded CROSS. Sage Weil created the Ceph storage system "as part of his Ph.D. work at UC Santa Cruz". See [CROSS](../entities/CROSS.md) and [CROSS homepage](CROSS%20homepage.md).

## Key claims

- Founding: "founded in 2015 by Carlos Maltzahn with a generous gift of $2,000,000 from alumnus Sage Weil and the founding memberships of Toshiba America Electronic Components, SK Hynix Memory Solutions, and Micron Technology."
- Leadership: "Faculty Director James Davis, Executive Director Stephanie Lieggi, and Associate Director Emily Lovell, with support of Event Coordinator Yelena Martynovskaya."
- Advice: a "CROSS Advisory Board consisting of UCSC-appointed open source software and entrepreneurship experts": Tom Callaway, Doug Cutting, Nithya Ruff, Karen Sandler, Nissa Strottman and Sage Weil.
- Three tasks: "Teach", "Fund" ("high-impact research with plausible path to successful open source projects") and "Incubate" ("developer communities around research prototypes").
- The model diagram: "Members of Industry & Government" and "UC Santa Cruz Faculty and Students" exchange "Talent, Projects, Technologies" through Education, Research and Incubator.
- What industry gets: "Leverage open source software to create and shape new markets"; "Network with talent and potential recruits"; "Influence education, research, and the next generation OSS systems".
- What the campus gets: "Efficiently transfer technology"; "Attract and fund talented graduate students".
- "CROSS transfers cutting-edge technology resulting from academic-based research to industry via successful open source projects."

## Evidence and method

The center's own page, in its own words. The advisory board is "UCSC-appointed", so the campus, not the members, appoints the advisers.

## Limits

- No fees, budget, member count or member list after the founding three.
- "Influence" is the only industry benefit in governance. The page does not say if members vote or have seats.
- The founding facts are from 2015. The page does not say which members fund CROSS today.
