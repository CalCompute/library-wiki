# Federal Reserve Beige Book main page (web)

## Citation

Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System. "Beige Book." Web page, last update October 14, 2026. https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/publications/beige-book-default.htm. Copy: [`raw/web/2026-11-11 Federal Reserve Beige Book main page.md`](../../raw/web/2026-11-11%20Federal%20Reserve%20Beige%20Book%20main%20page.md) (original: `.html` beside it). Read 2026-11-11.

## Bottom line

- The page is the index of the Beige Book, the Federal Reserve's "Summary of Commentary on Current Economic Conditions by Federal Reserve District".
- It lists seven 2026 reports, from January 14 to October 14, with HTML and PDF links. The next date it gives is November 25.

## Background

The Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System publishes this page. Each Federal Reserve Bank writes a report for its District, and one Bank writes the overall summary. The page links to an archive and to [Federal Reserve Beige Book FAQs (web)](Federal%20Reserve%20Beige%20Book%20FAQs%20%28web%29.md).

## Key claims

- "Commonly known as the Beige Book, this report is published eight times per year." (Summary of Commentary on Current Economic Conditions by Federal Reserve District)
- "Each Federal Reserve Bank gathers anecdotal information on current economic conditions in its District through reports from Bank and Branch directors and interviews with key business contacts, economists, market experts, and other sources." (same section)
- "The Beige Book summarizes this information by District and sector." (same section)
- "An overall summary of the twelve district reports is prepared by a designated Federal Reserve Bank on a rotating basis." (same section)
- The 2026 table lists "January 14", "March 4", "April 15", "June 3", "July 15", "September 2", "October 14" and "November 25". (2026 table)

## Evidence and method

An official index page. It describes the report in four sentences and lists the 2026 issues. The library did not read the reports themselves.

## Limits

The page holds no economic findings. The findings are in the linked reports, which the library has not read. The page does not name the District that covers California.
