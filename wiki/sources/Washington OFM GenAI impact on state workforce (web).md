# Washington OFM GenAI impact on state workforce (web)

## Citation

Washington Office of Financial Management, State Human Resources Division. "Impact of generative artificial intelligence on the Washington state workforce." Report required by Executive Order No. 24-01 section 9, December 2024. https://ofm.wa.gov/sites/default/files/public/publications/Impact_generative_AI_on_state_workforce.pdf. Read 2026-11-11. Rehosted copy: `raw/web/2026-11-11 Washington OFM GenAI impact on state workforce.pdf`.

## Bottom line

- The report assesses the effects of generative AI (GenAI) on Washington's state workforce and proposes strategies and training programs. Governor Inslee's Executive Order No. 24-01 required it.
- It says that Washington had no enterprise data or use case inventory for GenAI, so it relies on workgroup discussions and published research.
- Labor representatives and the State Human Resources Division did not agree on how far labor should take part in decisions to adopt GenAI. The report records both views.

## Background

The Office of Financial Management State Human Resources Division (OFM SHR) wrote the report. Its partners were Washington Technology Solutions (WaTech), the Workforce Training and Education Coordinating Board and labor organizations of state employees. Executive Order No. 24-01 of January 30, 2024, section 9, directed the work. The report covers positive and negative effects, effects on tasks and demographics, change management, workforce planning and skill programs. It describes training programs in other states, California among them.

## Key claims

- "the widespread move nationally and across sectors toward use of this technology means that the question is not whether GenAI should be adopted, but instead what actions Washington state should take to ensure the successful adoption of this technology while mitigating negative effects." (Key takeaways, p. 2)
- "Strategies suggested by labor representatives were to assess GenAI impacts with represented employees before widespread adoption and to require that employees displaced by GenAI receive reclassification or redeployment with no loss of pay or job security. Workgroup consensus was not reached on these strategies." (Key takeaways, p. 4)
- "They advocated for represented employees to have a voting seat at any table where GenAI decisions are being made — from identifying data standards to structuring training programs." (Key takeaways, labor representatives' perspective, p. 4)
- "OFM SHR emphasized its belief that co-determining the employer's use of technology with labor is likely to lead to costly delays and inadequate solutions for service delivery." (Key takeaways, OFM SHR's perspective, p. 5)
- "Adequate time for training during work hours to access skills development programs, with additional support for less tech-savvy employees." (Recommended approaches to GenAI skill development programs, p. 5)
- "Currently, there is a significant lack of available GenAI use cases in the public sector, which complicated the workgroup's efforts to assess its potential effects on the state workforce." (Introduction, p. 6)
- "California has launched a series of GenAI training courses for public employees, developed by the California Department of Human Resources in collaboration with InnovateUS." (Examples of federal and state GenAI training programs, p. 55)

## Evidence and method

A state government report. It draws on workgroup discussions, WaTech's GenAI use cases and a review of published research. It also uses a survey of workgroup members from September 23 to October 4, 2024 (Appendix A). It uses the AI Occupational Exposure (AIOE) index of Felten et al. (2021) for exposure by occupation (Appendix B). It has no Washington data on GenAI use.

## Limits

The report says that it has no enterprise-level data and that the effects are forecasts. Its description of California's training rests on news reports in Government Technology, not on California sources. It does not cover compute infrastructure. It records disagreement between labor and management, not a single position.
