# USITC publication 5531 large power transformers from South Korea 2024-08 (web)

## Citation

U.S. International Trade Commission (USITC). "Large Power Transformers from South Korea." Investigation No. 731-TA-1189 (Second Review). Publication 5531, August 2024. https://www.usitc.gov/publications/701_731/pub5531.pdf. Fetched 2026-11-20. Read 2026-11-20. Copy: [`raw/web/2026-11-20 USITC publication 5531 large power transformers from South Korea 2024-08.md`](../../raw/web/2026-11-20%20USITC%20publication%205531%20large%20power%20transformers%20from%20South%20Korea%202024-08.md) (original: `.pdf` beside it).

## Bottom line

- The USITC keeps the antidumping duty order on large power transformers (LPTs) from South Korea. It finds that revocation would likely lead to material injury to a U.S. industry.
- In 2023, U.S. producers report lead times of about 3.0 years, and importers about 1.5 years. Producers and importers make all shipments to order.
- Firms report that renewable energy, grid projects and the growth of data centers, AI data centers included, push LPT demand up.

## Background

This is the USITC's second five-year review of the antidumping duty order of Aug. 31, 2012 on LPTs from South Korea. The review began on Sept. 1, 2023, and the hearing was on June 20, 2024. The publication holds the determination, the Views of the Commission and a staff report in five parts. The staff report uses questionnaire responses of 9 U.S. producers, 12 importers and 17 purchasers.

## Key claims

- "revocation of the antidumping duty order on large power transformers from South Korea would be likely to lead to continuation or recurrence of material injury to an industry in the United States within a reasonably foreseeable time." (Determination, p. 1)
- The order covers "large liquid dielectric power transformers (LPTs) having a top power handling capacity greater than or equal to 60,000 kilovolt amperes (60 megavolt amperes), whether assembled or unassembled, complete or incomplete." (Commerce's scope, p. I-13)
- "For 2023, U.S. producers reported that 100 percent of their commercial shipments were produced-to-order, with lead times averaging approximately 3.0 years. For 2023, U.S. importers reported that 100 percent of their commercial shipments were produced-to-order, with lead times averaging approximately 1.5 years." (p. II-20)
- "an increase in energy demand due to the growth of data centers has also contributed to the increased demand for LPTs". (p. II-16)
- An importer "reported anticipating heightened future end-use for LPTs in industrial plant applications with a sharp increase in AI data centers." (p. II-12)
- "Apparent U.S. consumption by MVA top rated increased by 19.8 percent from 2021 to 2022 before declining by 1.1 percent in 2023, for a net increase of 18.5 percent during 2021-23. The share of apparent U.S. consumption for which U.S. producers accounted decreased during 2021-23 from 32.5 percent to 29.2 percent." (p. I-38)
- "Apparent U.S. consumption increased by 78.2 percent during 2021-23." (by value, p. I-39)

## Evidence and method

A determination of a federal agency in a five-year review, with its staff report. The evidence is questionnaire responses, a public hearing and the briefs of the parties. The agent read the determination and part of the Views of the Commission. In the staff report, the agent read the parts on demand, lead times and apparent consumption. The publication is about 79,000 words.

## Limits

The public version replaces confidential data with "***", for example the market shares of imports from South Korea and the names of most firms. The report covers the U.S. market and gives no California figures. It names data centers as one cause of demand among others, and gives no number for data center demand. Purchasers report that it takes 45 to 180 days to qualify a new supplier (pp. II-20 to II-21).

The DOE report to Congress on LPTs uses a threshold of 100 megavolt-amperes (MVA). This order uses 60 MVA ([DOE Large Power Transformer Resilience Report 2024-07 (web)](DOE%20Large%20Power%20Transformer%20Resilience%20Report%202024-07%20%28web%29.md)). The DOE report gives lead times of 36 months as common, and up to 60 months. This report gives 2023 averages of about 3.0 years for U.S. producers and 1.5 years for importers. The two documents use different definitions and periods.

The publication does not mention research compute or CalCompute.
