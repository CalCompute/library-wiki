# WEF AI Infrastructure in the Age of Sovereignty 2026 (web)

## Citation

World Economic Forum (WEF), AI Global Alliance, in collaboration with Bain & Company. "AI Infrastructure in the Age of Sovereignty: Requirements, Strategies and a Trusted Framework for Digital Embassies." White paper, World Economic Forum, May 2026. https://reports.weforum.org/docs/WEF_AI_Infrastructure_in_the_Age_of_Sovereignty_Requirements_Strategies_and_a_Trusted_Framework_for_Digital_Embassies_2026.pdf. Read 2026-11-09.

## Bottom line

- The paper divides artificial intelligence (AI) infrastructure into three building blocks: compute, connectivity and data storage. It puts national strategies on a "sovereignty spectrum" from trusted international partnerships to extensive domestic ownership.
- It says most economies use hybrid strategies, and that only China and the US come close to full domestic ownership. It launches a framework for "digital embassies", which host one country's data and compute in another under its own law.

## Background

The WEF AI Global Alliance wrote the paper with the consulting firm Bain & Company. It follows the WEF paper [Rethinking AI Sovereignty 2026](WEF%20Rethinking%20AI%20Sovereignty%202026%20%28web%29.md) in the same series. It writes for national economies, not for states or regions. The WEF says the findings do not necessarily represent its own views.

## Key claims

- "Cumulative investment in AI-dedicated infrastructure exceeded $600 billion between 2010 and 2024 and is projected to surpass $400 billion annually by 2030." (Foreword, p. 3)
- "Yet for most economies, self-sufficiency is unrealistic given the scale, complexity and concentration of AI infrastructure required." (Foreword, p. 3)
- "The AI infrastructure landscape comprises three building blocks, each corresponding to a different state of data" (Executive summary, p. 4)
- "Only a small number of economies – primarily China and the US – can come close to achieving extensive domestic ownership." (Section 2.1, p. 10)
- "The European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking, for example, allocates access to shared supercomputing infrastructure across participating European economies." (Section 2.1, p. 10)
- "In practice, few economies sit at either extreme of the AI sovereignty spectrum. Most adopt hybrid strategies, combining trusted partnerships with foreign countries or commercial entities with selective domestic ownership to balance control, affordability, speed and security." (Section 2.3, p. 16)
- "Singapore anchors sensitive capabilities domestically through its National Supercomputing Centre, which received SGD 270 million (Singaporean dollars) in 2024 to build a next-generation supercomputer." (Section 2.3, p. 16)

## Evidence and method

A white paper from an international membership organisation and a consulting firm. It gives a conceptual framework, two reference strategies and short country cases: Estonia, Monaco, Singapore, China and the European Union. Its figures on investment and cloud market share cite other reports in its endnotes. The digital embassy framework comes from a WEF process with outside stakeholders. The paper gives no primary data of its own.

## Limits

The paper is about national economies. It does not mention California or any subnational state program. It gives no cost model for building a compute facility. Its country cases are short examples, not evaluations. The Markdown copy mixes the two text columns of the PDF, so the quotes above follow the PDF's column order.
