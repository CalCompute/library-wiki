# Berkeley Savio condo cluster service (web)

## Citation

UC Berkeley Research IT. "Condo Cluster Service." Research IT documentation, Berkeley Research Computing (BRC), date not stated. https://docs-research-it.berkeley.edu/services/high-performance-computing/condos/condo-cluster-service/. Read 2026-11-20. Cached copy: `.cache/web/2026-11-20 Berkeley Savio condo cluster service.md`.

## Bottom line

- Principal investigators (PIs) buy most nodes of the Savio cluster at UC Berkeley with their own funds. BRC waives the operating costs, and other users can run jobs on idle nodes.
- A condo owner gets priority access equal to the nodes it bought. The minimum buy-in is four nodes of one type, and nodes have a five-year life.
- The page gives no prices. A buyer must contact BRC for current pricing.

## Background

Berkeley Research Computing runs Savio, the shared high performance computing (HPC) cluster of UC Berkeley. This page describes the condo program: who buys the nodes, how the scheduler shares them, the hardware on offer, storage and the first contributors. The page dates the launch of the BRC program to May 22, 2014.

## Key claims

- "Unlike traditional clusters, Savio is a collaborative system wherein the majority of nodes are purchased and shared by the cluster users, known as condo owners." (Summary)
- "Operating costs for managing and housing PI-owned compute nodes are waived in exchange for letting other users make use of any idle compute cycles on the PI-owned nodes." (Summary)
- "Compute node equipment is purchased and maintained based on a 5-year lifecycle." (Program Details)
- "There is a 4-node minimum buy-in for any given compute pool" (Program Details)
- "Generally, procurement takes about three months from start to finish." (Program Details)
- "We are currently supporting the Blackwell RTX PRO 6000 (4 GPUs) and the H200 (8 GPUs) as a standard node offering for the savio4_gpu partition." (GPU Computing Node)
- "We do not do pre-emptive scheduling where running jobs are killed in order to give immediate access to priority jobs." (Job scheduling)
- "Dedicating or reserving compute resources works counter to sharing, so this is not possible in the Condo model." (Job scheduling)
- A Private Pool is the alternative, but "researchers will have to cover the support costs for BRC staff to manage their compute nodes." (Job scheduling)

## Evidence and method

A program documentation page of about 2,600 words, most of it site navigation. It states the rules of the condo program and the specifications of four node types. It lists 35 charter contributors by name and department, two of them from UC San Francisco (UCSF). It gives no prices, no count of condo owners today and no figures on how much idle time other users get.

## Limits

The page does not give a node price or the Private Pool rates. It does not say who pays the waived operating costs. The storage section gives 200 GB of project space for condo accounts and 50 GB for Faculty Computing Allowance accounts. For that allowance, see [Berkeley Savio Faculty Computing Allowance (web)](Berkeley%20Savio%20Faculty%20Computing%20Allowance%20%28web%29.md).

> ⚠️ Contradiction: this page says the standard H200 node has "8 GPUs". The hardware table lists one savio4_gpu node with "4x H200" ([Berkeley Savio hardware configuration (web)](Berkeley%20Savio%20hardware%20configuration%20%28web%29.md)).
