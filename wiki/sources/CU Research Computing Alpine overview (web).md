# CU Research Computing Alpine overview (web)

## Citation

University of Colorado Boulder Research Computing (CURC). "Alpine." CURC user documentation, date not stated. https://curc.readthedocs.io/en/latest/clusters/alpine/index.html. Read 2026-11-18.

## Bottom line

- Alpine is the third-generation high-performance computing (HPC) cluster of CU Boulder Research Computing. It has 489 compute nodes and 32,368 CPU cores, with GPU options.
- Three institutions supply its hardware. Those institutions and the National Science Foundation (NSF) fund it jointly. All nodes are open to all users.

## Background

CURC runs research computing at the University of Colorado Boulder. This is the opening page of the Alpine section in its user documentation. It gives the release history and a short overview. [CU Research Computing Alpine allocations (web)](CU%20Research%20Computing%20Alpine%20allocations%20%28web%29.md) gives the allocation rules.

## Key claims

- "On May 18, 2022, CU Research Computing released phase 1 of Alpine, the third generation CURC High-Performance Computing Cluster. Phase 2 was released in September 2022. Phase 3 was released in July 2023." (Alpine)
- "Alpine is a heterogeneous compute cluster currently composed of hardware provided from University of Colorado Boulder, Colorado State University, and Anschutz Medical Campus." (Overview)
- "Alpine currently offers 489 compute nodes, a total of 32,368 CPU cores, and numerous GPU computing options." (Overview)
- "Alpine can be securely accessed anywhere, anytime using Open OnDemand or ssh connectivity to the CURC system. All nodes are available to all users." (Overview)
- "Alpine is jointly funded by the University of Colorado Boulder, the University of Colorado Anschutz, Colorado State University, and the National Science Foundation (award 2201538)." (page footer)

## Evidence and method

A short overview page in user documentation. It gives the release dates, the node and core counts, the access routes and the funders. The section's table of contents is a list of links to other pages.

## Limits

- The page has no date. The node and core counts are "currently" values.
- It does not give the number or type of graphics processing units (GPUs), the cost of the cluster or each funder's share.
- It does not give the size of the NSF award.
- It says that all nodes are available to all users, but the allocation page sets priority by tier and fairshare.
