## Bottom line

- CIRM funds regenerative medicine research in California. This FAQ says which project costs its awards pay for, and how applicants meet its co-funding rule.
- Costs count only after the Independent Citizens' Oversight Committee (ICOC) approves the award. Work by an out-of-state organization that keeps intellectual property (IP) or independent publication rights is not an allowable cost.
- A for-profit applicant can meet the co-funding rule with cash, with equity warrants issued to CIRM, or with both.

## Background

CIRM publishes this FAQ for applicants and awardees. It has two parts: allowable project costs, and co-funding. It points to CIRM's Award Management Policy for the full cost rules. Its definitions of "California Organization" and "Partner" are in [CIRM common requirements and definitions memo (web)](CIRM%20common%20requirements%20and%20definitions%20memo%20%28web%29.md).

## Key claims

- Purpose: "CIRM was created to benefit California researchers and organizations. Non-California organizations may only apply to the CLIN2: Clinical Trial Stage Projects, due to the nature of the projects and related activities." (Non-California Organization FAQs, question 1)
- Out-of-state work: "Allowable project costs do NOT include the costs of activities performed by a separate out-of-state organization that retains intellectual property or independent publication rights in any intellectual property (e.g., invention, technology, data) arising out of the CIRM-Funded Project." (Unallowable Costs, item b)
- Waiver of rights: "If the 3rd party waives both its IP and independent publication rights, then CIRM can fund such work." (question 18)
- Start of costs: "Allowable project costs are costs that are incurred after ICOC-approval." (question 8)
- Co-funding and costs: "CIRM considers allowable project costs to be those costs covered by CIRM funding as well as the Applicant’s Co-Funding (when required)." (question 7)
- Non-profit applicants: "If the Non-Profit Applicant does not have a For-Profit Partner for the proposed project at the time of Application submission, the minimum Co-Funding requirement does not apply." (Non-Profit Co-Funding Requirements, question 1)
- Warrants: "you may elect to provide Co-Funding with cash-only, Warrant-only, or a combination of cash and Warrants." (Warrant-Based Co-Funding Requirement, question 1)
- Warrant terms: "Is the Warrant Term Sheet negotiable? No." (Warrant-Based Co-Funding Requirement, question 7)
- Example: in the CLIN2 case, the award limit is "$15M" and co-funding is "50% of total allowable project costs". A "$12M" project gets "CIRM Award: $6M" with cash co-funding. With warrants, it gets "CIRM Award: $12M" (Warrant-Based Co-Funding Requirement, question 3, Example 1).

## Evidence and method

This is a primary document from CIRM's cirm.ca.gov website: a set of questions and answers with worked scenarios. It applies the U.S. Generally Accepted Accounting Principles (GAAP) accrual basis to say when a cost is "incurred". It gives four numbered examples of cash and warrant co-funding for a phase 2 trial. It gives no data on past awards.

## Limits

- The FAQ does not give the full cost policy. It refers to the Award Management Policy, which the library has not read.
- It does not give co-funding percentages for programs other than the CLIN2 example.
- It does not say how many awards use warrants, or what CIRM has received from them.
- It is about regenerative medicine grants, not compute. It names "data sharing/management" as a cost type, but it gives no rule for computing costs.
