# Stanford HAI National Research Cloud blueprint 2021 (web)

## Citation

Ho, Daniel E., Jennifer King, Russell C. Wald and Christopher Wan. "Building a National AI Research Resource: A Blueprint for the National Research Cloud." White paper, Stanford Institute for Human-Centered Artificial Intelligence (HAI), October 2021, V1.0. https://hai-production.s3.amazonaws.com/files/2021-11/Building-national-ai-research-resource.pdf. Read 2026-11-11.

## Bottom line

- The white paper designs a National Research Cloud (NRC) for academic and nonprofit AI research. Congress named this resource the National AI Research Resource (NAIRR).
- On compute, it recommends a "dual investment strategy": negotiated commercial cloud first, and a pilot of public infrastructure to test ownership in the long run.
- On structure, it recommends a Federally Funded Research and Development Center (FFRDC) in the short run and a public-private partnership (PPP) in the long run.

## Background

Stanford HAI commissioned the paper. A Stanford Law School Policy Lab team of students, staff and faculty wrote it over six months. The paper says HAI "originated the proposal for the NRC in partnership with 21 other research universities" (p. 10). The federal task force later wrote its own plan ([NAIRR Task Force final report 2023 (web)](NAIRR%20Task%20Force%20final%20report%202023%20%28web%29.md)). The 2023 announcement of California's SB 294 says CalCompute builds on Stanford's NRC proposal ([Wiener SB 294 announcement 2023-09-13](Wiener%20SB%20294%20announcement%202023-09-13.md)).

## Key claims

- "Computing power has become critical for the advancement of AI, but the high cost of compute has placed cutting-edge AI research in a position accessible only to key industry players and a handful of elite universities." (Executive Summary, p. 9)
- "In 2011, AI Ph.D.s were roughly as likely to go into industry as academia. Ten years later, two-thirds of AI Ph.D.s go into industry, and less than one quarter go into academia." (Executive Summary, p. 9)
- "The main policy choice will be whether to build public computing infrastructure or purchase services from existing commercial cloud providers." (Compute Model, p. 11)
- "It is well-established that, based solely on hardware costs, it is more cost-effective to own infrastructure when computing demand is close to continuous." (Compute Model, p. 11)
- "First, the compute model of the NRC can be quickly launched by subsidizing and negotiating cloud computing for AI researchers with existing vendors, expanding on existing initiatives like the NSF's CloudBank project." (Compute Model, p. 11)
- "Second, the NRC should invest in a pilot for public infrastructure to assess the ability to provide similar resources in the long run." (Compute Model, p. 11)
- "We recommend that the NRC eligibility start with academics who hold "Principal Investigator" (PI) status (i.e., most faculty) at U.S. colleges and universities, as well as "Affiliated Government Agencies" willing to contribute previously unreleased, high-value datasets to the NRC in return for subsidized compute resources." (Researcher Eligibility, p. 12)
- "We recommend a tiered access model: By default, researchers will gain access to government data that is already public; researchers can then apply through a streamlined process to gain access at higher security levels on a project-specific basis." (Data Access Model, p. 12)
- "We ultimately recommend that the NRC be instituted as a Federally Funded Research and Development Center (FFRDC) in the short run, and a public-private partnership (PPP) in the long run." (Organizational Form, p. 13)
- "In the long run, we recommend the creation of a PPP model, governed by officers from Affiliated Government Agencies, academic researchers, and representatives from the technology sector, which can house both compute and data resources." (Organizational Form, p. 13)

## Evidence and method

A policy white paper. The team "studied existing models for compute resources and government data, interviewed a wide range of government, computer science, and policy experts, and examined the technical, business, legal, and policy requirements" (p. 10). For this page the Executive Summary (pp. 9 to 13) was read.

## Limits

- The paper is of October 2021, before the NAIRR pilot.
- It reviews only academic and nonprofit use. It says that "some have argued the NRC should be open for commercial access" (p. 12).
- It plans a federal resource. It does not discuss a state program.
