# UC Protection Level Classification Guide (web)

## Citation

University of California Office of the President (UCOP), ITS, Systemwide CISO Office. "UC Protection Level Classification Guide: Protection Levels for Institutional Information and IT Resources." IT Security Committee Item SC-0003. PDF, 8 pages, last revision July 18, 2022. https://security.ucop.edu/files/documents/uc-protection-level-classification-guide.pdf. Read 2026-11-12.

## Bottom line

- The guide pre-classifies common types of UC information in four Protection Levels, P4 (highest) to P1 (lowest), with a reason for each.
- P4 includes Controlled Unclassified Information (CUI), protected health information and human subject research data with identifiers. It also includes research that an Institutional Review Board (IRB) puts at P4.
- Published research is P1.

## Background

UCOP's Systemwide CISO Office wrote the guide to meet UC's Institutional Information and IT Resource Classification Standard. Its revision history runs from Aug. 16, 2017, to July 18, 2022, and records approval by the IT Leadership Council (ITLC). The summary page is [UC Classification of Information and IT Resources (web)](UC%20Classification%20of%20Information%20and%20IT%20Resources%20%28web%29.md).

## Key claims

- Four levels: "all UC Institutional Information and IT Resources must be assigned one of four Protection Levels based on confidentiality and integrity requirements, with Protection Level 4 (P4) requiring the highest level of protection and Protection Level 1 (P1) requiring the lowest level of protection." (p. 2)
- Who decides: "Institutional Information Proprietors, with the support of their Subject Matter Experts (SMEs) and Unit Information Security Leads (UISLs), are responsible for determining the Protection Level for Institutional Information and IT Resources under their area of responsibility." (p. 2)
- Cost of over-classifying: "Over-classification may result in additional cost and compliance requirements, while under-classification may result in inadequate protections that could lead to data breaches." (p. 2)
- P4 research data: "Research information classified as P4 by an Institutional Review Board (IRB) or otherwise required to be stored or processed in a high-security environment." (P4 table, p. 3)
- Export control: "EAR can be P4 or P3. Contact the Export Control Office for a determination." (P4 table, p. 3)
- P2 research: "Research using publicly available data." and "Unpublished research work, drafts of research papers and intellectual property not classified as P3 or P4." (P2 table, p. 5)
- Export-controlled data location: "Current law requires that this data be stored in the U.S. and that only authorized U.S. persons be allowed access to it." (Key Terminology, p. 6)
- CUI: "IS-3 and supporting standards lay the foundation for protecting CUI. See NIST Special Publication 800-171 for requirements." (Key Terminology, p. 6)

## Evidence and method

This is a university system guide, in tables of information types with a justification column, and a glossary. The Markdown copy separates each table's type column from its justification column, so check the PDF before you pair a type with its reason. It gives classifications, not data.

## Limits

- The guide does not give the security controls for each level. The classification standard and IS-3 hold them.
- It names NIST SP 800-171 Revision 2. It does not mention Revision 3.
- It does not classify AI models, model outputs or compute logs.
