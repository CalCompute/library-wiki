# Choose NJ NVIDIA supercomputer agreement (web)

## Citation

Choose New Jersey. "New Jersey signs agreement with NVIDIA to create supercomputer." Reprint of a New Jersey Business Magazine story, January 21, 2026. https://choosenj.com/news/new-jersey-signs-agreement-with-nvidia-to-create-supercomputer/. Read 2026-11-09.

## Bottom line

- In January 2026 New Jersey, its higher education institutions, NVIDIA and the NJ AI HUB signed a memorandum of understanding (MOU) for a statewide supercomputer.
- The state investment is "approximately $25 million", which the New Jersey Economic Development Authority (NJEDA) manages.

## Background

Choose New Jersey is "a private non-profit economic development agency for New Jersey". This page reprints part of a New Jersey Business Magazine story and links the full story. The story reports a press conference at the NJEDA's Newark offices with Gov. Phil Murphy.

## Key claims

- "the state, higher education institutions, NVIDIA and the NJ AI HUB signed a memorandum of understanding (MOU) for the creation of a supercomputer" (first paragraph)
- "The investment is approximately $25 million that the NJEDA will manage and deploy." (fourth paragraph)
- "The money will be used to support a statewide supercomputer initiative for New Jersey’s institutions of higher learning." (Murphy, fourth paragraph)
- "The development of the NJ supercomputer initiative presents an opportunity for higher education, government, and industry to partner on a flagship, statewide approach to advancing shared AI infrastructure, education, and research capacity." (Teik Lim, President, New Jersey Institute of Technology)

## Evidence and method

A news reprint with quotations from the press conference. It gives one dollar figure and no hardware details. It is secondary: the primary record of the initiative is the Governor's release.

## Limits

The page does not give the size of the supercomputer, its operator or who can use it. The later [NJ Governor release 2026-10-20 (web)](NJ%20Governor%20release%202026-10-20%20%28web%29.md) gives a total of "more than $125 million", of which the state's NJEDA share is $25 million.
