# DOE Genesis Mission Collaboration page (web)

## Citation

U.S. Department of Energy (DOE), Office of the Under Secretary for Science. "Genesis Mission Collaboration." Web page, last updated Nov. 10, 2026. https://www.energy.gov/undersecretaryforscience/genesis-mission/genesis-mission-collaboration. Read 2026-11-18.

## Bottom line

- The page says that the 17 DOE National Laboratories organize their Genesis Mission work in five teams: data, infrastructure, models, national challenges, and public-private partnerships.
- It describes the Genesis Mission Consortium as a link between outside organizations and DOE and the National Laboratories. It lists five working groups and gives a DOE email address for organizations.

## Background

DOE runs the Genesis Mission under Executive Order 14363 ([Executive Order 14363 Launching the Genesis Mission (web)](Executive%20Order%2014363%20Launching%20the%20Genesis%20Mission%20%28web%29.md)). This page on the DOE website is about collaboration with the Genesis Mission. Each National Laboratory team has a link to a team fact sheet. The page names Under Secretary for Science Darío Gil as the DOE director for the Genesis Mission. The page uses "SI" and does not spell it out. The Genesis Mission Consortium FAQ gives "super intelligence (SI)" ([Genesis Mission Consortium FAQ (web)](Genesis%20Mission%20Consortium%20FAQ%20%28web%29.md)).

## Key claims

- "The Genesis Mission unites DOE’s National Labs, the National Nuclear Security Administration (NNSA), industry, academia, and other experts to harness SI for breakthroughs in science, energy, national security, health, space, and more." (page heading)
- "To support the Genesis Mission, the Labs’ expertise and resources are organized around teams that form the foundation of the world’s most powerful scientific platform." (National Laboratories)
- "The infrastructure team integrates advanced computing, cloud technologies, and DOE and NNSA research facilities to establish the infrastructure for the Genesis Mission Platform." (National Laboratories, Infrastructure)
- "The public-private partnerships team connects U.S. government resources with industry and academia to accelerate the productivity and impact of American science and engineering." (National Laboratories, Public-Private Partnerships)
- "The Genesis Mission Consortium unites the nation’s leading science and technology experts to solve critical challenges at unprecedented speed by connecting leading industry, academic, nonprofit, and philanthropic organizations with DOE and the National Laboratories and their resources." (The Genesis Mission Consortium)
- The working groups are "SI Model Development and Validation", "Data Integration and Standards", "High-Performance Computing and Cloud Infrastructure", "Robotics and Automation" and "Workforce Development and Training". (The Genesis Mission Consortium)
- "Organizations are encouraged to reach out to DOE at genesismission-partnerships@hq.doe.gov." (How Do I Get Involved?)

## Evidence and method

A federal program page. It gives one sentence for each National Laboratory team and links to five team fact sheets. It lists the working groups of the Genesis Mission Consortium and links to the consortium website. It gives no figures, no member list and no dates other than the update dates.

## Limits

- The page does not say how an outside organization gets access to the Genesis Mission Platform or to National Laboratory computing.
- It does not give a budget, member count or cost of membership.
- The library has read only the fact sheet of the public-private partnerships team: [DOE Genesis Mission P3 team fact sheet (web)](DOE%20Genesis%20Mission%20P3%20team%20fact%20sheet%20%28web%29.md). The other four team fact sheets are not in the library.
- The working group list has five groups. The launch release of Feb. 9, 2026 lists four and does not name workforce development ([DOE Genesis Mission Consortium launch release 2026-02-09 (web)](DOE%20Genesis%20Mission%20Consortium%20launch%20release%202026-02-09%20%28web%29.md)).
