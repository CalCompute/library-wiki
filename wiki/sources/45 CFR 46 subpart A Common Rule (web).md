## Bottom line

- Subpart A, the "Common Rule", applies to research with human subjects that a federal department or agency conducts, supports or regulates.
- An institutional review board (IRB) reviews and approves this research. The rule sets IRB membership, approval criteria, informed consent and records.
- A U.S. institution in cooperative research must rely on a single IRB for the U.S. part of the work, with some exceptions.

## Background

The Common Rule is the federal policy for the protection of human research subjects. HHS codifies it at 45 CFR part 46, subpart A, and the eCFR shows the current text. The 2017 revision is the source of most sections. The rule defines "research" and "human subject", and § 46.104 lists the kinds of research that are exempt. The copy is the eCFR rendering of the whole subpart, from § 46.101 to § 46.124.

## Key claims

- "this policy applies to all research involving human subjects conducted, supported, or otherwise subject to regulation by any Federal department or agency that takes appropriate administrative action to make the policy applicable to such research." (§ 46.101(a))
- "*Research* means a systematic investigation, including research development, testing, and evaluation, designed to develop or contribute to generalizable knowledge." (§ 46.102(l))
- "*Human subject* means a living individual about whom an investigator (whether professional or student) conducting research: (i) Obtains information or biospecimens through intervention or interaction with the individual, and uses, studies, or analyzes the information or biospecimens; or (ii) Obtains, uses, studies, analyzes, or generates identifiable private information or identifiable biospecimens." (§ 46.102(e)(1))
- "Each IRB shall have at least five members, with varying backgrounds to promote complete and adequate review of research activities commonly conducted by the institution." (§ 46.107(a))
- "Any institution located in the United States that is engaged in cooperative research must rely upon approval by a single IRB for that portion of the research that is conducted in the United States." (§ 46.114(b)(1))
- "An IRB shall conduct continuing review of research requiring review by the convened IRB at intervals appropriate to the degree of risk, not less than once per year" (§ 46.109(e))

## Evidence and method

A federal regulation, as the eCFR prints it. It states legal duties for institutions, IRBs and investigators. It gives no evidence or data.

## Limits

The copy is subpart A only. The other subparts of part 46 are not in it. The rule does not name artificial intelligence or computing services. It says nothing about who pays for IRB review. Each federal agency adopts the policy by its own action, and the copy does not list which agencies have done so.
