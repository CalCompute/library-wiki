## Bottom line

- This is the only legislative source with a cost figure for the consortium. The Government Operations Agency (GovOps) estimated "$2.5 million" for contractors, planning, staff, research and the Report.
- It says operating CalCompute in the University of California (UC) brings "unknown but potentially significant" costs, which private donations can partly offset.
- Its summaries describe CalCompute more broadly than the statute: "to create a public computing cluster" and "to support AI research and safety testing".

## Background

The Assembly Floor analysis goes to members before the final Assembly vote. This one covers SB 53 as amended September 5, 2025, the version the Governor signed. Its "Fiscal Comments" come from the Assembly Appropriations Committee.

## Key claims

- **Summary.** The bill "Provides, upon appropriation, for the creation of a framework to create a public cloud computing cluster."
- **Digest item 5.**
  > 5) Upon appropriation, establishes in the Government Operations Agency a consortium required to develop a framework for the creation of a public cloud computing cluster to be known as ″CalCompute″ that advances the development and deployment of AI, as prescribed.
- **Comments.** The bill "establishes a consortium within the Government Operations Agency to create a public computing cluster, known as CalCompute, to support AI research and safety testing."
- **According to the Author, the working group report.** The author calls the Governor's working group "a group of top experts tasked with charting a course forward on AI policy for the developers of the most advanced AI systems."
  > Their final report, released in June 2025, emphasized the growing evidence for risk of severe harm, such as ″AI-enabled hacking or biological attacks, and loss of control″ and argued ″California has a unique opportunity to continue supporting developments in frontier AI while addressing substantial risks that could have far-reaching consequences for the state and beyond.″
- **According to the Author.** SB 53 "establishes a consortium to help create CalCompute: a public AI research cluster that will provide startups and researchers with access to the resources needed to develop large-scale AI systems."
  > Finally, SB 53 would also set in motion CalCompute, a public cloud computing cluster for use by academics and startups in California. [...] This mirrors a similar computing cluster that is already being established in New York state.
- **Fiscal Comments 2), cost of the consortium.**
  > 2)  Costs (General Fund) to GovOps to establish and operate the CalCompute consortium until January 1, 2027, possibly in the high hundreds of thousands of dollars to low millions of dollars.  GovOps estimates total costs of $2.5 million for expert contractors, infrastructure planning, and staffing to manage the project, conduct research, and develop the required report.
The committee adds that GovOps gave no breakdown and expected contract workers "due to the short timeframe in the bill". Member expense reimbursement "may be in the thousands to low tens of thousands of dollars".
- **Fiscal Comments 3), cost to UC.**
  > 3)  Possible cost pressures (General Fund) of an unknown but potentially significant amount to the UC to operate CalCompute, should it be established within the UC.  State costs may be [...] offset to some extent by private donations, which the bill authorizes the UC to receive to implement CalCompute.

## Evidence and method

The analysis summarizes the bill and reports positions. The fiscal figure is the agency's own estimate, given without a breakdown. The committee's range ("high hundreds of thousands" to "low millions") is its own judgment.

## Limits

- The cost estimate is for producing the Report, not for building or running CalCompute. It gives no figure for the cluster.
- The words "Upon appropriation" shorten subd. (k).
- The author's statement quotes the working group report.
- The fiscal comment assumes a state-run consortium.

> ⚠️ Contradiction: The analysis says the consortium is "to create a public computing cluster, known as CalCompute, to support AI research and safety testing". § 11546.8 (a) says the consortium "shall develop [...] a framework for the creation of" CalCompute, and the statute does not mention safety testing ([SB-53 Gov Code 11546.8](SB-53%20Gov%20Code%2011546.8.md)). The statute governs.
