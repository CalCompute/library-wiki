# TACC LCCF Horizon allocations (web)

## Citation

Texas Advanced Computing Center (TACC), The University of Texas at Austin. "LCCF Allocations." NSF Leadership-Class Computing Facility (LCCF) web page, date not stated. https://lccf.tacc.utexas.edu/allocations/. Read 2026-11-18.

## Bottom line

- Horizon is the National Science Foundation (NSF) leadership-class computer at TACC. Its early operations period has one allocation track, the Leadership Resource Allocation (LRAC), of 125,000 to 500,000 Service Units (SUs) for six months.
- Any U.S. researcher in open research can apply through a principal investigator (PI). Eligible PIs include K-12 teachers, museums and U.S. commercial organizations. A peer review panel recommends awards, and NSF gives final approval.

## Background

TACC is a research computing center at The University of Texas at Austin. NSF funds Horizon, and NSF Award #2323116 supports construction of the LCCF. The page gives the eligibility rules, the review criteria, the proposal format and the charging rules for Horizon. Horizon is also a resource in the NAIRR Pilot's Deep Partnership Opportunity. [TACC user policies (web)](TACC%20user%20policies%20%28web%29.md) gives the TACC Acceptable Use Policy for all TACC systems.

## Key claims

- Scale: "Horizon offers an order of magnitude performance improvement over the previous Frontera system." (Getting an Allocation on Horizon Early Operations Period)
- Hardware: "Horizon provides a mix of CPU and GPU computing resources, including 4,750 Dell/NVIDIA Vera CPU nodes, and 2,000 Dell/NVIDIA Grace-Blackwell nodes." (Horizon Allocation Submission Guidelines)
- Eligibility: "Horizon is broadly available to any U.S. researcher working in open research. By “open”, the expectation is that any research performed on Horizon will result in publication in a broadly available journal or conference." (Eligibility)
- Industry: "U.S. commercial organizations, especially small businesses with strong capabilities in scientific or engineering research or education may apply for an allocation." Their projects must "submit their work in an open forum, and make the results of this work readily available to the public." (Eligibility)
- Charging: "A Horizon SU is defined as one wall-clock hour on a Grace-Blackwell (GB200) node, consisting of one 72-core CPU and two Blackwell GPUs." CPU nodes are "charged at one-fourth the GPU node rate (One SU = 4 CPU node hours)". (How is my time on Horizon charged?)
- Award: "Potential awardees will submit an allocation proposal to a peer review panel of independent experts that the Horizon team will invite and organize. Recommendations for award will be sent to NSF for their review and consensus agreement." (Mechanism of Award)
- Size: "LRAC requests can be submitted for up to 500k SUs for the early operations period for Horizon. Note that this represents nearly 5% of the full system annual capacity" (Allocation Size Limits)
- Other resources: "All PIs must describe their local resources and other non-Horizon resources available to them, including both local and other national resources." (Disclosure of Access to Other Compute Resources)
- One project: "An individual may be a PI on only one active Horizon allocation request/project at a given time." The rule also applies to Horizon allocations through the NAIRR program. (One Project Per PI)

## Evidence and method

An allocation guide for applicants. It gives rules and dates, not data on past awards. Its main points:

- Submissions ran from April 15, 2026, to June 15, 2026, with rolling reviews from May 15. Applicants submit through the Resource Allocation System (XRAS).
- The GPU portion opens in summer 2026. The CPU portion opens at the end of 2026 or early 2027, "depending on processor delivery schedules".
- Review criteria: intellectual merit, methodology, research plan, efficient use of resources and "Demonstrated need for a large scale resource". Requests with merit-reviewed grants "will not be subject to further scientific review by the LRAC".
- After merit review, the project team checks technical feasibility and the time available. Lower ranked proposals "will be reduced significantly".
- Reviewers do not review requests from their home institution, former students, advisors or recent collaborators. Reviews stay confidential.
- LRAC requests have a limit of 10 pages. Later tracks are to include start-up, classroom and community requests.

## Limits

- The page does not say whether Horizon allocations cost the user anything. It gives no price for an SU.
- It does not list past awards or say how many requests TACC received.
- It names the review body in three ways: "the resource allocation board", "the project advisory board" and "the Advisory Committee".
- It gives the Vista limit in two ways. One section says "up to 50,000 (Vista) for six months duration". Another says "up to 50K SUs per year".
- The page spells "Principle Investigators" for principal investigators. The "Submit Via XRAS" section has the text "Sentence about submitting here instead. Why would they?", which looks like a draft note left on the page.
- The PI rules here are wider than the PI rules in [TACC user policies (web)](TACC%20user%20policies%20%28web%29.md). That policy asks for a doctorate, evidence of work in the field or employment at UT Austin. This page also lets K-12 teachers and NSF Graduate Student Fellows be PIs. Neither page explains how the two rules fit together.
