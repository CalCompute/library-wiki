# CSUCI students promote HPC 2024 (web)

## Citation

California State University Channel Islands (CSUCI). "CSUCI students promote cutting-edge High-Performance Computing with national competitions and a campus club." CSUCI news release, December 19, 2024. https://www.csuci.edu/news/releases/2024-students-promote-hpc.htm. Fetched 2026-11-20; read 2026-11-20. ## Bottom line

- CSUCI students started an HPC club after a CSUCI team won the 2023 HPC Winter Classic Invitational Cluster Competition. Six students went to the SC24 conference in November 2024.
- The Texas Advanced Computing Center (TACC) gave CSUCI computing equipment. The club builds a cluster of more than 3,000 CPU cores from it, which students install and manage.

## Background

This is a campus news release. It describes student work on high-performance computing (HPC) at CSUCI, a California State University (CSU) campus in Camarillo. It quotes a computer science professor and two students.

## Key claims

Competition and club (middle of release):

- "Enthusiasm about HPC began about a year ago after CSUCI won the 2023 HPC Winter Classic Invitational Cluster Competition."
- "Then, in late November, Feister led a group of six students, including Berkdemir, to the International Conference for High Performance Computing, Networking, Storage, and Analysis in Atlanta, Georgia - the SC24 conference."

The donated cluster (closing paragraphs):

- "Thanks to a large donation of computing equipment to CSUCI from the Texas Advanced Computing Center (TACC, a world leader in HPC), Berkdemir said the club is also working on building a high-performance computer cluster at CSUCI."
- "This donated computer cluster will use more than 3,000 central processing unit (CPU)-cores - which is essentially the brain of a computer - all at once, with performance thousands of times higher than a student laptop."
- "It will be used, installed and managed by the students."

Demand for skills (opening paragraphs):

- "Job growth for HPC is expected to be significantly faster than the average for all occupations."
