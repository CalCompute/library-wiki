# Governor California 4th largest economy 2025-04-23 (web)

## Citation

Office of Governor Gavin Newsom. "California is now the 4th largest economy in the world." Press release, Sacramento, April 23, 2025. https://www.gov.ca.gov/2025/04/23/california-is-now-the-4th-largest-economy-in-the-world/. Read 2026-10-20.

## Bottom line

- From April 23, 2025, the Governor's office calls California the world's fourth-largest economy. It uses International Monetary Fund (IMF) and Bureau of Economic Analysis (BEA) data.
- California's nominal gross domestic product (GDP) was "$4.1 trillion" for 2024. Japan's was "$4.02 trillion".
- The release does not mention CalCompute or AI.

## Background

Before this release, the Governor's office called California the fifth-largest economy ([Governor ICYMI 32 of top 50 AI companies 2025-03-12 (web)](Governor%20ICYMI%2032%20of%20top%2050%20AI%20companies%202025-03-12%20%28web%29.md)). This release corrects that figure. The [Governor AI working group release 2025-03-18](Governor%20AI%20working%20group%20release%202025-03-18.md) is from the same period.

## Key claims

- "California has officially overtaken Japan to become the world's fourth-largest economy, according to newly released data from the International Monetary Fund (IMF) and the U.S. Bureau of Economic Analysis (BEA)."
- "According to the IMF's 2024 World Economic Outlook data released yesterday, and BEA data California's nominal GDP reached $4.1 trillion, surpassing Japan's $4.02 trillion, and placing California behind only the United States, China, and Germany in global rankings."
- "California's GDP figure is based on the latest state-level GDP data from the BEA."
- "In 2024, California's growth rate of 6% outpaced the top three economies: U.S. (5.3%), China (2.6%) and Germany (2.9%)."
- "average nominal GDP growth of 7.5% from 2021 to 2024."
- "Preliminary data indicates India is projected to surpass California by 2026."
- California is "the nation's top state for new business starts, access to venture capital funding, and manufacturing, high-tech, and agriculture."
- California has "over 36,000 manufacturing firms employing over 1.1 million Californians."

## Evidence and method

A government press release. It names its data sources and links the IMF World Economic Outlook database and the BEA state GDP page. The rank compares a state's BEA GDP with national IMF figures.

## Limits

- No CalCompute or AI content.
- No AI job-posting or venture capital (VC) amounts. The VC claim is a rank only.
- The rank depends on nominal GDP and exchange rates. The release gives no method beyond the two data sources.
