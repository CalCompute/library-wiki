## Bottom line

- The Governor announces a report on what Californians said about AI through Engaged California, the state's digital democracy platform.
- Three themes have the most support. They are accountability of AI companies for harm, protection from AI's resource needs, and rules for safe AI use.

## Background

Engaged California started as a pilot after the Los Angeles fires. Its first statewide topic was AI. The Government Operations Agency (GovOps) and the Office of Data and Innovation (ODI) run it with Carnegie California. The release links the report through a short link and lists the Governor's AI actions since 2023.

## Key claims

- "One message came through clearly: Californians want to be in the loop on AI policy—not just how people use AI, but how companies build it and how government regulates it." (body)
- "Hold AI companies accountable for the harm they cause"; "Protect people and the environment from the resource needs of AI"; "Set rules for where and how AI can be used safely" (What we heard)
- "Making sure that our governments are responsible to our citizens for the transparency regarding building and construction and implementation of data centers would be a helpful policy." (What we heard, a participant)
- "To advance Engaged California, the Government Operations Agency and the California Office of Data and Innovation (ODI) are working in partnership with Carnegie California, the West Coast office and program of the Carnegie Endowment for International Peace" (Partners in innovation)
- The design partners and advisors include "Stanford University’s Deliberative Democracy Lab, UC Berkeley" and "the Kapor Center" (Partners in innovation)
- "California is also establishing an AI Cybersecurity Officer in each agency" (AI, the California way: Cybersecurity)

## Evidence and method

A government press release with political statements. It gives the three themes and three quotations from participants. It gives no number of participants and no method. The full report is behind a short link that this library did not follow.

## Limits

The release does not mention CalCompute or public compute. Its framing of two quotations does not match their content. It introduces a data center quotation with "One participant urged strong protections for workers". It introduces a bias testing quotation with "Others cautioned against overregulation of industry". The library holds the release, not the report.
