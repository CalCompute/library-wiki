# UC Noyce Initiative cybersecurity (web)

## Citation

UC Noyce Initiative. "Cybersecurity & Privacy." Web page in the Research Thematic Areas section, date not stated. The page footer says "Last update: October 14, 2026". https://ucnoyce.org/cybersecurity. Read 2026-11-09.

## Bottom line

- The page describes Cybersecurity & Privacy, one of the three research thematic areas of the University of California (UC) Noyce Initiative. The other two are Computational Health and Quantum Science.
- It lists twelve subtopics, from health data privacy to equity in digital safety, and gives three examples of collaborative projects. It gives no funding amounts and no information about compute resources.

## Background

The UC Noyce Initiative website shows the UC Davis logo. Its footer gives a copyright of "The Regents of the University of California, Davis campus". The page has two parts, "Our Purpose" and "The Opportunity". Three short project summaries follow, each with a link to a news story or a researcher profile.

## Key claims

- "UC Noyce supports researchers and thinkers from all disciplines, career stages and backgrounds who are working to make digital systems more secure, protect personal data and make sure the benefits of digital innovation are shared fairly." (Our Purpose)
- "We aim to support collaborative research that brings together technical, legal, ethical and social perspectives on cybersecurity and privacy." (The Opportunity)
- "protecting power grids, hospitals, water systems and other essential services from cyberattacks" (The Opportunity, Security of networks and critical infrastructure)
- "UC Davis Associate Professor Zubair Shafiq and UC Irvine Professor Athina Markopoulou aim to develop novel auditing tools, using a combination of technological and policy approaches, to evaluate compliance to new and existing data privacy laws by consumers and regulators." (Evaluating Data Privacy Laws)
- "Their work has already gained national media attention and been cited in a Federal Trade Commission lawsuit against Amazon." (Evaluating Data Privacy Laws)

## Evidence and method

A program description on an institutional website. It gives a list of research topics and three project summaries. It gives no data, budgets, award sizes or project dates. The library has not read the linked news stories or researcher profiles.

## Limits

The page is thin. It does not say how the UC Noyce Initiative selects or funds projects. The funding pages for this area are separate, and the library has not read them. The page does not mention compute infrastructure, state government or CalCompute. The "Improving Human-AI Interactions" summary names three professors but not their campuses.
