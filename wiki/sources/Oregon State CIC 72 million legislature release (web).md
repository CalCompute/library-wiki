# Oregon State CIC 72 million legislature release (web)

## Citation

Oregon State University (OSU), News and Research Communications. Steve Lundeberg. "Oregon State’s collaborative innovation project receives $72 million from Oregon Legislature." News release, Corvallis, OR, June 27, 2023. https://news.oregonstate.edu/news/oregon-state%E2%80%99s-collaborative-innovation-project-receives-72-million-oregon-legislature. Read 2026-11-11.

## Bottom line

- The release says that the Oregon Legislature gave final approval to "$72 million in state bonding". The money is for the $213 million Jen-Hsun and Lori Huang Collaborative Innovation Complex (CIC) at OSU.
- The complex is to house a supercomputer "powered by next-generation NVIDIA CPUs, GPUs and networking".
- A $50 million gift from Jen-Hsun and Lori Huang to the OSU Foundation started the project.

## Background

OSU published this release on June 27, 2023, after the legislative vote. It ties the bond money to a state effort to grow the semiconductor industry. It quotes the OSU president, Jayathi Murthy, and the project lead, Belinda Batten. The bond amount is in [Oregon HB 5005 2023 A-Engrossed (web)](Oregon%20HB%205005%202023%20A-Engrossed%20%28web%29.md).

## Key claims

- Bonding: "Oregon State University is on track to break ground in December on the $213 million Jen-Hsun and Lori Huang Collaborative Innovation Complex, following final legislative approval Sunday of $72 million in state bonding from an Oregon Legislature eager to enlarge the state’s semiconductor footprint." (paragraph 1)
- Uses: "The complex to be built on OSU’s Corvallis campus will also harness one of the nation’s most powerful supercomputers and serve as a home for team-based research in artificial intelligence, materials science and robotics" (paragraph 3)
- Matching money: "The Legislature also approved $10 million for OSU and Oregon’s other public universities to use as matching funds when competing for federal grant awards." (paragraph 5)
- State context: "In total, lawmakers steered more than $500 million toward Oregon’s semiconductor industry in the form of grants, loans and tax credits" (paragraph 5)
- Gift: Oregon State announced plans for the complex "in October following a $50 million gift to the OSU Foundation from the Huangs, who are both Oregon State graduates." (paragraph 7)
- Supercomputer: "The supercomputer – powered by next-generation NVIDIA CPUs, GPUs and networking – is expected to be among the world’s fastest university supercomputers, powerful enough to train the largest AI models and perform complex digital twin simulations." (paragraph 8)
- Schedule: "The building, scheduled to open in 2026, will feature mass timber and electrochromic glass as part of the university’s goal to work toward net-zero carbon emissions" (paragraph 10)

## Evidence and method

This is a university news release. It gives the bond amount, the total project cost, the gift and the planned schedule. It gives no specification of the supercomputer and no funding table.

## Limits

- The release does not say how the $213 million divides between the building and the supercomputer.
- It does not say who may use the supercomputer or on what terms.
- It does not give the operating cost.

> ⚠️ Contradiction: this 2023 release says the building is "scheduled to open in 2026". The university's later FAQ says "Construction is scheduled to complete at the end of 2026 with a grand opening event in 2027" ([Oregon State Huang Complex FAQ (web)](Oregon%20State%20Huang%20Complex%20FAQ%20%28web%29.md)).
