# Fast Forward 2025 AI for Humanity Report (web)

## Citation

Fast Forward (Kevin Barenblat and Shannon Farley, co-founders; research by Nicole Dunn and Karly Nelson). "The 2025 AI for Humanity Report." Fast Forward, September 30, 2025. PDF, 18 pages. https://cdn.sanity.io/files/4cwcet86/production/16bc7518437d6a52dc94156c7059c288a0ddebeb.pdf. Read 2026-11-11.

## Bottom line

- A survey report on nearly 200 nonprofits that already use artificial intelligence (AI). It separates "AI-powered" nonprofits (APNs), which build AI into their programs, from "AI-assisted" nonprofits, which use AI in their operations.
- Cost is the top barrier for APNs, and 84% of them say that they need more funding. The report asks funders for "unrestricted, upfront capital".

## Background

Fast Forward is a nonprofit that backs technology nonprofits. Google.org supported the report. Faculty at Indiana University, the University of Minnesota and Wayne State University led the research and analysis. The report has four sections: AI-powered nonprofits, AI-assisted nonprofits, takeaways for nonprofits and takeaways for funders.

## Key claims

- "The social sector often lacks the funding, infrastructure, and tools to keep pace with this transformation." (Introduction, p. 1)
- "The costs of these early efforts are steep: nearly half (48%) of APNs cited cost of implementation as their biggest barrier." (Section 1, p. 3)
- "Nearly half (48%) of APNs report that adopting AI has raised their expenses, and 84% say additional funding is most needed to continue developing and scaling their work." (Section 1, Key Finding 2)
- "Building effective AI requires upfront investment in staff, infrastructure, and validation, long before the benefits show up for beneficiaries." (Section 1, Key Finding 2)
- "Data privacy is a top challenge in adopting AI, cited by 48% of respondents. Another 41% pointed to the lack of in-house technical expertise" (Section 1, Key Finding 4)
- "77% of APNs rely on public datasets, making them the most-used source overall." (Section 1, Emerging Risk)
- "Public data must be treated as essential digital infrastructure: protected, maintained, and expanded" (Section 1, Emerging Risk)
- "For now, nonprofits are leaning on off-the-shelf AI tools (89%) to save time on everyday tasks." (Section 2, Key Finding 3)
- "The biggest unlock you can provide is unrestricted, upfront capital that allows teams to experiment, learn, and build the infrastructure that makes scale possible." (Section 4, p. 15)
- "With funder support, these efforts can grow into shared infrastructure, sector-wide data resources, or open models where one build benefits many." (Section 4, Fuel collaboration, not duplication)

## Evidence and method

A survey report with two case studies (Bayes Impact and Visilant). Fast Forward opened the survey in April 2025 through its network, for two months. The respondents were 34 AI-powered only, 83 AI-assisted only and 73 both. Fast Forward also interviewed 12 nonprofit leaders and six funders. All quantitative findings come from the survey.

Other figures in the report:

- 40% of APNs have used AI for a year or less (Section 1).
- Half of APNs have 10 or fewer employees (Section 1, Key Finding 1).
- 43% of APNs have made their tools fully or partially open (Section 1, Key Finding 6).
- 82% of respondents use AI for internal operations (Section 2).
- 19% of respondents use operations tools that they built "completely in-house" (Section 2).

The funders section opens with a quotation. The page puts the name of Nick Cain, VP, Strategy and Innovation, Patrick J. McGovern Foundation, beside it.

## Limits

The report says that all respondents "were already using AI in some form" and that participation "was voluntary and self-selected". It says the sample "may not be representative of the entire sector". It does not give results for California. Its chart of impact regions gives North America for 54% of respondents. It does not discuss shared computing resources, CalCompute or the Report.
