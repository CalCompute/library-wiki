# NAIRR Pilot proposal instructions

## Citation

U.S. National Science Foundation, National Artificial Intelligence Research Resource (NAIRR) Pilot. "NAIRR Pilot Proposal Instructions." Web page, nairrpilot.org, publication date not stated. https://nairrpilot.org/nairr-pilot-proposal-instructions. Read 2026-10-16.

## Bottom line

- A NAIRR Pilot request is a three-page PDF, a short CV for the lead, and an online form. The form must match the PDF.
- Eligibility is wide: US researchers and educators at academic institutions, nonprofits, federal agencies, "state, local, or tribal agencies", and startups and small businesses with federal grants.

## Background

The NAIRR Pilot is a federal program led by the National Science Foundation (NSF). It matches research projects to compute, models and data from many providers. Its review and matching process is on the [NAIRR Pilot resource allocations (web)](NAIRR%20Pilot%20resource%20allocations%20%28web%29.md) page. This page tells applicants what to submit.

## Key claims

**What to submit**
- A project description "of no more than three (3) pages in PDF format", a CV or biographical sketch of the lead, optional references, and the "online submission form".
- Applicants "must use your institutional email address". Personal addresses such as Gmail "will not be considered".
- A lead may send more than one proposal for distinct projects. Duplicate requests "are not permitted and will not be reviewed".
- Projects run for a "twelve-month period".

**The project description has four parts**
- **B. Resource estimate.** About one page. Estimates "should be based on experience in prior work or benchmark runs". Compute requests give GPU-hours and say "Can this workload execute in a commercial cloud environment?" A reviewer "reading only your three-page PDF" must understand the request. The proposal also explains "how the request might be adjusted" if resources are short.
- **C. Support needs.** Whether provider staff support is "essential, helpful, or unnecessary". Any restrictions: "export-controlled code, ITAR restrictions, proprietary data sets, regional location of compute resources, or personal health information (PHI) or HIPAA restrictions".
- **D. Team.** Qualifications and readiness. Some resources are at federal sites, so applicants list each team member who needs an account, "along with their current citizenship status".

**Review**
- "Proposals for work not supported by a merit-reviewed funding grant are welcome but will undergo additional review regarding the scientific merits of the work."
- In the form, applicants enter only their first-choice resource. They describe alternatives "in the proposal text only".
- Proposals follow "NSF policies for use of AI" in preparation and review.

## Evidence and method

Program instructions on the program's own website. They are a primary source for the program's rules. The page gives no date.

## Limits

- The page says what applicants submit. It does not give the review criteria or the scoring. The [NAIRR Pilot resource allocations (web)](NAIRR%20Pilot%20resource%20allocations%20%28web%29.md) page covers review and matching.
- The rules fit a federal program with federal sites. The citizenship question comes from those sites.
