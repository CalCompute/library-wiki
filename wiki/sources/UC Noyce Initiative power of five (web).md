# UC Noyce Initiative power of five (web)

## Citation

UC Noyce Initiative. "The Power of Five." Web page in The Team section, date not stated. The page footer says "Last update: March 26, 2026". https://ucnoyce.org/the-power-of-five. Read 2026-11-09.

## Bottom line

- The University of California (UC) Noyce Initiative has five campuses: UC Berkeley, UC Davis, UC Irvine, UC San Francisco and UC Santa Barbara.
- It gives one paragraph on each campus. It says that UC Davis puts its emphasis on cybersecurity and that UC Santa Barbara is home to the Ann S. Bowers Women's Brain Health Initiative.

## Background

The page is part of The Team section of the UC Noyce Initiative website, with the Leadership and Researchers pages. An opening paragraph describes the cooperative spirit of the initiative. Each campus paragraph links to the campus homepage.

## Key claims

- "The power and potential of the UC Noyce Initiative lies in its cooperative spirit." (The Power of Five)
- "The campus has strengths in all UC Noyce Initiative research priority areas building on innovative, large-scale, multi-campus programs." (UC Berkeley)
- "Through the UC Noyce Initiative UC Davis' emphasis is in cybersecurity; but the university also has experts in quantum computing, medicine and health-related sciences and artificial intelligence." (UC Davis)
- "Founded in 1965, UC Irvine is the youngest member of the prestigious Association of American Universities" (UC Irvine)
- "UC Santa Barbara is home to the Ann S. Bowers Women's Brain Health Initiative, which is a vital part of the UC Noyce Initiative efforts to advance computational health." (UC Santa Barbara)

## Evidence and method

Promotional campus descriptions on an institutional website. The rankings in the page, such as the UC Irvine and UC San Francisco rankings, have no cited source. The page gives no figures for funding, staff or research output.

## Limits

The page does not say how the five campuses share funds or decisions. It does not say why the initiative includes only these five UC campuses. It gives a campus emphasis for UC Davis and UC Santa Barbara only. It does not mention compute infrastructure, state government or CalCompute.
