# NTEN Bridgespan State of Nonprofit AI 2026 (web)

## Citation

David Figueroa and Jacob Dwelle. "2026 State of Nonprofit AI: Adoption and Governance Report." NTEN and The Bridgespan Group, September 2026. PDF. https://content.nten.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/2026-State-of-Nonprofit-AI.pdf. Read 2026-11-11.

## Bottom line

- A survey of 917 nonprofit professionals on how their organizations adopt and govern artificial intelligence (AI). Most respondents are in the United States.
- AI use is common but shallow. Most executives report no budget for AI. Only 24% of executives report "Sufficient tools or infrastructure to use and deploy AI" in place now.

## Background

NTEN is a community of nonprofit professionals. The Bridgespan Group is a global nonprofit that advises social change leaders. Two alumni of NTEN's Open Source Fellowship designed the survey and wrote the report. The authors hope to repeat the survey each year. The report has nine sections. They include governance and budget, risks, shadow use, training and the outlook for the field.

## Key claims

- "AI use at organizations is widespread, with 98% of respondents reporting that AI is used in some capacity at their organization." (p. 7)
- "Most respondents access AI tools via features already embedded in existing software far more often than through dedicated subscriptions, and many use AI tools without any organizational support." (p. 6, Key findings)
- "The organizational infrastructure and governance needed to support responsible AI adoption are still mostly unbuilt or in development." (p. 25, Key findings)
- "57% of executives report that their organizations have no AI budget in place" (p. 26)
- "Even among organizations with budgets of $10 million or more, nearly half (48%) report having no dedicated AI budget, which suggests this gap isn’t simply a matter of resource scarcity." (p. 27)
- Executives cite "cost or funding constraints the most widely cited of all (52%)" as at least somewhat of a barrier. (p. 32)
- "One quarter (25%) of executives report their organization’s overall technology spending — covering hardware, software, services, and training — to be under $50,000 in 2025" (p. 33)
- "The biggest expected risk isn’t organizational, it’s structural — the widening gap between populations with and without access to AI-enabled tools and services." (p. 58, Key findings)
- "Dedicated funding, coalition partners, and access to technical expertise are what would most enable organizations to support external stewardship." (p. 58, Key findings)

## Evidence and method

A survey report. The survey was open "from late April to mid–June 2026". NTEN and Bridgespan sent it through their lists and partners, and the responses were anonymous. Some questions went only to staff or only to executives. The report splits some results by budget: under $10 million, and $10 million or more.

Executives rated "Sufficient tools or infrastructure to use and deploy AI (e.g., cloud storage, data pipelines, APIs, or computing resources)" (p. 26 and p. 27):

| Group | Not in place | In development | In place now | Not sure |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| All executives | 28% | 40% | 24% | 7% |
| Budget under $10 million | 35% | 37% | 22% | 7% |
| Budget of $10 million or more | 18% | 45% | 28% | 8% |

Other figures in the report:

- 61% of respondents use AI in an official capacity (p. 7).
- 47% of executives expect the gap in access to AI-enabled services to be a risk "to a great extent" (p. 64).
- Of the 752 respondents who gave a location, 82.31% are in the United States (p. 70).

## Limits

The report does not give results for California. It does not discuss shared public computing, CalCompute or the Report. The results are self-reported. On data awareness, the report says the result "reflects self-reported confidence rather than verified understanding" (p. 31).
