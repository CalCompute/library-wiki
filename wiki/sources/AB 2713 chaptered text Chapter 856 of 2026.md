## Bottom line

- AB 2713 amends Business and Professions Code section 22757.3.1, a part of the California AI Transparency Act.
- A large online platform must detect provenance data in the content that it distributes. It must show users if a generative artificial intelligence (GenAI) system or a capture device made the content.
- The platform must let users inspect system provenance data. It must not knowingly strip that data or a digital signature, "to the extent technically feasible".
- The section becomes operative on January 1, 2027.

## Key claims

- "A large online platform shall do all of the following: (1) Detect whether any provenance data is embedded into, attached to, or otherwise associated with content distributed on the large online platform." (Bus. & Prof. Code § 22757.3.1(a)(1))
- "Provide a user interface that reliably indicates whether any system provenance data or digital signature embedded into, attached to, or otherwise associated with content identifies the content as having been generated or substantially altered by a GenAI system or captured by a capture device." (§ 22757.3.1(a)(2)(A))
- "A large online platform shall not, to the extent technically feasible, knowingly strip any system provenance data or digital signature from content uploaded to, distributed on, or downloaded from the large online platform." (§ 22757.3.1(c))
- "This section does not require a large online platform to take any action with respect to provenance data, system provenance data, or digital signatures that are not compliant or interoperable with widely adopted specifications issued by an established standards-setting body." (§ 22757.3.1(d))
- "This section shall become operative on January 1, 2027." (§ 22757.3.1(e))

## Limits

The bill does not define "large online platform", "provenance data" or "system provenance data". Section 22757.1 defines them, and [SB 1000 chaptered text Chapter 861 of 2026](SB%201000%20chaptered%20text%20Chapter%20861%20of%202026.md) prints that section. The bill states no penalty. Section 22757.4, which SB 1000 prints, gives the civil penalty for a violation of the chapter and names a large online platform. The bill gives no cost estimate.

It does not mention research compute or CalCompute. The copy shows the bill as chaptered, so later amendments to the section do not show.
