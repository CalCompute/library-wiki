## Bottom line

- DOE reports to Congress on large power transformers (LPTs), the transformers of 100 megavolt-amperes (MVA) or more on the U.S. grid. It covers their threats, the supply chain, the spare programs of industry and a strategic reserve.
- Lead times for a new LPT are now commonly 36 months, and up to 60 months. In 2019, imports supplied 82 percent of the LPTs for U.S. use.
- DOE does not change its 2017 recommendation of a non-government reserve driven by industry.

## Background

Section 40103(d) of the Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act (Public Law 117-58) applies. It told the Secretary of Energy to assess an inventory of recovery transformers and to report to Congress. This report answers that section. It updates a 2017 DOE Report to Congress on a strategic transformer reserve. It gives data from industry, from U.S. Department of Commerce surveys and from interviews with U.S. manufacturers.

## Key claims

- "The Department of Energy (DOE) estimates that 90 percent of all electricity consumed in the U.S. passes through a LPT at some point in its journey from generation to user". (Section II.1, p. 2)
- "lead times for acquisition of an LPT have become exceptionally long, with 36-month lead times being commonly quoted and maximum lead times reaching as much as 60 months". (Section II.1, p. 2)
- "The U.S. Department of Commerce (DOC) estimated in 2019 that 137 LPTs (18 percent) were produced domestically for domestic use while 617 units (82 percent) were imported for domestic use, and only 4 units were exported." (Section III.1.1, p. 4)
- "According to a 2020 U.S. Department of Commerce industry survey, GOES and CTC each account for roughly 25 percent of final LPT production costs". GOES means grain-oriented electrical steel. CTC means continuously transposed conduction copper wire. (Section III.3.1, p. 12)
- "in interviews conducted for this report, U.S. transformer manufacturers reported experiencing much larger jumps in demand, as much as 70 percent year-over-year, in the last 1–2 years". (Section III.3.2, p. 13)
- A 2020 Commerce survey of LPT component manufacturers found that "eighty-nine percent of survey respondents reported having had difficulties in finding qualified or experienced workers, including 66 percent that identified the problem as an ongoing issue". (Section III.3.8, p. 16)
- "While this report does not advocate for a change in this recommendation, it does offer data indicating significant progress within the industry in addressing this issue." (Executive Summary)

## Evidence and method

- A government report to Congress. It reviews published studies, industry data and survey results, with numbered references.
- It describes industry spare programs: the Spare Transformer Equipment Program (STEP), SpareConnect, Grid Assurance and WattStock (Section IV.1).
- It compares the spare LPTs that utilities reported in 2016 and in 2023 (Section IV.4).

## Limits

- The report is about the national grid. It gives no figures for California.
- It does not name data centers as a source of demand. The demand drivers it names are aging units, transmission expansion, and renewable generation and storage (Section III.3.2, pp. 13-14).
- Many figures are from 2019 and 2020 surveys. The lead times and the demand growth come from interviews.
- The figures and tables are images in the PDF. The Markdown copy does not keep all of their values.
