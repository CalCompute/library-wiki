# OpenAI Usage Policies 2025-10-29

## Citation

OpenAI. "Usage policies." Policy web page, last changelog entry 2025-10-29. https://openai.com/policies/usage-policies/. Read 2026-10-15.

## Bottom line

- OpenAI has one set of usage policies for all of its products. The policies have four groups: "Protect people", "Respect privacy", "Keep minors safe" and "Empower people".
- The policies prohibit high-stakes decisions in 13 sensitive areas "without human review".
- OpenAI requires its "review and approval" for national security or intelligence use. This is an approval-based tier.

This page is one row of that table.

## Key claims

Introduction:

- "We hold people accountable for inappropriate use of our services, and breaking or circumventing our rules and safeguards may mean you lose access to our systems or experience other penalties."
- "We monitor and enforce policies with privacy safeguards in place and clear review processes."
- "You can appeal if you think we have made a mistake enforcing policy, and we will work to make things right."

"Protect people". "You cannot use our services for":

- "weapons development, procurement, or use, including conventional weapons or CBRNE"
- "destruction, compromise, or breach of another’s system or property, including malicious or abusive cyber activity"
- "threats, intimidation, harassment, or defamation"
- "terrorism or violence, including hate-based violence"
- "provision of tailored advice that requires a license, such as legal or medical advice, without appropriate involvement by a licensed professional"
- "unsolicited safety testing"
- "circumventing our safeguards"
- "national security or intelligence purposes without our review and approval"

"Respect privacy":

- "we don’t allow attempts to compromise the privacy of others, including to aggregate, monitor, profile, or distribute individuals’ private or sensitive information without their authorization."
- "facial recognition databases without data subject consent"
- "real-time remote biometric identification in public spaces"
- "evaluation or classification of individuals based on their social behavior, personal traits, or biometric data (including social scoring, profiling, or inferring sensitive attributes)"
- "assessment or prediction of the risk of an individual committing a criminal offense based solely on their personal traits or on profiling"

"Keep minors safe":

- "child sexual abuse material (CSAM), whether or not any portion is AI generated"
- "grooming of minors"
- "We report apparent child sexual abuse material and child endangerment to the National Center for Missing and Exploited Children."

"Empower people":

- "deceit, fraud, scams, spam, or impersonation"
- "political campaigning, lobbying, foreign or domestic election interference, or demobilization activities"
- "automation of high-stakes decisions in sensitive areas without human review". The policy lists 13 areas:
  - critical infrastructure, education, housing, employment
  - financial activities and credit, insurance, legal, medical
  - essential government services, product safety components, national security, migration, law enforcement

## Evidence and method

This is a policy document, not research. It states rules. It gives no data on how often OpenAI enforces them.

## Limits

- openai.com refused the download (HTTP 403). The copy comes from a web reader, so the live page is the authority. The copy can differ from the live page in layout.
- The copy has no "effective" date line. The date in the title is the latest changelog entry.
- The policies list categories only. They do not define terms such as "high-stakes decisions" or "human review".
- The page does not name a research exception. The policy prohibits "unsolicited safety testing". Thus solicited testing can be possible, but the page does not say how to ask for it.
- The policies do not name critical infrastructure as a prohibited use. Critical infrastructure appears only in the list of areas that need human review.
