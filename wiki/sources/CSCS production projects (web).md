# CSCS production projects (web)

## Citation

Swiss National Supercomputing Centre (CSCS). "Production Projects." CSCS website, User Lab, Allocation Schemes. No date on the page ("© CSCS 2026"). https://www.cscs.ch/user-lab/allocation-schemes/production-projects. Read 2026-11-18.

## Bottom line

- A production project proposer must hold at least a postdoctoral position at an academic institution. Only professors can apply for multi-year projects, of up to three years.
- Proposals ask for resources on the Alps system. Requests of 250,000 node hours or more go to the CHRONOS Tier-0 call instead.
- Large proposals get a technical feasibility check, a scientific review by an international pool and a rating by a panel committee.

## Background

This page of the CSCS website gives the rules for production projects, one of the User Lab allocation schemes ([CSCS User Lab allocation schemes (web)](CSCS%20User%20Lab%20allocation%20schemes%20%28web%29.md)). It covers eligibility, resources, the proposal format, multi-year rules and review. It gives the next deadline as Wednesday May 19, 2027, at 17:00 CEST, for allocations from October 1, 2027.

## Key claims

- Eligibility: "Proposers must hold at least the position of postdoctoral position at an **academic** institution. Only professors can apply for multi-year projects." (Who can apply?)
- Students: "PhD students **cannot** apply independently and must submit proposals in consultation with, and under the auspices of, their supervisor." (Who can apply?)
- International: "International applications (and collaborations) will be accepted only if proposers and their teams are affiliated with academic institutions and use of GPUs is clearly proved and supported by all appropriate technical data." (Who can apply?)
- No double awards: "Applicants must submit original proposals that have not been submitted and/or funded in any other HPC/AI programs." (Who can apply?)
- Size limit: "Production project proposals, which need **250'000 node hours or more** must be submitted under the CHRONOS Tier-0 call". (Resources)
- AI: "The ML Platform is available only for proposals that are not covered by the **Swiss AI Inititative**." (Resources)
- Renewals: "applicants may request no more than **20% additional resources** compared to the original **granted** resources." (Multi-year Submissions)
- Review: "Based on the technical and scientific reviews, a panel committee of scientific experts will rate the projects and make a final allocation recommendation" (Review Process)

## Evidence and method

This is an institutional call page. It lists three documents to submit (proposal, curriculum vitae and publication list), a 10-page limit and the mandatory sections of a proposal. These include a technical report with benchmarks and a resource justification in annual node hours. Every applicant without Alps access must first get a preparatory project. Codes that run only on central processing units (CPUs) go to the Eiger@Alps partition or to LUMI-C in Kajaani, Finland.

## Limits

- The page does not give the total node hours for a call, the success rate or the cost to the user.
- It says that applicants hear back "within 2-3 weeks from the SAC meeting", but does not say when the Scientific Advisory Committee (SAC) meets. [CSCS organization (web)](CSCS%20organization%20%28web%29.md) says it meets twice a year.
- It does not define the "ML Platform" or the Swiss AI Initiative.
- Its review steps cover proposals of more than 10,000 and fewer than 250,000 node hours. It does not give the steps for smaller production proposals.
