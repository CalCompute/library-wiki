## Bottom line

- SB 524 adds section 13663 to the Penal Code.
- Each law enforcement agency must keep a policy for an official report that an officer writes fully or partly with artificial intelligence (AI).
- The report must name each AI program, carry a disclosure statement and have the signature of the officer who prepared it.
- The agency keeps the first AI draft and an audit trail for as long as it keeps the official report.
- A contracted vendor must not share, sell or use the agency's information, except for the agency's purposes or a court order.

## Background

The bill's author is Senator Arreguín, as the Digest prints the name. The Governor approved it on Oct. 10, 2025. The text holds the Legislative Counsel's Digest, one new Penal Code section and a state mandate section. The library's copy comes from the Legislature's website through the Wayback Machine.

## Key claims

- "On each page of the official report, or within the body of the text, identify every specific artificial intelligence program used in a manner that makes such identification readily apparent to the reader and prominently state the following: “This report was written either fully or in part using artificial intelligence.”" (Pen. Code § 13663(a)(1))
- "If a law enforcement officer or any member of a law enforcement agency uses artificial intelligence to create an official report, whether fully or partially, the first draft created shall be retained by the agency for as long as the official report is retained." (§ 13663(b)(1))
- "Except for the official report, a draft of any report created with the use of artificial intelligence shall not constitute an officer’s statement." (§ 13663(b)(2))
- "A contracted vendor may access data processed by artificial intelligence for the purposes of troubleshooting, bias mitigation, accuracy improvement, or system refinement." (§ 13663(d)(2))
- "“Law enforcement agency” means any department or agency of the state or any local government, special district, or other political subdivision thereof, that employs any peace officer, as described in Section 830." (§ 13663(e)(4))

## Limits

The bill gives no cost estimate, no operative date and no penalty. In this section, "artificial intelligence" applies to two kinds of system. They draft police report narratives from camera audio or video, or from an officer's dictated report (§ 13663(e)(1)). The state pays local costs only if the Commission on State Mandates finds a state mandate (Section 2). The capture is of Aug. 24, 2026, so a later change does not show.

The bill does not mention research compute or CalCompute.
