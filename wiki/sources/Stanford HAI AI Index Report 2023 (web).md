# Stanford HAI AI Index Report 2023 (web)

## Citation

Maslej, Nestor, Loredana Fattorini, Erik Brynjolfsson, John Etchemendy, Katrina Ligett, Terah Lyons, James Manyika, Helen Ngo, Juan Carlos Niebles, Vanessa Parli, Yoav Shoham, Russell Wald, Jack Clark and Raymond Perrault. "The AI Index 2023 Annual Report." AI Index Steering Committee, Stanford Institute for Human-Centered Artificial Intelligence (HAI), Stanford University, April 2023. https://hai.stanford.edu/assets/files/hai_ai-index-report_2023.pdf. Read 2026-11-11.

## Bottom line

- The sixth AI Index reports that industry now makes most significant machine learning models: 32 in 2022, against three from academia.
- It says that state-of-the-art AI needs large amounts of data, compute and money, which industry has in greater amounts than nonprofits and academia.
- California had the most AI job postings in the United States in 2022: 142,154, or 17.9% of the national total.

## Background

The AI Index is a yearly data report from Stanford HAI. The 2023 edition has chapters on research and development, technical performance, ethics, the economy, education, policy and public opinion. It carries a licence: "Attribution-NoDerivatives 4.0 International". The library does not rehost it, because the suggestion gave no licence. The 2025 edition is [Stanford HAI AI Index Report 2025 (web)](Stanford%20HAI%20AI%20Index%20Report%202025%20%28web%29.md).

## Key claims

- "Industry races ahead of academia. Until 2014, most significant machine learning models were released by academia. Since then, industry has taken over. In 2022, there were 32 significant industry-produced machine learning models compared to just three produced by academia." (Report Highlights, Chapter 1)
- "Building state-of-the-art AI systems increasingly requires large amounts of data, computer power, and money—resources that industry actors inherently possess in greater amounts compared to nonprofits and academia." (Report Highlights, Chapter 1)
- "PaLM, one of the flagship large language models launched in 2022, had 540 billion parameters and cost an estimated $8 million USD—PaLM was around 360 times larger than GPT-2 and cost 160 times more." (Report Highlights, Chapter 1)
- "We should also ask questions about who is deploying it—as our analysis shows, AI is increasingly defined by the actions of a small set of private sector actors, rather than a broader range of societal actors." (From the Co-Directors)
- "The top three states in terms of postings were California (142,154), followed by Texas (66,624) and New York (43,899)." (Chapter 4, Figure 4.1.6, p. 178)
- "Last year 17.9% of all AI job postings in the United States were for jobs based in California, followed by Texas (8.4%) and New York (5.5%)" (Chapter 4, Figure 4.1.8, p. 178)

## Evidence and method

A data report. It collects data from outside sources, for example Epoch AI for models and Lightcast for job postings, and from its own analysis. The cost of training is an AI Index estimate. For this page the introduction, the Report Highlights and the state job figures of Chapter 4 were read.

## Limits

- The data stops at 2022.
- The model counts depend on which models the report calls "significant".
- The report does not measure compute access at universities or in California directly.
