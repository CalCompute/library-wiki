# WEF Global Risks Report 2026 (web)

## Citation

World Economic Forum. "The Global Risks Report 2026." 21st edition. World Economic Forum, January 2026. ISBN 978-2-940631-60-5. https://reports.weforum.org/docs/WEF_Global_Risks_Report_2026.pdf. Read 2026-11-09.

## Bottom line

- In a World Economic Forum (WEF) expert survey, "Adverse outcomes of AI technologies" moves from #30 (two-year outlook) to #5 (10-year outlook).
- The report gives very different estimates of AI's effect on jobs. It also reports large growth in the power that data centres need.

## Background

The WEF publishes this report each year. It ranks 33 global risks from its Global Risks Perception Survey (GRPS) 2025-2026. It adds national risk rankings from its Executive Opinion Survey (EOS). Chapter 2 has six analyses. Section 2.7, "AI at large", covers AI and labour markets, society and military use.

## Key claims

- "Adverse outcomes of AI is the risk with the largest rise in ranking over time, moving from #30 on the two-year outlook to #5 on the 10-year outlook." (Key findings, p. 9)
- "For example, AI adoption in North America (27% of the working-age population) is triple that in Sub-Saharan Africa (9%)." (Section 2.7, p. 61)
- "Only a handful of AI data centres are in developing regions, with the United States, Europe and Eastern Asia dominating capacity." (Section 2.7, p. 61)
- "Estimates of labour-market impacts vary widely." (Section 2.7, p. 62)
- "with 170 million new roles set to be created and 92 million displaced, resulting in a net increase of 78 million jobs globally by 2030." (Section 2.7, p. 62)
- "A more negative view suggests that AI could eliminate up to 50% of entry-level, white-collar jobs within the next five years in the United States, potentially driving unemployment to 10–20%." (Section 2.7, p. 62)
- "By 2030–2035, data centres alone could consume up to 20% of global electricity, placing strain on already overburdened power grids." (Section 2.3, p. 38)
- "The data centre capex of the top eight US hyperscalers (very large cloud services providers) alone amounted to $258 billion in 2024 and is projected to more than double to $525 billion in 2032." (Section 2.4, p. 44)
- "For example, it has been estimated that the power needed by AI data centres in the United States alone could rise 30 times within the next decade." (Section 2.5, p. 46)

## Evidence and method

The report is a perception survey with expert analysis. GRPS responses came from "over 1,300 experts" between Aug. 12 and Sept. 22, 2025 (Overview of methodology, p. 5).

The EOS had "over 11,000 business leaders in 116 economies" (p. 5). The analyses in Chapter 2 cite outside sources in endnotes. The 170 million and 92 million figures cite a 2025 WEF publication (endnote 155). The 50 percent figure cites a news article in Axios (endnote 156).

## Limits

The rankings are what respondents think, not measured outcomes. The report gives no results for California or any U.S. state. It does not discuss public compute platforms or workforce programs for them.
