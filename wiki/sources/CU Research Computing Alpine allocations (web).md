# CU Research Computing Alpine allocations (web)

## Citation

University of Colorado Boulder Research Computing (CURC). "Alpine Allocations." CURC user documentation, date not stated. https://curc.readthedocs.io/en/latest/clusters/alpine/allocations.html. Read 2026-11-18.

## Bottom line

- Every user of the Alpine cluster needs an allocation. CU Boulder users get three tiers. Trailhead is automatic. Ascent gives 450,000 service units (SUs) a year without committee review. Peak gives up to 7,500,000 SUs after committee review.
- Users of the Rocky Mountain Advanced Computing Consortium (RMACC) get Trailhead and a 250,000-SU Ascent tier. Colorado State University (CSU) and the Anschutz Medical Campus (AMC) manage their own allocations.

## Background

Alpine is the third-generation high-performance computing (HPC) cluster of CURC ([CU Research Computing Alpine overview (web)](CU%20Research%20Computing%20Alpine%20overview%20%28web%29.md)). This page in the user documentation explains allocations, fairshare priority, and how to apply and renew. The page gives separate tabs for CU Boulder, RMACC, CSU and AMC users.

## Key claims

- "Allocations are required to run on CURC clusters. They help us keep track of system usage for reporting purposes and to ensure we have enough resources to accommodate all of our users." (What are allocations and why do I need one?)
- "jobs submitted by people who have underutilized their allocated resources get higher priority, while jobs submitted by people who have overutilized their allocated resources get lower priority." (Fairshare Scheduling)
- "If there are no other pending jobs, and enough resources are available, then your job will run regardless of your previous usage." (Level Fairshare, note)
- "The Trailhead pool is 20% of the UCB share of Alpine. It is shared among all UCB users" (Comparison of Allocation Tiers, CU Boulder)
- "Equivalent to 0.15% of the system (currently 450,000 SUs) for one year" and "Approved quickly and without review by the Allocations Committee" (Ascent Allocation, CU Boulder)
- "Variable (based on user’s request), but must not exceed the number of SUs equivalent to 2.5% of the system (currently 7,500,000 SUs) over a one-year period" (Peak Allocation, CU Boulder)
- "Once the team member is satisfied with the proposal, it will be reviewed by the Allocations Committee during the next monthly meeting" (Peak Allocation, approval process)
- "At this time, RMACC users are not eligible for Peak allocations." (RMACC, Peak)
- "Allocations for CSU and AMC are managed by those institutions." (Allocations, warning)
- "Alpine is jointly funded by the University of Colorado Boulder, the University of Colorado Anschutz, Colorado State University, and the National Science Foundation (award 2201538)." (page footer)

## Evidence and method

User documentation with two comparison tables. For each tier, the tables give the required information, the size, the approval process and the renewal rule. These are the main rules:

- An Ascent request asks for a project description, funding information, the research area, the principal investigator (PI), collaborators and Slurm partitions.
- A Peak request adds example jobs and a software list.
- Ascent and Peak allocations expire one year after they start. Users renew by form.
- Trailhead allocations do not expire.
- The Ascent limit for a group is "0.6% of the system (currently 1.8 M SUs) per research group/PI".

## Limits

- The page has no date. The SU figures are "currently" values and can change.
- It does not define an SU in core-hours or GPU-hours, or give a price. It does not say whether use is free.
- It does not say who can join RMACC or how an RMACC user gets an account.
- It does not give the CSU and AMC allocation rules. It refers those users to their own institutions.
- It does not give the size of the NSF award.
