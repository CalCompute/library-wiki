## Bottom line

- This document gives the eligibility rules and definitions that apply to all CIRM funding opportunities.
- A "California Organization" must have more than half of its W-2 employees domiciled full-time in California, and a Principal Investigator (PI) located in California.
- Applicants must certify good standing, show solvency for 180 days if they are for-profit, and commit co-funding where a program requires it.

## Background

CIRM funds regenerative medicine research in California. The cover memo goes to the members of the Independent Citizens' Oversight Committee (ICOC), which approves CIRM awards. It lists four changes for agenda item 9 of an ICOC meeting, among them a new "Denied Parties" requirement. CIRM incorporates the attached document by reference into its Concept Plans, Program Announcements and Requests for Applications. [CIRM allowable costs and cofunding FAQ 2025-04 (web)](CIRM%20allowable%20costs%20and%20cofunding%20FAQ%202025-04%20%28web%29.md) applies these definitions to project costs.

## Key claims

- Incorporation: "The requirements and definitions below are incorporated by reference, and as amended from time to time, into CIRM’s Concept Plans, Program Announcements (PAs), and Requests for Applications (RFAs)." (p. 1)
- Payroll test: a California Organization "Employs at least one W-2 employee" and "More than 50% of its W-2 employees, whether part-time or full-time, who are paid in any manner (e.g., wage, salary, commission, equity), must be domiciled full-time in California and be required to file California state income taxes due to their employment with the organization." (p. 1, Eligibility Requirements (1)(i))
- Location of the PI: "The Principal Investigator (PI) must be physically located in California while overseeing all project activities." (p. 1, (1)(ii))
- Solvency: "For-profit organizations must provide documentation that shows cash on hand or funding from committed sources that will cover the organization’s expenses for 180 days from the date of application submission." (p. 4, (6))
- Co-funding: "co-funding may come from any funding source arranged by the applicant but may not include “in-kind” or similar types of support." (p. 4, (7))
- Non-profit: "“Non-profit organization” means: (1) a governmental entity of the state of California; or (2) a legal entity that is tax exempt under Internal Revenue Code section 501(c)(3) and California Revenue and Taxation Code section 23701d (2024)." (p. 5, Definitions)
- Data role: the "Data Project Manager" is responsible for "interfacing with CIRM’s planned data infrastructure, reporting progress on data management and sharing as well as maintaining the integrity of data during ingestion." (p. 5, Definitions)
- Denied parties: applicants must disclose any Key Personnel on lists such as "the System for Award Management (SAM.gov) exclusions list" and "the Bureau of Industry and Security Denied Parties List". (pp. 3 to 4, Good Standing, item G)

## Evidence and method

This is a primary document from CIRM's cirm.ca.gov website: a staff memo to the ICOC and a six-page rule document in numbered sections. The copy shows tracked changes as run-together text, for example "California Oorganization" and "F.G." in the Good Standing list. It gives rules and definitions, not data.

## Limits

- The memo does not give the ICOC vote or the date the board adopted the revisions.
- The document does not give co-funding percentages. Each Program Announcement sets them.
- It describes "CIRM’s planned data infrastructure" but does not say what it is or who runs it.
- The Good Standing list has two items lettered "F" in the copy. The memo calls the new item section "E". The copy prints it as "F.G."
