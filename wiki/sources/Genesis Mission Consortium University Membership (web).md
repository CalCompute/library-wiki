# Genesis Mission Consortium University Membership (web)

## Citation

Genesis Mission Consortium. "Genesis Mission Consortium: University Membership." PDF, 6 pages, July 21, 2026. https://genesismissionconsortium.org/pdf/Genesis_Mission_Consortium_University_Membership_21JUL2026.pdf. Read 2026-11-18.

## Bottom line

- Each university member of the Genesis Mission Consortium must do one of two things, or both. It connects a "high-value" resource to the Genesis Mission platform, or it makes an institution-level commitment to AI-for-science workforce development.
- A connected resource "must be discoverable and accessible to Consortium members". Working groups are to define access controls and "how cost recovery might work".

## Background

The Genesis Mission Consortium published this engagement plan for universities on July 21, 2026. It says the plan comes from the Genesis Mission University-Philanthropy Summit and from a request for information (RFI) on workforce development. It sets out the opportunities of membership, the core engagement, more engagement options, five working groups and the steps to join. RTI International is the "Consortium Operator and Facilitator".

## Key claims

- "Two interconnected tracks are envisioned for university engagement with the Genesis Mission Consortium: (1) building an integrated platform of computing, models, data, and experimental resources needed to apply AI at scale, and (2) establishing an ecosystem, including talent, ideas and partnerships, to support AI-for-science R&D activities." (pp. 1 to 2)
- "Core university membership is defined by two elements, where each university member contributes to at least one of these elements" (Core Engagement, p. 2)
- "University members may connect (federate) one or more high-value resource(s) to be linked to the Genesis Mission platform." Eligible types include a "High-performance or specialized computing resource". (Core Engagement, item 1, pp. 2 to 3)
- "The connected resource(s) must be discoverable and accessible to Consortium members. A more complete definition, including how workflows can be triggered, what access controls apply, and how cost recovery might work, will be defined by the relevant Consortium working groups." (p. 3)
- "University members may make a meaningful, institution-level commitment to preparing their students and researchers to work at the intersection of AI and science or engineering." (Core Engagement, item 2, p. 3)
- Institutions can establish "reciprocal agreements, such as receiving access to computational resources and expert support in exchange for conducting research and providing student fellowships or research commitments." (Strategic & Collaborative Initiatives, p. 4)
- "Institutions may take on leadership roles within the consortium by nominating representatives to serve on a governing board, strategic planning groups, and organizing committees for major events." (Leadership, p. 4)
- "RTI International serves as the Consortium Operator and Facilitator in its role as Partnership Intermediary and provides neutral, third-party management and administrative support." (Administrative Support, p. 5)

## Evidence and method

An institutional membership plan. It describes the terms and options of university membership, with two tables: the opportunities for universities and the working groups. It gives no figures, member names or commitments already made.

## Limits

- The document says that the plan "will be refined by the Consortium as needed". The terms can change.
- It does not set access controls or cost recovery for a connected resource. It leaves these to working groups.
- It does not say how members form the governing board or what powers the board has.
- It names the summit report and the RFI analysis in footnotes. The library has not read them.
- The [Genesis Mission Consortium FAQ (web)](Genesis%20Mission%20Consortium%20FAQ%20%28web%29.md) gives the dues terms. This document does not mention dues.
