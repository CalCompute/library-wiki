# CloudBank Acceptable Use Policy (web)

## Citation

CloudBank (The Regents of the University of California, for UC San Diego and UC Berkeley, and the University of Washington). "CloudBank Acceptable Use Policy v4.0." Web page, May 16, 2023. https://www.cloudbank.org/aup. Read 2026-11-12.

## Bottom line

- The CloudBank Acceptable Use Policy (AUP) is the user agreement for CloudBank. CloudBank gives researchers access to public cloud resources with National Science Foundation (NSF) funds.
- Users must shut down cloud services they do not need and must not run services that cost more than their award. The policy bans commercial use and "financial gain".
- It sets out a written notice and a chance to respond before a permanent sanction, and warnings at 20%, 10% and 5% of an allocation.

## Background

CloudBank is a collaboration of UC San Diego, UC Berkeley and the University of Washington. NSF funds it through its Cloud Access program. The cloud providers host and operate the resources; CloudBank gives the access and the support. This is version 4.0 of the policy, dated May 16, 2023. The page links to versions 1.0 (2020) to 3.0 (2021).

## Key claims

- Scope: CloudBank "offers services and support to the computer science research and education community for access to public cloud resources, funded via the National Science Foundation’s (NSF) Cloud Access program." (preamble)
- Hosting: "Whereas CloudBank provides access to these resources, the resources themselves are hosted and operated by public cloud providers." (preamble)
- Age: use is for people "who are 18 years of age and over, emancipated minors, or who possess parental or legal guardian consent". (section 2)
- Spending: "Responsively spend NSF funds by shutting down unnecessary cloud services". (section 3.1)
- Prohibited use: "Use CloudBank or public cloud resources for commercial purposes, financial gain, personal gain, or any unlawful purpose". (section 4.4)
- Budget cap: "Run cloud services that will incur costs beyond the amount awarded in your CloudBank fund". (section 4.6, a thing users agree not to do)
- Reporting: "CloudBank will use information about public cloud cost and usage records as part of its regular reporting to NSF." (section 5.5)
- Sanctions: "Written or verbal warnings; Revocation of access privileges to CloudBank; Termination of project(s) on CloudBank; Referral to law enforcement authorities." (section 6)
- Warnings: "PI and users of a project associated with the PI will receive notifications when they have 20%, 10%, and 5% of their allocation remaining". (section 7.3)
- Due process: "A User accused of a violation will be notified in writing of the charge(s) and will have an opportunity to respond in writing before CloudBank makes a final decision about the violation or imposes a permanent sanction." (section 8)
- Protected data: "In no cases will PIs or users store these protected categories of data on the CloudBank user Portal." The terms of an NSF project may permit such data on the cloud providers, and its management "is entirely the responsibility of the PI and project users". (section 9)
- Data at the end: "all data associated with an allocation will be removed from public cloud upon expiration of the project." (section 10)

## Limits

- Version 4.0 is from 2023. The page does not say if it is still the current version.
- The policy names "the computer science research and education community". The [CloudBank FAQ (web)](CloudBank%20FAQ%20%28web%29.md) says CloudBank "is available to any U.S. researcher or educator through the ACCESS and NAIRR Pilot programs". The policy does not name ACCESS or the National Artificial Intelligence Research Resource (NAIRR) Pilot.
- It does not define "commercial purposes" or "financial gain".
- It does not say how the cloud providers' own policies and this policy rank if they differ.
