## Background

The Policy Innovation Lab (PIL) is "an intensive, applied policy course at Heinz College that prepares students to become public interest technologists". Students work "directly with a client from a government office or public service nonprofit" and "aim to develop and deliver a Minimum Viable Product (MVP)". The lead faculty member is "Professor Christopher Goranson, Distinguished Service Professor & Senior Director for the School of Public Policy and Management". The Public Interest Technology University Network Challenge Fund supports the course materials and the fellowships.

He also linked Christopher Goranson's faculty profile: [Goranson CMU Heinz profile](Goranson%20CMU%20Heinz%20profile.md).

## Key claims

- Method: students "design and build public interest technology services with human-centered design" and follow "an agile methodology" (course description).
- Open materials: "seven weekly course modules, 12 assignment instructions and rubrics, a sample syllabus, instructor notes". They are free on OER Commons, pitcases.org and Canvas Commons (section "Policy Innovation Lab Course Materials").
- GovScan: "a generative AI application that helps researchers find the information they seek in a matter of seconds, not hours" in state plans for programs "federally funded and state run (for example: Medicaid and child care subsidies)" (section "Finding the Needle in the Haystack: GovScan").
- The RIF Impact Framework helps "public institutions document and interpret reductions in force (RIFs)". It maps "lost job functions, program impacts and geographic relevance" (section "The RIF Impact Framework").
- A labor trafficking project used "a large language model (LLM) to analyze the content, flag potential scam offerings" in job postings (section "Labor Trafficking").
- An "Accelerator Model" pilot encourages students "to explore how to improve government through the launch of new products and ventures" (section "Accelerator Model").
