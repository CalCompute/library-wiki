# UF Research Computing Groups and Allocations (web)

## Citation

University of Florida (UF), UF Information Technology (UFIT) Research Computing. "Groups and Allocations." Procedures page, date not stated. https://it.ufl.edu/rc/documentation/procedures/groups-and-allocations/. Read 2026-11-18.

## Bottom line

- At UFIT Research Computing, a UF faculty member who buys resources becomes an "investor". The investor sponsors other users, and every user must have a sponsor.
- An investment becomes a resource allocation: Normalized Computing Units (NCUs) for compute, and storage units for storage. Investments are hardware for a five-year life, or service for a shorter period.
- Other allocation types are allocations that a department or college shares, a one-time trial loan for new faculty, and one-semester teaching allocations.

## Background

UFIT Research Computing runs HiPerGator ([UF AI HiPerGator page (web)](UF%20AI%20HiPerGator%20page%20%28web%29.md)). This procedures page explains how investments, sponsor groups and allocation groups work on the system. It ends with a worked example for a department.

## Key claims

- Investor: "A faculty member with a UFIT Research Computing investment is known as an investor. The investor may sponsor other users to work with the resources." (introduction, item 1)
- Sponsor: "Every user of UFIT Research Computing resources must have a sponsor. Every user’s primary group will be that of their sponsor." (introduction, item 2)
- Storage rule: "All groups must have an active Blue Storage (BSU) allocation in order to use HiPerGator services." (introduction, item 4)
- Conversion: "When a UF faculty member invests funds with UFIT Research Computing, their investment is converted to a resource allocation." (Allocations)
- Investment types: "Hardware investments, wherein a group purchases equipment for the full extent of its useful lifetime (5 years)" and "Service investments, wherein a group purchases the use of resources for a fragmentary period of the equipment’s lifetime" (Allocations)
- Size: "The allocation will be commensurate with the level of investment." (Research group allocation)
- Trial loan: a new faculty member "can request a short-term try-and-buy allocation for computing or storage in HiPerGator or HiPerGator-RV as a loan. Such a loan can only be given once." (Allocation loan (trial allocations))
- Teaching: "A special short-term allocation can be created for the duration of one semester to support faculty teaching a course or workshop that involves data intensive or computing intensive work" (Teaching allocations)

## Evidence and method

A procedures page with rules and one example. The example: the Statistics department invests 100 NCUs, and Research Computing creates a group called "statistics" for it. Other points:

- An investment's start date "cannot be more than 30 days from the date of an investment request form submission".
- A department, college, center or institute can invest. A faculty member is then the investor of a "collaborative allocation group".
- A trial loan typically lasts "between one and six months". An investor can also get an "expansion loan" of one or two months, once a year.
- Teaching accounts "are deleted at the end of the semester or at the end of the workshop".

## Limits

- The page gives no prices. It links a purchase request page that the library has not read.
- It uses "NGUs" but does not define the unit.
- It does not say who decides a trial loan or how large one can be. The size "needs to be negotiated" with Research Computing staff.
- The page covers UF faculty. [UF Research Computing Other Universities policy (web)](UF%20Research%20Computing%20Other%20Universities%20policy%20%28web%29.md) covers faculty at other institutions.
