## Bottom line

- The 2025 AI Index reports that nearly 90% of notable AI models in 2024 came from industry, up from 60% in 2023. Academia stays the top source of highly cited research.
- Training compute for notable models doubles about every five months. The cost to use a model at the level of GPT-3.5 fell more than 280-fold in about 18 months.
- California had 15.7% of U.S. AI job postings in 2024, the most of any state.

## Background

The AI Index is a yearly data report from Stanford HAI. This edition adds analyses of AI hardware and new estimates of inference costs. It carries a licence: "Attribution-NoDerivatives 4.0 International". The library does not rehost it, because the suggestion gave no licence. The 2023 edition is [Stanford HAI AI Index Report 2023 (web)](Stanford%20HAI%20AI%20Index%20Report%202023%20%28web%29.md).

## Key claims

- "Nearly 90% of notable AI models in 2024 came from industry, up from 60% in 2023, while academia remains the top source of highly cited research. Model scale continues to grow rapidly—training compute doubles every five months, datasets every eight, and power use annually." (Top Takeaways, item 10, p. 4)
- "Driven by increasingly capable small models, the inference cost for a system performing at the level of GPT-3.5 dropped over 280-fold between November 2022 and October 2024. At the hardware level, costs have declined by 30% annually, while energy efficiency has improved by 40% each year." (Top Takeaways, item 7, p. 4)
- "Open-weight models are closing the gap with closed models, reducing the performance difference from 8% to just 1.7% on some benchmarks in a single year." (Top Takeaways, item 7, p. 4)
- "Governments are investing at scale: Canada pledged $2.4 billion, China launched a $47.5 billion semiconductor fund, France committed €109 billion, India pledged $1.25 billion, and Saudi Arabia's Project Transcendence represents a $100 billion initiative." (Top Takeaways, item 8, p. 4)
- "In 2024, U.S. private AI investment grew to $109.1 billion—nearly 12 times China's $9.3 billion and 24 times the U.K.'s $4.5 billion." (Top Takeaways, item 3, p. 3)
- "In the U.S., 81% of K–12 CS teachers say AI should be part of foundational CS education, but less than half feel equipped to teach it." (Top Takeaways, item 9, p. 4)
- "15.7% of all AI job postings in the United States were for jobs based in California, followed by Texas (8.8%) and New York (5.8%)." (Chapter 4, p. 231)

## Evidence and method

A data report. It draws on outside datasets, for example Epoch AI for models and compute and Lightcast for job postings, and on its own analysis of costs. These parts were read for this page:

- the Top Takeaways, pages 3 to 4;
- the Chapter 1 highlights, page 12;
- the state job figures, pages 230 to 231;
- the California items in Chapter 6, page 334.

## Limits

- The data stops at 2024.
- The report measures industry and countries. It does not measure compute access at universities or in California directly.
- Its cost figures are prices of commercial services, not the cost of owning hardware.
