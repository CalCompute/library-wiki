# California Policy Lab AI-Unemployment Tracker launch (web)

## Citation

California Policy Lab (CPL). "California Launches First-in-the-Nation Tool Linking AI Exposure to Unemployment Insurance Trends." News release, Sacramento, June 25, 2026. https://capolicylab.org/news/california-launches-first-in-the-nation-tool-linking-ai-exposure-to-unemployment-insurance-trends/. Read 2026-11-09.

## Bottom line

- The California AI-Unemployment Tracker (CAIT) links unemployment insurance (UI) claims with measures of AI exposure by occupation. The Governor's Office, the Employment Development Department (EDD) and CPL at the University of California built it.
- Through May 2026 there is "no evidence of a statewide surge in layoffs" in highly AI-exposed occupations. Claims rose for college-educated workers in those occupations, in the Bay Area and in some technology sectors.

## Background

CPL is a nonpartisan research center at the University of California. This release announces the tracker and a report by researchers at CPL's UCLA site. The tracker updates monthly. It is on the CPL website and the EDD website.

## Key claims

- "The California AI-Unemployment Tracker (CAIT) links unemployment insurance (UI) claims with measures of occupational AI exposure to monitor labor market changes as they unfold." (opening)
- "Developed through a partnership with the Governor's Office, the California Employment Development Department (EDD), and the nonpartisan California Policy Lab (CPL) at the University of California". (opening)
- "Since the release of ChatGPT-3.5 in 2022, statewide UI claims through May 2026 show no evidence of a surge in layoffs among workers in highly AI-exposed occupations." (Key Findings)
- "Unemployment insurance claims among master's and PhD holders in highly AI-exposed occupations have remained elevated, moving from a baseline average of 13,000 claims per month in November 2022 to between 16,000-22,000 claims per month since mid-2023." (Key Findings)
- "The tracker cannot determine whether AI directly caused specific layoffs, and other factors, including post-pandemic shifts in technology industries and broader economic conditions, may also be contributing to observed trends." (body)
- "The California AI-Unemployment Tracker will be updated monthly and includes breakdowns by region, industry, age, education, race and ethnicity, and gender. The authors note that it is intended as an early warning system, not a definitive measure of AI's impact on jobs." (body)

## Evidence and method

A news release from a university research center. It summarises a report that uses state UI claims data and two measures of AI exposure. One measure is what AI could do in a task; the other is how often people use AI for it.

## Limits

The release is a summary; the full report and the tracker were not read. It does not show cause: the authors say other factors can explain the trends. It does not cover public sector employers or compute.
