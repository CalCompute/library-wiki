# Executive Order N-10-26 (web)

## Citation

Office of the Governor of California. Gavin Newsom. "Executive Order N-10-26." Executive Department, State of California, signed Sept. 30, 2026. https://www.gov.ca.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/SIGNED_EO-N-10-26_9.30.26.pdf. ## Bottom line

- Executive Order N-10-26 tells all agencies and departments under the Governor's authority to use the terms "Artificial Intelligence" and "AI".
- They use these terms "notwithstanding any rebranded or different terminology used by the federal government, unless inconsistent with the law".
- The order is effective immediately. It sets no deadline and gives no other duty.

## Background

The Governor signed this one-page order on Sept. 30, 2026. On the same day he signed 13 bills on artificial intelligence (AI). His press release links to the order ([Governor AI bill signings including AB 2392 2026-09-30 (web)](Governor%20AI%20bill%20signings%20including%20AB%202392%202026-09-30%20%28web%29.md)). The order has two recitals and one directive. The attest line for the Secretary of State has no signature in this copy.

## Key claims

- "WHEREAS California dominates Artificial Intelligence (“AI”) innovation, even as no state has taken more aggressive action to strengthen the safety, security, and consumer privacy of AI" (recitals)
- "WHEREAS, while language and choice of terminology can have significance, purporting to change something's name cannot distract a person of normal intelligence from recognizing the impotent and craven failure to take action to address well-documented emerging security and safety risks posed by that thing." (recitals)
- "IT IS HEREBY ORDERED THAT: All agencies and departments subject to my authority shall refer to Artificial Intelligence and AI as “Artificial Intelligence” and “AI,” notwithstanding any rebranded or different terminology used by the federal government, unless inconsistent with the law." (order)
- "This Order is not intended to, and does not, create any rights or benefits, substantive or procedural, enforceable at law or in equity, against the State of California, its agencies, departments, entities, officers, employees, or any other person." (closing)

## Evidence and method

The legal text of an executive order: two recitals, then one order. The recitals give no source for their statements.

## Limits

The order does not name the federal terminology that it refers to. It applies only to agencies and departments under the Governor's authority. It does not mention CalCompute or § 11546.8.
