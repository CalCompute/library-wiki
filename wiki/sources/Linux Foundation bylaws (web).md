# Linux Foundation bylaws (web)

## Citation

The Linux Foundation. "Amended and Restated Bylaws of The Linux Foundation (an Oregon nonprofit mutual benefit corporation)." Web page, dated 2024-03-07 in the library's record; the page prints no date of adoption. https://www.linuxfoundation.org/legal/bylaws. Read 2026-11-11.

## Bottom line

- These are the bylaws of the Linux Foundation, an Oregon nonprofit mutual benefit corporation under Section 501(c)(6).
- Members are in paid classes: Platinum, Gold and Silver vote; Associates, for nonprofit and government entities, do not vote.
- Each Platinum Member appoints one director. Gold and Silver Members elect directors as classes. The Board can appoint up to five At-Large Directors.

## Background

The Linux Foundation publishes its bylaws on its website. They have nine articles: offices, purposes, membership, membership meetings, the Board of Directors, officers, committees, miscellaneous, and effective date, amendments and dissolution. The Oregon Nonprofit Corporation Act governs the corporation. The Linux Foundation holds assets in custody for Project Jupyter ([Jupyter Executive Council page (web)](Jupyter%20Executive%20Council%20page%20%28web%29.md)).

## Key claims

- Purpose: "This corporation is a nonprofit mutual benefit corporation, organized and operated to engage in any lawful activity permitted by Section 501(c)(6) of the Internal Revenue Code of 1986, as amended. The purposes of this corporation are to support, promote, protect and standardize Linux and other open source software and technologies." (Section 2.1)
- Classes: "There shall be three classes of full membership in this corporation: (i) Platinum; (ii) Gold; and (iii) Silver, and one class of non-voting participants, as provided for by Section 3.2(d), called Associates." (Section 3.1)
- Associates: "Non-profit and government entities shall be eligible for membership in the Associate class of membership only." (Section 3.2(d))
- Dues: "Each class of members shall pay such dues, fees and assessments (if any, in the case of Associate Members) as may from time to time be approved by the Board of Directors". (Section 3.5)
- Platinum seats: "Each Platinum Member shall individually have the right to appoint a single director, up to a maximum of twenty (20) directors so appointed." (Section 5.3(a))
- Gold seats: "The Gold Members, voting as a class, shall elect three (3) directors or such fewer number as equals the total number of Gold Members." (Section 5.3(b))
- Silver seat: "The Silver Members, voting as a class, shall elect one (1) director." (Section 5.3(c))
- At-Large seats: "The Board of Directors may appoint up to five (5) directors known as the “At-Large Directors” or the “Directors At-Large”." One seat goes to a representative of the Technical Advisory Board (TAB). (Section 5.3(d))
- Balance: "at no time shall the number of At-Large Directors be equal to or greater than the aggregate number of Platinum, Gold and Silver Directors." (Section 5.4)
- Board size: "The minimum authorized number of directors is ten (10)." (Section 5.2)
- Amendments: the authority to amend the bylaws "is vested exclusively in the Board of Directors and may be exercised upon approval of a majority of directors then in office without the vote or consent of any member(s) or third parties", unless an amendment would reduce a class's right to vote. (Section 9.2)

## Evidence and method

This is the governing document of a nonprofit mutual benefit corporation. It states membership, board composition, voting, quorum, officers and committees. It gives no dues amounts and no list of members.

## Limits

- The bylaws do not give the dues of each class.
- They do not name the members, the directors or the officers.
- They do not describe the directed funds or projects that the Linux Foundation hosts, for example the Jupyter Foundation.
- The page prints no date of adoption or of the latest amendment.
