# NAIRR Task Force RFI 2021 responses (web)

## Bottom line

- This is the federal compilation of the 84 public responses to the first request for information (RFI) of the NAIRR Task Force. The comment period ran from July 23 to Oct. 1, 2021.
- NAIRR is the National Artificial Intelligence Research Resource.
- The responses come from companies, universities, national laboratories, nonprofits and individuals. They do not state the views of the U.S. Government or the task force.
- The responses read for this page discuss build or buy for compute, who is eligible, base-level access, networks, sign-in and public-private partnerships.

## Background

OSTP and NSF asked for comment to inform the task force's implementation road map for the NAIRR (p. 1). Each response has a cover page and the respondent's text. By the RFI instructions, only the first 10 pages of each response count (p. 1).

The task force's final plan says that it read responses to two RFIs ([NAIRR Task Force final report 2023 (web)](NAIRR%20Task%20Force%20final%20report%202023%20%28web%29.md), p. iii). The second RFI is [NAIRR Task Force RFI 2022 responses (web)](NAIRR%20Task%20Force%20RFI%202022%20responses%20%28web%29.md).

## Key claims

Page numbers are the pages of the PDF.

- "This document contains the 84 responses received from government, academic, and industry stakeholders. In accordance with the RFI instructions, only the first 10 pages of content were considered for each response. Please note that these responses do not represent the views or opinions of the U.S. Government or the NAIRR Task Force." (introduction, p. 1)
- Stanford Institute for Human-Centered Artificial Intelligence (HAI): "The main policy choice will be whether to build public computing infrastructure or purchase services from existing commercial cloud providers." (p. 596)
- Stanford HAI: "It is well established that, based solely on hardware costs, it is more cost-effective to own infrastructure when computing demand is close to continuous." (p. 596)
- Stanford HAI: "We recommend that the NRC eligibility start with academics who hold "Principal Investigator" (PI) status (i.e., most faculty) at U.S. colleges and universities, as well as to "Affiliated Government Agencies" willing to contribute previously unreleased, high-value datasets to the NRC in return for subsidized compute resources." (p. 597)
- Stanford HAI: "In order to keep the award processing costs down, we recommend a base-level of compute access to meet the majority of researcher computing needs." (p. 597)
- Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory: "There are many barriers preventing researchers from accessing conventional HPC or commercial cloud resources, for example, lack of technical know-how on how to scale from laptop resources to extreme concurrencies, or how to package a workflow into a container, etc., that are not solved simply by providing resources without technical help that will provide on-ramps for researchers with varying levels of skill." (p. 359)
- Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory: "As a way to build up capability quickly, a national resource could be composed as an array of compute and data resources, composed of existing high-performance computing resources, or extensions to resources at existing HPC Centers, commercial cloud options, and a layer of software tools." (p. 363)
- Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory, on the COVID-19 High Performance Computing Consortium: "Representatives from each institution formed a governing body to set basic policy, and separate committees were set up to evaluate proposals submitted by researchers applying for access." (p. 364)
- Internet2: "The hybrid models should include on-premise, national centers, commercial clouds, in-network, and edge computing scenarios." (p. 346)
- Internet2: "The associated data egress fees have been seen as a barrier by the research community." (p. 347)
- Internet2: "To develop resident expertise, the NAIRR should include support for a variety of training programs and communities of practice for researchers, campus research computing and data professionals, and campus identity management professionals." (p. 347)

## Evidence and method

A government compilation of public comments. Each response gives its own views and evidence; the compilation does not weigh them.

For this page the introduction and table of contents (pp. 1 to 4) were read, with parts of three responses: Internet2 (pp. 345 to 349), Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory (pp. 358 to 364, all of its text) and Stanford HAI (pp. 591 to 597).

A search of the whole text for California, state and regional terms found the other passages that name California. The Stanford HAI response encloses the executive summary of its white paper ([Stanford HAI National Research Cloud blueprint 2021 (web)](Stanford%20HAI%20National%20Research%20Cloud%20blueprint%202021%20%28web%29.md)).

## Limits

- The responses are the views of their authors. The document says that they do not represent the views of the U.S. Government or the task force (p. 1).
- This page reads parts of 3 of the 84 responses. The others are not summarized here.
- The table of contents does not list every response. For example, the responses of Sean Ekins (p. 187), Ashik Ghosh (p. 365) and Steve Xiao (p. 575) have cover pages but no line in it.
- The responses are from 2021. They come before the task force's interim and final reports and before the NAIRR pilot.
- The copy is the Wayback Machine record of the ai.gov address. The document gives no publication date. The PDF properties give Oct. 29, 2021.
