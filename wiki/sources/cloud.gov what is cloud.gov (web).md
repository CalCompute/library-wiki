## Citation

## Bottom line

- The page describes cloud.gov, a Platform as a Service (PaaS) that GSA operates for federal agencies.
- The customer team is responsible for its own code. The platform handles the security and maintenance of everything under it.
- The page says cloud.gov runs on Amazon Web Services (AWS), is open source and has a FedRAMP authorization at the Moderate impact level.
- On Nov. 19, 2026, the address redirects to the cloud.gov homepage. This page reports the capture of Aug. 14, 2025.

## Background

cloud.gov is a website of GSA, as the page footer says. This is its overview page for prospective agency customers. It has three parts: the platform, compliance and the case for a government platform. FedRAMP is the Federal Risk and Authorization Management Program. The FedRAMP Marketplace data in the library also has a record for cloud.gov ([FedRAMP Marketplace data (web)](FedRAMP%20Marketplace%20data%20%28web%29.md)).

## Key claims

- "cloud.gov is a secure and compliant Platform as a Service (PaaS). cloud.gov helps federal agencies deliver the services the public deserves in a faster, more user-centered way." (opening)
- "The customer team is responsible for their own product’s code, and the cloud.gov platform handles the security and maintenance of everything underneath." (Platform as a Service)
- "cloud.gov runs on top of industry-provided infrastructure (currently Amazon Web Services is the “Infrastructure as a Service” provider)." (Platform as a Service)
- "And because it’s based on the open source Cloud Foundry project, it provides **portability** to other cloud providers or your existing on-premise solution." (Platform as a Service)
- "cloud.gov has a FedRAMP Joint Authorization Board (JAB) Provisional Authority to Operate (P-ATO) at the Moderate impact level." (cloud.gov provides Compliance as a Service)
- "A centralized, open source PaaS model, one that is already compliant with federal rules, **reduces the work and cost of cloud adoption**." (Why a government Platform as a Service works)

## Evidence and method

A government web page that describes the agency's own service. It gives no prices, usage numbers or customer list. The quotes leave out the page's link addresses.

## Limits

The copy is 15 months old, and the address no longer serves the page. The library has no later copy, so it cannot say what changed. The page is for federal agencies; it does not say if a state agency or a university can use cloud.gov. It does not mention GPUs, AI workloads or research computing.

The page does not mention California or CalCompute.
