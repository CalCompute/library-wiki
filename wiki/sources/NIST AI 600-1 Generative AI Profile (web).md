# NIST AI 600-1 Generative AI Profile (web)

## Citation

National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST). "Artificial Intelligence Risk Management Framework: Generative Artificial Intelligence Profile." NIST AI 600-1. U.S. Department of Commerce, July 2024. Approved by the NIST Editorial Review Board on 07-25-2024. https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.AI.600-1; file at https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.600-1.pdf. ## Bottom line

- NIST AI 600-1 is a cross-sectoral profile of the AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0) for generative AI (GAI). It is for voluntary use.
- It defines 12 risks that GAI creates or makes worse, from CBRN information to value chain integration. It then gives suggested actions to govern, map, measure and manage them.
- It covers only risks with an existing evidence base. It leaves out speculative risks of future systems.

## Background

NIST wrote the profile under section 4.1(a)(i)(A) of Executive Order 14110, as a companion to NIST AI 100-1. The NIST Generative AI Public Working Group gave input on four topics: governance, content provenance, pre-deployment testing and incident disclosure. Section 2 describes the risks. Section 3 lists the suggested actions, each with an identifier such as GV-6.1-001 and the risks it addresses. Appendix A covers the four primary considerations.

California guidance names the AI RMF as the base of state GenAI risk assessments. See [California GenAI guidelines for public sector procurement uses and training 2024-03 (web)](California%20GenAI%20guidelines%20for%20public%20sector%20procurement%20uses%20and%20training%202024-03%20%28web%29.md) and [SAM 4986.9 GenAI procurement (web)](SAM%204986.9%20GenAI%20procurement%20%28web%29.md).

## Key claims

- "This document is a cross-sectoral profile of and companion resource for the AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0) for Generative AI, pursuant to President Biden's Executive Order (EO) 14110 on Safe, Secure, and Trustworthy Artificial Intelligence." (Section 1, p. 1)
- The AI RMF "is intended for voluntary use and to improve the ability of organizations to incorporate trustworthiness considerations into the design, development, use, and evaluation of AI products, services, and systems." (Section 1, p. 1)
- "This document focuses on risks for which there is an existing empirical evidence base at the time this profile was written; for example, speculative risks that may potentially arise in more advanced, future GAI systems are not considered." (Section 2, p. 3)
- "CBRN Information or Capabilities: Eased access to or synthesis of materially nefarious information or design capabilities related to chemical, biological, radiological, or nuclear (CBRN) weapons or other dangerous materials or agents." (Section 2, risk 1, p. 4)
- "Environmental Impacts: Impacts due to high compute resource utilization in training or operating GAI models, and related outcomes that may adversely impact ecosystems." (Section 2, risk 5, p. 4)
- "Currently available pre-deployment TEVV processes used for GAI applications may be inadequate, non-systematically applied, or fail to reflect or mismatched to deployment contexts." (Appendix A, Pre-Deployment Testing)

## Evidence and method

A federal technical publication of more than 50 pages. It builds on public working group input and Requests for Information. Its risk descriptions cite published studies in footnotes and Appendix B. Its suggested actions are guidance, not rules.

## Limits

This page reads the front matter, Section 1, the risk list of Section 2, sample actions of Section 3 and the start of Appendix A. It does not read every suggested action or the full Appendix B. The profile does not set rules for compute providers, and it does not cover the cost of risk management. It cites Executive Order 14110; the copy does not say whether that order is still in force.
