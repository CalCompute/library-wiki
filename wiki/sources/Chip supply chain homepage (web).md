# Chip supply chain homepage (web)

## Citation

Sharma, Yashvardhan. "How AI chips are made." *Chip Supply Chain*, chipsupplychain.org. Web page, updated 31 October 2026. https://chipsupplychain.org/. Read 2026-11-09.

## Bottom line

- The homepage explains the supply chain for AI chips in 14 stages, from chip design software to data centers and power. It rates 7 of the 14 stages "Extreme" for concentration.
- It says few firms supply each stage, and that most are in the United States, Japan, Taiwan, South Korea and the Netherlands. It also gives costs, such as about $6,400 to build one Nvidia B200, from Epoch AI.

## Background

The site says Yashvardhan Sharma made it "(with Claude)", and gives the address contact@chipsupplychain.org for corrections. The homepage has the 14 stages, two chapters on cost and control and ten flashcards. It also has a country map of 226 supplier firms and an export controls timeline. It also shows the China share of revenue at 24 firms, from their filings. Each fact line links a source, such as a company filing, the U.S. Geological Survey (USGS) or the Center for Security and Emerging Technology (CSET).

## Key claims

- 7 of the 14 stages "are rated Extreme, meaning three or fewer firms could supply each today, or one firm holds more than half." (The chain at a glance)
- "Every advanced chip passes through ASML’s machines, so export controls on ASML affect all advanced chipmaking." (Lithography)
- "TSMC runs the biggest, and all six of its largest plants are in Taiwan." (Foundries and Fabs)
- "Only SK hynix, Samsung and Micron make HBM in volume." (Memory and HBM)
- "On the July 2026 earnings call, Wei said TSMC’s packaging capacity was so tight that it limited customers’ growth, and that back-end capacity was still in shortage" (Advanced Packaging)
- "Getting that much electricity to the site is harder than putting up the building." (Data Centers and Power)
- "Epoch AI estimated in December 2025 that a B200 cost about $6,400 to build, within a range of $5,700 to $7,300." (The Economics of AI Chips)
- "Export controls work only when a country’s own firms sell something the buyer cannot get elsewhere, so few governments can use them to much effect." (Who Controls the Chain)

## Evidence and method

A self-published explainer. Each stage has a plain description and two fact lines, each with a linked source. The sources include filings with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC), USGS and U.S. Department of Energy reports, CSET data, Epoch AI and company investor materials. The country shares come from the site's own "chokepoint matrix" and supplier list, and the table marks some as "in-house estimate".

## Limits

The homepage is long: the library's Markdown copy has about 8,500 words. The library read only the homepage. The library did not read the sixteen chapters or check the linked sources. One person made the site with an AI model, and it names no review process or institution. It does not discuss research computing, universities or any state compute program. Its figures date from 2019 to 2026, and the year changes from stage to stage.
