# SDSC data-driven agriculture 2026-01-13 (web)

## Citation

Scott Paton, San Diego Supercomputer Center (SDSC). "Farm-to-Table via Supercomputer: A New Era of Data-driven Agriculture Dawns in a California Vineyard." News release, Jan. 15, 2026. https://www.sdsc.edu/news/2026/PR20260113-data-driven-ag.html. Read 2026-11-09.

## Bottom line

- CENIC leads a precision agriculture testbed at Iron Horse Vineyards in Sonoma County. SDSC processes its data and builds a "digital twin" of the vineyard.
- Students from Sonoma State University and Santa Rosa Junior College work on the project through internships, on site and on the National Research Platform.

## Background

SDSC published this feature on its news site. The address carries the date 2026-01-13, and the page says "Published January 15, 2026". It describes the testbed, CENIC's 10-gigabit link to the vineyard, and SDSC's role in data analysis. It names the partners. It quotes the CENIC President and CEO, the SDSC Director and an SDSC engineer.

## Key claims

- "The initiative is being driven by CENIC (the Corporation for Education Network Initiatives in California), whose statewide high-speed research network brings advanced connectivity to institutions across the state." (first section)
- "Sterling’s vineyard is now serving as a living agricultural laboratory, working closely with CENIC, SDSC, UC Agriculture and Natural Resources, Sonoma State University, Santa Rosa Junior College, AT&T, Emergent and other partners." (A Vineyard Becomes a Living Laboratory)
- "Since the project’s launch in late 2024, the team has laid down the final leg of a fiber connection to the vineyard, installed a 10-gigabit-per-second CENIC router and deployed a first wave of field sensors" (A Vineyard Becomes a Living Laboratory)
- "In June 2025, CENIC extended a 10-gigabit link directly to Iron Horse Vineyards with the help of AT&T" (CENIC Network as the Data Grapevine)
- "CENIC and SDSC engaged students from Sonoma State University and Santa Rosa Junior College  through internships at both on-site labs and the virtual National Research Platform." (Opening New Paths for Students and Researchers)
- "It is the first chapter in a larger initiative to create testbeds that advance state-of-the-art practices across critical fields and build pathways to new careers in AI-related fields for all Californians." (CENIC President and CEO Louis Fox)

## Evidence and method

An institutional feature story. It describes one testbed and its partners, its equipment and the student internships. It gives one outcome figure, from a different test bed. There, sensors by Emergent Connext helped one Central Valley farm "cut its water use by 10 percent". It gives no source for that figure. It describes CalREN as an "8,000-mile" network.

## Limits

The story gives no cost, no funding source and no count of students. It does not say which SDSC systems process the data. It names SDSC's parent school as "UC San Diego’s School of Computing, Information and Data Science". [SDSC National Data Platform release 2026-08-10 (web)](SDSC%20National%20Data%20Platform%20release%202026-08-10%20%28web%29.md) names it the "Halıcıoğlu School of Data Science and Computing". It gives the title of John Graham as "senior development engineer", "senior research scientist" and "research scientist" in different places. [CENIC AIR initiative (web)](CENIC%20AIR%20initiative%20%28web%29.md) describes the CENIC AI Resource Network that the story names.
