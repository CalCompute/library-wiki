# UCSF Wynton pricing (web)

## Citation

UCSF Wynton HPC. "Wynton HPC Compute and Storage Pricing." Wynton HPC website, marked "2018-10-06: This is a mockup page with mockup information." https://wynton.ucsf.edu/hpc/about/pricing.html. Read 2026-11-20. Cached copy: `.cache/web/2026-11-20 UCSF Wynton pricing.md`.

## Bottom line

- The page calls itself "a mockup page with mockup information", dated Oct. 6, 2018. Its figures are of October 2018.
- It describes the Wynton co-op model at UC San Francisco (UCSF). All members get baseline compute free. Contributors get priority in proportion to what they contribute.
- As of October 2018, a compute unit of four nodes (112 cores) cost about $37,000. A group could also bring its own hardware.

## Background

The Wynton HPC team publishes this page. It has a table of what free members, compute contributors and storage contributors get, and short sections on compute and storage pricing. It says that the team has not yet defined a storage pricing model. UCSF retires Wynton at the end of February 2027 ([UCSF Wynton HPC home (web)](UCSF%20Wynton%20HPC%20home%20%28web%29.md)).

## Key claims

- "2018-10-06: This is a mockup page with mockup information." (Header)
- "The Wynton HPC cluster is built on a co-op model where every contributing research group has immediate access to the cluster computing power and/or storage capacity proportionate to their contribution." (Wynton HPC Compute and Storage Pricing)
- "In addition, all members (free and contributing), have access to a baseline fraction of the cluster computing power and storage capacity regardless of their contribution." (Wynton HPC Compute and Storage Pricing)
- "GPUs are currently only available to groups who have contributed with their own GPU-equipped compute nodes but there is a plan to provide baseline GPUs for all users." (Footnote 1)
- "Contributions toward compute can be done either in cash (“buy in”) or by integrating existing hardware (“bring your own”)." (Compute pricing)
- "As of October 2018, a compute unit with four nodes (112 cores), 384 GiB RAM, ~0.8 TiB local scratch, 10 Gbps network card, is ~37,000 USD." (Buy new compute)
- "We are currently in the process of defining the storage pricing model, which will be available as soon as we have identified all the costs involved." (Storage pricing)

## Evidence and method

A web page of about 800 words, with site navigation. It marks itself as a mockup. Its one price is of October 2018. It gives no current prices and no count of contributors.

## Limits

The library records this page as not citable, because the page calls its own information "mockup information". The 2018 price is not a current price. The page gives no storage price.

> ⚠️ Contradiction: this page gives home storage of "200 GiB" and GPUs for contributors only. The specifications page gives "500 GiB/user" and 88 communal GPUs on 22 nodes ([UCSF Wynton cluster specifications (web)](UCSF%20Wynton%20cluster%20specifications%20%28web%29.md)).
