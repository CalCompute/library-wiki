# Ahmed Thompson Brookings industry influence in AI research 2023-12-05 (web)

## Citation

Nur Ahmed and Neil C. Thompson. "What should be done about the growing influence of industry in AI research?" Article, Brookings Institution, Economic Studies, Center on Regulation and Markets, December 5, 2023. https://www.brookings.edu/articles/what-should-be-done-about-the-growing-influence-of-industry-in-ai-research/. Read 2026-11-11.

## Bottom line

- The article says that a few private companies hold most of the data, talent and computing power for modern AI research. It says that academia has lost ground.
- It gives figures for the gap. Industry hired nearly 70 percent of AI Ph.D. holders in 2020. Industry models were 29 times larger than academic models in 2021.
- It proposes public computing resources, support for academic researchers, immigration and diversity as responses. It calls the NAIRR commitment of $2.6 billion over six years modest.

## Background

The authors work at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT): Nur Ahmed is a postdoctoral associate, and Neil C. Thompson directs the FutureTech project. The article is part of a Brookings series on the economics and regulation of AI. It summarises their 2023 paper in Science, "The Growing Influence of Industry in AI Research". The FutureTech project gets grants from Accenture, Microsoft, IBM, Good Ventures, the MIT-AF accelerator, MIT Lincoln Laboratory and the National Science Foundation (Acknowledgements and disclosures).

## Key claims

- "However, modern AI research requires expensive resources that are often only available to a small group of for-profit firms." (The issue at a high level)
- "To let academic researchers work on cutting-edge projects, investments will need to be made in public computing platforms and data." (The issue at a high level)
- "In 2020, nearly 70% of AI Ph.D. holders were recruited by industry, up from only 21% in 2004 (Figure 1A)." (Inputs to AI research, Human capital)
- "Using data from ImageNet, a well-known benchmark for image recognition, we find that in 2021 industry models were on average a staggering 29 times larger than their academic counterparts" (Inputs to AI research, Computing power)
- "For example, in Canada, demand from academia for the graphics processing units (GPUs) used to train machine-learning models has surged 25-fold since 2013" and "In recent years, the platform was only able to fill 20% of this demand." (Inputs to AI research, Computing power)
- "industry accounted for 96% of the biggest AI models in 2021, compared with 11% in 2010 (Figure 1E)" (Increasing dominance of industry in AI research)
- "However, the financial commitment to this initiative is $2.6 billion over a six-year period (Coldewey 2023), which remains modest in comparison to overall spending by industry." (Suggested policy solutions, Access to computing)
- "The Canada Research Chair program, for example, provides additional funds to pay competitive faculty salaries and grants to select researchers, enabling the country to recruit and retain top academics" (Suggested policy solutions, Supporting academics)

## Evidence and method

A policy article from a research institution. It uses data on Ph.D. hiring from the Computing Research Association, papers at the top 10 AI conferences, model sizes on ImageNet, and 20 AI benchmarks. Most figures come from the authors' 2023 paper in Science. It cites the Digital Research Alliance of Canada for the Canadian GPU demand figure.

## Limits

The data stop in 2022. The NAIRR figure of $2.6 billion is the Task Force proposal as TechCrunch reported it, not an appropriation. The article does not mention California or state compute programs. The policy proposals are the authors' views; Brookings says each publication "represents the sole views of its author(s)".
