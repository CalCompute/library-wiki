# Canada Directive on Automated Decision-Making (web)

## Citation

Treasury Board of Canada Secretariat. "Directive on Automated Decision-Making." Policy instrument under the Policy on Service and Digital. Effective April 1, 2019; page date modified 2025-06-24. https://www.tbs-sct.canada.ca/pol/doc-eng.aspx?id=32592. Read 2026-11-19.

## Bottom line

- The directive sets rules for the automated decision systems of Canada's federal departments. It covers each system in production that makes or supports an administrative decision about a client.
- Before production, the department must publish an algorithmic impact assessment. The assessment sets one of four impact levels, and each level has its own rules for notice, explanation, peer review, training, human involvement and approval.
- Systems for research and experimentation only, and systems in test environments, are outside the directive.

## Background

The Treasury Board of Canada Secretariat issues the directive under the Policy on Service and Digital. It took effect on April 1, 2019. Systems in place before June 24, 2025, have until June 24, 2026, to meet the updated rules. The directive has ten sections and three appendices: definitions, the four impact levels, and the requirements for each level. The Secretariat keeps the Algorithmic Impact Assessment tool.

## Key claims

- "This directive applies to any automated decision system in production used to make an administrative decision or a related assessment about a client." (5.1)
- "This directive excludes automated decision systems used solely for research and experimentation purposes, and those operating in test environments." (5.2)
- "Completing, approving and publishing the final results of an algorithmic impact assessment in an accessible format on the Open Government Portal prior to the production of any automated decision system." (6.1.1)
- "The department responsible for the automated decision system retains the right to access, test and monitor the automated decision system, including all released versions of proprietary software components, in case it is necessary for a specific audit, investigation, inspection, examination, enforcement action, or judicial proceeding, subject to safeguards against unauthorized disclosure." (6.2.6.1)
- "Before an automated decision system is in production, testing the data, information, and underlying model for accuracy, unintended biases and all factors that may unintentionally or unfairly impact the outcomes or violate human rights and freedoms." (6.3.1)
- "The final decision must be made by a human." (Appendix C, human involvement, Level III)
- Approval to operate: "Assistant deputy minister responsible for the program" for Levels I and II, "Deputy head" for Level III and "Treasury Board" for Level IV. (Appendix C, section 6.3.14)

## Evidence and method

A government policy instrument. It states duties for federal departments, by impact level. It gives no data on the systems that it covers.

## Limits

The directive covers only federal institutions under Canada's Policy on Service and Digital. It does not cover provinces, universities or private companies. It does not name generative AI. The page does not show the Algorithmic Impact Assessment questions or how they score a level. The scoring method and the four score ranges are in [Canada Algorithmic Impact Assessment tool (web)](Canada%20Algorithmic%20Impact%20Assessment%20tool%20%28web%29.md). It excludes research use, so it does not set rules for a research computing service.
