# Imagine Canada State of AI Adoption in Canadian Nonprofits (web)

## Citation

David Lasby and Jodene Baker. *The State of Artificial Intelligence Adoption in Canadian Nonprofits.* Imagine Canada, in collaboration with the Canadian Centre for Nonprofit Digital Resilience (CCNDR), 2026. ISBN 978-1-55401-467-5. https://imaginecanada.ca/sites/default/files/the-state-of-ai-adoption-in-canadian-nonprofits-en.pdf. Read 2026-11-09.

## Bottom line

- A survey report on how Canadian charities and nonprofits use AI, from 963 responses in 2025.
- 80% of respondents use AI in some way, but most use it for few tasks. Skills, time and knowledge predict use more than money does. Few organizations have AI policies or outside support.

## Background

Imagine Canada calls itself "a national, bilingual charitable organization whose cause is Canada’s charities". It published the report with CCNDR. The project was "made possible thanks to our partnership with Microsoft Elevate". The report says it is "an initial benchmark of AI adoption in the sector". Its licence is Creative Commons Attribution-NonCommercial-NoDerivatives 4.0.

## Key claims

- "Eighty percent of survey respondents say their organization is using AI in some way. For many, this use is modest: half use AI for three or fewer organizational activities and only a fifth use it for seven or more." (Key findings, p. i)
- "Skills, time and knowledge are stronger enablers of AI adoption than financial resources." (Key findings, p. i)
- "While financial resources matter less for getting started, they do shape how extensively AI is applied: sufficient funding increases the number of AI-supported activities by 1.3 on average." (Key findings, p. i)
- "Smaller organizations, with annual revenues less than $2 million and/or no paid staff, are substantially less likely to report using AI." (p. 7)
- "Only one tenth of organizations have formal AI-related policies (10%) and another fifth are developing them (21%)." (Key findings, p. ii)
- "Just 16% of organizations have drawn on a formal or informal group or network for AI-related support, and only 9% have engaged an external consultant." (Key findings, p. ii)
- "Building shared infrastructure, such as training programs, model policies and examples of practical use, can help ensure AI is applied responsibly, equitably and to the benefit of the full nonprofit community." (Summary and discussion, p. 19)

## Evidence and method

An online survey of charities and nonprofits from June 26 to September 6, 2025, with 963 responses. The report weights the responses by organization size, presence of paid staff, sub-sector and region (p. 1). It used two groups (p. 20):

- Registered charities that are not religious congregations, with annual revenues of $30,000 or more, reached by email.
- Other nonprofits, reached through a CCNDR mailing list and an email to nonprofit customers of Microsoft Canada.

The survey counts an organization as an AI user if it uses AI for at least one of 16 tasks. Multivariate analysis controls for size, sub-sector and region (pp. 12, 20). The report marks some estimates "use with caution" and withholds others (p. 21).

Other findings:

- Two thirds of organizations use AI for communications and fundraising. About half use it for data and information tasks (p. i).
- Organizations with annual revenues of $10 million or more drew on networks at 43%. Organizations with 100 or more paid staff drew on them at 44% (p. 18).
- Training is the most common support from networks, at 67% (p. ii).

## Limits

- The report says its 80% figure "should be viewed as the upper range of current adoption" (p. 2).
- It covers Canada only. Its dollar figures do not name a currency.
- It does not cover computing infrastructure, CalCompute or the Report.
