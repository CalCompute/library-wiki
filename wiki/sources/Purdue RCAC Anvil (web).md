# Purdue RCAC Anvil (web)

## Citation

Purdue University, Rosen Center for Advanced Computing (RCAC). "Anvil." RCAC website, no date. https://www.rcac.purdue.edu/compute/anvil. Fetched 2026-11-20; read 2026-11-20. Cached copy: `.cache/web/2026-11-20 Purdue RCAC Anvil.md`.

## Bottom line

- Anvil is a Purdue supercomputer funded by a $10 million National Science Foundation (NSF) award. It serves researchers through NSF's ACCESS program.
- It has 1,000 CPU nodes of 128 cores and 32 large memory nodes. It also has 16 nodes with 4 NVIDIA A100 GPUs and 21 nodes with 4 H100 GPUs.
- RCAC says that Anvil "enters production in 2021 and serves researchers for five years".

## Background

RCAC is Purdue University's research computing center in West Lafayette, Indiana. This is its overview page of Anvil. It gives the funding, the build partners, the investigators and the hardware of each sub-cluster. The page links a "Purchase Access" order page. Purdue's industry page sells Anvil time to companies ([Purdue RCAC industry partners Anvil external rates (web)](Purdue%20RCAC%20industry%20partners%20Anvil%20external%20rates%20%28web%29.md)).

## Key claims

- "Anvil, which is funded by a $10 million award from the National Science Foundation, significantly increases the capacity available to the NSF's [Advanced Cyberinfrastructure Coordination Ecosystem: Services & Support (ACCESS)](https://access-ci.org/) program, which serves tens of thousands of researchers across the U.S.." (Overview)
- "Anvil enters production in 2021 and serves researchers for five years. Additional funding from the NSF supports Anvil's operations and user support." (Overview)
- "Anvil is built in partnership with Dell and AMD and consists of 1,000 nodes with two 64-core AMD Epyc "Milan" processors each and will deliver over 1 billion CPU core hours to ACCESS each year, with a peak performance of 5.3 petaflops." (Overview)
- "Anvil also serves as an experiential learning laboratory for students to gain real-world experience using computing for their science, and for student interns to work with the Anvil team for construction and operation." (Overview)
- "| H | 21 | Two Intel Xeon Platinum 8468 CPUs @ 2.1GHz + Four NVIDIA H100 GPUs | 96 | 1 TB |" (Anvil Sub-Clusters table: sub-cluster, nodes, processors, cores, memory)
- "Anvil is funded under NSF award number 2005632." (Overview)

## Evidence and method

The operator's description of its own system. The hardware table is the operator's list. The figures for core hours and peak performance are the operator's statements, with no method.

## Limits

- The text names the A100 nodes but not the 21 H100 nodes of sub-cluster H. The page does not say when they came, or who paid for them.
- By its own words, Anvil serves for five years from 2021. The page does not say what happens after that period.
- It gives no prices. It does not say how a researcher applies through ACCESS.
