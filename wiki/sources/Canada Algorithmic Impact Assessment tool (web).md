## Bottom line

- The Algorithmic Impact Assessment (AIA) is the mandatory questionnaire of the Government of Canada for an automated decision system. It has 65 risk questions and 41 mitigation questions.
- The score puts a system at one of four impact levels, from Level I (little to no impact) to Level IV (very high impact). The level sets the mitigations that the *Directive on Automated Decision-Making* requires.
- A department completes the AIA at the start of design and again before production, and publishes the final results on the Open Government Portal.

## Background

TBS publishes this page to explain the AIA. The AIA supports the *Directive on Automated Decision-Making*, which is in [Canada Directive on Automated Decision-Making (web)](Canada%20Directive%20on%20Automated%20Decision-Making%20%28web%29.md). The page gives the risk and mitigation areas, the scoring method, the impact levels and instructions. The questionnaire itself is an online tool on the Open Government Portal.

## Key claims

- "The Algorithmic Impact Assessment (AIA) is a mandatory risk assessment tool intended to support the Treasury Board’s *Directive on Automated Decision-Making*." (1. Introduction)
- "It is composed of 65 risk questions and 41 mitigation questions." (1. Introduction)
- "It was developed in the open and is available to the public for sharing and reuse under an open licence." (1. Introduction)
- Table 3 gives a maximum raw impact score of 169 from six risk areas, and Table 4 a maximum mitigation score of 77. (2.1 Scoring)
- "if the mitigation score is 80% or more than the maximum attainable mitigation score, 15% is deducted from the raw impact score to yield the current score" (2.1 Scoring)
- "Level I | Little to no impact | 0% to 25%" and "Level IV | Very high impact | 76% to 100%" (Table 5)
- "While the measures are intended to reduce the identified risks, their implementation does not alter the impact level assigned to a project." (2.2 Impact levels)
- "The AIA should be completed a second time, prior to the production of the system, to validate that the results accurately reflect the system that was built." (3.1 When to complete the AIA)
- "The legal services unit must be consulted to identify and address legal risks arising from the development, procurement or use of an automated decision system." (3.2, Engage with legal services)
- "Departments are responsible for publishing the final results of the AIA in an accessible format and in both official languages on the Open Government Portal (subsection 6.1.1)." (3.3 Publishing the AIA)

## Evidence and method

An official government guidance page. Its tables give the structure of the questionnaire. They list six risk areas and two mitigation areas, each with its number of questions and maximum score. Another table gives the score ranges of the four impact levels. It says that the AIA draws on TBS consultations with "public institutions, academia and civil society".

## Limits

The page does not give the questions or their weights. It does not give the number of published assessments. It applies to federal departments of Canada, not to California or to a research computing service. It refers to the requirements of each impact level in Appendix C of the directive, and does not list them.
