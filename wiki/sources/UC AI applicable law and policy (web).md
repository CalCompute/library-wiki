# UC AI applicable law and policy (web)

## Citation

University of California Office of the President (UCOP). "Applicable Law and UC Policy." Web page of the University of California Artificial Intelligence website, date not stated. https://ai.universityofcalifornia.edu/governance-transparency/applicable-law-and-policy.html. Copy: [`raw/web/2026-11-09 UC AI applicable law and policy.md`](../../raw/web/2026-11-09%20UC%20AI%20applicable%20law%20and%20policy.md) (original: `.html` beside it). Read 2026-11-09.

## Bottom line

- The page lists 33 laws, policies and guidance documents. It says how each one applies to the use of AI at the University of California (UC).
- Most items cover privacy, data security, records, non-discrimination, conduct and procurement. The UC items are general policies, not policies about AI alone.

## Background

The page is in the AI Governance and Transparency section of the UC AI website. Each item has a heading, a link to the policy or law, and one paragraph. The items fall into these groups:

- UC ethics and conduct: the Statement of Ethical Values, the Standards of Ethical Conduct and Regents Policy 1111;
- UC non-discrimination policies: the Anti-Discrimination Policy, the Title IX policy and PACAOS-140 on disability;
- UC information security and records: IS-3, the Account and Authentication Management Standard (AAMS), RMP-2 and RMP-7;
- UC privacy: Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA) policies, the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act (GLBA) Compliance Plan, PACAOS-130 and the UC Statement of Privacy Values;
- the Academic Personnel Manual (APM): APM 010, APM 015 and APM 160;
- Business and Finance Bulletins: BUS-43 on procurement, BUS-49 and BUS-80;
- California: Executive Order N-12-23, the Government Operations Agency report on generative AI, the State Constitution and the Information Practices Act of 1977;
- federal orders: the Executive Order on the Safe, Secure, and Trustworthy Development and Use of AI;
- federal law: GLBA, HIPAA and the Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act (FERPA);
- other law: the European Union (EU) AI Act;
- guidance from the World Health Organization (WHO) and the United Nations Educational, Scientific and Cultural Organization (UNESCO);
- two documents from the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST).

## Key claims

- "Laws and UC policies —old and new— govern our use and deployment of AI by regulating data privacy and security, transparency and accountability, and protection against biases." (Applicable Law and UC Policy)
- "When using identified data, such as for training AI models, patients must be informed about how their data will be used and must provide consent for its use." (HIPAA: Policies and Glossary)
- "AI should be treated as any other software system in its effect on academic freedom." (APM 010: Academic Freedom)
- "In the context of AI, faculty should be vigilant about biases and errors in AI tools that can affect equity and academic rigor." (APM 015: Faculty Code of Conduct)
- "This policy ensures AI-related acquisitions follow university-wide guidelines, promoting responsible use and compliance with ethical and legal standards in AI system deployment and management." (BUS-43: Materiel Management)
- "was the first global legal framework on AI, and establishes clear requirements for AI developers and deployers." (The Privacy Act of 1974, 5 U.S.C. § 552a: The EU AI Act (European Commission))

## Evidence and method

An institutional guide. It links to each policy or law and gives UC's reading of how it applies to AI. The paragraphs are summaries, not policy text. This library has not read the linked documents.

## Limits

- The page does not say when it was last updated. It links the federal Executive Order on AI at a White House address dated Oct. 30, 2023. It does not say whether the order is still in force.
- It does not cover compute, research infrastructure or CalCompute.
- One heading names two laws: "The Privacy Act of 1974, 5 U.S.C. § 552a: The EU AI Act (European Commission)". Its text describes only the EU AI Act.
- Two items link to the same file, `riskmanagementgenerativeai.pdf`: the Government Operations Agency report on generative AI and the draft NIST Generative AI Profile.
- The item headed "AI Risk Management Framework" links to NIST AI 600-1, which its own text calls the "Generative AI Profile".
