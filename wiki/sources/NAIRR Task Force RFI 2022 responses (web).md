## Bottom line

- This is the federal compilation of the 23 public responses to the second request for information (RFI) of the NAIRR Task Force. The comment period ran from May 25 to June 30, 2022.
- NAIRR is the National Artificial Intelligence Research Resource.
- The RFI asked for comment on the task force's interim report and on how to carry out its recommendations. The responses do not state the views of the U.S. Government or the task force.
- The responses read for this page discuss who gets access first, build or buy for compute, proposal review, user agreements and the operating entity.

## Background

OSTP and NSF asked for comment on "the initial findings and recommendations contained in the interim report" of the task force (p. 1). Each response has a cover page and the respondent's text. By the RFI instructions, only the first 10 pages of each response count (p. 1).

The responses refer to the interim report's recommendations by number, for example 3-4 and 3-10. The first RFI is [NAIRR Task Force RFI 2021 responses (web)](NAIRR%20Task%20Force%20RFI%202021%20responses%20%28web%29.md). The task force's final plan came in January 2023 ([NAIRR Task Force final report 2023 (web)](NAIRR%20Task%20Force%20final%20report%202023%20%28web%29.md)).

## Key claims

Page numbers are the pages of the PDF.

- "This document contains the 23 responses received from academia, the private sector, and civil society. In accordance with the RFI instructions, only the first 10 pages of content were considered for each response." (introduction, p. 1)
- Stanford Institute for Human-Centered Artificial Intelligence (HAI): "We recommend the Task Force limit access to NAIRR to researchers at U.S. higher education institutions during the first three years of a pilot run." (p. 114)
- Stanford HAI: "In the short run, scaling up cloud credit programs, using commercial cloud services (similar to NSF’s CloudBank program), provides numerous efficiency advantages. In the long run, our research shows that it is more cost-effective to own infrastructure when computing demand is close to continuous." (p. 115)
- Stanford HAI: "Researchers should gain access to base-level compute and data access by default and then apply through a streamlined process to gain access at resources beyond the base level on a project-specific base." (p. 115)
- University of Southern California (USC) Information Sciences Institute: "To reduce this risk, setting up a consortium of partner organizations would be a reasonable approach. The Open Science Grid (OSG) is an example of a successful consortium to enable sharing of computing resources to serve the community." (p. 143)
- USC Information Sciences Institute: "Alternatively, a few (3-5) organizations could be responsible for day-to-day operations." (p. 144)
- USC Information Sciences Institute: "There should be provisions to tie some of the NAIRR allocations more explicitly to research awards." (p. 144)
- Internet2: "Internet2 suggests that NAIRR should work to integrate with existing agreements and technical implementations in place at academic institutions and incorporate any specifically negotiated terms for into existing agreements." (p. 88)
- Internet2: "In many cases, the agreements already in place for cloud services are heavily negotiated to meet a complex set of institutional requirements, state laws and requirements, and federal laws." (p. 88)

## Evidence and method

A government compilation of public comments. Each response gives its own views and evidence; the compilation does not weigh them.

For this page the introduction and table of contents (pp. 1 and 2) were read, with parts of four responses: Internet2 (pp. 87 to 88), Software & Information Industry Association (p. 112), Stanford HAI (pp. 113 to 115) and USC Information Sciences Institute (pp. 143 to 145).

A search of the whole text for California, state and regional terms found the other passages that name California.

## Limits

- The responses are the views of their authors. The document says that they do not represent the views of the U.S. Government or the task force (p. 1).
- This page reads parts of 4 of the 23 responses. The others are not summarized here.
- The introduction gives 23 responses (p. 1). The table of contents lists 24 entries (p. 2), and the document has 24 response cover pages.
- The page numbers in the table of contents are two less than the pages of the PDF.
- The copy is the Wayback Machine record of the ai.gov address. The document gives no publication date. The PDF properties give July 18, 2022, changed Aug. 11, 2022.
