# ACCESS acceptable use policy (web)

## Citation

ACCESS (Advanced Cyberinfrastructure Coordination Ecosystem: Services and Support), U.S. National Science Foundation. "Acceptable Use Policy." Version 1.4, web page, December 9, 2025. https://access-ci.org/acceptable-use/. Read 2026-11-12.

## Bottom line

- The ACCESS Acceptable Use Policy (AUP) is the user agreement for ACCESS services and for the Resource Provider systems that ACCESS allocates.
- Users accept it when they make an ACCESS ID and "every 12 months thereafter". It bans use "for financial gain nor any unlawful purpose".
- It has three parts: rules for all users, rules for Resource Provider resources, and rules for principal investigators (PIs).

## Background

ACCESS is the National Science Foundation (NSF) program that allocates national research computers. This page is version 1.4 of its AUP, dated December 9, 2025. The [ACCESS Allocations policy](ACCESS%20Allocations%20policy.md) refers to this address. Resource Providers such as Jetstream2 require compliance with it ([Jetstream2 policies (web)](Jetstream2%20policies%20%28web%29.md)).

## Key claims

- Renewal: "When you first create your ACCESS ID, and every 12 months thereafter, you will be asked to accept and re-acknowledge your responsibilities regarding use of ACCESS systems and services, as well as use of ACCESS-allocated resources at ACCESS Resource Provider sites." (preamble)
- Penalty: "Violations of this ACCESS AUP or Resource Provider policies may result in loss of access to both ACCESS services and Resource Provider resources." (preamble)
- Identity: "Your primary e-mail address must be your current organizational e-mail address." (all users, item 1)
- One account: "You will have only one ACCESS ID." (all users, item 4)
- Prohibited use: "You will not use ACCESS services nor allocated resources for financial gain nor any unlawful purpose, nor attempt to breach or circumvent any ACCESS or Resource Provider administrative controls or security controls." (all users, item 5)
- No guarantee: "You are responsible for backing up your data. Use of ACCESS services and allocated resources is at your own risk." (all users, item 7)
- Logging: "Logged information, including information provided by you for registration purposes, is used for administrative, operational, accounting, monitoring and security purposes." (all users, item 8)
- Open research: "Allocations on Resource Provider resources are awarded for open research intended for publication together with support for educational and training activities." (Resource Provider resources, item 6)
- Who can stop access: "Access-granting organizations, your ACCESS allocation’s Principal Investigator (PI), and ACCESS Resource Providers are entitled to regulate, suspend or terminate your access, and you agree to comply with their instructions." (Resource Provider resources, item 5)
- PI vetting: "PIs are responsible for properly vetting all people that they add as authorized members of their project allocations". (PIs, item 1)
- Research security: "PIs are responsible for ensuring that all authorized users of their project allocations are free from any research security prohibitions defined by the National Science Foundation or other federal agencies." (PIs, item 3)
- Generative AI: "allocation request proposals and supporting documentation are prohibited from being uploaded to non-approved generative AI tools." (PIs, item 4)

## Evidence and method

This is a policy text. It has no data. It gives a version number and a date.

## Limits

- The policy does not define "financial gain". It does not say if a startup or a company can use ACCESS for open research.
- It names no steps between a violation and loss of access, such as a warning.
- It does not say how long ACCESS keeps logged information.
- It refers to the ACCESS Code of Conduct and Privacy Policy, which the library has not read.
- Its text is close to the [NRP Acceptable Use Policy (web)](NRP%20Acceptable%20Use%20Policy%20%28web%29.md) on renewal every 12 months, "financial gain", backups and logging. Neither text says which came first.
