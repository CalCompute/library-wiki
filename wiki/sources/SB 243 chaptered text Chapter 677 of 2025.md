## Bottom line

- SB 243 adds Chapter 22.6, "Companion Chatbots", to Division 8 of the Business and Professions Code, from section 22601.
- An operator must tell a user that a companion chatbot is not human, if a reasonable person would think that it is.
- An operator must keep and publish a protocol against suicidal ideation, suicide and self-harm content.
- For a user that the operator knows is a minor, the operator must disclose the artificial intelligence (AI) and give a reminder every three hours.
- From July 1, 2027, an operator reports each year to the Office of Suicide Prevention.
- A person with an injury in fact can sue for the greater of actual damages or $1,000 for each violation.

## Background

The bill's author is Senator Padilla, as the Digest prints the name. The Governor approved it on Oct. 13, 2025. The text holds the Legislative Counsel's Digest and the new chapter in six sections. A 2026 bill by the same author removes the minor-user rules from section 22602. It adds a chapter on companion chatbots and children ([SB 1119 companion chatbot child safety bill text](SB%201119%20companion%20chatbot%20child%20safety%20bill%20text.md)). The library's copy comes from the Legislature's website through the Wayback Machine.

## Key claims

- "“Companion chatbot” means an artificial intelligence system with a natural language interface that provides adaptive, human-like responses to user inputs and is capable of meeting a user’s social needs, including by exhibiting anthropomorphic features and being able to sustain a relationship across multiple interactions." (Bus. & Prof. Code § 22601(b)(1))
- "If a reasonable person interacting with a companion chatbot would be misled to believe that the person is interacting with a human, an operator shall issue a clear and conspicuous notification indicating that the companion chatbot is artificially generated and not human." (§ 22602(a))
- "The operator shall publish details on the protocol required by this subdivision on the operator’s internet website." (§ 22602(b)(2))
- "Provide by default a clear and conspicuous notification to the user at least every three hours for continuing companion chatbot interactions that reminds the user to take a break and that the companion chatbot is artificially generated and not human." (§ 22602(c)(2))
- "The office shall post data from a report required by this section on its internet website." (§ 22603(c))
- "Damages in an amount equal to the greater of actual damages or one thousand dollars ($1,000) per violation." (§ 22605(b))

## Limits

The bill gives no cost estimate and no operative date for the chapter. Only the yearly report has a start date. "Companion chatbot" does not include a customer service bot, some video game bots or a voice assistant device (§ 22601(b)(2)). The minor-user rules apply only to a user that the operator "knows is a minor". The copy shows the 2025 text; SB 1119 gives the later change to section 22602.

The bill does not mention research compute or CalCompute.
