# Campus Explorer university extension programs (web)

## Citation

Campus Explorer. "What's an Extension Program?" Student resources article, September 27, 2021. https://www.campusexplorer.com/student-resources/university-extension-programs/. Read 2026-11-05.

## Bottom line

- A commercial college-search site defines a university extension program as non-credit, non-degree classes open to anyone. It separates extension from continuing education, which leads to certificates or licences.
- It gives UC San Diego and UCLA Extension as examples.

## Background

A short explainer on a college-search website. It defines extension programs, who they serve, how they differ from other adult education and from continuing education, and gives "quick tips".

## Key claims

- "An extension program is a department within a college that offers classes to local residents interested in learning new skills or information. Extension programs were designed to educate entire communities. Unlike four-year universities and colleges, and community colleges, extension programs aren't completed for credit." (What's an extension program?)
- "Anyone, no matter his or her age or education level, can take part in an extension program. Though classes are taught at a college level, there are no enrollment requirements." (Who are these programs for?)
- "Workforce training is a great example of an extension program. Throughout the country, workforce training programs offer working adults the chance to learn new technology and keep up-to-date on new information regarding their career field." (What kinds of extension programs exist?)
- "Continuing education is designed for adults who are seeking additional certifications or licenses, while extension program students don't typically receive any kind of documentation." (Are extension programs any different from continuing education courses?)

## Evidence and method

An unsourced explainer. No data and no author named.

## Limits

The definitions are the site's own. UC Extension units do grant certificates, which the article's non-credit definition does not cover. Do not cite this page in a public report; use a UC Extension source instead.
