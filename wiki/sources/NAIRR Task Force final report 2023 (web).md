# NAIRR Task Force final report 2023 (web)

## Citation

National Artificial Intelligence Research Resource Task Force. "Strengthening and Democratizing the U.S. Artificial Intelligence Innovation Ecosystem: An Implementation Plan for a National Artificial Intelligence Research Resource." Final report, U.S. National Science Foundation (NSF) and White House Office of Science and Technology Policy (OSTP), January 2023. https://nsf-gov-resources.nsf.gov/files/NAIRR-TF-Final-Report-2023.pdf. Read 2026-11-11. Copy: `raw/web/2026-11-11 NAIRR Task Force final report 2023.pdf`.

## Bottom line

- The federal task force gives a plan for a National Artificial Intelligence Research Resource (NAIRR). It is a federated set of computing, data, testbed and software resources from many providers, with one portal.
- One federal agency is the administrative home. A Steering Committee of agencies sets direction, and an independent Operating Entity runs daily operations but not all of the hardware.
- The task force estimates $2.6 billion over an initial six years. $2.25 billion of that is for the resources.

## Background

The National AI Initiative Act of 2020 told NSF, with OSTP, to set up the task force. OSTP and NSF launched it in June 2021 with 12 members from academia, government and private organizations. The NSF Director and the OSTP Director sign its cover letter to the President and Congress. The report also presents "a pilot option" (p. vii). NSF launched the NAIRR pilot on Jan. 24, 2024 ([NSF NAIRR pilot launch release 2024-01-24 (web)](NSF%20NAIRR%20pilot%20launch%20release%202024-01-24%20%28web%29.md)).

## Key claims

- "OSTP and NSF formally launched the NAIRR Task Force in June 2021, appointing 12 leading experts equally representing academia, government, and private organizations." (cover letter, p. iii)
- "The NAIRR should be established with four measurable goals in mind, namely to (1) spur innovation, (2) increase diversity of talent, (3) improve capacity, and (4) advance trustworthy AI." (Executive Summary, p. v)
- "These users should be U.S.-based or affiliated with U.S. organizations, to include academic institutions, non-profit organizations, and startups or small businesses." (Executive Summary, p. v)
- "The NAIRR administration and governance should follow a cooperative stewardship model, whereby a single Federal agency serves as the administrative home for NAIRR operations and a Steering Committee comprising principals from Federal agencies with equities in AI research drives the strategic direction of the NAIRR." (Executive Summary, p. v)
- "A Program Management Office within the administrative home agency should provide funding and oversight for an independent Operating Entity that manages the day-to-day operations of the NAIRR." (Executive Summary, p. v)
- "The Operating Entity should not itself operate the totality of the computer hardware that composes the NAIRR; instead, computing, along with data, testing, and training resources, should be delivered as services by partner resource providers selected through Federal agency or multi-agency funding opportunities." (Executive Summary, p. v)
- "The Operating Entity should design the NAIRR cyberinfrastructure to consist of multiple tiers, starting with two primary zones: an open science zone "NAIRR-Open" and a secure zone "NAIRR-Secure."" (Executive Summary, p. vi)
- "In phase one, Congress should authorize and appropriate funds to establish the NAIRR." (Executive Summary, p. vi)
- "In order to achieve its vision and goals, the Task Force estimates the budget for the NAIRR as $2.6 billion over an initial six-year period. The bulk of this investment ($2.25 billion) is to fund the resources to be made accessible via the NAIRR, through appropriations to multiple Federal agencies." (Executive Summary, p. vii)
- "Resource providers should be brought online every two years with a six-year lifetime, so that a new $750 million investment is made every two years to ensure that the NAIRR resources remain state-of-the-art. The Operating Entity will require between $55 million and $65 million per year to support the coordination and management of NAIRR activities." (Executive Summary, p. vii)

## Evidence and method

A federal advisory report with an implementation plan. The task force held 11 public meetings, heard 65 experts and read responses to two requests for information (cover letter, p. iii). The budget comes from "recent costs of advanced computing resources", estimated usage and expected growth of the user community (p. vii). For this page the cover letter and the Executive Summary (pp. ii to vii) were read, with searches of the body for the governance terms.

## Limits

- The report is a plan of January 2023. It does not report on the pilot that followed.
- Its budget figures are estimates from 2022 and 2023 costs.
- It plans a federal resource. It does not discuss state programs.
