## Bottom line

- SB 1120 amends Health and Safety Code section 1367.01 and Insurance Code section 10123.135.
- A health care service plan or disability insurer must meet a list of conditions when it uses artificial intelligence (AI) or other software for utilization review.
- The tool must use the patient's own clinical information and must not decide from a group dataset alone.
- The tool must not deny, delay or modify care on medical necessity. Only a licensed physician or a competent licensed health care professional decides medical necessity.
- The tool is open to inspection by the Department of Managed Health Care.

## Background

The bill's author is Senator Becker, as the Digest prints the name. The Governor approved it on Sept. 28, 2024. The text holds the Legislative Counsel's Digest and three sections. Sections 1 and 2 print the two amended code sections in full. The AI text is subdivision (k) of section 1367.01, and subdivision (j) of section 10123.135. The library's copy comes from the Legislature's website through the Wayback Machine.

## Key claims

- "The artificial intelligence, algorithm, or other software tool does not base its determination solely on a group dataset." (Health & Saf. Code § 1367.01(k)(1)(B))
- "The artificial intelligence, algorithm, or other software tool does not supplant health care provider decisionmaking." (§ 1367.01(k)(1)(D))
- "The artificial intelligence, algorithm, or other software tool is open to inspection for audit or compliance reviews by the department pursuant to Section 1381 and by the State Department of Health Care Services pursuant to applicable state and federal law." (§ 1367.01(k)(1)(G))
- "The artificial intelligence, algorithm, or other software tool’s performance, use, and outcomes are periodically reviewed and revised to maximize accuracy and reliability." (§ 1367.01(k)(1)(I))
- "Notwithstanding paragraph (1), the artificial intelligence, algorithm, or other software tool shall not deny, delay, or modify health care services based, in whole or in part, on medical necessity." (§ 1367.01(k)(2))

## Limits

The bill gives no cost estimate and no operative date. The copy does not mark which words of the two sections are new. The conditions apply to utilization review "based in whole or in part on medical necessity". State guidance can follow federal rules "within one year" (§ 1367.01(k)(5)); the bill does not say if a federal rule exists. The capture is of Aug. 1, 2026, so a later change does not show.

The bill does not mention research compute or CalCompute.
