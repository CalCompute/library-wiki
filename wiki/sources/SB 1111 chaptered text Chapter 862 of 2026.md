## Bottom line

- SB 1111 amends Civil Code section 3344, the civil action for the use of a name, voice, signature, photograph or likeness with no consent. A voice or likeness now includes a digital replica.
- It adds Penal Code section 540: where a crime requires false impersonation, the use of a digital replica to impersonate another person counts.
- It removes the presumption in section 3344 for the photograph or likeness of an employee that is incidental to a publication.

## Background

Senator Ashby wrote the bill. The text holds the Legislative Counsel's Digest and two sections. Section 1 prints Civil Code section 3344 in full. Section 2 adds Chapter 9, "Offense Involving the Use of Artificial Intelligence Technology", to Title 13 of Part 1 of the Penal Code. The original is the Legislature's XML file of the chaptered text, in its California Markup Language (CAML) format.

## Key claims

- "For the purposes of this section, a voice or likeness includes a digital replica, as defined in Section 3344.1." (Civ. Code § 3344(f))
- "For the purposes of any provision of this code in which the false impersonation of another is a required element, including, without limitation, Sections 528.5, 529, and 530, false impersonation includes the use of a digital replica with the intent to impersonate another." (Pen. Code § 540(a))
- The Digest defines a digital replica as "a computer-generated, highly realistic electronic representation that is readily identifiable as the voice or visual likeness of an individual in which the actual individual either did not actually perform or appear, or the actual individual did perform or appear, but the fundamental character of the performance or appearance has been materially altered." (Legislative Counsel's Digest)
- A person who violates section 3344 is liable for "an amount equal to the greater of seven hundred fifty dollars ($750) or the actual damages suffered by them as a result of the unauthorized use". (Civ. Code § 3344(a)(1))
- The respondent must obey a court order to stop the use "within two business days from the day the order is served, unless otherwise required by the order." (§ 3344(a)(2))

## Limits

The bill text does not define "digital replica" itself. It points to Civil Code section 3344.1, and the library has no source page that quotes that section. The copy does not mark which words of section 3344 are new. The Digest says that subdivision (f) is the addition and that the bill removes the employee presumption. The bill gives no operative date.

The bill does not mention research compute or CalCompute.
