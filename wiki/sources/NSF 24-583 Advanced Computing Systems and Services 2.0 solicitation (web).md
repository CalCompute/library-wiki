## Bottom line

- The Advanced Computing Systems and Services (ACSS) 2.0 solicitation raises Category I awards to between $10,000,000 and $20,000,000. It lowers the operating cost share to 15 percent of acquisition cost a year. Category II stays at up to $5,000,000.
- Allocation can now go through the National Artificial Intelligence Research Resource (NAIRR) as well as the Advanced Cyberinfrastructure Coordination Ecosystem: Services & Support (ACCESS). The program is "not currently accepting proposals" and waits for a new solicitation.

## Background

NSF posted this solicitation on June 18, 2024. It replaces NSF 23-518 ([NSF 23-518 Advanced Computing Systems and Services solicitation (web)](NSF%2023-518%20Advanced%20Computing%20Systems%20and%20Services%20solicitation%20%28web%29.md)). It has two deadlines: Oct. 29, 2024 for Category I and June 24, 2025 for Category II. The award type is a cooperative agreement. The anticipated funding amount is $60,000,000 for one to three awards.

## Key claims

- "The award amount for Category I has been updated to be between $10,000,000 and $20,000,000 per award for up to five years." (Revision Notes)
- "User support and operating costs have been updated to be up to 15% of the total resource acquisition cost per year for each deployed Category I or Category II system/service for up to five years; projected annual operating costs above 15% of the total resource acquisition cost must be discussed with a cognizant Program Officer prior to submission" (Revision Notes)
- "Such allocation and support services are expected to be coordinated through the NSF-funded Advanced Cyberinfrastructure Coordination Ecosystem: Services & Support (ACCESS) , the National AI Research Resource, or an NSF-approved alternative as may emerge." (Synopsis of Program)
- "User support may be provided via various means, e.g., training sessions, documentation, direct engagement in response to tickets created via the ACCESS program, or integration of novel, NSF-funded software tools." (Synopsis of Program)
- "Submission from or partnership with EPSCoR institutions and institutions that have not previously received ACSS awards is encouraged." (Synopsis of Program)
- Category I proposals need performance and reliability metrics, "including minimization of carbon output and energy usage, that will be used by NSF for acceptance of the resource or service" (II, Category I)
- "The cooperative agreements will have an extensive section of Special Conditions relating to the period of performance, Project Execution Plan including plan to develop performance metrics, recipient responsibilities, NSF responsibilities, joint NSF- recipient responsibilities, funding and funding schedule, reporting requirements, key personnel, and other conditions." (VII.B)
- Extra reporting "may include, for example, monthly and quarterly reports reverse-/site visits, and other requirements to enable NSF oversight of the award." (VII.C)

## Evidence and method

A federal funding solicitation. The library read the revision notes, the summary of program requirements and Sections I to IV. In Section V it read the reliability, Concept of Operations and budget rules. It also read the additional review criteria in Section VI and Section VII on award conditions and reporting.

The solicitation keeps the 5 percent monthly downtime limit and the ban on voluntary committed cost sharing. It names the Established Program to Stimulate Competitive Research (EPSCoR). Other federal agencies and federally funded research and development centers (FFRDCs) can apply after they contact the program.

## Limits

- The solicitation sets rules for proposals. It does not report awards. [NSF award 2614022 Bridges-3 API record (web)](NSF%20award%202614022%20Bridges-3%20API%20record%20%28web%29.md) is a Category I award of $10,000,000 from the same program office.
- Section III prints "$20,00,000" as the Category I upper limit. The summary and Section V.B give "$20,000,000".
- The 96 percent job completion rule of NSF 23-518 does not appear in this copy. The "Operations Plan" section is now "Concept of Operations".
- It does not say how a resource splits its time between ACCESS and NAIRR.
