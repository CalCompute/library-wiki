# WEF Making Agentic AI Work for Government 2026 (web)

## Citation

World Economic Forum (WEF), in collaboration with Capgemini. "Making Agentic AI Work for Government: A Readiness Framework." Insight report, World Economic Forum, April 2026. https://reports.weforum.org/docs/WEF_Making_Agentic_AI_Work_for_Government_A_Readiness_Framework_2026.pdf. Read 2026-11-09.

## Bottom line

- The report scores 70 core government functions on agentic AI potential and implementation complexity, and puts each in a high, medium or low readiness area. It finds that 50 percent combine significant potential with manageable complexity.
- It calls agentic AI a model of augmentation rather than replacement for public servants. It names workforce capabilities, cloud readiness and legal clarity as conditions for deployment.

## Background

The WEF wrote the report with the consulting firm Capgemini. Its forewords come from digital officials of Berlin, Bavaria and the United Arab Emirates. Agentic AI is AI that can plan, decide and act across the steps of a workflow. The report writes for national and regional governments in general. The WEF says the findings do not necessarily represent its own views.

## Key claims

- "The review of 70 core government functions indicates that 50% combine significant agentic AI potential with manageable implementation complexity" (Executive summary, p. 5)
- "Crucially, agentic AI is best understood as a model of augmentation rather than replacement." (Section 1.1, p. 7)
- "Agentic systems benefit from scalable compute resources and modern development environments." (Section 2.4, p. 23)
- "Governments with relevant in-house expertise or that are accessible through trusted partners are better positioned; those facing skills gaps will need to invest in recruitment, training or external support before ambitious deployments become feasible." (Section 2.4, p. 23)
- "Two primary workforce risks emerge with agentic AI: excessive reliance on automation and resistance to change." (Section 2.4, p. 24)
- "Therefore, it is critical to invest in AI literacy, skills development and change management to drive adoption and responsible use." (Section 2.4, p. 24)
- "Governments that begin with high-readiness functions and treat pilots as structured learning exercises are better positioned to scale responsibly." (Conclusion, p. 30)

## Evidence and method

An insight report from an international membership organisation and a consulting firm. Appendix A1 describes the method: a literature review, consultation with government officials and experts, and expert scoring of each function on sub-criteria. The scores are global baselines. Section 3 gives case studies of deployments, such as infrastructure permit procedures in Germany.

## Limits

The scores are expert judgments, not measured results. The report says that "Global scores are baselines" and that each jurisdiction must adapt them (Executive summary, p. 5). It does not mention California. It is about the use of AI in government operations, not about a public compute resource.
