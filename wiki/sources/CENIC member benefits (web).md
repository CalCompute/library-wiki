# CENIC member benefits (web)

## Citation

Corporation for Education Network Initiatives in California (CENIC). "Benefits of CENIC Membership." Web page, date not stated. https://cenic.org/about/member-benefits. Read 2026-11-18.

## Bottom line

- CENIC lists what its members get from the California Research and Education Network (CalREN). There are four groups: networking expertise, economies of scale, community and governance.
- CENIC charges no member per bit. It buys circuits and equipment in bulk, obtains federal and state subsidies for eligible members, and gives each member a project manager. Charter Associates sit on the Board.

## Background

CENIC is the nonprofit that runs CalREN for California's research and education institutions ([CENIC About (web)](CENIC%20About%20%28web%29.md)). This page sets out the benefits of membership. [CENIC membership (web)](CENIC%20membership%20%28web%29.md) defines the five membership classes.

## Key claims

- "Each year, 20 million Californians at nearly 12,000 institutions use CalREN, enjoying supremely reliable, efficient, and cost-effective access, as well as these benefits:" (introduction)
- "CalREN’s settlement-free peering services (with nearly 100 peers) provide high-speed access to many data-intensive sites frequented by researchers, students, and educators, such as AWS, Google, and Microsoft, as well as to other research and education networks around the world." (Cloud connectivity and content services)
- "CENIC does not charge any members on a per-bit basis." (Unlimited broadband usage)
- "CENIC represents the networking needs of California’s entire K-20 research and education community, enabling bulk purchasing power through our economies of scale that results in deeply discounted pricing for network hardware and software that would never be available to an individual institution purchasing these on its own." (Substantial discounts on circuits and equipment)
- "CENIC obtains federal E-Rate discounts, ranging from 20% to 90%, on both one-time and recurring costs for Internet access and upgrades for eligible libraries and K–12 schools." (Federal and state subsidy assistance)
- "Each member organization is assigned a CENIC project manager who works with their IT leaders to facilitate that organization’s networking needs" (Program Management Office)
- "CENIC Charter Associates are represented on the Board of Directors and participate extensively in CENIC decision-making." (Decision-making power regarding your high-speed network)
- "CENIC accomplishes this by functioning both as a high-performance network operator and as an administrator and fiduciary as may be required under specific legislative appropriations." (Advocating for your technology needs at the state and federal level)

## Evidence and method

A member-facing page on the publisher's own website. It gives the benefits in four groups and a few figures: the user and institution counts, the number of peers and the E-Rate discount range. It also names the 50% California Teleconnect Fund discount and a 24/7/365 Network Operations Center (NOC). It names the collaboration groups C2TAG (CENIC Community Technology Affinity Group) and SEC TAC (CENIC Security Technical Advisory Council), and a biennial member conference.

## Limits

- The page has no date and gives no fees.
- It does not say which benefits each membership class gets, apart from the network portfolio for Charter Associates.
- It gives no figures for the size of the bulk discounts or the subsidies obtained. [CENIC 2024-2026 Community Report PDF (web)](CENIC%202024-2026%20Community%20Report%20PDF%20%28web%29.md) gives subsidy figures.
- It does not name the legislative appropriations for which CENIC acts as "administrator and fiduciary".
- The page says CENIC began "nearly thirty years ago". [CENIC membership (web)](CENIC%20membership%20%28web%29.md) gives 1996 as the founding year.
