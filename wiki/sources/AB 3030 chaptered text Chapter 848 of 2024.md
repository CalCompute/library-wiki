## Bottom line

- AB 3030 adds Chapter 2.13, "Artificial Intelligence in Health Care Services", to Division 2 of the Health and Safety Code. The chapter is one section, 1339.75.
- A health facility, clinic or physician's office that uses generative artificial intelligence (GenAI) for patient communications about clinical information must add a disclaimer.
- The communication must also tell the patient how to contact a human.
- The rule does not apply when a licensed or certified human provider reads and reviews the communication.

## Key claims

- "A disclaimer that indicates to the patient that the communication was generated by generative artificial intelligence." (Health & Saf. Code § 1339.75(a)(1))
- "For audio communications, the disclaimer shall be provided verbally at the start and the end of the interaction." (§ 1339.75(a)(1)(C))
- "Clear instructions describing how a patient may contact a human health care provider, employee of the health facility, clinic, physician’s office, or office of a group provider, or other appropriate person." (§ 1339.75(a)(2))
- "If a communication is generated by generative artificial intelligence and read and reviewed by a human licensed or certified health care provider, the requirements of subdivision (a) do not apply." (§ 1339.75(b))
- "“Patient clinical information” means information relating to the health status of a patient. This information does not include administrative matters, including, but not limited to, appointment scheduling, billing, or other clerical or business matters." (§ 1339.75(c)(7))

## Limits

The bill gives no cost estimate, no operative date and no penalty amount. It points to existing enforcement (§ 1339.75(d)). For a health facility or clinic, that is the licensing rules. For a physician, it is the Medical Board of California or the Osteopathic Medical Board of California. The copy shows the 2024 text.

The bill does not mention research compute or CalCompute.
