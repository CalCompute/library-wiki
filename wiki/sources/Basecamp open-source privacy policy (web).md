# Basecamp open-source privacy policy (web)

## Citation

37signals LLC. "Privacy policy." Last updated April 20, 2023. Published as a Markdown file in the basecamp/policies repository on GitHub. https://raw.githubusercontent.com/basecamp/policies/master/privacy/index.md. Read 2026-11-19.

## Bottom line

- 37signals, the maker of Basecamp and HEY, publishes its privacy policy as an open text file. The policy says the company collects only what it needs and never sells user data.
- It gives fixed deletion times. Account content goes within 60 days after account deletion. Trashed content stays about 25 days, then up to 30 more days on servers.
- The company answers government requests for user data only when legal process compels it, or in some emergencies.

## Background

37signals LLC is a U.S. software company. The policy covers all its products. It covers site visitors, prospective customers and customers. It does not cover data about a customer's end users that 37signals processes for the customer. The file is in a public repository of the company's policies. The copy read gives no licence statement.

## Key claims

- "Our guiding principle is to collect only what we need." (What we collect and why)
- "We keep this content as long as your account is active. If you delete your account, we’ll delete the content within 60 days." (Product interactions)
- "No 37signals human looks at your content except for limited purposes with your express permission" (When we access or disclose your information)
- "Our policy is to not respond to government requests for user data unless we are compelled by legal process or in limited circumstances in the event of an emergency request." (When required under applicable law)
- "At 37signals, we strive to apply the same data rights to all customers, regardless of their location." (Your rights with respect to your information)
- "Anything you trash in your product accounts while they are active will be kept in an accessible trash can for about 25 days (it varies a little by product)." (What happens when you delete content in your product accounts)

## Evidence and method

A company privacy policy of about 3,700 words in plain language, in Markdown. It states practices, a list of user rights and the laws it uses for government requests, such as the Stored Communications Act.

## Limits

It is the policy of one commercial software company, not a research computing service. It does not say which licence covers reuse of the text; the library did not read the repository's licence file. Its last update is April 20, 2023.
