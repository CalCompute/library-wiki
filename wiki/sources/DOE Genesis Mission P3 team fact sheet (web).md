## Bottom line

- The public-private partnerships (P3) team of the Genesis Mission connects DOE and the National Nuclear Security Administration (NNSA) with outside organizations.
- Representatives from each of the 17 National Laboratories make up the team. A rotating council of six people assesses new partnerships and gives partners a single point of contact. The Genesis Mission Consortium does non-technical contract management.

## Background

This one-page fact sheet is one of five DOE sheets on the National Laboratory teams of the Genesis Mission ([DOE Genesis Mission Collaboration page (web)](DOE%20Genesis%20Mission%20Collaboration%20page%20%28web%29.md)). It has three parts: execution across DOE and NNSA, partner intake and management, and "Hooks", the integration points for partners.

## Key claims

- "The ambition of the Genesis Mission extends beyond the Department of Energy (DOE) to encompass the entirety of the nation’s AI capabilities and needs." (introduction)
- "P3 supports conversations and matchmaking between potential partners, documents Genesis Mission capabilities, and facilitates integration of partners with the American Science and Security Platform." (introduction)
- "A rotating council of six people will assess new partnerships from a technical perspective and provide a single point of contact for partners and assist in matching partners with the Genesis Mission." (Execution Across DOE and NNSA)
- "This consortium will do non-technical contract management and meeting planning for collaborators of the Genesis Mission." (Partner Intake and Management)
- "This will allow partners to bring data, models, computing, and other resources through open APIs and services to the platform." (Hooks)

## Evidence and method

A short federal fact sheet. It describes the make-up and tasks of one team and the split of work between the team and the Genesis Mission Consortium. It gives no names, figures or dates.

## Limits

- The sheet does not name the six council members or say how often the council rotates.
- It does not name the open application programming interfaces (APIs) or say what a partner must do to connect a resource.
- It does not give a budget or a partner count.
