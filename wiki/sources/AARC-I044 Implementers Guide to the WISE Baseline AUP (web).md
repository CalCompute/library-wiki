# AARC-I044 Implementers Guide to the WISE Baseline AUP (web)

## Citation

Groep, David; Ian Neilson; and WISE SCI Working Group members. "Implementers Guide to the WISE Baseline Acceptable Use Policy." Document AARC-I044. GÉANT on behalf of the AARC project, Feb. 26, 2019. PDF, 15 pages. https://aarc-community.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/03/AARC-I044-Implementers-Guide-to-the-WISE-Baseline-AUP.pdf. Read 2026-11-12.

## Bottom line

- The guide tells research infrastructures how to use the WISE Baseline Acceptable Use Policy (AUP) in practice.
- Its aim is that a user sees one AUP, "preferably just a single time", and can then use many services. The ten baseline clauses stay "identical"; a provider adds its own terms on top.
- It gives worked examples for a single research community and for a "user-first" service that hosts many communities.

## Background

GÉANT published this guide on behalf of the AARC project, with Horizon2020 funding under Grant Agreement No. 730941 (AARC2). It explains the template in [WISE Baseline AUP v1 (web)](WISE%20Baseline%20AUP%20v1%20%28web%29.md). The guide uses the terms "membership management service (MMS)" and "Virtual Organization (VO)".

## Key claims

- Burden on the user: "to reduce the burden on the user and increase the likelihood that they will read the AUP, the number of times a user is presented with such notices must be kept to a minimum, preferably just a single time." (section 1, p. 3)
- Aim: to "provide a common baseline set of criteria for acceptable use and terms and conditions for the professional use of IT infrastructures for research globally – and thereby ease the trust of users across infrastructures" (section 1, p. 3)
- Fixed text: "For a Baseline AUP it is therefore critical that the basic AUP requirements are both common as well as immutable." (section 2, p. 4)
- Layers: "the Baseline AUP is a set of ten ‘commandments’ that are identical and equally applicable to all services and infrastructures" (section 2, p. 4)
- Where to show it: "the membership management service (MMS) is the most appropriate component to present and manage AUP acceptance." (section 1, p. 3)
- Purpose binding: "This purpose binding should be done when the user petitions access to a VO." (section 6.1, p. 10)
- Auto-approval: "auto-approval flows should be limited to those VOs that only connect Services owned and operated by or on behalf of the MMS operator itself." (section 6.1, p. 11)

## Evidence and method

This is a technical guide from a research project. It gives a model, sample AUP text and a sample enrolment form. It has no data on adoption or on how many users read an AUP.

## Limits

- The guide does not name the infrastructures that use the baseline AUP.
- It has a European focus. It names the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) for privacy notices, and no U.S. law.
- It dates from 2019. It does not say if later versions of the AUP change the advice.
- The library read sections 1 to 6.2. It did not read the end of section 6.2, section 6.3 or the references.
