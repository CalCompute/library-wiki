# TACC user policies (web)

## Citation

Texas Advanced Computing Center (TACC), The University of Texas at Austin. "TACC Acceptable Use Policy." Web page, August 11, 2026. https://tacc.utexas.edu/use-tacc/user-policies/. Read 2026-11-12.

## Bottom line

- The TACC Acceptable Use Policy (AUP) sets the rules for all users of TACC computers, networks and data. Users reverify their identity and accept the latest AUP "at least once a year".
- A principal investigator (PI) must hold a PhD, show work in the field or be a UT Austin employee. The PI must work at "a US domiciled institution", which can be a for-profit entity. Users can be outside the United States if they work with a U.S. PI.
- Data belongs to the PI and the project. TACC keeps project data for 30 days after a project ends.

## Background

TACC is a research computing center at The University of Texas at Austin (UT Austin). This AUP has a revision history from December 1, 2014, to August 11, 2026. The last change, a "Yearly Review", concerns email account validity.

## Key claims

- Scope: "Individuals who receive access or an allocation to any TACC Information Resource, regardless of how they receive it, are required to comply with UT Austin’s Information Resource Policy as well as UT Austin’s Code of Conduct". (General)
- Yearly check: users shall "Reverify their identity and institution affiliation as well as accept the latest version of this AUP at least once a year." (Users shall)
- No profit: users shall not "Be paid, or otherwise profit from non-sanctioned activities using an Information Resource (cryptocurrency generation, etc.)" (Users shall not)
- PI qualification: a PI shall "Either hold a Doctor of Philosophy (PhD) degree from an accredited university, or provide evidence of work in their proposed field of study, or be an employee of UT Austin." (A PI shall)
- Eligible institutions: a PI and Allocation Manager shall "Be currently employed and/or appointed by a US domiciled institution that is eligible for receiving and executing US federal, state, or local research grants. This includes educational institutions, government labs, non-profit or for-profit entities within the United States (note: Users may come from outside the US, if affiliated with a US PI)." (A PI and Allocation Manager shall)
- Known users: a PI admits only users who meet one of two identity criteria. The first is that the PI or Allocation Manager "has established a personal, academic or professional relationship with the User". The second is the same relationship with the user's advisor or supervisor. (same section)
- Termination: "TACC may terminate or modify a User’s access to Information Resources for any reason at any time without notice." (Information Resource Access, Use and Security)
- Data ownership: "Data created on TACC systems is considered the property of the PI and Project (subject to applicable rules from funding agencies and the PI’s employment agreement with their home institution), and neither TACC nor UT shall assert any additional IP rights to this data". (Data)
- Retention: "When a Project is terminated, all Project Data stored in the /home and /work directories on Information Resources shall be retained for a period of 30 days." (Data Retention)
- Protected data: the NSO is the "Networking, Security and Operations team". "Access to Protected Data shall be strictly limited to TACC personnel and Project Users that are authorized by the NSO." (Protected Data)
- Sanctions: the list starts with "Written or verbal warnings" and ends with "Civil or criminal prosecution". (Non-Compliance)

## Evidence and method

This is a policy text with a dated revision history. It has no data.

## Limits

- The AUP does not say how a user gets an allocation. It links to a separate allocations page that the library has not read.
- It does not give a notice or an appeal step before access ends. It says that TACC can end access "without notice".
- It does not say which TACC systems it covers or what they cost.
