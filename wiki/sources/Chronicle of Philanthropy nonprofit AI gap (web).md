# Chronicle of Philanthropy nonprofit AI gap (web)

## Citation

Ben Gose. "The nonprofit AI gap: Bosses are bullish, staffs are wary." The Chronicle of Philanthropy, September 11, 2026. https://www.philanthropy.com/news/the-nonprofit-ai-gap-bosses-are-bullish-staffs-are-wary/. Read 2026-11-09.

## Bottom line

- A news story on a survey of more than 900 nonprofit workers by NTEN and Bridgespan.
- Executives see more promise in AI than staff do. Few nonprofits have AI policies, an AI plan or an AI budget, and few get grants or in-kind help for AI.

## Background

The Chronicle of Philanthropy publishes news on the nonprofit world. A senior editor wrote this story. It reports a survey by NTEN, a network of nonprofit professionals, and Bridgespan, a consulting firm. The survey authors are David Figueroa and Jacob Dwelle. The story quotes Amy Sample Ward, the chief executive officer (CEO) of NTEN.

## Key claims

- "More than 60 percent of executives view AI as a way to reduce staff burdens and increase efficiency, compared with fewer than half of staff members." (opening)
- "44  percent of executives strongly agree that they have meaningful untapped opportunities to use AI, compared with just 23 percent of staff." (opening)
- "Just 4 percent said their nonprofit had “fully integrated” AI into their operations." (Few are fully integrating AI)
- "Most groups lacked written policies on safe and responsible AI usage — fewer than 40 percent had them in place." (Few are fully integrating AI)
- "More than half of executives cited data, privacy, and security concerns as the biggest barrier to greater AI adoption." (Few are fully integrating AI)
- "Very few executives have been able to find outside sources of financial support for AI use, and more than half of executives surveyed said they had no AI budget at all. Only 12 percent had received a grant for an AI project, and just 15 percent said they’d received in-kind donations of tools, training, or services." (A lack of financial support)
- "I think we’ve seen some recognition that nonprofits might need a different pricing model," and "But what that model is, and how it’s accessed, has already changed three times." (A lack of financial support, Amy Sample Ward)

## Evidence and method

A news story about a survey report. The story gives percentages from the survey. About 60 percent of the more than 900 respondents were executives. The story links to the report at NTEN. The library has no copy of the report itself.

Uses of AI that the story gives:

- Marketing and communications: 70 percent of respondents.
- Program delivery and fundraising: half of respondents.
- Strategic planning: 40 percent.

Staff were most worried about the environmental impact of AI. Only 36 percent had significant concern about job loss or a forced change of role.

## Limits

The story does not mention computing infrastructure, CalCompute or the Report. It does not give the survey dates or the country of the respondents. It does not say which AI companies changed their nonprofit pricing.
