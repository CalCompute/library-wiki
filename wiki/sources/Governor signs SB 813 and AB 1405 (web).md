# Governor signs SB 813 and AB 1405 (web)

## Citation

Office of the Governor of California. "Governor Newsom signs first-in-the-nation AI safeguards to protect Californians, calls on the federal government to do its part." Press release, Sept. 9, 2026. https://www.gov.ca.gov/2026/09/09/governor-newsom-signs-first-in-the-nation-ai-safeguards-to-protect-californians-calls-on-the-federal-government-to-do-its-part/. ## Bottom line

- On Sept. 9, 2026, the Governor signed SB 813 (McNerney), a framework for independent verification organizations that assess AI systems and models.
- He also signed AB 1405 (Bauer-Kahan), a state registry for AI auditors.
- The release calls on the federal government to set national AI rules.

## Background

The release announces the two bills and quotes the Governor and both authors. It lists the Governor's earlier AI actions from 2023 to 2026. The downloaded page holds the release text twice. The two copies differ in a few words: the first says "third-party audits and independent assessments", the second "independent assessments".

## Key claims

- "The Governor signed Senate Bill 813, authored by Senator Jerry McNerney (D – Pleasanton), which establishes a first-in-the-nation framework for independent verification organizations that can assess AI systems and models for compliance with state law." (body)
- "Governor Newsom also signed Assembly Bill 1405, authored by Assemblymember Rebecca Bauer-Kahan (D-Orinda), creating a state registry for AI auditors and establishing standards for their independence, transparency, and integrity." (body)
- "This landmark law codifies one of the primary recommendations of the governor’s blue-ribbon panel on AI by establishing independent, third-party evaluations of artificial intelligence to help ensure that the technology remains safe and beneficial." (Senator McNerney)
- "We cannot expect industry to simply grade its own homework; third-party auditors are essential to ensuring AI is safe for our communities and critical infrastructure." (Assemblymember Bauer-Kahan)
- "Earlier this year, Governor Newsom issued an executive order to further strengthen privacy and civil liberties protections around California’s procurement processes and raise the trust and safety bar for artificial intelligence companies seeking to do business with the state." (California is building safeguards as AI accelerates)

## Evidence and method

A government press release. It gives the bills' purpose in one sentence each, without bill text. The "blue-ribbon panel" is the group that wrote the California Report on Frontier AI Policy; the release does not name the report.

## Limits

The release does not say which agency runs the verification framework or the auditor registry, or what they cost. It does not mention CalCompute. The page holds two slightly different copies of the release.
