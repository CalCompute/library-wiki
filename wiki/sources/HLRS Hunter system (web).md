# HLRS Hunter system (web)

## Citation

High-Performance Computing Center Stuttgart (HLRS). "HPE Cray EX4000 (Hunter)." HLRS website. No date on the page ("© 2026 HLRS"). https://www.hlrs.de/solutions/systems/hunter. Read 2026-11-11.

## Bottom line

- Hunter is the flagship supercomputer of HLRS in Stuttgart, an HPE Cray EX4000 system with 752 AMD Instinct MI300A accelerated processing units (APUs). It started operation in February 2025.
- Its theoretical peak performance is 48.1 PFlop/s. HLRS says it uses about 80% less energy at peak than its predecessor, Hawk.
- The German federal research ministry and the Baden-Württemberg science ministry fund Hunter through the Gauss Centre for Supercomputing.

## Background

HLRS is a central unit of the University of Stuttgart and a member of the Gauss Centre for Supercomputing (page footer). This page is the system page for Hunter. It gives the funding, the technical details and related news.

## Key claims

- Shift to GPUs: "Hunter marks a milestone for HLRS, as it moves away from the center’s previous CPU-oriented approach to make greater use of GPUs." (introduction)
- Performance and energy: "With a theoretical peak performance of 48.1 PFlop/s, Hunter's speed is nearly double that of its predecessor, Hawk, while slashing energy requirements at peak performance by approximately 80%." (introduction)
- Transition: "HLRS conceived Hunter as a transitional system that will enable system users to prepare for its upcoming supercomputer, Herder." The installation of Herder "is scheduled to begin in 2027." (introduction)
- Funding: "Funding for the Hunter supercomputer is provided by the German Federal Ministry of Research, Technology and Space and the Baden-Württemberg Ministry for Science, Research and Arts through the Gauss Centre for Supercomputing." (Funding)
- Size: the technical table gives "752 MI300A" APUs, "512 AMD EPYC 9374F" CPUs, 188 APU nodes and 256 CPU nodes. (Technical details)
- Power: the table gives an average energy use of "560 kW". (Technical details)
- Energy rank: "In June 2025, Hunter debuted at number 12 on the Green500 List of the world's most energy efficient supercomputers." (introduction)

## Evidence and method

This is a system specification page from the operator. It gives the funders, the hardware, the storage (25 PB) and the average power. It gives no cost.

## Limits

- The page gives no cost and no funding amounts.
- It does not say who can use Hunter or how to apply.
- The performance figure is a theoretical peak, not a measured result.
