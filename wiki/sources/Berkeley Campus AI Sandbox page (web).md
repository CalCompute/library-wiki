# Berkeley Campus AI Sandbox page (web)

## Citation

UC Berkeley, AI Hub (ai.berkeley.edu), website sponsor Provost's Advisory Council on AI. "Campus AI Sandbox (BETA)." Web page, no date. https://ai.berkeley.edu/resources/campus-ai-sandbox. Read 2026-11-11. Cached copy: `.cache/web/2026-11-11 Berkeley Campus AI Sandbox page.md`.

## Bottom line

- The Campus AI Sandbox is a web chatbot with models from several vendors. It uses LibreChat and runs on the UC contract with Amazon Web Services (AWS).
- The pilot started on March 20, 2025. It is open to Berkeley faculty, staff and student workers for administrative work, and to all Haas and College of Engineering students. One aim is to learn the cost drivers for "a sustainable funding model".

## Background

The page is on UC Berkeley's AI Hub site. The Provost's Advisory Committee on Artificial Intelligence asked for the pilot. The page names an executive sponsor, the campus Chief Information Officer (CIO), and three functional sponsors.

## Key claims

What it is (section "What is the Campus AI Sandbox?"):

- "The Campus AI Sandbox is a web-based tool that lets you use leading AI models like ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and others, all in one place. It's built on the LibreChat open source software package"
- "This is not available generally to students or for academic work at this time."

Purpose of the pilot (section "What is the purpose of the pilot?"):

- "The pilot began on March 20, 2025, and is ongoing."
- "Analyzing the drivers of cost and how people benefit so we understand the costs of making generative AI tools widely available, and also to bring information to PAC-AI, the CIO and campus leaders to enable a sustainable funding model for this."

Security (section "Is the Campus AI Sandbox secure?"):

- "Currently in BETA, as of October 2, 2025 approved for P3 data security levels."
- "The AI Sandbox has been designed to run in the cloud using our UC Contract with AWS"
- "All of the models currently offered leverage the privacy, intellectual property, and security agreements purchased via UC contracts."

Who can use it (section "Who can participate in the pilot?"):

- "Currently, the pilot is open to all Berkeley faculty, staff and student workers, for administrative uses in their daily work. All students at the Haas School of Business and College of Engineering also already have access."
- Others "can submit this short form to sign up to participate in the pilot. This is a manual process and adding approved users may take 1-2 business days."

## Evidence and method

This is a service page. It states the scope, rules and sponsors of the pilot. It gives no usage or cost figures.

## Limits

- The page does not give the cost of the pilot or who pays for it.
- It links the original proposal and the Terms of Use as Google Docs. This library has not read them.
- It does not say when the pilot ends.
