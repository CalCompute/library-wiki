## Bottom line

- The FPPC asks for $360,000 General Fund in 2026-27 and $350,000 each year after, with 1.5 permanent positions. The money is for AB 2355 (Chapter 260, Statutes of 2024).
- AB 2355 requires a disclosure on a campaign advertisement with image, audio or video that AI generated or substantially altered.
- The request includes $100,000 each year for the license of AI-detection software.

## Background

A Budget Change Proposal (BCP) is a department's request for a change to its budget, on the Department of Finance form DF-46. The Political Reform Act of 1974 set up the FPPC. The FPPC says that its earlier proposal for the 2024 bills had no money for AI-detection software, because the costs were unknown. This proposal asks for that money after "months of market research". The library holds no copy of the text of AB 2355.

## Key claims

- "The Fair Political Practices Commission (FPPC) is requesting $360,000 General Fund in 2026-2027 and $350,000 in 2027-2028 and ongoing, including $100,000 AI-detection software annual license fee, and one and a half permanent positions to implement, interpret, enforce, and educate the provisions of Chapter 260, Statutes of 2024 (AB 2355)." (Budget Request Summary)
- "AB 2355 excludes an advertisement from the disclaimer requirement if the media was immaterially altered by artificial intelligence, including a cosmetic adjustment, color edit, cropped image, or resized image." (A. Problem Statement)
- "After months of market research, the FPPC has identified a solution capable of delivering state-of-the-art performance in detecting AI-generated content and would like to request additional funding to cover $100,000 for the software’s annual license fee." (B. Justification)
- "This proposal adds 1.5 positions: one Investigator in the Enforcement Division, and a half-time Attorney position in the Legal Division." (B. Justification)
- "The FPPC will seek the Department of Technology’s approval in January 2026, begin the procurement process with DGS in March 2026, and implement the software by July 1, 2026." (E. Implementation Plan)

## Evidence and method

A state budget request on the DF-46 form, with a resource history and a fiscal detail sheet in thousands of dollars. The positions are 1.0 Special Investigator and 0.5 Counsel from July 1, 2026. The resource history gives the FPPC 111.8 authorized positions and $20,304,000 of authorized expenditures in the current year. The cover sheet marks the proposal as one with information technology components.

## Limits

- The proposal does not say whether the Legislature approved the request. It is not the enacted budget.
- The 2026-27 floor report lists $360,000 in 2026-27 and $350,000 a year after, with the AI-detection license ([Assembly Budget floor report 2026-27 budget 2026-06-27 (web)](Assembly%20Budget%20floor%20report%202026-27%20budget%202026-06-27%20%28web%29.md), p. 115).
- It does not name the AI-detection software or its vendor.
- The cover sheet in the copy gives no names in the Prepared By and Department Director lines.
- It says nothing about CalCompute.
