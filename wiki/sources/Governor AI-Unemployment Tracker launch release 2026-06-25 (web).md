# Governor AI-Unemployment Tracker launch release 2026-06-25 (web)

## Citation

Office of the Governor of California. "California becomes the first state to launch a tool to monitor and track artificial intelligence’s impacts on the workforce." Press release, Sacramento, June 25, 2026. https://www.gov.ca.gov/2026/06/25/california-becomes-the-first-state-to-launch-a-tool-to-monitor-and-track-artificial-intelligences-impacts-on-the-workforce/. Read 2026-11-11. Rehosted copy: `raw/web/2026-11-11 Governor AI-Unemployment Tracker launch release 2026-06-25.html`.

## Bottom line

- The Governor announces the California AI-Unemployment Tracker, a public dashboard from the California Policy Lab's UCLA site and the Employment Development Department (EDD). The release says that it "will be updated monthly".
- The first data show "no evidence of rising statewide unemployment claims in AI-exposed occupations", but claims rose for some groups of high-exposure workers.

## Background

The Office of the Governor publishes this release. It places the tracker under the Governor's executive order on AI and the workforce. It quotes the Governor, the Secretary of the Labor and Workforce Development Agency, the EDD Director and two of the tracker's co-authors.

## Key claims

- "Governor Gavin Newsom today announced the launch of California’s first-in-the-nation tool to proactively track AI-related job loss trends – an early warning system to help the state monitor, track, and anticipate job loss." (opening)
- "The California AI-Unemployment Tracker is a publicly available dashboard developed in partnership between the researchers at the California Policy Lab’s UCLA site and the California Employment Development Department." (Tracking and monitoring AI impacts)
- "Paired with the tracker is a comprehensive analysis of the data, which at this time shows no evidence of rising statewide unemployment claims in AI-exposed occupations." (Initial data shows no evidence of rising unemployment from AI)
- "For example, claims from college-educated workers in occupations with high AI exposure increased after ChatGPT-3.5’s release in 2022, and workers in occupations with high potential AI exposure in the San Francisco Bay Area also experienced a  sustained increase." (Initial data shows no evidence of rising unemployment from AI)
- "Since Governor Newsom took office, the state has supported more than 674,000 earn-and-learn training opportunities, including over 250,000 registered apprenticeships." (Creating a stronger AI-ready workforce)
- The state has "invested nearly $750,000 in the California Workforce Association to develop a statewide AI workforce strategy that will help local workforce boards prepare workers for emerging job opportunities." (Creating a stronger AI-ready workforce)

## Evidence and method

A press release. It summarizes the findings of the California Policy Lab report and gives no data of its own. It also lists earlier AI actions of the Governor, among them SB 53 and the March 2026 executive order on AI procurement.
