# GovOps AI Safety in 2026 and Beyond (web)

California Government Operations Agency (GovOps). "AI Safety in 2026 and Beyond: Recommendations for Legislative Enhancements to California's AI Safety Laws." ## Bottom line

- GovOps gives the Governor recommendations for legislation in four areas: standards, independent verification organizations (IVOs), critical safety incident reporting and emergency shutdown.
- It recommends: "Stand up CalCompute with a surcharge on fees that model developers pay for IVOs". The state can then reserve capacity for safety research.
- It also says that the state needs to hire AI talent and give researchers access to public compute.

## Background

GovOps wrote the report in response to Executive Order N-9-26 ([Executive Order N-9-26 AI oversight (web)](Executive%20Order%20N-9-26%20AI%20oversight%20%28web%29.md)). The California Office of Emergency Services and outside AI experts took part (p. 5). The report builds on SB 53 and on the IVO laws SB 813 and AB 1405 ([SB 813 bill text](SB%20813%20bill%20text.md); [AB 1405 bill text](AB%201405%20bill%20text.md)). Its chapters are Introduction, Standards for Safe AI Development, Independent Verification Organizations, Critical Safety Incident Reporting and Managing Severe AI Crises. The acknowledgments thank four outside AI experts and a lead drafter, who took part "in their personal, individual scholarly capacity" (p. 84).

## Key claims

- "In response to Executive Order N-9-26, the California Government Operations Agency, in consultation with the California Office of Emergency Services and outside AI experts, has developed recommendations for legislative action." (Recommendations for Legislation, p. 5)
- "Stand up CalCompute with a surcharge on fees that model developers pay for IVOs. SB 53 set out a framework to create CalCompute. Since then, the need for publicly owned and operated compute has become more pressing as the demand for chips has risen, pricing out talented researchers who are working on vital safety and security issues." (Recommendations for Legislation, p. 7)
- "A surcharge on IVO fees from the industry can help launch a CalCompute cluster to serve the public interest. The state can reserve capacity for independent safety and security research that serves the public and enhances the capacity for public oversight." (Recommendations for Legislation, p. 7)
- "The state will need a capacity-building strategy to ensure a supply of technical experts as well as sufficient access for researchers to public compute (see section on CalCompute later in this report)." (Standards for Safe AI Development, p. 18)
- "The state should also jumpstart CalCompute, which was outlined in SB 53. The law floated the concept of a public cloud computing cluster, but up until now it has not been funded." (Building State Capacity and Standing Up CalCompute, p. 50)
- "To begin funding CalCompute, we recommend that the state impose a surcharge on the fees that large frontier developers pay for IVO oversight. While the specific surcharge level must be worked out by the legislature, having the developers pay for public compute alongside the fees they pay to IVOs ensures that these companies contribute to a healthy ecosystem that promotes safe and secure AI systems." (Building State Capacity and Standing Up CalCompute, p. 50)
- "As the legislature considers providing further authorities to the state, it should also provide the resources for the state to hire people who can exercise those authorities judiciously and are able to adapt as the technology and its impact on consumers, California infrastructure, systems, and institutions keep changing." (Building State Capacity and Standing Up CalCompute, p. 49)
- "partnering with IVOs as well as national and international AI safety and security institutions – and drawing on public compute and public-interest research by jumpstarting CalCompute – the state should develop its own capacity to detect AI incidents and respond to them effectively." (Critical Safety Incident Reporting, p. 54)
- The state's AI incident monitoring program could work "drawing on available public compute, such as CalCompute once it is up and running." (Critical Safety Incident Reporting, State detection, p. 68)

## Evidence and method

A state agency report to the Governor with recommendations for legislation. It cites research papers, company disclosures, incident reports, polls and news reports in footnotes. It cites [Heim et al 2024 Governing Through the Cloud (web)](Heim%20et%20al%202024%20Governing%20Through%20the%20Cloud%20%28web%29.md) on the role of compute providers (footnote 97, p. 67).

The library read the Introduction and the summary of recommendations (pp. 2 to 8), the passages on CalCompute and public compute (pp. 18, 49 to 50, 54 and 67 to 68) and the acknowledgments. It did not read the other parts of the four chapters in full.

## Limits

The report gives recommendations for legislation, not law. It does not give a surcharge level: "the specific surcharge level must be worked out by the legislature" (p. 50). It does not say who would operate a CalCompute cluster or where it would be. It does not say how much capacity it would reserve for safety research. It does not mention the consortium or the Report under Government Code section 11546.8.
