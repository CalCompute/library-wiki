## Bottom line

- AB 1979 adds Chapter 25.5, "AI Clinical Decision Support", to Division 8 of the Business and Professions Code. It also amends Civil Code sections 56.05 and 56.06.
- A health facility, clinic or physician's office must take reasonable steps so that a licensed provider keeps independent professional judgment. This applies when a clinical decision support system informs the care.
- These places must not use artificial intelligence (AI) to do, alone, a clinical function that the law gives to a licensed person.
- A business that offers a health care chatbot to a consumer is a provider of health care under the Confidentiality of Medical Information Act (CMIA).

## Key claims

- "A health facility, clinic, physician’s office, or office of a group practice shall take reasonable steps to ensure that a licensed health care provider, acting within their scope of practice, retains the ability to exercise independent professional judgment in their care of a patient whenever that care is informed by the output of a clinical decision support system." (Bus. & Prof. Code § 22758.5(a))
- "Independently perform any clinical function that is required by law to be performed by a person with a professional license." (§ 22758.5(b)(1)(B), one of the prohibited uses)
- "“Clinical decision support system” means an artificial intelligence system that produces a prediction, classification, recommendation, evaluation, or analysis that aids clinical decisionmaking related to timing of care, diagnosis, or treatment." (§ 22758.5(e)(4))
- "A violation of this section by a physician is subject to the jurisdiction of the Medical Board of California or the Osteopathic Medical Board of California, as appropriate." (§ 22758.5(c)(1))
- "Any business that offers a health care chatbot to a consumer for the purpose of allowing the individual to manage the individual’s information, or for the diagnosis, treatment, or management of a medical condition of the individual, shall be deemed to be a provider of health care subject to the requirements of this part." (Civ. Code § 56.06(f))

## Limits

The bill gives no cost estimate and no operative date. The copy does not mark which words of sections 56.05 and 56.06 are new. The Digest says that the health care chatbot text is the addition. Section 22758.5 does not apply to automated decision systems for documentation and communication "that does not involve the application of professional judgment" (§ 22758.5(d)). It does not stop a trainee in a supervised program from using a tool (§ 22758.5(b)(2)). The Digest says "licensed health care professional" where section 22758.5(a) says "licensed health care provider".

The Governor's release lists SB 503 with the same subject line ([Governor AI bill signings including AB 2392 2026-09-30 (web)](Governor%20AI%20bill%20signings%20including%20AB%202392%202026-09-30%20%28web%29.md)). The library has no copy of the chaptered SB 503.

The bill does not mention research compute or CalCompute.
