# Cal-Secure 2.0 (web)

## Citation

California Department of Technology (CDT), Office of Information Security (OIS). "Cal-Secure 2.0: State of California Executive Branch Multi-Year Information Security Maturity Roadmap." PDF, 20 pages; no date printed (the PDF file was made July 16, 2026). https://www.cdt.ca.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Cal-Secure-2.0.pdf. Copy: [`raw/web/2026-11-14 Cal-Secure 2.0.md`](../../raw/web/2026-11-14%20Cal-Secure%202.0.md) (original: `.pdf` beside it). Read 2026-11-14.

## Bottom line

- Cal-Secure 2.0 is the five-year cybersecurity roadmap of California's Executive Branch. It follows Cal-Secure of 2021.
- It has three components, People, Process and Technology, with ten priorities and fifteen initiatives. It uses the NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF) 2.0 and a risk-based approach.
- It adds the secure use of generative AI, AI-driven security operations and post-quantum cryptography. It gives shared services to state entities of all sizes.

## Background

The State Chief Information Officer (CIO), Chris Given, and the State Chief Information Security Officer (CISO), Vitaliy Panych, sign its opening message. CDT and OIS wrote it with four partners. They are the California Cybersecurity Integration Center (Cal-CSIC), the Governor's Office of Emergency Services (CalOES), the California Highway Patrol (CHP) and the California Military Department (CMD). It aligns with the state technology strategy "Envision 2026". The Governor's release of Aug. 10, 2026 lists Cal-Secure 2.0 as a 2026 update ([Governor AI Cyber Defense Program release (web)](Governor%20AI%20Cyber%20Defense%20Program%20release%20%28web%29.md)).

## Key claims

- "From 2019-2020, California has invested over $1 billion to enhance cybersecurity across state government. As a result, Executive Branch Agencies and Departments have reached over 80% completion on all 4 Cal-Secure capability priorities" (Cal-Secure 2021 Retrospective, p. 7; the PDF's two-column layout splits this text in the Markdown copy)
- "Within these components, ten key priorities and fifteen forward-looking initiatives provide structure, while toolkits offer mechanisms for entities to understand their cybersecurity maturity journey and implement new technical capabilities." (Executive Summary, p. 6)
- "It recognizes that no single entity defends California alone, Cal-Secure 2.0 is delivered in partnership with private-sector cloud, telecom, and technology providers; federal partners; academia; local governments; and the broader cybersecurity community." (Executive Summary, p. 6)
- "Formalize collaboration with the private sector, federal partners , academic institutions, local government, and information-sharing organizations." (Process initiative, Strengthen Public-Private and Cross-Government Collaboration, p. 13)
- "In partnership with the Cal-CSIC, reinvigorate and expand the existing cyber range to serve as a shared platform for jointly developing, testing, and validating emerging technologies and defense strategies before production deployment." (Technology initiative, Statewide Cybersecurity Innovation Lab, p. 16)
- "Use AI agents to discover vulnerabilities in state-owned code and third-party software before adversaries do" (Technology initiative, Accelerate Vulnerability and Patch Management, p. 17)
- "Develop and implement a statewide toolkit that aligns all cybersecurity roles to the NICE framework, outlines growth trajectories, and provides agencies with strategies and resources for talent retention." (People initiative, p. 10)

## Evidence and method

A state roadmap. It gives one investment figure, one completion figure and its own inputs: "10+ Working Sessions", "500+ Working Hours", "12+ Entities" (p. 9). It gives no budget for the new initiatives. It refers to a separate Cal-Secure 2.0 Toolkit (Capability Map, Organizational Profiles, Maturity Matrix, Service Catalog), which this library does not hold.

## Limits

The roadmap covers the security of state Executive Branch systems. It does not discuss research compute or CalCompute. It gives no dates for each initiative. The Markdown copy mixes the two-column text of some pages; the quotes come from the PDF's reading order.
