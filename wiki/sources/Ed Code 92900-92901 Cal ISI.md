# Ed Code 92900-92901 Cal ISI

## Citation

## Bottom line

- Section 92900 lets the Regents of the University of California (UC) establish four California Institutes for Science and Innovation at separate UC campuses.
- A panel that the Governor selects runs a competitive application process for each institute. Each institute can work with the private sector and with other California colleges.
- Section 92901 states the Legislature's intent for $400,000,000 in state money. Each state dollar must have a match of at least two dollars of nonstate funds.

## Background

Chapter 13 is in the part of the Education Code on UC, and it has two sections. Section 92900 sets up the institutes and their fields. Section 92901 states the funding intent and the match rule. The library's copy comes from the Legislature's code website through the Wayback Machine. The Legislative Analyst's Office (LAO) names the four institutes and their campuses ([LAO 2021-22 Cal Institutes for Science and Innovation (web)](LAO%202021-22%20Cal%20Institutes%20for%20Science%20and%20Innovation%20%28web%29.md), Figure 1). One of them describes the group in its own words ([CITRIS mission and history (web)](CITRIS%20mission%20and%20history%20%28web%29.md)).

## Key claims

- "The Regents of the University of California may establish four California Institutes for Science and Innovation at separate campuses of the University of California for the purpose of combining technological and scientific research and training and educating future scientists and technological leaders." (§ 92900(a))
- "Each institute shall be created pursuant to a competitive application process conducted by a panel selected by the Governor and administered by the University of California." (§ 92900(b))
- "In order to utilize the vast array of research and intellectual resources available from throughout the state, each institute may develop programs in cooperation with the private sector and with California’s other public and independent colleges and universities." (§ 92900(c))
- "Funding for the state’s share of operating and facilities costs under this chapter is subject to appropriation in the annual Budget Act." (§ 92900(e))
- "That the University of California receive seventy-five million dollars ($75,000,000) for each year for four years, for a total of three hundred million dollars ($300,000,000) for the 2000–01 fiscal year to the 2003–04 fiscal year, inclusive, for capital and operating budget purposes." (§ 92901(a))
- "Upon completion of each project, the level of ongoing funding for the operating budget of the institutes will be determined by the Governor and the Legislature through the annual budget process." (§ 92901(c))
- "That the University of California will not seek further state funding for capital outlay associated with these four institutes beyond that provided within the four hundred million dollar ($400,000,000) total." (§ 92901(d))
- "Every dollar of state funds appropriated for these institutes shall be matched by at least two dollars ($2) of nonstate funds, including, but not necessarily limited to, federal and private funds." (§ 92901(e))

## Evidence and method

The statute text, word for word, with its history notes. Section 92900(d) lists six possible fields for an institute:

1. Medicine.
2. Bioengineering.
3. Telecommunications and information systems.
4. Energy resources.
5. Space.
6. Agricultural technology.

Section 92901(b) adds $100,000,000 over the 2001-02 to 2004-05 fiscal years to the $300,000,000 in subdivision (a). Subdivision (c) limits operating spending to 5 percent of the annual appropriation.

## Limits

- Section 92901 states intent. It does not appropriate money, and it does not say how much the state paid.
- The LAO reports $170 million in one-time General Fund over two years for construction. It reports $4.8 million a year from 2002-03 ([LAO 2021-22 Cal Institutes for Science and Innovation (web)](LAO%202021-22%20Cal%20Institutes%20for%20Science%20and%20Innovation%20%28web%29.md), p. 1). These amounts are lower than the $400,000,000 intent total.
- The chapter does not name the four institutes or their campuses.
- It does not set a governance structure for an institute beyond the Regents and the selection panel.
- The capture is from Sept. 22, 2025, and later amendments are not shown.
