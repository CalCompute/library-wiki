# Cal Poly AI Factory unveiling release (web)

## Citation

Cal Poly University Communications and Marketing. "Cal Poly Unveils NVIDIA-Powered AI Factory to Expand AI Education and Research." Press release, October 29, 2026. https://ucm.calpoly.edu/news/cal-poly-unveils-nvidia-powered-ai-factory-expand-ai-education-and-research. Read 2026-11-11. Cached copy: `.cache/web/2026-11-11 Cal Poly AI Factory unveiling release.md`.

## Bottom line

- Cal Poly formally unveiled its AI Factory on Oct. 22, 2026. It has four NVIDIA DGX B200 systems and cost $3 million. The Noyce School of Applied Computing pays for it.
- Faculty use it for research that needs local training, for example on protected patient data that "cannot be sent to outside AI services".

## Background

This release follows the announcement, [Cal Poly AI Factory release (web)](Cal%20Poly%20AI%20Factory%20release%20%28web%29.md). It reports the unveiling at AI Day. It quotes three computer science professors and describes two student projects.

## Key claims

The system (opening paragraphs):

- "New $3 million system gives students and faculty direct access to advanced AI computing"
- "Funded by the Noyce School of Applied Computing, the factory brings together four NVIDIA DGX B200 systems with the storage and networking needed to train large AI models using university-controlled resources. Cal Poly formally unveiled the AI Factory during AI Day on Oct. 22."

Ownership (quote of Alex Bisberg, professor):

- "Owning the hardware means owning our digital destiny"

Research uses (middle of release):

- Jonathan Ventura's system for drone images of eelgrass "matched the quality of that analysis while processing them roughly 10 times faster."
- "Because protected patient information cannot be sent to outside AI services, the AI Factory allows researchers to train models locally."
- Paul Anderson, professor: "Before the AI Factory, we couldn't work on a problem like this" and "Now we have the compute to train frontier models."

## Evidence and method

This is a press release. It reports the unveiling and faculty uses with quotes. It gives no usage or cost data beyond the $3 million.

## Limits

- The release does not say how a user gets time on the system, or if users outside Cal Poly can.
- It does not give operating costs.
