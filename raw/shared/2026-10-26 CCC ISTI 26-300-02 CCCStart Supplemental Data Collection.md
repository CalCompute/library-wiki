CCCStart: Guidance for Supplemental Data Collection and Local Student Onboarding

Purpose

The California Community Colleges Chancellor’s Office is transitioning colleges and districts from
CCCApply to the CCCStart platform through the Reimagine Apply initiative. CCCStart is designed
to provide a simpler, more streamlined, and more consistent statewide admissions experience for
all prospective students.

As part of this work, the Chancellor’s Office and Reimagine Apply Steering Committee have
worked with admissions, student services, data, research, technology, regulatory, and other
subject-matter experts from the Chancellor’s Office, colleges, districts, and system partners to
review the information historically collected through CCCApply.

Given the barriers created for prospective students when the admissions application asks an
extensive number of questions, a central focus of that review has been what information is truly
necessary at the time of admission.

Streamlining of student entry into the system has been achieved by removing or revising
questions in the following cases: they were duplicative;  the data is no longer meaningfully used;
the data is available through other sources or processes; questions are primarily related to post-
admission activities; and/or questions did not reliably produce the intended information. The
result is an intentionally shorter and more focused student experience.

This memo provides guidance for preserving that approach as colleges transition to CCCStart,
particularly when considering supplemental data collection.

Key Principles
These principles should guide local implementation of CCCStart:

1.  CCCStart is the statewide admissions platform. Information collected during the admissions

process should be limited primarily to what is necessary to admit or process the student,

establish the student record, or meet a requirement that must specifically be addressed at

admission, such as eligibility for priority registration. Questions regarding citizenship status

are no longer included or appropriate for the statewide admissions platform.

2.  Admission and onboarding are distinct. Information beyond admission data that helps a

college better understand, engage, or support a student is important, but generally should be

collected through local onboarding mechanisms.

3.  CCCStart Supplemental questions should be limited. Supplemental functionality should

address legitimate needs that cannot reasonably be met elsewhere and should not be used to

recreate the length or complexity of CCCApply.

4.  Residency questions in CCCStart are simplified and sufficient. The streamlined residency

questions included in CCCStart provide sufficient information for districts to determine

residency for the purpose of calculating enrollment fees, in compliance with Chancellor’s

Office updated guidance (see memorandum FS 26-05 CCCStart Transition and Audit

Considerations Related to Residency dated August 27, 2026).

A common statewide admissions experience also provides greater consistency for students
moving among California community colleges, while preserving substantial local flexibility in how
colleges onboard and serve students after admission.

Admission and Local Onboarding
For purposes of this guidance, admission information is information necessary at the point of
admission to:

•  determine or process admission;

•  create or establish the student record;

•  determine eligibility for an admission category; or

•  satisfy a legal, regulatory, or institutional requirement that specifically must be addressed

at the time of admission.

Onboarding information is information used by a college after admission to better understand,
engage, or personalize support for a student. Examples include information used to connect

advising and counseling, or develop a more complete local student profile. Both types of

Local student portals, customer relationship management systems (CRMs), student information

admission form and delaying student entry to the institution. These approaches can also connect
the information collected more directly to the service, outreach, or institutional action it is
intended to support.

Guidance for Supplemental Data Collection
Districts’ use of supplemental data collection in CCCStart should be minimal. Limit supplemental
data collection to exceptional cases rather than using it to expand the core CCCStart question set.
The availability of supplemental functionality should not be interpreted as an opportunity to
recreate questions previously included in CCCApply. Historical collection of data elements, local
interest in the information, or the possibility that information may be useful later does not, by
itself, establish a need to collect it during admission.

Before proposing a supplemental question, colleges and districts should determine:

•  whether CCCStart or another available source already provides sufficient information;

•  whether there is a clear and documented business, regulatory, or institutional need for

the information;

•  whether the information is necessary at the point of admission; and

•  whether the information could reasonably be collected after admission through another

institutional process.

The primary test is:

Is this information necessary at the time of admission, or can it reasonably be collected after the
student has been admitted?

Information primarily supporting outreach, student engagement, service referrals, program
awareness, or further development of a student profile should generally be collected through
local onboarding rather than through CCCStart supplemental questions. Examples of onboarding
questions may include interest in athletics, clubs, transportation assistance, childcare, housing
assistance, counseling, basic-needs services, or similar programs and supports.

Colleges and districts should also avoid asking students for information that has already been
collected elsewhere or is available through another reliable data source. Repeated collection
increases student burden and can create conflicting or duplicative records.

PROCESS TO PROPOSE SUPPLEMENTAL QUESTIONS

To maintain a streamlined and consistent statewide experience, the Chancellor’s Office and
Reimagine Apply Steering Committee will maintain a supplemental question review process and
an Approved Supplemental Question List.

Colleges and districts seeking to use the supplemental collection capability provided by the
Chancellor’s Office through CCCStart should first review the approved list. If an already-approved
question meets the identified need, the standardized question and response options should be
used rather than creating a local variation. College or district personnel who have access to the
supplemental question management tool can then add the already-approved question to their
CCCStart environment.

If no approved question meets the need, a college or district may propose a new question.
Requests should include, minimally:

•  the proposed question and response options;

•  why the information is needed;

•  how the information will be used;

•  the business process or requirement it supports; and

•  why the information must be collected during admission rather than later in the student

journey.

The Chancellor’s Office will first review requests for duplicate or substantially similar questions.
New questions will then proceed through the Reimagine Apply Steering Committee process for
review and a recommendation. The college/district will be notified if their request is approved,
modified or declined. Approved questions will be available for reuse by other colleges and
districts with the same need, and the Approved Supplemental Question List will be reviewed
regularly.

MIS REPORTING AND TRANSITION FLEXIBILITY

The Chancellor’s Office recognizes that changes to information collected through CCCStart may
affect local processes used to populate Management Information Systems (MIS) data element
submission.

Colleges and districts should not add supplemental admission questions solely to reproduce
historical CCCApply data collection or preserve an existing MIS workflow, unless Chancellor’s
Office guidance specifically requires that the information be collected from the student at
admission.

The Chancellor’s Office recognizes the need for flexibility during the implementation, and the
Information Services, Technology and Innovation (ISTI) Division MIS Unit will provide additional
guidance regarding data elements affected by the transition to CCCStart. This may include

MOVING FORWARD

CCCStart represents more than a replacement for CCCApply. It reflects a systemwide effort to
simplify how students begin their educational journey with the California Community Colleges
and to create greater consistency in that experience across colleges and districts.

Colleges and districts retain significant flexibility to design local onboarding, communications,
student services, and engagement processes. That flexibility should be exercised primarily after
admission rather than by expanding the statewide admissions instrument.  Use of third-party CRM
or similar systems for student onboarding should align with and complement the CCCStart
principles and guidance as indicated above.

The overall goal is to reduce the burden for prospective students to gain admission by keeping
the statewide application succinct and focused, while giving colleges the information and tools
needed to effectively serve students.

Additional implementation information regarding the Approved Supplemental Question List, the
supplemental-question review process, and CCCStart supplemental data functionality will be
distributed as those resources become available. Updates to MIS will be handled through the
regularly scheduled MIS webinars and update processes.
