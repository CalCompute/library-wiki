A.        Problem Statement
According to the author of AB 979, “California has a compelling interest in supporting the
development and deployment of AI for the benefit of our constituents and our economy. The
Legislature’s role in crafting AI policy must continue to focus on creating opportunities for
transparency between developers and users to build trust, acceptance, and a sense of
security. By creating a California AI Cybersecurity Playbook, the state can facilitate
information sharing across the artificial intelligence community and strengthen our collective
cyber defenses against emerging threats.”
OIS is an office within the California Department of Technology (CDT) which regularly
collaborates with the California Cybersecurity Integration Center (Cal-CSIC) on statewide
cybersecurity coordination. State departments are adopting new AI components into their
systems at a rapid rate, which continue to present and introduce new cybersecurity risks to the
state. Due to current resource limitations, OIS is not able to assist Cal-CSIC with both creating
the playbook and monitoring the evolving AI risk to the state. Without dedicated resources to
address this security initiative and update policies/standards based on risk to the State, OIS
would be unable to support the AI security initiatives outlined in AB 979.

B.        Justification
To support the goals of AB 979 (Irwin) and ensure effective statewide security oversight of AI
systems, OIS would require $408,000 in General Fund in FY 2026-27 and $286,000 In FY 2027-28.

Funding will support temporary help that will reside within the OIS Security Risk and
Governance unit.
The temporary help would develop, implement, and continuously update the AI security
standards required by the bill. They would work directly with state agencies assessing AI use
cases (i.e. innovative ideas and projects), identifying vulnerabilities, and conducting risk
assessments/consultations. In addition, the limited term position will work with various teams
within SRG to ensure new and existing policies and standards specific to AI use cases are
addressed for all state entities.
C. Departmentwide and Statewide Considerations
As stated in Government Code 11549, OIS was established within CDT to promote and protect
privacy as part of the development and operations of state systems and applications to
maintain the confidence and trust of the residents of this state. These critical security efforts
include ensuring the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of all state systems/applications.
The duty of OIS, under the direction of the State Chief Information Officer, is to provide
direction for information security and privacy to state government agencies, departments,
and offices.
D. Outcomes and Accountability
The temporary position will work collaboratively with OIS management, Cal-CSIC, and state
agencies to assess AI use cases, identify vulnerabilities, and conduct risk assessments while
providing consultations throughout the process.

E. Implementation Plan
Once resources are provided, OIS plans to have the position trained and working with Cal-
CSIC on the development of the playbook prior to September 2026. This will allow 4 months of
dedicated resources to ensure that the statewide AI playbook is created by the January 2027
deadline that is mentioned in the bill.
