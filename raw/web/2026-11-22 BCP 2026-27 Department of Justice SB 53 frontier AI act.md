A.  Problem Statement
Senate Bill 53 establishes the Transparency in Frontier Artificial Intelligence Act (TFAIA), a first-in-the-
nation framework providing regulatory oversight for the development and use of frontier artificial
intelligence (AI) models. The Act establishes disclosure and reporting requirements applicable to
the developers of frontier AI models to guard against catastrophic and other harms.  SB 53
charges the Attorney General with the exclusive and ongoing authority to enforce these
provisions, creating a clear legislative expectation that the Department of Justice will investigate
violations and ensure compliance. The Act also requires the Attorney General accept
whistleblower reports and prepare an annual report to the Legislature regarding those reports.
These are entirely new statutory obligations for the Attorney General’s office. Because they
concern novel issues at the intersection of technology, law and public safety, effective
implementation will require specialized expertise and sustained resources. This work is most
appropriately housed in the Public Rights Division’s Consumer Protection Section (CPS), which
already leads the Departments technology-related enforcement in coordination with other DOJ
programs and offices.

B. Justification
The passage of SB 53, the Transparency in Frontier Artificial Intelligence Act (TFAIA), imposes
significant new requirements on the developers of frontier artificial intelligence models. The Act
establishes disclosure and reporting related to the management, assessment, and mitigation
of catastrophic risks, as well as reporting of critical safety incidents. It also creates new
whistleblower protections and establishes procedures for receiving and reviewing those
complaints.
SB 53 permanently expands the Attorney General’s enforcement responsibilities by granting
exclusive authority to enforce the Act’s provisions, with civil penalties of up to $1,000,000 per
violation. This first-in-the-nation framework establishes essential guardrails to prevent frontier AI
technology from causing catastrophic harm—such as loss of life, the creation of weapons of
mass destruction, or other severe impacts to the state, its residents, and its resources.
Enforcing these provisions will be resource-intensive, requiring sophisticated technical and
factual investigations. Additionally, the Act mandates that the Attorney General receive
whistleblower reports and submit an annual report to the Legislature summarizing those
complaints.
C. Departmentwide and Statewide Considerations
The Attorney General is the State’s chief law officer and is charged by SB 53 with the exclusive
enforcement of the Transparency in Frontier Artificial Intelligence Act’s reporting, disclosure,
and other requirements regarding frontier artificial intelligence models. These requirements
are intended to manage, assess, and mitigate the risk of significant harm to the state, its
people, its environment, and its economy that could result from catastrophic events involving
the AI models covered by the law. Adequately resourcing enforcement ensures that
California remains the national leader in ethical AI governance and public safety in emerging
technologies.
D. Outcomes and Accountability
The Attorney General will use these resources to identify, investigate, and as appropriate bring
enforcement actions to address potential violations of the Act, deterring non-compliance.
The Attorney General will also use these resources to prepare the mandated annual report to
the Legislature.
