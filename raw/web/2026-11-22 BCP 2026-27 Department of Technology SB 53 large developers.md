Budget Request Title
Artificial intelligence models: large developers (SB 53 Weiner)
Budget Request Summary
The California Department of Technology (CDT) requests General Fund (GF) budget authority
(ongoing) for 1.0 Information Technology Manager II (ITM II) and $360,000 (one-time) for temp
help/consultants in Fiscal Year (FY) 2026-27 to implement Chapter 138, Statutes of 2025 (SB 53).
The requested resources will ensure CDT meets statutory deadlines, builds durable in-house expertise,
and delivers on the Governor’s goal of establishing commonsense guardrails that balance innovation
with public safety and reinforce public trust for California.

A. Problem Statement
SB 53 is landmark legislation on Artificial Intelligence (AI) safety, on par with the European AI
Act. The statute carries significant workload with aggressive deadlines requiring timely reviews,
assessments, and consultations with stakeholders and experts within the AI community. Without
new dedicated resources and expertise, CDT will fail to meet the first statutory reporting
deadline, exposing the state to noncompliance with SB 53 and undermining California’s and
the Governor’s credibility and leadership in AI governance. The international community and
the rest of the United States is watching and following California’s lead on AI.
SB 53 assigns CDT new and ongoing statutory responsibilities beginning January 1, 2027.
• First, this bill requires CDT to annually review and, if necessary, recommend updates to
statutory definitions for “frontier model,” “frontier developer,” and “large frontier
developer.” These definitions determine the scope of California’s regulatory authority
and establish thresholds for developer obligations. If definitions are not updated in line
with rapid technological change, they will quickly become obsolete, leaving critical
gaps in oversight and may pose reputational risk on the governance and enforcement
of artificial intelligence to California’s Legislature and the Governor. This bill requires CDT
to stay continuously abreast and be trained in state, federal, and international AI
standards, guidelines, and regulations, and transparency disclosures set forth by AI
developers in order to keep up with the latest developments of this emerging
technology and its impact on 40 million Californians.
• Second, CDT must conduct technical and risk assessments to include assessing
potential technical and social impacts and risks from the frontier AI models to the state
of California and its residents.
• Third, CDT is required to be trained in, track and incorporate developments in federal
and international standards continuously. This is important as federal and international
standards on AI safety and adoption are continuously evolving, including from the U.S.
Federal Government, the European Union, and allied nations and partners of California
such as Canada, the United Kingdom, Australia, and others.
• Fourth, per this legislation, CDT is required to lead continuous extensive structured
stakeholder consultation with academic researchers, civil society advocates, industry
representatives, and government entities. These consultations may need to be
complex with multiple working groups and processes to achieve desired outcomes to
meet this legislative statute.
• Fifth, and finally, CDT is required to capture in an annual legislative report submitted
under Government Code Section 9795 overall developments on AI governance, safety
as described above. This report will serve as the state’s official position on frontier AI risks,
governance, safety, and adoption, and will serve as a model for other U.S. federal,
state, and international government stakeholders.
These requirements represent a new, significant, and specialized workload that cannot be
absorbed by CDT’s current staff. The expertise and requirements outlined in this bill are new for
CDT. Existing staff within CDT’s Office of Digital Services are fully engaged in delivering
statewide web standards, data and geospatial services, accessibility compliance, digital
services oversight, and other policy initiatives and technology and digital strategy initiatives.
CDT does not have the bandwidth or the technical, policy, or governance expertise required
to conduct the annual AI assessments and consultations mandated by SB 53 without
dedicated staffing.
Finally, SB 53 references CDT’s role in supporting the Office of Government Operations Agency
(GovOps) in the development of CalCompute, a statewide public cloud computing cluster.
While CDT must be prepared to contribute governance and technical expertise to
CalCompute, the department’s immediate statutory requirements are the definition reviews,

technical assessments, consultations, and legislative reports due beginning in 2027. CDT will
contribute governance and technical expertise in coordination with its existing cloud services
team. SB 53 also requires large frontier developers to publish transparency frameworks that
demonstrate how they adopt national, international, and industry standards. The ITM II will
provide the necessary oversight for reviewing these transparency disclosures. CDT must review
these disclosures as they relate to CDT’s deliverable to establish definitions and standards as
outlined in this bill.
The scope of SB 53 requires CDT to engage in continuous coordination with GovOps, the
California Office of Emergency Services (CalOES), and the Attorney General, since
stakeholder engagement, incident reporting, and enforcement are interdependent processes
that cannot be managed in isolation. The law establishes mechanisms for reporting critical
safety incidents to CalOES and provides whistleblower protections and civil penalties
enforceable by the Attorney General. CDT must work closely with these enforcement partners
to ensure its reviews and assessments incorporate real-world safety data, reflect the latest
trends, and are aligned with agency and statewide priorities.
B. Justification
This proposal is necessary to ensure CDT has the staffing and expertise to meet its statutory
obligations under SB 53. The mandates create two distinct cost drivers:
1. Statutory obligations that CDT must fulfill, including annual definition reviews, technical
and risk assessments, stakeholder consultations, and legislative reporting.
2. CDT’s supportive role in CalCompute.
Permanent Staffing Needs
CDT requires ongoing funding to support 1.0 ITM II in order to build durable, internal expertise in
AI governance. This ITM II position is required to serve as the primary statewide liaison with
GovOps, OES, and the Attorney General to ensure alignment, avoid duplication of effort, and
manage coordination across statewide AI safety responsibilities. In order to fulfill this request,
CDT will reclassify a vacant internal position.
Working within CDT’s ODS, the requested ITM II will:
• Conduct technical reviews of frontier AI models, including evaluating scaling trends, risk
potential, and compliance thresholds.
• Draft annual legislative reports and respond to legislative inquiries with accurate and
timely information.
• Benchmark California’s statutory framework against federal and international standards
to maintain alignment.
• Design and manage stakeholder consultations that are structured, transparent, and
inclusive of vulnerable communities.
• Coordinate with GovOps and CalOES on CalCompute governance.
• Review transparency disclosures required under SB 53.
Consulting Resources
Consultants are required to supplement the anticipated SB 53 workload requirements in the
initial year. In turn, the proposal includes a one-time request for $360,000 in consulting
resources in FY 2026-27. These resources will provide expertise in frontier model auditing,
adversarial testing, risk modeling, and international regulatory analysis.

CDT does not currently have these skills internally, and they cannot be recruited and trained
quickly enough to meet the statutory deadlines. Consulting resources provide immediate
readiness while permanent staff are trained. Consultants will support CDT during the first
implementation year while the ITM II establishes ongoing processes and internal capacity.
Consultants will also support CDT in evaluating transparency frameworks and incorporating
incident reporting into technical assessments.
Consultants will also provide early expertise in GenAI system auditing and risk evaluation while
CDT develops internal capabilities. Without consultants in the initial year, statutory deadlines
would be missed.
Consequences of Not Funding
Without funding, CDT will not meet the statutory requirements of SB 53. The department will be
unable to complete its first definition review and deliver the required legislative report by June
2027, placing the state in violation of law, and posing reputational risk to the state of California
on AI policy and adoption.
Without the ITM II, CDT would lack the dedicated managerial capacity necessary to
coordinate SB 53 implementation with GovOps, CalOES, and the Attorney General. The
absence of a dedicated resource would result in outdated definitions, insufficient technical
analysis, and inadequate stakeholder consultation. This would erode California’s credibility,
create regulatory gaps, and increase risks to residents. The State would also remain
dependent on external consultants, significantly increasing costs over time compared to
building in-house expertise.
Additionally, CDT would not be prepared to provide technical or governance support for
CalCompute, weakening statewide coordination on AI infrastructure. A lack of resources
would also prevent CDT from implementing SB 53’s transparency oversight, integrating safety
incident reporting, and coordinating whistleblower protections and civil penalties. This failure
would undercut the Governor’s goal of establishing commonsense guardrails that balance
innovation with safety and reinforce public trust.
Failure to invest in GenAI talent recruitment and training would further widen the gap between
the State and private sector, leaving California without the skilled workforce needed to meet
its statutory responsibilities under SB 53.
C. Departmentwide and Statewide Considerations
Departmentwide impacts:
• Provides CDT with dedicated staff for SB 53 implementation, preventing diversion from
other core mandates such as IT oversight, security, and digital services.
• Establishes a dedicated point of coordination within ODS for SB 53 implementation.
• Reduces reliance on vendors over time by institutionalizing expertise within CDT.
• Ensures CDT can coordinate directly with the Office of Emergency Services on incident
reporting and with the Attorney General on enforcement provisions, both of which are
required under SB 53.
• The ITM II position is required to serve as the primary liaison with GovOps, OES, and the
Attorney General to ensure alignment, avoid duplication of effort, and manage
coordination across statewide AI safety responsibilities.
Statewide impacts:

• Reputation on AI Leadership: ensures that the state of California and the Governor
maintain a leading role in AI innovation while taking into account AI safety and risk
mitigation.
• Public Safety and Risk Mitigation: Ensures statutory definitions evolve with technology,
closing regulatory gaps that could otherwise expose Californians to harm.
• Federal and International Alignment: Keeps California’s framework harmonized with
national and global standards, reinforcing credibility and avoiding unnecessary
compliance burdens on developers.
• Equity and Inclusion: Structured consultation processes will capture diverse input,
ensuring voices from vulnerable and marginalized communities are included in
decision-making.
• Clarity and Verifiability: Policy design will prioritize clarity and enforceability, giving
developers certainty and ensuring regulators can verify compliance.
• Innovation and Economic Growth: Provides clarity for AI developers, encouraging
responsible innovation within safe guardrails.
• Cross-Agency Collaboration: Strengthens coordination with GovOps, CalOES, and the
Attorney General’s Office to ensure a coherent statewide approach to AI governance.
• Transparency and Accountability: Implements SB 53’s requirement that large frontier
developers publish safety frameworks, ensuring accountability to the public.
• Enforcement Complements: Reinforces SB 53’s whistleblower protections and civil
penalty provisions through CDT’s coordination with OES and the Attorney General.
CDT will coordinate with GovOps on technical and governance considerations for advanced
computing infrastructure on CalCompute. These responsibilities also advance the Governor’s
framing of SB 53 as commonsense guardrails that balance innovation with safety, reinforcing
public trust in California’s leadership on AI.
D. Outcomes and Accountability
Category A – Statutory Deliverables (mandatory):
• Annual AI definition reviews completed.
• Annual legislative reports submitted to the Legislature.
• Technical and risk assessments integrated into reports.
• Stakeholder forums conducted and documented.
• Recommendations aligned with federal and international frameworks and standards.
• Coordination activities with GovOps, CalOES, and the Attorney General documented.
Category B – Contingent Deliverables:
• CDT technical and governance support to GovOps for CalCompute.

E. Implementation Plan
Pre-Implementation (FY 2026-27):
• Q1: Recruit and onboard the ITM II who will manage SB 53 implementation and
coordinate with GovOps, CalOES, and the Attorney General.
• Q2: Initiate consulting contracts and develop engagement and reporting protocols.
The ITM II will establish coordination processes with GovOps, CalOES, and the Attorney
General.
• Q2: Review existing definitions and transparency reports and requirements from frontier
AI developers.
• Q2: Launch multistakeholder consultations and dialogues.
First Deliverables (January–June 2027):
• Q3: Complete first definition review, continue stakeholder consultations, begin risk
assessments, and implement interagency coordination processes led by the ITM II.
• Q4: Submit first legislative report by June 2027, finalize stakeholder documentation,
provide CalCompute input as needed, and deliver an interagency coordination report
outlining activities with GovOps, OES, and the Attorney General.
Ongoing Annual Cycle (FY 2027-28 and beyond):
• Q1–Q2: Conduct reviews, consultations, and risk assessments, with the ITM II ensuring
integration of safety incident data reported to OES and enforcement actions overseen
by the Attorney General.
• Q3: Draft and validate recommendations.
• Q4: Deliver annual legislative report, document statewide interagency coordination
activities, and support CalCompute deliverables.
Risk Mitigation
• Early recruitment, front-loaded consulting to address compressed timelines, and
quarterly progress reviews to ensure statutory compliance.
