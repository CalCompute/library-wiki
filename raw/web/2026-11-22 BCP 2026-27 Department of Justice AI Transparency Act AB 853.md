A. Problem Statement
Assembly Bill 853 (Chapter 674, Statutes of 2025) requires companies that use generative AI
systems or operate large online platforms to disclose when digital content has been created
or altered by AI. Because these requirements create new consumer-protection obligations,
DOJ will be responsible for monitoring compliance and enforcing violations under its existing
Unfair Competition Law (UCL) authority. Beginning January 1, 2027, the bill requires a large
online platform, as defined, to detect whether any provenance data that is compliant with
widely adopted specifications adopted by an established standards-setting body is
embedded into or attached to content distributed on the large online platform. The law also
requires, beginning January 1, 2028, a capture device manufacturer, with respect to any
capture device the capture device manufacturer first produced for sale in the state on or
after January 1, 2028, to provide a user with the option to include a latent disclosure in content
captured by the capture device that conveys certain information, including the name of the
capture device manufacturer. The law defines “capture device” to mean a device that can
record photographs, audio, or video content, including, but not limited to, video and still
photography cameras, mobile phones with built-in cameras or microphones, and voice
recorders. Also, beginning January 1, 2027, the law prohibits a GenAI system hosting platform,
as defined, from knowingly making available a system that lacks required disclosures.
Collectively, these provisions impose significant new compliance obligations on AI platforms,
online services, and capture device manufacturers, and a corresponding responsibility for the
Attorney General to ensure and enforce statewide compliance.
However, DOJ currently lacks the legal and technical resources necessary to evaluate AI-
generated disclosures, detect non-compliance, and investigate deceptive or misleading AI
practices at the scale required by AB 853. Without additional staffing and technical expertise,
the Department will be unable to effectively enforce the law or protect consumers from
unlawful or deceptive uses of AI-generated content.
The Consumer Protection Section (CPS) primarily enforces state consumer-protection laws,
both civil and criminal, that prohibit unlawful, deceptive, or unfair business acts, including laws
relating to data privacy and security. CPS investigates and litigates consumer protection cases
of statewide and national importance, seeking injunctions, civil penalties, restitution to victims,
incarceration (in criminal cases), and other equitable relief on behalf of the People of the
State of California. These cases often involve complex litigation that affects potentially millions
of people. Typical cases include litigating at the trial and appellate levels in state and federal
courts; conducting in-depth investigations and other legal and factual research; negotiating
and drafting settlement and restitution plans; and working with other law-enforcement
agencies, including district attorneys throughout California, attorneys’ general in other states,
and federal consumer-protection agencies. The Section also engages in significant legislative
advocacy, amicus practice in state and federal appellate courts, and widespread consumer
outreach and education.

B.  Justification
The requested resources, consisting of 2.0 DAGs and 1.0 AGPA, will provide the Attorney
General with resources that will be necessary to identify potential violations, pursue
investigations using formal and informal tools as well as open-source investigative techniques,
and prosecute enforcement actions against companies that violate the requirements of this
law.
C.  Departmentwide and Statewide Considerations
AB 853 is intended to create a comprehensive disclosure and detection framework that would
enable the large-scale classification of content as either authentic or artificial, helping guard
against deepfakes that can cause harm to the state and its people.
D.  Outcomes and Accountability
The requested resources will allow the Attorney General to identify potential violations, pursue
investigations using formal and informal tools as well as open-source investigative techniques,

and prosecute enforcement actions against companies that have violated the requirements
of this law.
