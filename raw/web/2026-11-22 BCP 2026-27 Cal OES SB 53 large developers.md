A. Problem Statement
The Office of Emergency Services requests two positions and $618,000 General Fund in
FY 2026-27 and ongoing to establish a mechanism to be used by a frontier developer or the
public to report critical safety incidents and a mechanism to confidentially submit summaries
of assessments of potential catastrophic risk resulting from use of frontier models, in order to
implement Chapter 138, Statues of 2025 (SB 53)
Artificial Intelligence (AI) remains largely unregulated in the United States, and this legislation
establishes guardrails for large developers in how they create foundation models. This creates
new standards for safety and accountability while clarifying pathways to report catastrophic
risks.
Chapter 138, Statutes of 2025 amends the Business and Professions Code Section 22757.13, et
seq, to require the Office of Emergency Services (Cal OES) to establish a mechanism to be
used by a frontier developer or the public to report critical safety incidents and a mechanism
to confidentially submit summaries of assessments of potential catastrophic risk resulting from
use of frontier models. In addition, Cal OES must report findings of critical incidents to the
Legislature by January 1, 2027, and annually thereafter. This section grants Cal OES the
authority to draft regulations meeting federal guidance.
B. Justification
California is home to 32 of the top 50 AI companies worldwide. Senate Bill (SB) 53 contains a
first of its kind requirement, directing companies to disclose safety incidents involving
dangerous or deceptive behavior by autonomous AI systems. For example, developers
typically place controls on AI systems to prevent systems from assisting with the construction of
a bioweapon or other highly dangerous tasks set by users. If developers catch the AI system in
a lie about how well those controls are working during routine testing, and that lie materially
increases the risk of a catastrophic harm, the developer would be required to disclose that
incident to Cal OES under SB 53. To support these efforts and fulfill the new requirements set
forth in Business and Professions Code Section 22757.13, et seq, (SB 53) Cal OES requests two
positions and $618,000 annually from the General Fund. With this funding, Cal OES will:
• Establish a mechanism to be used by a frontier developer or the public to report a
critical safety incident or confidentially submit summaries of assessments of potential
catastrophic risk resulting from use of frontier models.
• Submit annually a report of findings of critical incidents to the Legislature.
• Draft regulations, as needed, that meet federal guidance.
Cal OES is requesting one (1.0) Associate Government Program Analyst within the California
Cybersecurity Integration Center (Cal-CSIC) to create the reporting mechanism, collect and
aggregate relevant data for the report, and draft the annual legislative report. This includes:
• Leading the effort to create the public facing reporting mechanism.
• Leading the effort to create the confidential reporting mechanism.
• Reviewing the submitted critical incident reports by frontier developers and reviewing
the reports from the public.
• Producing an annual legislative report with anonymized and aggregated information
about critical safety incidents that have been reviewed by Cal OES.
Cal OES is requesting one (1.0) Attorney III to provide legal advice and counsel in the drafting
of regulations, as needed, and the development of the reporting mechanism, including the
drafting and negotiating of software contracts and agreements regarding information storage
and sharing. This includes:

• Drafting and negotiating software contracts and agreements regarding information
storage and sharing.
• Researching relevant state and federal laws impacting Artificial Intelligence to provide
counsel in developing the public facing and confidential reporting mechanisms, while
evaluating legal risks and potential for litigation from developers, and whether
rulemaking is needed to effectively execute the statutory requirements.
• Should rulemaking need to occur, providing legal advice and counsel during the
rulemaking process.
• Providing ongoing legal advice and counsel regarding the development of the
reporting mechanism as well as maintaining the program once it is in place.
C. Departmentwide and Statewide Considerations
Cal OES' mission is to protect lives and property, build capabilities, and support communities
for a resilient California. This proposal will specifically work toward the goals within the Cal OES
Strategic Plan:
• Goal 1: Anticipate and enhance prevention and detection capabilities to protect our
state from all hazards and threats.
• Goal 2: Strengthen California's ability to plan, prepare for, and provide resources to
mitigate the impacts of disasters, emergencies, crimes, and terrorist events.
• Goal 3: Effectively respond to and recover from both human-caused and natural
disasters.
Additionally, this proposal aligns with four of the ten goals set out in the 2021 – 2024 California
State Security Strategy as the 2025 California Homeland Security Strategy is pending
approval).
• Goal 1: Enhance Information Collection, Analysis, and Sharing, in Support of Public
Safety Operations across California.
• Goal 2: Protect Critical Infrastructure and Key Resources from All Threats and Hazards.
• Goal 3: Strengthen Security and Preparedness across Cyberspace.
• Goal 4: Strengthen Communications Capabilities through Planning, Governance,
Technology, and Equipment.
D. Outcomes and Accountability
Cal OES will create a public facing and confidential reporting mechanism, track the
information, and provide an annual report to the California legislature. In turn, this will help
achieve the State’s goals of stronger transparency, innovation, safety, accountability, and
responsiveness related to AI.
E. Implementation Plan
Implementation will begin July 1, 2026.
