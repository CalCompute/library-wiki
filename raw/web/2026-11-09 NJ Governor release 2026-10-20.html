Governor Sherrill Launches NJ Supercomputer Initiative to Drive Innovation, Create Inclusive Economic Opportunity, and Solve Shared Statewide Challenges

Posted on 10/20/2026

More Than $125 Million in Public-Private Investment Includes Support from Co-Founder of NVIDIA, SHI International, and the State of New Jersey

Initiative Will Expand New Jersey’s Capacity for Cutting-Edge Research, Advance Education and Workforce Development, and Create Good-Paying Jobs

NEW BRUNSWICK – Governor Mikie Sherrill today announced the launch of the New Jersey Supercomputer Initiative (NJSCI), a groundbreaking, more than $125 million public-private partnership that will drive the next generation of research, innovation, education, and workforce development across the Garden State.

With support from leaders across New Jersey’s higher education institutions, State agencies, and the NJ AI Hub, as well as commitments from technology industry leaders, NJSCI will establish a supercomputer that serves as a cutting-edge, shared resource to meet the advanced computing needs of researchers, students, entrepreneurs, businesses, and other organizations across the state. The supercomputer will allow faculty and students to conduct advanced research on key challenges like cancer research and flood risk mapping, in a cost-effective, timely way that would be impossible with conventional computers. The initiative is expected to double the current supercomputing capacity available in aggregate across all of New Jersey’s research universities and dramatically increase available capacity for the most advanced supercomputing hardware, while also expanding world-class education and training, unlocking economic opportunity, and creating good-paying jobs for New Jerseyans.

“From Thomas Edison to Bell Labs and beyond, New Jersey has earned its reputation as the Innovation State through groundbreaking research and discoveries that transform lives. Today, we are building on that proud legacy by launching the New Jersey Supercomputer Initiative,” said Governor Sherrill. “This major initiative will ensure that New Jersey strengthens its longstanding leadership in using new technologies to uncover scientific breakthroughs, to help our students build the skills and experience they need to compete for jobs in high-demand fields, to expand opportunities for our workforce, and to improve quality of life throughout our communities. We look forward to seeing how this game-changing investment in New Jersey’s future will accelerate cures for diseases, strengthen environmental resilience, and solve many other challenges facing our state.”

This initiative will be supported by more than $125 million in public and private investment. That includes a $75 million personal gift from Chris and Melody Malachowsky. Chris is a co-founder of NVIDIA and a Monmouth County native. The initiative is also benefitting from a $25 million investment from SHI International, a leading IT company based in Somerset County. SHI is working alongside the State and its university partners to provide the technical and strategic guidance needed to bring the initiative forward. SHI’s work spans architecture and design, integration of the partner technology ecosystem, implementation, and the hosting and day-to-day operation of the infrastructure. Through the New Jersey Economic Development Authority, the State is also investing $25 million, which was appropriated for the initiative in the Fiscal Year 2026 budget.

The NJ Supercomputer Initiative is a partnership between all 8 of New Jersey’s higher education research institutions — Kean University, Montclair State University, the New Jersey Institute of Technology, Princeton University, Rowan University, Rutgers University, Seton Hall University, and Stevens Institute of Technology — as well as the NJ Council of County Colleges. As part of this partnership, each institution plans to provide annual contributions to the initiative’s budget. Pathways for additional higher education institutions from across the state to participate will be announced in 2027.

“New Jersey is where my story began, and it means a great deal to give back to the community that raised me. I am grateful to Governor Sherrill and our partners across the state for making this a reality,” said Chris Malachowsky, NVIDIA co-founder and fellow, who is providing, together with his wife Melody, a personal donation of $75 million. “Through the New Jersey Supercomputer Initiative, we can unlock the talent that exists all around us, transform schools, businesses, and the workforce, and together, tackle our most pressing challenges.”

“New Jersey is positioned to show what's possible when government, higher education and industry come together around a shared goal,” said Thai Lee, President and CEO of SHI International. “SHI is proud to support an initiative that reflects the power of public-private partnership, bringing together the expertise and technology needed to help address real challenges facing our communities. By expanding access to advanced AI resources, this initiative is designed to give New Jersey the capacity to accelerate research, strengthen education and drive innovation. We're pleased to stand alongside the state and its leading institutions in an effort that will help shape how New Jersey competes and leads for years to come.”

The supercomputer will be administered starting in 2027 by NJSCI, an independent nonprofit organization, and guided by principles including transparent governance and equitable access. As part of the Sherrill Administration's whole-of-government approach, several agencies, including the New Jersey Innovation Authority, the New Jersey Economic Development Authority, the New Jersey Department of Labor and Workforce Development, and the New Jersey Office of the Secretary of Higher Education, are providing support to this effort.

“NJSCI will ensure that the transformative capabilities of new technology are readily available to New Jersey’s leading innovators and are put to work for the benefit of all New Jerseyans,” said Dave Cole, New Jersey State Chief Innovation Officer. “This initiative brings public and private partners together to ensure New Jersey has the capacity we need to solve the complex challenges of the 21st century.”

“From advanced manufacturing to biotech, to the life sciences and artificial intelligence, Governor Sherrill is ensuring that companies in high-growth industries aren’t locked out of the resources they need to make discoveries and expand in the Garden State,” said NJEDA Chief Executive Officer Evan Weiss. “The AI Supercomputer initiative exemplifies the impact of partnerships between government, academia, and the private sector in driving innovation, and the breakthroughs made in New Jersey through this plan will advance economic opportunity and startup creation.” 

"Strategic partnerships like this are how we create real economic opportunity — connecting New Jersey workers to good-paying jobs while keeping our businesses competitive," said Kevin D. Jarvis, Acting Commissioner of the NJDOL. "The apprenticeships and training programs tied to this initiative will help New Jersey workers develop the skills needed to succeed in AI, data science, and advanced computing fields where employers are actively hiring."

“Technology is moving at an exponential pace. New Jersey’s supercomputer initiative opens new possibilities for our institutions of higher education to advance meaningful research, prepare students for the jobs of tomorrow, and translate innovation into real-world solutions,” said Margo Chaly, New Jersey Secretary of Higher Education. “Expanding access to advanced computing and research opportunities empowers our students to be leaders of an innovative future with the high-quality education they’ve obtained right here in the Garden State.”

Dozens of projects are being reviewed for potential supercomputer use in 2027, including those that leverage high-performance computing and artificial intelligence capability to model flooding and protect coastal communities, detect and treat cancer and other diseases earlier, accelerate drug development, and improve the safety of large language models.

Research infrastructure is only part of the equation. The State is also focusing on translating these capabilities into broader opportunities for New Jersey residents. As part of this effort, the AI Higher Education and Workforce Collaborative is working across the state’s colleges and universities to strengthen education and workforce pathways. This includes developing new curricula, hands-on training programs, and apprenticeship pathways ranging from AI literacy and responsible use to advanced computing, data science, and related disciplines. Students will gain direct experience with cutting-edge computing infrastructure through their research projects, capstones, and coursework, while developing skills that employers are actively seeking.

The supercomputer infrastructure will be hosted and operated by SHI in its existing New Jersey facilities and the NJSCI will bring new clean energy onto New Jersey’s grid, in line with the Sherrill Administration’s overall commitment to energy affordability and sustainability. To learn more about the New Jersey Supercomputer Initiative, visit NJSupercomputer.org, where additional information will be posted as it becomes available.

What They’re Saying:

“We are proud to help bring this extraordinary vision to life alongside partners who share our ambition for New Jersey’s future,” said Liat Krawczyk, Executive Director of the NJ AI Hub. “This investment is about much more than computing power. It is about democratizing access to the tools of scientific discovery, giving researchers and entrepreneurs the ability to tackle problems once beyond their reach, and creating new pathways for students and workers to participate in the AI economy. Together, we are building the foundations for a future in which breakthrough innovation and economic opportunity reinforce one another.”

“The New Jersey Supercomputer Initiative is a strategic investment in our state’s research capacity, economic competitiveness and future prosperity,” said Kean University President Lamont O. Repollet, Ed.D. “By expanding access to advanced computing, we can accelerate discovery and prepare students not merely to enter emerging fields, but to shape them. Kean’s participation reflects our conviction that research excellence and equitable opportunity must advance together — translating technological innovation into social mobility and lasting public benefit.”

“The promise of supercomputing lies not only in its extraordinary technological capabilities, but in ensuring access to cutting edge tools is broad and equitable,” said Montclair University President Jonathan Koppell. “At Montclair State University, we are committed to making the benefits of our environmental science, community health, and AI research accessible to our State’s diverse population in order to accelerate solutions to societal challenges that were previously beyond our computational reach.”

“Large-scale computing has become one of the real limits on what we can discover. Many good ideas, whether in a lab or a classroom, wait because the machine needed to test them is out of reach,” said New Jersey Institute of Technology Interim President John Pelesko. “The New Jersey Supercomputer Initiative lowers that barrier for researchers and students across the state and we are grateful to Governor Sherrill, to Chris and Melody Malachowsky for their generosity, and to SHI and our fellow universities for building it together. As New Jersey’s public polytechnic university, NJIT will work to make sure that access turns into new discoveries, and into graduates ready for an economy that AI is rapidly reshaping.”

“The New Jersey Supercomputing Initiative will provide scientists and engineers with access to state-of-the-art computing resources that are essential for research that catalyzes discovery with potential to improve health, security, and prosperity in New Jersey and beyond,” said Princeton University President Christopher L. Eisgruber. “Princeton University is grateful to Governor Sherrill and Chris Malachowsky for their leadership on this powerful collaboration.”

“The NJ Supercomputing Initiative will help shape the future of education, research and industry by accelerating scientific discovery and opening new opportunities for innovation,” said Rowan University President Dr. Ali A. Houshmand. “Rowan is committed to being at the forefront of that future, advancing research, driving innovation and preparing the next generation to lead in an AI-driven world.”

“Responsible innovation changes lives, and this initiative puts that principle into practice. Access to advanced computing will allow Rutgers researchers and students to accelerate discoveries in health and life sciences, strengthen climate modeling and resilience planning, and prepare the next generation of the workforce,” said Rutgers President William F. Tate IV. “We are grateful to Governor Sherrill, Chris and Melody Malachowsky, SHI International, and our fellow institutions for a collaboration that will ensure these powerful tools serve the people of New Jersey.”

“New Jersey has always understood that investing in education and innovation ultimately means investing in people. By giving our faculty and students access to cutting-edge computing capabilities, this initiative will open new possibilities for research, discovery and learning, while preparing our students to lead in a rapidly changing world. In accordance with the Catholic Intellectual Tradition, Seton Hall recognizes that innovation must serve a greater purpose: advancing knowledge, upholding human dignity and contributing to the common good,” said Seton Hall President Msgr. Joseph Reilly. “On behalf of Seton Hall, I am grateful to Governor Sherrill, Chris and Melody Malachowsky, and all of the public and private partners whose investment and vision are expanding opportunities for our students and helping ensure that New Jersey remains one of the best places in the nation to live, work and learn.”

“Stevens Institute of Technology is proud to be a founding member of the New Jersey Supercomputer Initiative, creating exciting opportunities to accelerate research, expand collaboration and prepare the next generation of talent to lead in an increasingly technology-driven world. At Stevens, we are advancing AI-driven research across a range of critical fields, from predicting floods and strengthening coastal resilience to developing financial technologies and autonomous robotics,” said Stevens Institute of Technology President Nariman Farvardin. “Access to advanced computing will build on this expertise, enabling our students and researchers to push the boundaries of discovery, accelerate AI-driven innovation and translate research into solutions with meaningful real-world impact. By bringing together New Jersey’s research universities and industry partners, this initiative will strengthen our state’s innovation economy, advance its leadership in technology and improve lives across New Jersey.”

“The New Jersey Supercomputer Initiative will greatly expand cutting-edge research and educational opportunities for community college students across the state and will enhance the efforts of the state's 18 community colleges to prepare all New Jerseyans to thrive in an AI economy,” said New Jersey Council of Community Colleges President Aaron Fichtner.

“Advanced computing can help turn New Jersey’s research strengths into economic opportunity,” said Amy Herbold, President and CEO of Choose New Jersey. “From accelerating drug discovery to helping companies bring new technologies to market, the New Jersey Supercomputer Initiative will support innovation, attract investment, and create new opportunities for business and residents across our state.”
