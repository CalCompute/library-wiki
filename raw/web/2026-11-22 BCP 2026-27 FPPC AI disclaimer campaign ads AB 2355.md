A. Problem Statement
The FPPC was created by the Political Reform Act of 1974 (the “Act”) as an independent non-
partisan agency whose main duties are to ensure transparency in political campaign
spending and that government officials comply with state government ethics laws. The FPPC
has primary responsibility for the impartial and effective administration and implementation of
the Act. The FPPC regulates and enforces actions performed by governmental officials and
agencies and requires extensive disclosure reports to provide the public with access to
government processes.
AB 2355 amended the Act to require a campaign committee that creates, originally publishes
or originally distributes a political advertisement that is an image, audio, or video that was
generated or substantially altered using artificial intelligence, as defined, to include on the
advertisement a disclosure that the advertisement was generated or substantially altered using
artificial intelligence.
AB 2355 specifies that an image, audio, video, or other media is generated or substantially
altered using artificial intelligence if either:
1. It was entirely created using artificial intelligence and would falsely appear to a
reasonable person to be authentic.
2. It was materially altered by artificial intelligence such that a reasonable person would
have a fundamentally different understanding of the altered media when comparing it to
an unaltered version.
AB 2355 excludes an advertisement from the disclaimer requirement if the media was
immaterially altered by artificial intelligence, including a cosmetic adjustment, color edit,
cropped image, or resized image.
The Act requires the FPPC to investigate alleged violations of the Act and pursue legal remedies
when evidence of violations is found, including seeking injunctive relief to compel compliance
and pursuing administrative or civil remedies. The Act also requires the FPPC to provide advice
and guidance to those subject to the requirements of the Act.

B. Justification
AB 2355 went into effect on January 1, 2025. AB 2355 requires a disclaimer statement on
campaign advertisements with image, audio, or video that was generated or substantially
altered using artificial intelligence. The bill authorizes the commission to enforce violations of
these disclosure requirements.
The FPPC submitted a BCP last fiscal year to address various enacted 2024 legislation. As noted
in the previous BCP, the proposal did not include additional costs for artificial intelligence
detection software or licenses to effectively implement AB 2355. Due to limited resources in the
market at the time, the costs for artificial intelligence detection software or licenses were

unknown. It had stated that the FPPC would continue to seek effective tools; future BCP
requests would follow to cover software costs. After months of market research, the FPPC has
identified a solution capable of delivering state-of-the-art performance in detecting AI-
generated content and would like to request additional funding to cover $100,000 for the
software’s annual license fee.
In addition, the FPPC received three positions from the approved BCP to implement the 2024
legislation. Since the software is not yet available this year, only one Attorney position was
included in the previous request to begin implementing AB 2355, while two positions were
allocated to implement the remaining 2024 bills. The newly hired attorney will develop new
investigation plans and evaluate evidence that the Enforcement Division currently does not
evaluate. Attorney involvement is increased when investigations are contentious. The
Enforcement Division anticipates a significant amount of argumentation/litigation regarding
what would falsely appear to a reasonable person to be authentic, and also whether an
artificial intelligence alteration would cause a reasonable person to have a fundamentally
different understanding of the media in question. Furthermore, the complaints generated will
often be perceived as potentially election-affecting because of the alleged deception and
potentially damaging nature of the created or altered media.
With the artificial intelligence detection software procurement expected in 2026 and an
election year approaching, the Enforcement Division anticipates receiving a large number of
complaints that artificial intelligence has been used without the required disclosure. Most of
these complaints will likely not be susceptible to determination based on initial visual analysis.
Furthermore, many of these creations and alterations are likely to be deceptive in nature, as
the bill itself assumes. Therefore, for investigation staff, requests will need to be made to obtain
source documents to determine authenticity or the level of alteration. It is believed that an
unusually high percentage of these types of complaints will lead to uncooperative
investigations, causing an enhanced need for subpoenas, interviews, and third-party efforts.
Enforcement Division would expect the need for significant training or the hiring of
investigators with experience in the use of artificial intelligence, a significant increase in
complaints, and a significant number of difficult investigations with well-financed
individuals/committees having a desire not to be forthright in an area of technical complexity.
In addition, the Legal Division anticipates numerous advice requests will result from this new
law, both as to the interpretation and application of the new requirement itself as well as its
impact on existing ad disclosure requirements. As artificial intelligence is a new and rapidly
evolving technology, the advice requests will likely involve novel questions and concepts that
will require extensive research by the attorney preparing the advice. Further, educational
materials covering advertising disclosure (e.g., advertising disclosure charts, fact sheets, and
training materials) will need to be supplemented to address the new requirement. A Legal
Division attorney will be responsible for preparing/reviewing those updated materials.
This proposal adds 1.5 positions: one Investigator in the Enforcement Division, and a half-time
Attorney position in the Legal Division. These positions will perform all functions necessary to
advise committees on their obligations under these new state mandates and enforce those
new mandates.
C. Departmentwide and Statewide Considerations
The FPPC is charged with the primary responsibility for the administration and enforcement of
the Act. This request is consistent with that mandate.
D. Outcomes and Accountability
The FPPC will have the necessary resources to implement the provisions of AB 2355 effectively.

E. Implementation Plan
The FPPC will seek the Department of Technology’s approval in January 2026, begin the
procurement process with DGS in March 2026, and implement the software by July 1, 2026.
Hiring and training will begin upon approval to address the increased workload for the
Enforcement and Legal Divisions.
